# Mental-Models for Multi-Agent Systems

**Source**: Gani et al., arXiv, 2026-10-08

**Lead**: Large foundation models converse fluently but still lag humans on interactive reasoning—inferring what other agents know, want, and are likely to do under partial observability. This paper introduces *mental-model-enabled agents*: a framework that learns an amortized latent Theory-of-Mind (ToM) representation of a counterpart from interaction history, couples it with a belief-conditioned reward model, and trains the policy under that signal. At inference time only the standalone policy runs, while the gains from explicit partner modeling persist across language-only and multimodal benchmarks.

## Background

Most LLM multi-agent systems improve behavior through shaping or scaffolding—instruction tuning, preference RL, self-critique/reflection prompts, memory, or synthetic self-play—rather than explicit partner-state estimation. Partner dynamics live only implicitly in prompt text or trajectory summaries, so there is no belief state with clear semantics and quantified uncertainty that can be reused across tasks or plugged into different policy learners. Classical work on interactive POMDPs shows effective multi-agent behavior depends on maintaining beliefs over hidden state (other agents' objectives, information states, internal models), but extending that principle to foundation-model agents is nontrivial: natural-language hypotheses appended to prompts are inexpressive and tightly coupled to prompt formulation.

## Amortized Recursive Mental-Model Learning

The acting agent observes history $h_t$ and selects action $a_t$, while the counterpart's hidden variables (goals, beliefs, knowledge, strategies) are denoted $z_t$. Since the exact posterior $p(z_t \mid h_t)$ is intractable, the method learns an amortized approximation $q(z_t \mid h_t)$ mapping observable history to a compact latent belief state.

ToM is inherently multi-order, so the latent is factorized recursively into first-order partner state $z_t^{(1)}$ and higher-order state $z_t^{(2)}$ conditioned on it, each decomposed into belief, intent, and thought subspaces and modeled as multivariate Gaussians (VAE-style). The latent is grounded with a compressed inference objective combining a ToM discrepancy term against mental-state annotations and a recursive variational bottleneck.

## Mental-Model-Aware Utility and Policy Learning

A latent alone cannot act, so the framework jointly learns a utility predictor scoring candidate actions conditioned on the sampled mental state, marginalized over plausible partner states. Joint optimization is deliberate: a mental model trained only on annotations becomes interpretable but decision-irrelevant, while a reward model trained without the latent explains scores through surface features. The joint objective combines candidate reward, mental-state inference, and auxiliaries (rationale-conditioned utility, future-action prediction, hard-negative ranking).

Policy learning then freezes the mental-state encoder and scorer, samples candidate actions from the current policy, scores them with the mental-model-aware reward, and optimizes with GRPO plus a KL term toward the reference policy. Deployment uses only the trained policy—no mental-model inference at serving time.

## Experiments

**Setup**: LoRA adapters on 7B-scale backbones (Qwen2.5-7B primary; LLaMA2-7B-Chat and Mistral-7B-Instruct as additional Sotopia comparisons; Qwen2.5-VL for MMRole). Sotopia-$\pi$ episodes augmented with ToM labels and utility annotations; 5K synthetic BigToM-style samples for BigToM/ToMi training; MMRole training set augmented with mental labels. Craigslist-bargain and FANToM used only as transfer evaluations.

**Benchmarks**: SOTOPIA (open-ended social interaction), Craigslist-bargain (negotiation), ToMi, BigToM (controlled ToM reasoning), MMRole (multimodal role-play), FANToM (zero-shot multi-party transfer).

**Results**:

| Benchmark | Metric | Base | Ours |
|-----------|--------|------|------|
| Sotopia-all (Qwen2.5-7B, GPT-4o-mini partner) | Overall score | 3.290 | 3.856 |
| Sotopia-all (LLaMA2-7B) | Overall score | 3.330 | 3.541 |
| Sotopia-all (Mistral-7B) | Overall score | 3.511 | 3.784 |
| BigToM Forward Belief (paired TB&FB, no init belief) | Accuracy | 77.0 | 96.0 |
| BigToM Backward Belief (paired TB&FB, no init belief) | Accuracy | 77.5 | 95.5 |
| ToMi (BigToM-trained policy, OOD) | Average / Joint | 87.92 / 55.16 | 89.16 / 60.46 |
| MMRole | Normalized overall | 0.994 (MMRole-Agent) | 1.023 |

Qwen2.5-7B gains 11.5% on average across partner settings on Sotopia; paired BigToM accuracy improves by 26.7pp on average across the six reported settings. A Sotopia-trained agent transfers to Craigslist-bargain without task-specific fine-tuning (deal rates ~94%, fewer walkaways), and zero-shot transfer to FANToM holds. Ablations show the recursive first/second-order factorization and the joint mental-reward objective each matter; flat latents conflate distinct estimation problems.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

Explicit partner modeling is a reusable pattern for agentic coding systems: a review bot, pair-programming copilot, or multi-agent workflow (planner–executor–reviewer) must infer a counterpart's intent, conventions, and hidden constraints from partial history. This paper shows how to train that capability into the policy while keeping deployment cheap—a frozen scorer guides training, and serving runs only the policy. The recipe (augment interaction logs with mental-state/utility labels, train a belief-conditioned reward, distill via GRPO) ports directly to agent fine-tuning pipelines, and the cross-domain transfer results suggest partner-modeling data from one setting (e.g., code review dialogues) can help in another (e.g., incident negotiation).
