# DiffusionART: Efficient, Stable and Scalable Reinforcement of Diffusion Models

**Source**: Wan et al., arXiv, 2026-10-08

**Lead**: Online RL fine-tunes diffusion models with black-box rewards, but reverse-process methods (Flow-GRPO) suffer poor credit assignment while forward-process methods (DiffusionNFT) are efficient yet unstable without carefully tuned per-task EMA schedules. This paper diagnoses both failure modes precisely—a hidden forward–reverse consistency force that joint positive–negative training cancels only partially, and EMA stabilizing through restoration rather than off-policy data—and introduces DiffusionART, which removes the consistency force by construction via aggregation-first reward tilting and replaces EMA with drift-adaptive restoration. It needs no differentiable reward, works with few samples per prompt, and supports pairwise reward models.

## Background

Online diffusion RL evaluates clean samples from the evolving generator with black-box rewards, facing a credit-assignment problem: output-level feedback must become updates across many denoising states. Reverse-process RL optimizes sampled denoising transitions with trajectory-level advantages (poor per-transition credit assignment, needs ODE-to-SDE conversion). Forward-process RL (DiffusionNFT) forward-noises generated endpoints and regresses velocities with reward-weighted positive/negative objectives—efficient but unstable without joint positive–negative training plus a per-task-tuned EMA rollout policy (e.g., the default EMA schedule collapses on OCR and fails to transfer from SD3.5-M/GenEval to FLUX).

## Diagnosis: Consistency Force and Restoration

The paper's first contribution is theory: NFT targets built from forward-noised endpoints implicitly assume the model's sampling velocity equals the ideal forward velocity induced by its own endpoints. The gap $e$ introduces a non-reward-improving consistency force—positive branch pulls toward the ideal forward velocity, negative pushes away. Joint training sums the guidance ($g + g$) but opposes along $e$, leaving residual $(2q-1)e$ that reward centering balances only on average across samples, never per state; the residual grows as denoising reveals endpoint quality. Second, EMA's stabilizer is identified as the quadratic restoration term toward the lagged reference in the loss, not off-policy rollouts (ablation: EMA rollouts without the old-policy anchor still collapse). But EMA lag must be tuned per task—too little collapses, too much stalls—and two-updates-per-rollout without EMA avoids collapse at 15.2x slower training because heterogeneous guidance cancels across states while restoration dominates.

## DiffusionART Method

**Aggregation-first guidance**: exponentially tilt each prompt's rollout distribution toward higher rewards; the first-order endpoint-mean shift equals the reward–endpoint covariance. Estimate it from the prompt's $K$ endpoints, RMS-normalize to $G(c)$ (removing reward scale/spread, no guidance coefficient), and regress every visited reverse-rollout state's velocity toward cached prediction $+ G(c)$. Since the target never involves the forward velocity, the consistency force is removed by construction, and each state gets an explicit reward-improving direction instead of a scalar advantage through likelihood ratios.

**Two-update restoration**: split each rollout batch into two disjoint minibatches with the same frozen target. The second update's gradient combines guidance $G(c)$ with restoration $-d(s_t)$ proportional to each state's drift—adaptive per state, no schedule to tune. Sharing $G(c)$ across a prompt's states reduces the cross-state cancellation that let restoration dominate in NFT.

**Trust-region masking** for ODE rollouts adds a final safeguard against excessive updates (matters most for video; the no-mask run drops around step 200). A pairwise-feedback extension (DiffusionART-Pairwise) pools same-prompt pairs before computing covariance.

## Experiments

**Setup**: T2I on SD3.5-M (LoRA r=32, 512×512, GenEval/OCR/PickScore + 4-reward joint) and FLUX.1-dev 12B (GenEval); T2V full-parameter fine-tune of HunyuanVideo-13B (53 frames, 480×480, 16 steps, VideoReward TA/VQ/MQ); I2V on Wan2.1-I2V-14B. Baselines: Flow-GRPO, Flow-GRPO-Fast, DanceGRPO, Flow-DPPO, DiffusionNFT ± EMA.

**Results**:

| Setting | DiffusionART vs. baseline |
|---------|---------------------------|
| SD3.5-M: reach Flow-GRPO step-500 reward (GenEval / OCR / PickScore) | 10.0x / 5.2x / 9.6x fewer steps |
| HunyuanVideo T2V, VQ:MQ=1:1: reach baselines' late-stage mean reward | 12.02x fewer GPU hours than DiffusionNFT, 11.72x fewer than Flow-GRPO-Fast |
| HunyuanVideo T2V: mean reward over final 50 steps | 13.018 vs. 10.105 (NFT, +28.8%) and 8.510 (Fast, +53.0%) |
| HunyuanVideo T2V (7:2:1 weights): VQ 5.5 | 24 steps / 63.4 GPU-h vs. 273 steps / 1,401.4 GPU-h for NFT-EMA (11.4x steps, 22.1x GPU-h) |
| HunyuanVideo T2V eval: VQ / MQ | +31.4% / +57.4% over strongest baseline |
| Wan2.1 I2V: reach MQ 6 | 45.8 vs. 220.3 GPU hours (4.8x fewer) |
| Group size $K=2$ vs. $K=8$ (pairwise, same 400 steps) | equal-or-better final VQ at 3.4x fewer GPU hours |

DiffusionART matches NFT-EMA efficiency on SD3.5-M with no EMA or per-task tuning, learns fastest on FLUX where transferred NFT hyperparameters fluctuate, and retains gains through 400 T2V steps. Code is released.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

Anyone fine-tuning generative image/video models on human-preference or quality rewards (black-box, non-differentiable) faces exactly this stability tax: per-task EMA/schedule tuning that silently fails when transferred to a new backbone. DiffusionART's two ideas port beyond diffusion: (1) replace per-sample advantage-weighted updates with a prompt-level aggregated direction normalized to remove reward scale—simpler and robust to reward-model swaps; (2) get stability from a second disjoint-minibatch update with drift-proportional restoration instead of a tuned EMA schedule. The $K=2$ result is operationally significant: one pair per prompt suffices, cutting rollout compute 4x. The trust-region masking ablation also warns that for long-horizon rollouts (video), an explicit update cap is worth adding even when training curves look healthy early.
