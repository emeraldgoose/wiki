# Ego Guidance: Enhancing Robot Policy Learning with Action-Free Human Egocentric Videos

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13685)
**Date**: 2026-11-19

## Overview

This paper investigates whether human egocentric videos, which are abundant and easy to collect but almost never carry action annotations, can improve robot policy learning without any action supervision. Rather than adopting a World Action Model that diffuses in a generative latent space, the authors start from a well-optimized pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) and expand it into a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT). The resulting architecture, Shared Prediction on Action and Representation (SPAR), couples an action-prediction expert with a generation expert that predicts representations of the future visual state, so that visual state prediction provides an independent and complementary learning signal alongside action prediction.

A central finding is that representations learned from action-free egocentric videos improve downstream performance on out-of-distribution robot tasks, and that this benefit grows as the amount of video data scales. The authors also study which prediction objectives work best, which visual targets to predict, and how data curation affects the value of egocentric video, distilling practical guidance for using action-free footage as scalable supervision for robot learning.

The work is motivated by the observation that precise action annotation is expensive, so most egocentric resources exist only as raw video. SPAR is designed so that heterogeneous data can train a shared backbone: robot data supervises both experts, while egocentric video supervises only the generation expert. The generation expert is discarded at inference time.

## Key points

- SPAR builds on a pretrained VLM and an MoT design with two independently parameterized transformer stacks: an action expert and a generation expert, both extending the shared backbone.
- The action expert cross-attends to per-layer VLM hidden states and predicts an action chunk with flow matching.
- The generation expert mirrors the action expert and predicts a representation of the future visual state; robot data trains both experts, egocentric video trains only the generation expert.
- Visual targets matter: supervision in a learned visual feature space is more useful for policy learning than reconstructive latents; no single target dominates every perturbation type.
- Predicting a single future frame in SigLIP2's representation space is nearly as good as predicting four frames (74.32% vs 74.62% average success), so the default design predicts one frame.
- Data curation is decisive: raw Ego4D clips underperform a robot-only baseline, while clips passing both quality gates (stable motion plus visible hands) surpass it.
- Scaling action-free egocentric video from 0 to 1,500 hours steadily improves LIBERO-Plus success while in-domain LIBERO stays roughly flat; the generation expert is removed at inference.

## Details

Method. Given the current observation and task instruction at time t with horizon H, the VLM language model produces per-layer hidden states, and the action expert cross-attends to them to emit an action chunk via flow matching. Alongside it, the generation expert is trained to predict the representation of the future visual state, providing an auxiliary signal that robot trajectories do not need to supply alone and that action-free human video can supply abundantly. Because the two experts are independently parameterized, the backbone can be optimized with a heterogeneous mix of robot and egocentric data, with egocentric video contributing gradients only through the generation pathway.

Results and analysis. The authors compare different encoders and prediction objectives for the future-state target. Representation-space supervision beats reconstructive latents overall, though no target wins everywhere: DINOv2 achieves the strongest diffusion-based scores under sensor noise and object layout shifts, while Qwen-ViT leads under lighting and background-texture changes. Direct regression of future visual features also yields useful supervision, broadly comparable to diffusion for several targets (notably SigLIP2 and DINOv2), so the benefit of visual prediction does not hinge on a diffusion objective. Predicting one future frame versus four under the same diffusion objective costs only 0.30 percentage points of average success, motivating single-frame SigLIP2 prediction as the default.

On the data side, Ego4D videos are split into uniform 5-second clips and filtered with two cumulative EgoSmith-based gates: Gate 1 keeps clips with limited camera motion and reliable optical flow (about 1,893 hours, 48% of raw duration); both gates together keep clips with at least one sufficiently visible hand (975 hours, about 25%). A locally deployed Qwen3.6-35B-A3B generates a short English step description from 15 frames per clip, used as the language instruction for future-state prediction. Training on raw Ego4D underperforms the robot-only baseline, Gate 1 alone gives at most a small gain, and both gates together give the strongest result. Finally, scaling ego-video pretraining from 0 to 1,500 hours leaves in-domain LIBERO success largely unchanged but steadily raises LIBERO-Plus success, indicating that action-free egocentric video primarily buys out-of-distribution robustness.
