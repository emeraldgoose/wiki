# SAVIOR: Sample-efficient Adaptation of Vision-Language Models for OCR Representation

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.27319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27319) · **Published**: 2026-11-20 (v1)
**Authors**: Akshata A Bhat, Sharath Naganna, Saiful Haq, Prashant Khatri, Krishna Chaitanya Reddy Tamataam, Neha Arun, Niyati Chhaya, Pushpak Bhattacharyya

## Overview

OCR pipelines and vision-language models score highly on standard OCR and document-understanding benchmarks, yet systematically underperform on document patterns that are operationally critical in financial workflows: vertical or rotated text, logo-embedded vendor names, fine-print regulatory clauses, degraded scans, and complex multi-column layouts. These patterns are underrepresented in public datasets but constitute a large share of real-world failure cases, and existing benchmarks such as olmOCR-Bench are dominated by standard financial documents, with hierarchical text and other failure-inducing attributes only a small fraction.

SAVIOR addresses this with sample-efficient curation rather than corpus expansion: it screens a large collection of financial documents, keeps samples whose OCR predictions exhibit at least one recurring failure pattern, and supplements underrepresented orientations (e.g. vertical text) with controlled synthetic material. The resulting SAVIOR-Train contains 2,234 curated document-OCR pairs concentrating multiple failure-inducing patterns per document. To measure what matters downstream, the authors also introduce PaIRS, a structure-aware evaluation metric that quantifies layout fidelity by comparing pairwise spatial relationships between predicted and ground-truth word positions.

## Key points

- SAVIOR-Train: 2,234 curated document-OCR pairs selected for coverage of high-impact failure scenarios (vertical/rotated text, logo-embedded fields, fine print, multi-column layouts, handwritten annotations), instead of scaling data broadly.
- SAVIOR-Bench: 509 expert-annotated documents for evaluating transcription robustness and structural fidelity; sources are UCSF (46.95%), in-house synthetic (17.88%), miscellaneous real-world public documents (17.49%), Inv3D (9.04%), and FUNSD (8.64%).
- PaIRS metric: measures layout fidelity via pairwise relational similarity between predicted and ground-truth token positions, capturing spatial correctness and downstream usability.
- Results: fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct with SAVIOR-Train achieves word recall 0.93; using the layout-aware OCR output as context for document QA attains F1 0.89, outperforming open- and closed-source models including GPT-4o, Mistral-OCR, PaddleOCR-VL, and DeepSeek-OCR.
- Annotation quality: inter-annotator agreement over 187 annotation pairs shows mean fuzzy string similarity 0.761 (min 0.307, max 0.987, std 0.117).
- Accepted to WACVW (WACV workshop).

## Details

The construction pipeline first screens financial documents to identify failure scenarios that pretrained VLMs consistently mishandle (mixed handwritten and printed content, loss of hierarchical cues such as section headers or bullet lists, vendor names inside logos, small regulatory clauses or handwritten notes over printed tables). olmOCR-Bench's attribute distribution shows these critical failure modes are severely underrepresented, which limits its utility for real enterprise robustness. SAVIOR-Train therefore packs multiple failure patterns into a compact dataset and adds controlled synthetic samples only where a pattern (e.g. vertical orientation) was underrepresented. Training uses Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and larger Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct variants with task-specific prompt design. Evaluation on SAVIOR-Bench emphasizes end-to-end impact on downstream tasks such as information extraction and regulatory content extraction, combining transcription accuracy with structural layout preservation.
