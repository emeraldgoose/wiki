# Route Anything: A Study of Model and Policy Routing for General-Purpose AI Systems

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05275) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Pengfei Zhou, Yixing Ma, Hexin Wang, Weidong Tang, Zhenglin Wan, Wangbo Zhao, Yang You

## Overview

Artificial general intelligence requires understanding, generation, reasoning, and action across diverse domains and modalities. Rather than relying on a single model to master every capability, a promising path is to orchestrate a heterogeneous ecosystem of specialized models, tools, and policies into a unified AI system. Route Anything studies dynamic selection and coordination of the most capable candidate for each task while balancing performance and computational cost. It develops a theoretical foundation for optimal routing under quality-cost trade-offs and builds an omni-modal trajectory matrix spanning 25 benchmarks across language, image, video, speech, coding, workplace agents, and robotic manipulation. The matrix comprises 374,401 scored runs from 452 candidate configurations.

## Key points

- Routing orchestrates heterogeneous models, tools, and policies.
- The framework spans 25 benchmarks and 374,401 scored runs.
- 452 candidate configurations are compared.
- Per-input oracle routing outperforms the best fixed candidate by 5.4 points.
- Learned routers outperform the fixed candidate on several domains.
- Routing raises efficiency by 8.82 times on average relative to the reference.

## Details

Per-input oracle routing gains 5.4 points over the best fixed candidate, while learned routers outperform it by 0.3 points across 16 benchmarks, 2.1 points on video generation, and 1.1 points on robot manipulation while using 24 percent less policy time. The results indicate substantial opportunity for capability orchestration, though learned-router gains are modest on aggregate and strongly domain dependent. Routers also require reliable confidence estimation and access to candidate performance signals.
