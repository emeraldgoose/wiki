# Do Multimodal Models Decide Consistently on Equivalent Optical Spectra?

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.00428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00428) · **Published**: 2026-10-30
**Authors**: Ziyuan Wang, Genliang Zhu, Xiaobo Chen

## Overview

The same optical spectrum can be presented as a table in nanometers, a table in photon energy, or a plot; the physical response is identical, so a model's decision should not depend on the rendering. Likewise, when the data needed to answer are missing — say the segment containing the relevant dip was cut away — the model should declare the question undetermined rather than guess. The paper tests both properties on measured optical spectra of silicon metasurfaces (MOCLIP) and gold nanoparticles, with labels computed directly from the data arrays.

Every spectrum appears in six representations: four tables (nanometers, micrometers, scientific notation, eV) and two plots (nm and eV), all built from the same underlying arrays. Partial versions remove data inside or outside the queried range, creating a clean pair of controls: an outside cut leaves the answer available, while an inside cut that removes the relevant extremum makes the question unanswerable. The evaluated models are the decision models d3-nano and Jev 1.13, three GPT-6 Luna configurations, and the Qwen3.5-2B base model.

Two findings dominate. Equivalent representations change decisions by up to 41 percentage points within one model, mostly in photon-energy coordinates. And adaptation can backfire: LoRA fine-tuning of d3-nano learns to abstain, but largely through a shortcut — it abstains whenever the input is truncated — which training with answerable truncations eliminates in all six seeds.

## Key points
- MOCLIP contributes 528 core variants and gold 140; MOCLIP has four task types (Extreme, Compare, Specification, Sufficiency), each built from 48 original instances, with a minimum dip separation of 40 nm in Compare.
- On gold spectra, switching to eV tables lowers accuracy by 41 points for Jev, 21 for Luna gen. none, 20 for d3-nano, and 13 for Luna Decisions.
- Plots cut differently: Luna gen. none loses 34 points on gold eV plots and 20 on MOCLIP nm plots, while Luna Decisions gains 15 and 9 points on nm plots.
- With the same evidence, asking in photon energy instead of wavelength helps some model–view pairs by 16–50 points and hurts others by up to 25.
- Agreement across all six representations ranges from 31% to 78% on MOCLIP; low-reasoning Luna's 78% agreement matches its accuracy ceiling, and 12 of d3-nano's 37 agreement points are consistent but wrong.
- When the relevant extremum is removed, d3-nano, Jev, and Luna Decisions never answer "undetermined" in any representation; low-reasoning Luna abstains on 100% of nm tables but 0% of eV tables and only 0–19% of plots.
- After an inside cut, 54–81% of d3-nano responses and 31–54% of Jev responses select the band containing the visible extremum; in specification tasks, 75–100% of errors pick a candidate that is deep enough but outside the required window.
- Unadapted API configurations almost never abstain on outside cuts (accuracy changes at most seven points), with scope discrimination between inside and outside cuts of only +14 for Jev and +55 for low-reasoning Luna.
- LoRA (rank 16, scale 32, dropout 0.05 on the language projection layers) trained with answerable truncations avoids the shortcut in all six seeds, showing a 90–100 point abstention gap between inside and outside cuts on held-out structures; a matched amount of extra original-task data avoids it in only one of six seeds.
- On gold spectra the two training sets perform similarly, with table discrimination of +67 to +97 versus +70 to +75.

## Details

### Paired task construction
Instances are built from measured transmission spectra with deterministic labels. Variants change one thing at a time — number format, coordinate, modality, evidence order, question direction, or data coverage — and each change has a defined expected answer; reversed question directions check cases where the answer should actually change. Uncertainty is reported as 95% percentile intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of original task instances, with each instance's variants kept together; the intervals are descriptive, without correction for multiple comparisons.

### Representation effects
Within wavelength coordinates, number-format changes are small for most models (usually within 5 points, the largest being 12 points down for d3-nano on gold scientific-notation tables), while energy coordinates cause the largest shifts. On MOCLIP, low-reasoning Luna loses 10 points on eV tables despite near-ceiling accuracy in other representations. Because agreement rates track accuracy, the paper uses paired accuracy differences per instance as the main measure of representation effects.

### Abstention and evidence sufficiency
d3-nano, Jev, and Luna Decisions never abstain after an inside cut. Unadapted API configurations treat inside and outside cuts almost identically, so their discrimination is near zero; on gold, Jev, Luna Decisions, and Luna gen. none choose "undetermined" on only 0–5% of middle-gap requests, while low-reasoning Luna reaches 20–75% on tables but 0% on plots. A common wrong answer after an inside cut is the band containing the visible extremum.

### LoRA training and the truncation shortcut
Only d3-nano is adapted: LoRA modules (rank 16, scale 32, dropout 0.05) are added to the language-model projection layers and trained with AdamW at learning rate 0.0001, gradient accumulation over eight examples, and gradient clipping at 1.0. The arm trained with answerable truncations reaches MOCLIP table discrimination of +95, +90, and +100 across seeds with outside-cut changes of −2 to 0, while the matched arm trained on a similar amount of original-task data keeps the shortcut, with outside-cut changes of −99, −35, and 0. Transfer remains limited: plot comparison stays weak, performance on an unseen task varies widely, and accuracy on a second optical dataset falls.
