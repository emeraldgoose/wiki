# From Autonomous Agents to Cognitive Symbionts: Toward AI-Augmented Human Intelligence

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05376) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Hao Fei

## Overview

Progress in AI has largely been measured by autonomous machine capability, even though foundation models and personal agents can already write code and perform multistep tasks. The paper argues that AI should also augment individual human intelligence, especially when people forget, miss important cues, struggle to express ideas, or face choices without access to their own history.

It introduces AI-augmented human intelligence (AHI) and a cognitive symbiont: a lifelong, embedded, real-time, private, low-friction, proactive companion connected through external devices. Such a system senses what the person senses, preserves context over years, models the person and their situation, supports thinking at the right moment, performs context-aware tasks, and learns as both person and system change.

The conceptual core is the cognitive gap between what a person could contribute and what they can hold in working memory. The design space is organized as a symbiosis loop of sensing, keeping, understanding, living alongside, acting for, thinking with, and growing, enclosed by a personal runtime and trust. The paper also recognizes governance challenges ranging from covert assistance to social pressure.

## Key points

- Contrasts autonomous AI with AI-augmented human intelligence.
- Introduces the cognitive symbiont as a lifelong personal companion.
- Defines the cognitive gap between potential contribution and working memory capacity.
- Organizes the design space as a symbiosis loop.
- Includes trust and personal runtime as enclosure dimensions.
- Addresses governance risks such as covert assistance and adoption pressure.
