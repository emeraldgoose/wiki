# DeepStress: Stress-Testing Deep Search Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2607.13920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13920) · **Published**: 2026-07-15
**Authors**: Ismael Rousseau, Geraldine Damnati, Frederic Bechet

## Overview

Search agents interleave reasoning with iterative calls to search tools, yet most evaluations reduce their behavior to a single final-answer accuracy number. The authors argue this hides qualitatively different failure modes: poor retrieval, misleading evidence, or endless unproductive searching can all produce the same score. Their answer is a stress-testing laboratory, in the spirit of crash simulation for autonomous vehicles: the agent's search calls are intercepted and answered by a generator that fabricates one document per query, with three properties — trustworthiness, relevance, and factuality — sampled independently at rates of 0%, 50%, or 100%.

Because answers are judged under degraded evidence, the paper replaces binary accuracy with a five-way classification of final answers: correct, incorrect, abstention, exceeding the tool budget, and missing answer tag, adjudicated by GPT-4.1. Two aggregate metrics summarize behavior: the Reliability-Aware Score (RAS), which credits correct answers fully, gives incorrect or malformed answers nothing, and awards abstentions partial credit equal to one minus the realized evidence reliability; and CoPRAS, the computation cost per unit of RAS.

Twelve systems are evaluated, spanning GPT-5-nano and GPT-5-mini at three reasoning efforts and open-weight RL-trained agents such as Search-R1, AutoRefine, SpiqaDR, ASearcher, and DR-Tulu. Two findings stand out: several open-weight agents answer confidently even when sources carry blatant credibility red flags, and many HotpotQA questions are answered from parametric knowledge even when every returned document is factually wrong.

## Key points
- The evaluation comprises 12 systems × 2 datasets (HotpotQA and BrowseComp-Plus) × 200 questions × 7 degradation scenarios, or 33,600 traces, with a budget of 100 tool calls per question and documents generated by Gemma-4-31B.
- A human sanity check of 50 synthetic documents by three annotators found 96.4% agreement for trustworthiness labels and 85.5% for factuality.
- Under 0% trustworthiness, Search-R1 remains between 78% and 80% correct, whereas DR-Tulu's correct rate falls from 59.8% to 40.9% and its no-answer rate grows from 10.8% to 34.6%.
- GPT systems respond to untrustworthy sources by searching rather than answering: GPT-5-mini (low) abstention rises from 14.0% to 22.5%, and GPT-5-mini (high) tool-budget exhaustion rises from 32.0% to 62.7%.
- At 0% relevance, Search-R1's incorrect-answer rate jumps from 19.5% to 79.2%, and AutoRefine's missing-answer-tag rate climbs from 18.2% to 61.9%.
- At 0% factuality, gpt-5-mini (medium) still answers 60% of HotpotQA questions correctly and every system at least 10%, while BrowseComp-Plus drops to almost zero — direct evidence of parametric-knowledge answering.
- Degraded evidence raises the average cost per run from 10.9k to 20.5k tokens; GPT-5-nano (medium)'s cost grows by a factor of 3.5 while AutoRefine's barely changes.
- At 0% document quality, average correctness is 0.30 but average RAS is 0.61; TPC rises from 18.1k to 75.7k while CoPRAS rises only to 36.8k.

## Details

### Simulation environment and metrics
DeepStress manipulates three source attributes. Trustworthiness is whether a source looks credible, with low-trust documents given plausible titles on misleading or fake domains sampled from the OpenSources list. Relevance is whether the document addresses the agent's query; irrelevant documents are instead built from the verified evidence of an unrelated question. Factuality is whether claims are correct, with correct documents grounded in gold paragraphs or human-verified evidence. For each simulated search call the labels are sampled from the scenario probabilities — a factuality rate of 80% means the document is factually correct with probability 0.8 — and converted into generation instructions. Run cost follows the TPC formulation, weighting input tokens at 0.25 and each search call at 500, and RAS is defined per run as 1 for correct answers, 0 for incorrect or malformed ones, and 1 minus the product of realized trustworthiness, relevance, and factuality rates for abstentions and budget exhaustion.

### Experimental setup
HotpotQA and BrowseComp-Plus are deliberately complementary. HotpotQA questions need a few reasoning hops over Wikipedia passages and can often be answered from parametric knowledge; BrowseComp-Plus questions cannot be answered without documents because the corpus is too recent for current models. One reliability axis is varied at a time (0%, 50%, 100%) while the other two stay at 100%, giving seven scenarios. The twelve systems include six GPT tool-calling baselines plus SpiqaDR (a 3B Qwen2.5 agent trained with RL for scientific literature search), Search-R1 (7B), AutoRefine (3B, with explicit evidence-refinement steps), ASearcher (7B, local-RAG variant), and DR-Tulu (8B), evaluated both with and without explicit reasoning.

### Results by degradation axis
Trustworthiness degradation barely moves the open-weight agents: they keep consuming context despite almost caricatural low-credibility signals. Relevance degradation is stronger and failure styles diverge — Search-R1 and DR-Tulu without reasoning produce confident wrong answers, SpiqaDR mixes wrong answers and abstentions, AutoRefine suffers format failures, and high-effort GPT models keep searching until the budget runs out. Factuality degradation amplifies the pattern: DR-Tulu reaches 59.1% incorrect answers at 0% factuality versus 33.8% at 0% relevance, and SpiqaDR abstains less (15.2% versus 38.0%), showing that irrelevant documents are easier to reject than relevant but false ones.

### Reliability-aware scoring in practice
RAS separates robust non-answering from overconfident failure. GPT-5-nano (medium) keeps a stable RAS while its correctness falls from 0.83 to 0.41, whereas Search-R1 falls in both (0.80 to 0.35 correctness and 0.80 to 0.37 RAS), receiving little reliability credit. Cost-normalized metrics shift the ranking at 0% quality: TPC rewards cheap correctness and favors AutoRefine, while CoPRAS rewards cost-efficient reliability and favors GPT-5-nano (low) and GPT-5-mini (low). The authors also note limits: synthetic documents are cleaner and less diverse than real web pages, and grounding factually correct documents requires per-question supporting evidence.
