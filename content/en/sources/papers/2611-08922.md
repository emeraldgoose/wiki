# Glyph: An Interpretability Engine for AI-Driven Scientific Discovery in Clinical Data

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08922)
**Published**: 2026-11-06

## Overview

Glyph is a multistage framework from Stanford's Center for Artificial Intelligence in Medicine and Imaging that converts foundation-model (FM) embeddings of clinical data into interpretable, coded clinical concepts and then tests those concepts as candidate markers of future disease. Manual phenotyping cannot scale to population-level multimodal data, and pre-defined feature sets cap what discovery systems — including agentic AI — can find. Glyph instead mines what FMs already encode: it fits sparse autoencoders (SAEs) per modality on embeddings of abdominal CT volumes, CT radiology reports, and laboratory measurements, interprets the resulting features as clinical concepts, and evaluates them against prognostic outcomes.

The study applied Glyph to data from 41,628 patients across multiple institutions. At the discovery site, 4,285 SAE features were paired with 141 prognostic outcomes, yielding 604,185 candidate associations. After a Bonferroni-corrected significance filter, 4,463 associations survived, collapsing to 397 high-risk concepts. The majority of these associations reproduced automatically, without recalibration, in held-out cohorts at other sites — evidence that the concept space captures generalizable biology rather than site-specific artifacts.

Glyph goes beyond correlation: because the concepts are sparse and interpretable, they can enter double machine learning confounder adjustment alongside standard covariates, separating disease-specific markers from markers of general illness severity. The authors also benchmarked Glyph concepts against an automated literature search (342,647 publications screened, 412 testable markers), showing that adding ten Glyph concepts per outcome to literature-derived marker sets significantly improved held-out prognostic AUC. Glyph is released open source at github.com/StanfordMIMI/Glyph, including a Glyph MCP interface for agentic, open-ended investigation.

## Key points

- Data: 41,628 patients across discovery, internal test, and two external sites; modalities = abdominal CT volumes, radiology reports, laboratory measurements.
- SAEs found 3,315 report features, 343 imaging features, and 627 lab features (4,285 total); the same SAEs re-extracted features zero-shot at other sites.
- 604,185 concept–outcome pairs tested at the discovery site; 4,463 associations (0.739%) passed a Bonferroni threshold of p ≤ 8.28e-8 with OR > 1.0.
- Replication without recalibration: 3,095 (69.3%) on the Internal Test cohort (3,667 patients), 3,314 (74.3%) at External Site #1 (17,224 patients), and 1,701 (54.7%) of report/imaging associations at out-of-state External Site #2.
- Confounder adjustment via double machine learning (16 confounders per outcome: top-10 associated concepts plus age, sex, race, BMI, alcohol use, smoking): 1,198 (26.8%) of the 4,463 associations retained OR > 1.0, attenuating general-illness-severity markers while preserving disease-specific ones.
- Literature comparison: automated PubMed/Paperclip search screened 342,647 publications → 412 testable markers (~19.6 per axis across 21 outcomes). Literature baselines achieved 0.749 held-out AUC; adding ten Glyph concepts raised this to 0.774 (95% CI 0.762–0.785, p = 2.66e-28), with the largest gains in metabolic, haematologic, and infectious outcomes (relative AUROC +6.64%).
- Top-ten Glyph concepts reproduced in the pooled held-out cohort 64.8% of the time vs 28.2% for the literature marker set and 41.2% for the best ten literature markers.
- Glyph MCP exposes the concept space to agents: targeted disease modeling, natural-language concept ranking for an outcome, individualized risk explanation, and patients-like-me retrieval.

## Details

- Code: https://github.com/StanfordMIMI/Glyph (open source, CC BY 4.0).
- Modalities and prefixes: R = radiology report concepts, I = imaging concepts, L = laboratory concepts.
- High-risk concepts clustered into liver, kidney, cardiovascular, musculoskeletal, malignant, inflammatory, and cholesterol-related neighborhoods.
- Reported rediscoveries include cholestasis (L108), splenomegaly (I65), and ascites-related imaging concepts as predictors of hepatic disease progression.
- The paper notes associations reflect clinical workflows as well as biology, motivating the DML confounder analysis rather than causal claims.
- External Site #2 had lab values for under a third of patients, so lab-concept replication was not run there.
