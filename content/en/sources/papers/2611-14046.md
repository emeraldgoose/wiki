# Structured Confidence Perturbation via Fisher Geometry for Adversarial Training

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.14046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14046) · **Published**: 2026-11-10
**Authors**: Chenlong Wei, Hanling Wang, Chong Zhang, Qinkai Yu, Fangyu Wu, Xiaobo Jin

## Overview

Adversarial training is often undermined by pseudo-attacks: perturbations that inflate the loss yet leave the model's confidence ordering intact, so the defense looks strong while the belief structure is untouched. The authors propose attacking and defending confidence-structure deformation directly, with two complementary methods.

The first, SPP, defines a hybrid attack loss that maximizes cross-entropy plus the KL divergence between clean and perturbed predictions, so an adversarial example must both induce misclassification and disrupt the whole output distribution. The second, SCP, reformulates attack generation as geometric optimization on the confidence manifold. A local analysis shows the KL between two conditional distributions approximates half a Mahalanobis distance whose covariance is the inverse Fisher information matrix, and by the Rayleigh-Ritz theorem the optimal perturbation aligns with the principal eigenvector of the FIM, gaining half the squared radius times the largest eigenvalue in KL. Because forming the high-dimensional FIM directly is impractical, an efficient decomposition avoids the quadratic cost.

Experiments cover CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet on ResNet-18/34/50, plus DenseNet121 and ViT-B/16; both methods beat gradient-attack baselines, with SCP strongest on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 and SPP steadier on Tiny-ImageNet.

## Key points

- Hybrid-loss ablation (CIFAR-10, ResNet-18): CE-only reaches 78.87% clean accuracy, 62.37% AutoAttack, and 20.92% ASR; KL-only 80.72%, 64.93%, 19.56%; CE+KL (SPP) 81.90%, 70.15%, 14.35%.
- CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50: SCP posts 83.24% standard accuracy, 72.50% AutoAttack, and 12.90% ASR; SPP 83.12%, 70.99%, 14.59%; TRADES and PGD sit at 19.34% and 17.10% ASR.
- CIFAR-100 with ResNet-50: SCP suppresses ASR to 24.59% against 29.65% for MIFGSM and 30.32% for PIFGSM.
- Tiny-ImageNet favors SPP: SCP has lower standard accuracy at all three depths and higher ASR at two of three; on ResNet-34, SPP records 30.69% ASR versus SCP's 28.89%.
- Cross-architecture: on DenseNet121 SCP reaches 82.49% standard accuracy and 69.79% AutoAttack, while on ViT-B/16 every method degrades; diagnostics show ViT-B/16 input-gradient variation of 0.345 versus 0.247 for ResNet-18 and 0.268 for DenseNet-121.
- Transfer robustness: targets trained with SCP are least affected by transferred AutoAttack-L2 examples, keeping 94.65% robust accuracy on DenseNet-121 and 93.10% on ViT-B/16 with a PGD source.
- FIM spectrum (ResNet-18, CIFAR-10): SCP's median max eigenvalue is 0.0287 versus 0.1438 for PGD and 0.3011 for TRADES, and 45.03% of samples fall at or under 0.01.
- Complexity: an indirect decomposition reduces SCP from quadratic space and O(Kd^2 + d^3) time to O(dK) space and O(dK^2 + K^3) time, with K far smaller than d (K = 10/100/200, d = 3072/12288).

## Details

### From loss maximization to manifold geometry

TRADES already uses KL divergence as a robustness regularizer in its defense objective; SPP instead repurposes CE+KL maximization for attack generation within a small radius, then trains on the generated examples. SCP goes further: Theorem 1 approximates the KL between clean and perturbed confidence distributions as half the Mahalanobis distance with inverse-FIM covariance, so the worst perturbation follows the FIM principal eigenvector and gains half the squared radius times the largest eigenvalue in KL. The training consequently targets the geometry of the belief manifold rather than a scalar loss value.

### Efficient algorithm

Direct SCP would need the FIM's principal eigenvector over the full input dimension d, with quadratic storage. The decomposition rewrites the problem in terms of a K-dimensional confidence basis tied to the number of classes, cutting space to O(dK) and time to O(dK^2 + K^3). Since K is tiny relative to d in classification, the geometric paradigm stays practical for larger models and datasets.

### Where SCP wins and fails

SCP's single-step geometric perturbation is most reliable on well-converged confidence manifolds: it leads robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, resists transferred attacks best, and its t-SNE visualization shows confidence collapsing for SCP-generated examples while CW pseudo-attacks linger on the high-confidence plateau. On Tiny-ImageNet, 200 classes inflate FIM estimation variance and underfitting violates the manifold assumption, so multi-step SPP is steadier; the noisy global-attention gradients of ViT-B/16 likewise degrade every local-geometry method, pointing to the need for architecture-specific adaptation.
