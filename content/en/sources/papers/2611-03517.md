# Ask, Don't Equip: Question-Driven Coding Agents for Deep Kernel Optimization

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03517) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Gabriele Oliaro, Jaeseong Lee, Zhaoyuan Su, Sreeram Vennam, Zhihao Jia, Samyam Rajbhandari

## Overview

LLM agents for GPU kernel optimization sit at two ends of a structure-freedom tradeoff. Scripted agents fix what the model does at each step through learned policies, optimization menus, or fixed roles; they are sample-efficient where the recipe applies and stall where it does not. Open-ended agents let a self-directed model mutate a population of kernels and pay for the freedom in trials: thousands of evaluations, repeated edits, and wrong candidates. Both respond to hard targets by equipping the agent with more, such as parsed profiler output, disassembly analysis, retrieval over documentation and libraries, and lesson stores. The paper argues that both lack direction, a hypothesis about where the remaining gap is, and that a short question asked at the right moment supplies it without narrowing what the agent may do next. It presents KernelSeek, a question-driven agent that makes an existing CUDA kernel faster with no operator-specific knowledge and no faster implementation to imitate. KernelSeek combines a general-purpose coding agent, a minimal harness that enforces a verifiable goal, and questions at decision points, first from a human then from a bank the agent applies to itself. On the same task, evaluator, hardware, and week, a tool-rich autonomous agent plateaued at 0.50x FlashAttention-3 after 133 model-driven steps, while KernelSeek reached 1.13x. KernelSeek then produced dependency-free FlashAttention forward kernels for Hopper and Blackwell as single CUDA files with no CUTLASS, CuTe, cuBLAS, or cuDNN, correct on all 182 scenarios per architecture and achieving 1.16x FlashAttention-3 and 1.46x FlashAttention-4 geometric mean. Across 162 human messages and 11.5K agent tool calls, every large gain followed a question that moved the agent to a different layer or rejected a claimed limit, not one that supplied information. The paper releases the kernels, harness, question bank, and all traces.

## Key points

- Scripted and open-ended agent approaches each fail: recipes stall, while freedom costs thousands of trials.
- Adding more tools does not replace a hypothesis about where the remaining performance gap is.
- A short question at the right decision point supplies direction without constraining the agent's next actions.
- KernelSeek reached 1.13x FlashAttention-3 where a tool-rich agent stalled at 0.50x after 133 steps.
- It produced dependency-free FlashAttention kernels with 182/182 correctness and 1.16x/1.46x speedups; every large gain followed a diagnostic question.

## Details

Evaluation uses a fixed task, evaluator, hardware, and one-week budget. Human questions (162) and agent tool calls (11.5K) are logged. The released artifacts include kernels, harness, question bank, and traces; deeper analysis is in the linked source.
