# Judging in Latent Space: Efficient Generative Reward Modeling via Semantics-Preserving Compression

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09788)
**Date**: 2026-10-08

## Overview

Reward modeling for RLHF typically requires generating explicit textual reasoning over multiple evaluation criteria, which incurs substantial inference cost. This paper introduces LatentGRM, a framework that performs reward modeling in a compressed latent space. By leveraging the structure of rubric-guided evaluations, LatentGRM learns to compress semantic chunks of the evaluation reasoning into continuous trajectories, then performs autonomous pairwise judgments directly in latent space without generating textual output token by token. The approach achieves comparable judgment quality to generative reward models while reducing inference compute by an order of magnitude.

## Background

Generative reward models (GRMs) verbalize the evaluation process—explaining why one response is better than another—yielding interpretable judgments but at high token cost. Recent work on latent reasoning (e.g., latent CoT, COAT) suggests continuous representations can support multi-step reasoning more compactly. LatentGRM applies this insight to reward modeling: instead of generating evaluation text, it compresses rubric-structured evaluations into latent trajectories and judges in that space.

## Methodology

LatentGRM operates in two stages:
1. **Semantic Chunking & Compression**: Rubric-guided evaluations are segmented into semantic chunks (one per criterion). A compressor maps each chunk to a continuous latent vector, trained to reconstruct the chunk text.
2. **Autonomous Latent Evaluation**: Given a pair of responses, the model produces latent trajectories for each, then a judge head predicts the preference directly from the latent states.

Training uses a combination of reconstruction loss (for compression fidelity) and preference loss (for judgment accuracy). At inference, only the compressor and judge head run—no token generation.

## Key Findings

- **Efficiency**: LatentGRM reduces inference FLOPs by ~10x vs. generative RM baselines while maintaining judgment agreement with human annotators.
- **Rubric recovery**: The latent trajectories can be decoded back to rubric-level evaluations, preserving interpretability.
- **Scaling**: The approach scales to longer rubrics without proportional token cost increase.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

For teams deploying RLHF pipelines, LatentGRM offers a path to cheaper reward modeling without sacrificing evaluation granularity. The rubric-structured compression also provides a natural audit trail—latent states can be decoded to show which criteria drove a judgment.
