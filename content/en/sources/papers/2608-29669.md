# Wasserstein-Barycentric Interaction Fields for Spatial Factor Models: Evidence from Language-Model Representations

Source: Marcus Gawronsky, Chun-Sung Huang, arXiv:2608.29669 (q-fin.ST), Aug 30, 2026.

**Lead**: Spatial return models take the interaction matrix as given and leave the feedback channel uninterpreted. This paper constructs a **bandwidth-free field** from firms' language-model article embedding distributions using **target-anchored Wasserstein barycentric reconstruction**. A quadratic exposure-adjustment problem then maps that feedback into a **peer-misalignment penalty ratio**. For 52 firms, with the field **frozen from 2018–2022 news** and evaluated on 2023–2026, the method yields a penalty ratio of **3.46** (95% interval [2.89, 4.17]) and higher conditional quasi-likelihood than equal-weighted peer support or RBF weighting of the same distances.

## The gap: interactions given, not explained

Spatial factor models assume assets influence each other. The interaction matrix — which asset pushes which — is normally **taken as given**. That leaves the feedback channel uninterpreted: the model knows *that* stocks are connected but not *what kind* of similarity the connection encodes, and certainly not whether it reflects anything economic rather than statistical artefact.

This paper builds the field from text instead. The input is each firm's **language-model embedding distribution over news articles**, and the construction is deliberately **bandwidth-free** — no smoothing bandwidth to tune.

## Method

**Target-anchored Wasserstein barycentric reconstruction.** The key construction: for a target firm, reconstruct its embedding distribution as a barycentric combination of other firms' distributions, where the weights solve an optimal transport problem. The Wasserstein geometry gives a distribution-level distance — how far apart two *distributions of embeddings* are — rather than comparing single vectors.

**Quadratic exposure adjustment.** The resulting field is mapped into an interpretable quantity: a **peer-misalignment penalty ratio**, the relative cost of being misaligned with one's peer group. Because the field is anchored, the reconstruction is conditioned on the specific target rather than being a global similarity matrix.

## Results

For **52 firms**, with the field constructed from **2018–2022 news and frozen** — then evaluated on **2023–2026** — the paper reports:

- A **penalty ratio of 3.46**, 95% interval **[2.89, 4.17]**
- **Higher conditional quasi-likelihood** than both comparison baselines:
  - **equal-weighted peer support** — the naive no-field baseline
  - **RBF weighting of the same distances** — same information, conventional kernel

The second baseline is the meaningful one: it holds the distances fixed and changes only the weighting scheme, so the improvement is attributable to the Wasserstein barycentric construction rather than to having richer data.

**Joint penalty ratios.** For the barycentric field combined with a **news co-mention** field, joint ratios are **2.33** and **0.86** respectively, with boundary-calibrated tests that **reject both exclusions** — i.e. neither field is subsumed by the other.

## The out-of-sample design

The freeze matters more than usual here. The field is built from 2018–2022 news and never updated, then scored on 2023–2026. That means the reported performance is not a fit statistic from the same period that generated the inputs — it is a genuine forecast-horizon evaluation.

This is also the honest limit on interpreting the numbers: 52 firms is a small panel, and the confidence intervals are correspondingly wide. The construction is the contribution; the point estimate is a demonstration.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Text-derived structure is only interesting if it beats a trivial baseline. Here it beats both equal weighting and a standard kernel on identical distances — that is the comparison that makes it credible.
- Barycentric reconstruction against a target is a reusable pattern: it produces something anchored and interpretable rather than a global similarity blob.
- Distribution-level comparison (Wasserstein over embedding distributions) captures information that pairwise vector distance throws away.
- **Freeze the derived field and evaluate on a later period.** In-sample fit of a text-derived structure says very little.
