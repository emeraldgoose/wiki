# SnapBench: Benchmarking Snap-and-Ask Multimodal Retrieval for Mobile Interactions

Source: Zirong Chen, Fuda Ye, Kuan Zhang and 7 others (Yuanbao Technical Report), arXiv:2608.29607, Aug 30, 2026. Accepted to Findings of EMNLP 2026.

**Lead**: Mobile AI increasingly works as a **visual oracle** — snap a picture, ask a question. But photos are often blurry and text questions may be short or mistyped, so both modalities arrive degraded. Existing benchmarks test only clean inputs or fail to isolate **paired robustness** across the two. SnapBench is the first paired benchmark for this: **1,145 queries, 9,085 gallery items, 53 controlled corruption conditions** with human annotations, evaluating **16 multimodal retrievers** across dual-tower encoders and embedding-based VLMs. The headline result is counterintuitive: **clean image-only retrieval often outperforms joint retrieval**, exposing a *coarse-text drag* and a lack of cross-modal fallback under noise. The paper also proposes MOOR, a simple adaptive fusion.

## The deployment reality the benchmark encodes

Snap-and-ask is not one interaction among many — it is one of the **most common entry points for mobile AI**. That framing matters for benchmark design, because it dictates what to corrupt.

In real use:

- The **photo is blurry** — motion blur, poor lighting, a moving subject, an unsteady hand
- The **text question is short** — "what is this?" rather than a well-formed sentence
- The **text question is mistyped** — one-handed typing, autocorrect producing something else

So a benchmark that uses clean photos and well-formed queries measures a distribution that no user ever encounters. SnapBench's design point is to corrupt **both modalities in a paired way**, so you can measure how a retriever behaves when the entire input is degraded together — which is what actually happens.

## Scale and coverage

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| Queries | **1,145** |
| Gallery items | **9,085** |
| Controlled corruption conditions | **53** |
| Retrievers evaluated | **16** |

Both major architectures are covered: **dual-tower encoders** and **embedding-based VLMs**. The 53 conditions are controlled rather than incidental, and the corruption set carries **human annotations** — so you can distinguish a model failure from an ambiguous or badly-labelled query.

## What the results show

**Image corruptions substantially degrade retrieval.** Expected — a blurry photo genuinely loses information — but the magnitude matters for deployment.

**Text corruptions mainly affect text-only retrieval, with limited impact on joint retrieval.** Also reasonable: with a degraded image, a noisy query query contributes less marginal harm because the image is already carrying the signal.

**The surprising result: clean image-only retrieval often outperforms joint retrieval.** This is the finding that matters. Adding a short, noisy text query to a good image does not merely fail to help — it actively *hurts*.

Two mechanisms explain it, and both are actionable:

1. **Coarse-text drag** — a short or vague query supplies a weak but non-zero constraint that pulls the retrieval away from the image's actual best match
2. **No cross-modal fallback** — under noisy input, a joint model has no mechanism to fall back to whichever modality is still reliable; it fuses regardless and gets dragged

The practical implication: **a joint retriever is not automatically better than an image-only retriever for this interaction pattern.** If the text channel is unreliable, the fusion needs to know that.

## MOOR

The paper proposes **MOOR** — Modality-anchored, Outlier-aware, Optimal Reweighting — as a simple adaptive fusion approach. The naming states the design: anchor on a reliable modality, treat the other as an outlier rather than an equal input, and reweight accordingly.

The contribution here is less the specific algorithm than the **framing**: reliability-aware modality calibration. Static fusion weights assume the modalities are equally trustworthy in all conditions, which the benchmark results show is false under corruption.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Evaluate retrieval under the corruption your users actually produce, not a clean test set. If photos are blurry in the field, the benchmark needs blurry photos.
- Test image-only, text-only and joint paths separately. On this benchmark, joint was often the worst of the three — which you would never learn from an aggregate score.
- Short or vague queries can be *actively harmful* in fusion. Consider treating query confidence as an input, not a constant.
- Static fusion weights are an assumption of equal reliability. That assumption fails under noise.
