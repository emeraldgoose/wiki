# WorldBridge: Bridging Video and Action for Task Generalization in World Action Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40748) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Jun Cen et al.

## Overview

Broad task generalization in embodied models is studied through World Action Models. The paper frames generalization as two complementary capabilities: video generalization, predicting the visual evolution of new tasks, and action generalization, producing actionable control for those tasks.

It introduces WorldBridge, a WAM that couples video prediction and action generation through a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. Separate video and action experts are trained with a joint flow-matching objective, while a structured attention mask bridges the two modalities.

## Key points

- Studies task generalization rather than only robustness to changed conditions.
- Defines video generalization and action generalization as the two needed capabilities.
- Reports limited generalization from in-distribution video-action data alone.
- Uses an MoT architecture with separate video and action experts.
- Trains the experts jointly via flow matching.
- Uses a structured attention mask to connect visual prediction and action generation.

## Details

Embodied generalization research has often focused on robustness to variations in object arrangement, camera viewpoint, lighting, or visual appearance. WorldBridge instead examines whether models can perform visually and control-wise on tasks outside their training distribution.

By coupling dense video supervision with action generation, it aims to learn physical interactions beyond action-labeled robot data. The architecture is designed to let video and action experts share relevant structure while preserving their distinct roles.
