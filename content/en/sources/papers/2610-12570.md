# Contextual Correction of Parallel Drafts for Speculative Decoding

**Source**: Eisenstadt et al., arXiv, 2026-10-08

**Lead**: Parallel diffusion drafters (e.g., DFlash) generate a whole draft block in one forward pass, but each position is predicted without observing the realized tokens at preceding positions, so individually plausible predictions can form incoherent continuations that the verifier rejects. DCOCO (DFlash Contextual Corrector) restores these intra-block dependencies without reintroducing a sequential module: it keeps multiple candidate tokens per slot from a single drafter pass, scores all adjacent candidate transitions in parallel with a lightweight MLP, and selects with a prefix-first greedy rule matched to speculative verification. Across four verifier backbones and ten benchmarks it posts the best average throughput in every configuration.

## Background

Speculative decoding splits generation into drafting (cheap proposals) and verification (parallel target-model check preserving the output distribution); speedup depends on accepted length and draft latency, which are in tension. Autoregressive drafters (small LMs, EAGLE family) keep cross-position conditioning but pay sequential cost; parallel diffusion drafters (DFlash) cut latency by predicting the block jointly but lose intra-block conditioning. Recent fixes (DSpark's semi-autoregressive head, Domino's recurrent causal correction) recover acceptance with a recurrence over the block—putting learned compute back on the critical path of every drafting cycle.

## DCOCO: Parallel Scoring, Prefix-First Selection

Two observations drive the design. First, the drafter usually already suspects the right token: on Qwen3-8B, top-16 candidates contain the verifier's token in 59% of slots where top-1 is wrong, with the coverage gap widening deeper in the block—so correction can be re-selection over existing candidates (all $(B-1)K^2$ transitions scored in one batched pass) rather than new generation. Second, verification accepts a contiguous prefix and halts at the first rejection, so later-position improvements are worthless unless earlier slots survive—correction should prioritize the early prefix.

Concretely, each slot's top-$K$ candidates form a grid; a shared two-layer MLP scores each adjacent transition as the successor's draft logit plus a learned correction conditioned on slot-pair state, candidate representations (fused token embedding + slot hidden state), and both logits. The MLP output is zero-initialized so selection reproduces the drafter's proposal at step zero. Training uses exponentially down-weighted cross-entropy over jointly softmaxed transition scores ($\rho = 4$), emphasizing early slots. Selection is prefix-first greedy: keep the first slot's argmax, then repeatedly take the best successor of the chosen predecessor—$O(BK)$ walk after the $O(BK^2)$ scoring pass, with no extra drafter forward pass.

## Experiments

**Setup**: vLLM serving on a single B200 GPU; verifiers Qwen3-4B/8B/Coder-30B-A3B and Llama-3.1-8B; block size 16; ~800K-prompt common training corpus; baselines DFlash, DSpark, Domino retrained on the same corpus with identical backbone/stack. Ten benchmarks (MATH-500, AIME-25, GSM8K, HumanEval, MBPP, LiveCodeBench, MT-Bench, Arena-Hard-v2, Alpaca, SpeedBench) under greedy ($T=0$) and sampling ($T=1$), with and without extended thinking.

**Results** (Qwen3-8B, thinking off, concurrency 1; speedup vs. vanilla):

| Domain | Dataset | DFlash | Domino | DSpark | DCOCO |
|--------|---------|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| Math | MATH-500 ($T=0$) | 4.88x | 5.33x | 4.74x | 6.13x |
| Math | AIME-25 ($T=0$) | 4.30x | 4.85x | 4.51x | 5.45x |
| Code | LiveCodeBench ($T=0$) | 4.09x | 4.40x | 4.11x | 5.03x |
| Code | HumanEval ($T=0$) | 3.28x | 3.57x | 3.22x | 4.04x |
| General | MT-Bench ($T=0$) | 1.63x | 1.60x | 1.45x | 1.86x |
| Average (10 suites) | $T=0$ / $T=1$ | 2.95x / 2.45x | 3.16x / 2.53x | 2.88x / 2.37x | 3.62x / 2.87x |

DCOCO is best in all twelve verifier × thinking-mode cells (8–19% over the strongest baseline), with 7–8% longer accepted prefixes *and* 6% shorter cycles than the sequential correctors. The advantage persists under load: at 4–32 concurrent streams DCOCO beats the strongest alternative by 6–23%, while Domino and DSpark fall *below* uncorrected DFlash at high concurrency as their per-cycle overhead dominates. A selection-rule ablation over identical trained scores shows prefix-first greedy matches geometrically discounted global selection on acceptance at higher throughput. Code and models are released.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

For teams serving LLMs (vLLM-based inference, coding assistants, agentic rollouts), DCOCO is a drop-in throughput win: higher accepted length at lower per-cycle cost, with the margin *growing* under concurrent load where sequential correctors become net slowdowns. The design lesson generalizes: when a pipeline stage has prefix/halt semantics (speculative verification, cascading filters, early-exit checks), optimize the early-prefix quality rather than an aggregate block score, and keep corrective compute off the critical path by batching it into one parallel pass. Practically, math/code workloads see 3.7–6.1x speedups while open-ended chat sees ~1.8x—profile per workload before sizing GPU fleets.
