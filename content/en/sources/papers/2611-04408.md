# Halley: A Predictive Control Plane for Optical AI Data Center Networks

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04408) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Manav Mishra, Krishna M. Sivalingam

## Overview

A frontier AI model is trained in an AI data center on tens of thousands of accelerators joined by a dedicated network, and every training step ends with all of them exchanging data at once. Between racks, that network is optical, and each of its four layers, from lasers to switches, runs a fixed or after-the-fact policy. Lasers sharing an enclosure, cooling, and power can fail together, and at a fixed spare ratio correlated failure provably caps a pool's availability no matter how large it grows. The paper presents Halley, a comprehensive predictive control plane framework that reads each resource's next state and acts before failure occurs. It has four components, each with a formal guarantee: LightReserve sizes the laser pool to provide a coverage bound, FoldFit schedules the switch within 11/9 of the optimum, ThermaLead holds a microring with one-step-ahead control, and TriageGuard protects the highest-risk modules first. Halley gives each layer a predictive controller that defers to the deployed policy when unsure. Composed from simulations, if a window that exhausts its spares is lost, effective goodput rises from 65.2% to 76.5%; if only the dark channels' bandwidth is lost, it reaches 67.4%. Per megawatt this is up to 1.03-1.17 times the training throughput, or for the same training up to 3.3-14.7% less data center capacity. Halley's fallback circuits are formally verified, shown to achieve close timing on a commercial FPGA, and pass a 130 nm signoff.

## Key points

- Correlated laser failures cap availability of a pool at a fixed spare ratio, regardless of pool size.
- Halley predicts each resource's next state and acts proactively across four network layers.
- Components provide formal guarantees for laser pool sizing, switch scheduling, microring control, and risk triage.
- Controllers fall back to deployed policies when uncertain.
- Simulated goodput improves from 65.2% to 76.5% for full window loss and reaches 67.4% for bandwidth loss, with 3.3-14.7% less capacity for the same training.

## Details

The framework composes component-level controllers. Formal verification, FPGA timing, and 130 nm signoff support practical deployment. Fuller metrics and component derivations are in the linked source.
