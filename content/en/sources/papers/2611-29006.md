# NoHairCache: Calibrate-Once KV Cache Eviction with One Power-Law Exponent

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.29006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29006) · **Published**: 2026-11-21 (v1)
**Authors**: Shuyang Xiang

## Overview

Long-context decoding is bottlenecked by the KV cache: memory grows linearly and attention compute quadratically, so token eviction is unavoidable, and the open question is by what criterion to evict. The two dominant paradigms — accumulating historical attention (H2O) and recency-based sliding windows (StreamingLLM) — are usually treated as unrelated heuristics. This paper shows they are opposite endpoints of one power-law family: scoring each cached token i at step t as s_i = A_i · (t − i)^(−α), where A_i is cumulative attention and α ≥ 0 is an age penalty. H2O is exactly α = 0 and a pure sliding window is the α → ∞ limit (StreamingLLM additionally hard-codes an attention-sink patch, which the paper treats as orthogonal to α).

The key empirical claim is that α is an intrinsic property of the model, not of the data: calibrating once on WikiText-103 (100 minimally-differing prompt pairs per axis across 4 axes, 8 shared continuations each, fitting the log-log slope of the prompt-influence profile over octave windows 16…1024) yields per-model mean α between 0.602 and 0.663 with coefficient of variation under 4% across five models spanning 410M–1.5B parameters and two architectures (Pythia, Qwen2.5), and the frozen α transfers unchanged to PG19 and arXiv. This agrees with the prior "no-hair" bound that token influence decays as t^(−α) with α in [0.2, 0.7] for the bulk of layers.

NoHairCache is the resulting drop-in scorer: replace the scoring function of standard top-k eviction with the calibrated power law. Once the per-layer exponents are frozen and age-decay tables D_l[d] = d^(−α(l)) are precomputed, online scoring costs one table lookup and one multiplication per candidate — a single extra scalar multiplication per evicted entry over H2O.

## Key points

- Unified family: s_i(l,h)(t) = A_i(l,h)(t) · (t − i)^(−α(l)); Proposition 1: α = 0 recovers the H2O score exactly; Proposition 2: for α above a finite threshold the score becomes strictly monotone in token age, recovering a pure sliding window. An old high-attention token can outrank a recent low-attention one up to a crossover exponent (illustrated at α_c ≈ 0.67, inside the measured range).
- Why power law over exponential: exponential decay imposes a characteristic length scale (an artificial age cutoff), while power law is scale-free; and its heavy tail keeps old-but-relevant anchors (system prompts, entity definitions) above the eviction threshold instead of purging them (e.g. α = 0.5 decays only ~3.16x over a tenfold span, vs ~4 orders of magnitude for 1/λ = 100 exponential).
- Measured α across five models (Pythia-410M/1B/1.4B, Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B-Instruct): means 0.602–0.663, bulk intervals inside [0.533, 0.676]; Qwen2.5-1.5B range [0.556, 0.822]. α does not drift with scale (Pythia means 0.637/0.663/0.634 non-monotonic) nor much with architecture; first layers carry the highest exponents, middle layers flat. Calibration cost: ~20–79 min per model on a single MacBook M3 (6,400 forward passes).
- Measured α matches the perplexity optimum: an α-sweep on WikiText/PG19/arXiv at budget 512 is flat on [0.6, 0.8] and rises for α > 1 on every dataset; all three per-dataset minima (WikiText/PG19 at 0.8, arXiv at 0.6–0.7) lie inside the hidden-state measurement range [0.556, 0.822]. Calibration granularity barely matters: global scalar α = 0.61, per-layer vector, and even a shuffled per-layer vector land within 0.04 PPL of each other; the theory default α = 0.5 is slightly worse.
- Results: lowest perplexity on all three datasets with no per-dataset tuning (paired Wilcoxon p < 0.05 vs both H2O and StreamingLLM; 0.06–0.10 PPL better than StreamingLLM at budget 512). On downstream four-way multiple-choice QA (NarrativeQA, HotpotQA, Qasper) it is never worse than StreamingLLM across all nine (task, budget) cells — e.g. 98.2% of full-cache accuracy on NarrativeQA at 25% retention vs 91.1% for StreamingLLM — but per-cell McNemar tests are not significant, so QA is corroborating only.
- Deployment tiers: (i) theory default α = 1/2 with zero calibration, (ii) one frozen scalar α calibrated offline, (iii) per-layer α(l) for finer granularity — all under one config switch. Calibration is like LayerNorm running statistics: a structural quantity estimated once per model, not tuned per task.

## Details

Section 3 formalizes the prompt-offset influence profile (RMS over coordinates of the residual-stream difference between minimally differing prompts sharing a continuation), states the no-hair theorem α ∈ [0.2, 0.7] for bulk layers, proves the two endpoint propositions, and argues the attention sink is orthogonal to α. Section 3.3 gives Algorithm 1: offline calibration (fit per-layer exponents, freeze, precompute age tables) then online eviction at each step (update cumulative attention, score with the table, evict lowest). Section 4 evaluates on 50 sequences of 2,048 tokens per dataset with chunk size 128, simulating eviction via attention mask; cross-dataset generalization, budget curves, and cross-model verification all use the single WikiText calibration. Section 5 discusses limitations; calibration script and drop-in scorer are released. Appendices give the full per-layer profile for Qwen2.5-1.5B, budget curves, the full Proposition 2 proof, and LongBench multiple-choice details.
