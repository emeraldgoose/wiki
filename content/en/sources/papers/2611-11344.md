# Spatial Harness: Grounding Spatial Reasoning in Executable World States with Physics Engines

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.11344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11344) · **Published**: 2026-11-08
**Authors**: Chentao Cao, Zhanke Zhou, Sangni Duan, Bo Han, Hang Li

## Overview

Spatial reasoning asks how objects and observers are arranged in a scene and how their relations change when the viewpoint moves. Vision-language models (VLMs) normally handle both steps internally: they form an implicit scene interpretation from partial views and then imagine the consequences of a spatial transformation. This leaves two distinct failure modes entangled in a single answer — the model may misread the layout and reason consistently from a wrong scene, or read the layout correctly and still misapply the requested transformation — and a fluent explanation provides no independent check of either step. The difficulty is real rather than theoretical: GPT-5 reaches 47.59% on MindCube, against 32.35% for random guessing.

The paper's premise is that a scene representation should determine the consequences of spatial operations. An explicit world state makes estimated objects, poses, and relations inspectable, but handing those facts to a model as text still leaves their consequences to internal reasoning; making the state executable, by contrast, lets an engine compute measurements and render views from the declared geometry, so successive operations share one scene against which the model can check its judgments. Execution does not remove perceptual uncertainty, but it separates the assumed layout from the results of the operations performed on it.

Spatial Harness implements this with a Perceiver and a Reasoner in MuJoCo. The Perceiver combines specialized visual estimates with constraints stated in the question — including camera movements that relate observations across views — to place objects and cameras in a shared coordinate frame. The resulting state is instantiated in the physics engine, and the Reasoner queries it through Python, using viewpoint changes, geometric measurements, and rendered views to resolve the question. A worked example in the paper shows the loop in action: after executing a requested 180° turn the first rendering shows a window ahead, while the question asks what is behind, so the Reasoner turns again to inspect the opposite direction, identifies the grey-green decorative wall, and picks the right option.

## Key points

- On MindCube, Spatial Harness achieves the highest overall accuracy with all four VLMs tested and beats the strongest baseline, pySpatial, by 5.40 points on average; with Qwen3.5-27B it reaches 71.14%, which is 18.44 points above the same model answering directly and 7.52 points above pySpatial.
- Holding the scene fixed isolates reasoning from scene estimation. Given identical ground-truth maps of object and camera poses, Qwen3.5-27B scores 71.81% with direct reasoning and 98.25% with Spatial Harness (26.44 points higher), and Qwen2.5-VL-72B gains 42.76 points to 84.22% — so the improvement cannot be explained by a better scene estimate.
- A supplied map does not reliably help direct reasoning: Qwen2.5-VL-7B drops from 35.59% to 30.35% when given the map, while models that exploit it better gain 19.11 points (Qwen3.5-27B) and 30.60 points (Kimi K2.5) — grounding each step in engine feedback helps in both regimes.
- Alternatives fall short for structural reasons: MindJourney, which imagines new viewpoints with a video world model, stays within 4.42 points of the base model because its generated views are not constrained by an explicit scene, and pySpatial, which reasons over a pixel-reconstructed scene, cannot capture information given only in the question, such as camera motion between views, and falls below base accuracy on some question categories.
- Across SAT, MMSI-Bench, and SpatialRGPT-Bench, Spatial Harness has the best average accuracy with both tested models, 15.26 and 14.20 points above the strongest baseline; it reaches 71.60% and 83.20% on SAT, 84.11% and 83.31% on SpatialRGPT-Bench, and lifts Qwen3.5-27B from 33.40% to 39.50% on MMSI-Bench.
- The gains on SpatialRGPT-Bench, roughly 43 and 35 points above the strongest baseline, come from reading distances and object sizes out of metric perception tools and engine measurements rather than from appearance.

## Details

Ablations on MindCube with Qwen3.5-27B and a fixed Reasoner show that the world state has to be constructed carefully. Letting the VLM generate the map directly from images and question, without the Perceiver, collapses accuracy from 71.14% to 41.33%, so structured estimation matters. Removing the text channel, which extracts the relevant entities from the question, costs 3 points (68.38%), while removing the visual channel and having the VLM estimate geometric fields instead of the specialized visual models costs much more (52.95%). Within the text channel, dropping camera and view constraints lowers accuracy to 62.29%, mainly on Rotation questions (81.50% to 48.00%) that state the camera rotation; within the visual channel, replacing the relative-pose estimator is the most damaging substitution at 54.10%, mainly on Among and Around questions where the camera changes position between views, while replacing the orientation estimator costs least (70.57%) because only 36 of the 1,050 questions depend on an object's facing.

The authors are candid about limits. One failure case in the paper shows the model overriding the engine's feedback, and on MMSI-Bench with Qwen2.5-VL-72B the system merely matches the base model (29.00% versus 29.60%) because the model often misreads the coordinates in the world state and does not act on engine feedback. Estimated-state quality therefore still gates performance, and the paper analyses sensitivity to state errors as well as run-to-run variation. The code is publicly released by the TMLR group at Hong Kong Baptist University together with ByteDance Seed.
