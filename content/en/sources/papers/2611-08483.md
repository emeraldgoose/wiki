# SwarmEvolve: Latent Agent Swarms for Evolutionary Discovery and Optimization

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.08483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08483) · **Published**: 2026-11-05
**Authors**: Qile Jiang, George Em Karniadakis

## Overview

LLM-guided evolutionary search improves programs for mathematical and computational problems by iteratively refining solutions and combining high-scoring methods. It is often slow because each round uses a long textual chain of thought and must repeat it many times to maintain population diversity.

SwarmEvolve replaces this bottleneck with hundreds of low-cost agents per round working in swarms. Agents reason in latent space without decoding to text and share information by passing KV caches. Individual swarms study the problem from different angles, and a novelty filter keeps the most distinct ideas by directly measuring distances between hidden state vectors.

Crossover emerges naturally in latent space as a summarizer agent receives the combined KV caches of swarm members. Across 21 mathematical discovery and scientific computing problems, SwarmEvolve achieves the highest average score and needs only 3 to 8 minutes to reach best scores other state-of-the-art frameworks reach in 60 minutes. On several problems it improves on solutions first found by AlphaEvolve using frontier LLMs, demonstrating fast low-cost program search for scientific problems.

## Key points

- Replaces repeated long textual chain-of-thought with latent agent reasoning.
- Uses hundreds of low-cost agents per evolutionary round.
- Shares information through passing KV caches.
- Encourages diversity through a latent-space novelty filter.
- Performs idea crossover through a summarizer agent.
- Substantially reduces time to reach top scores on scientific problems.

## Details

The framework targets mathematical discovery and scientific computing. Results include comparison with AlphaEvolve solutions. The paper demonstrates that latent-space agent communication can improve both cost and speed of program search.
