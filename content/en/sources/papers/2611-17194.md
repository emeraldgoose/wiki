# eDMD: Endpoint Distribution Matching for Few-Step Diffusion Distillation

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.17194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17194) · **Published**: 2026-11-12
**Authors**: Tong Zhao, Xianfang Zeng, Yucheng Han, Ruoyu Wang, Mingkun Lei, Gang Yu, Chi Zhang

## Overview

Distribution matching distillation (DMD) is a leading paradigm for compressing diffusion models into few-step students: an auxiliary "fake" score model tracks the student's distribution, and the discrepancy between teacher and fake predictions drives the student toward the teacher. In multi-step distillation, however, existing methods train the fake model on intermediate states or across all denoising steps even though only the student's final output is ever delivered. The student's update signal thus becomes entangled with intermediate distributions — a mismatch that can distort the matching direction and remains nonzero even after the student's final outputs already match the teacher distribution.

A Zhejiang University / Westlake AGI Lab / StepFun team proposes Endpoint Distribution Matching (eDMD), which trains a single shared fake model exclusively on the student's final outputs and uses it to correct every denoising step. The theoretical contribution is exact: with this endpoint reference, the distribution-matching term vanishes if and only if the student's final output distribution matches the teacher's, regardless of what intermediate distributions look like.

Beyond the fake reference, eDMD strengthens teacher supervision with a precise teacher (PT) that replaces single-step guided predictions with short multi-step trajectories on the final noise interval, paired with a calibrated query density for intermediate steps.

## Key points

- Core mechanism: one shared fake model, trained only on final student outputs, provides the correction signal at every denoising step; the endpoint reference provably makes the matching term vanish exactly when final distributions agree.
- Evaluated on SD3-Medium, FLUX.2-klein (4B and 9B), and Qwen-Image-2.1 for 4-step generation: eDMD achieves the highest HPSv3++ at both the peak checkpoint and fixed training budgets across all three teachers.
- It also leads on DPG-Bench and HPSv2.1 across all three teachers, and on HPSv3, PickScore, and ImageReward on both FLUX.2 models; on Qwen-Image-2.1 it leads the 4-step students on all seven reported metrics.
- Component ablation trained from scratch on SD3-Medium (HPSv3++): full method 11.171 vs 10.122 without the endpoint reference, 11.026 without the precise teacher, 10.798 without calibrated query density — the endpoint reference is the largest single contributor.
- 4-step samples distilled from Qwen-Image-2.1 use a tenth of the teacher's 40 steps while rendering photorealistic scenes, legible text, and diverse artistic styles.
- Code released at github.com/Westlake-AGI-Lab/eDMD, with proofs for the endpoint reference and mixture reference, toy constructions, implementation details, and baseline descriptions in appendices.

## Details

The paper first characterises the reference distribution of the fake model in multi-step matching and verifies its "reference residual" on a 2D synthetic distribution before scaling up. Further sections cover the endpoint reference and local updates, teacher integration in a Gaussian, calibration of the query density, update schedule and training cost, and the application of the endpoint reference to TDM in an appendix, together with training curves, rerun noise, and qualitative results. The method positions itself against DMD2-style multi-step extensions where fake models are updated on intermediate states, and the fixed-budget comparisons rule out peak-checkpoint cherry-picking.
