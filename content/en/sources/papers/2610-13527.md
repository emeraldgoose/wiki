# S-Space: Exploring Spatial Workspace in Multimodal Language Models

**Source**: Shi et al., arXiv, 2026-10-09

**Lead**: Multimodal LLMs show promising spatial reasoning, but how they represent space internally is poorly understood. The authors uncover S-Space, a three-dimensional linear subspace of intermediate activations in which object-token representations encode continuous coordinates along horizontal, vertical, and distance axes. They show S-Space works as an internal spatial workspace—perception writes locations into it, reasoning transforms it, and verbal answers read from it—and use it to diagnose why models fail at viewpoint-changing questions.

## Background

Spatial reasoning matters for reconstruction, navigation, planning, and manipulation, yet MLLMs still lag humans on tasks like perspective-taking. Final answers alone do not reveal where failures come from: bad perception or bad computation over good perception. Inspired by the human "mental workspace" idea, the paper asks whether MLLMs maintain an internal spatial workspace with a unified geometry for encoding, transforming, and verbalizing spatial information.

## Identifying S-Space

S-Space is defined per model and layer (middle layers) as $\mathcal{S} = \mathrm{span}\{v_x, v_y, v_z\}$, three unit vectors for left-right, above-below, and close-far. Each axis is found with a gradient procedure: for relative questions such as "Is the apple to the left or right of the cup?", take the answer logit contrast (e.g. $s_{p,x} = \mathrm{logit}(\mathrm{right}) - \mathrm{logit}(\mathrm{left})$), differentiate with respect to query and reference object-token activations, form the role-antisymmetric difference, and average over templates, object orders, and examples. Readout is a single forward pass plus linear projection $c_t = V^\top h_t$, and a relative judgment is just the coordinate margin $m_g = c_{q,g} - c_{r,g}$, whose sign picks the answer.

## S-Space as Internal Spatial Workspace

Three properties establish S-Space as a workspace rather than a readout artifact:

- **Accuracy**: Projected coordinates correlate strongly with ground-truth object positions, and coordinate-only QA matches the models' own verbal answers, generalizing across CV-Bench, EmbSpatial, and SpatialTunnel—including distributions not used for axis identification.
- **Causality**: Editing S-Space coordinates and writing them back into hidden states changes spatial judgments (vs. random edits), while unrelated color judgments are largely preserved—so the model causally and selectively uses S-Space for spatial answers.
- **Persistence**: The same fixed axes keep supporting accurate spatial QA even when prompts change to single-object queries or non-spatial color questions, indicating S-Space is intrinsic, not prompt-induced.

Case studies show S-Space localizes off-screen entities and even the observer token ("you"), shifts with linguistic context ("moving past you" vs. "moving toward you"), and inherits language biases—"socialist" projects left, "conservative" right on the horizontal axis.

## Experiments

**Setup**: S-Space identified for Molmo2-ER, Qwen3.5-4B, and Qwen3.6-27B from constructed COCO relative-spatial questions; evaluated on CV-Bench, EmbSpatial, SpatialTunnel, SpinBench (perspective-taking), and MMSI-Bench (cardinal directions).

**Perspective-taking results (SpinBench accuracy, %)**:

| Model | Direct | +Reasoning | S-Space + Rotation |
|-------|--------|-----------|--------------------|
| Molmo2-ER | 60.96 | — | 94.52 (+33.56) |
| Qwen3.5-4B | 60.96 | 62.33 (+1.37) | 91.10 (+30.14) |
| Qwen3.6-27B | 60.96 | 83.56 (+22.60) | 95.21 (+34.25) |

With oracle source-view premises supplied, S-Space + rotation reaches 97.92–99.31% while direct answers still lag (60–73%), exposing computation—not perception—as the bottleneck. Tracking S-Space through chain-of-thought shows coordinates progressively rotate toward the target viewpoint, but imperfectly. Cardinal-direction tokens reveal reference-frame coupling: "east" aligns with image-right, "north" with image-up in S-Space.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

S-Space offers a practical debugging lens for vision-language applications:

- **Separate perception from reasoning failures**: probe internal coordinates before blaming the vision encoder—if S-Space is accurate, the fix belongs in the reasoning/transformation step, not retraining perception.
- **Oracle transforms as guardrails**: for viewpoint-dependent tasks (robotics, AR), an explicit coordinate rotation over read-out positions can outperform asking the model to reason it out.
- **Watch for frame coupling**: systems mixing map directions (N/E) with image directions (left/up) should validate outputs, since the model partially shares these representations.
