# Percolation Dynamics in Optimization: Variance Cascades and Nested Symmetry

Source: Sai Niranjan Ramachandran, Suvrit Sra, arXiv:2609.02373, Sep 2, 2026.

**Lead**: Stochastic Gradient Descent is known to steer deep networks toward invariant sets corresponding to simpler subnetworks — but *how* that steering unfolds over time is poorly understood. This paper models stochastic gradient flow as a **percolation process**: nested architectural symmetries force subnetworks to merge in discrete blocks rather than by single-edge attachment, and those structural transitions register as **variance spikes** in a macroscopic order parameter, echoing physical phase transitions. The authors further state sufficient conditions under which the trapping argument carries over to **Adam and AdamW under heavy-tailed gradient noise**, and measure them on a trained Transformer. (Note: the v1 subtitle "Discrete Scale Invariance" became "Nested Symmetry" in the Sep 27 v2, which also corrected the Adam theory and added appendix experiments.)

## Percolation as the mechanism

The framing borrows from statistical physics: training is not smooth descent but a sequence of discrete structural events. Symmetries nested in the architecture quantize the merging process — whole blocks snap together at once — so the observable signature is a cascade of variance spikes in an order parameter rather than gradual change. That gives practitioners something concrete to look for in training traces: sudden variance structure marks a symmetry-driven transition, not (only) instability or bad hyperparameters.

## From SGD to Adam

The headline theoretical move is portability: the trapping argument is not SGD-specific. Under stated sufficient conditions it extends to Adam/AdamW even with heavy-tailed gradient noise — the noise regime real Transformer training lives in — and the paper backs this with measurements on a trained Transformer. The 49-page scope (9 in the main text) suggests the conditions and verification are technical; the v2 revision history signals the Adam results needed correction, so readers should follow the v2 statements.

## Caveats

This summary is abstract-based: the order parameter's definition, the sufficient conditions, and the Transformer measurements all need the full text, and no quantitative effect sizes are available from the abstract. The v1→v2 theory correction means early citations of the Adam claims may describe superseded results. As a largely theoretical contribution, there are no benchmark wins to anchor practical impact — treat it as a lens for interpreting training dynamics, not a recipe.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Variance spikes during training may be structural transitions, not bugs — before killing a run, check whether loss/trajectory structure resumes normally afterward.
- If you log training dynamics, log distributional statistics (variance structure), not just mean loss: the signal in this paper lives in the second moment.
- Heavy-tailed gradient noise is assumed, not wished away — clipping/normalization choices interact with exactly the regime this theory addresses.
- When citing the Adam/AdamW extension, cite v2 (Sep 27) — v1's theoretical statements were corrected.
