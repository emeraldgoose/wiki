# Embodied-Harness: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement for Robotic Manipulation

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.27689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27689) · **Published**: 2026-11-20 (v1; v2 revised 2026-11-23)
**Authors**: Zhenxuan Fan, Zhuoya Wang, Yuqian Yuan, Wenqiao Zhang, Juncheng Li, Jun Xiao, Siliang Tang, Yueting Zhuang

## Overview

VLM-based agentic frameworks can plan and adapt robot actions without task-specific training, but they suffer from three gaps: poor memory organization across long-horizon episodes, failure diagnoses that are not grounded in what actually happened, and reflections that never feed back into later attempts. Embodied-Harness addresses all three with a dual-agent design in which an Actor executes manipulation through a robot-control interface while a frozen Reflector turns each execution trajectory into reusable operational guidance. Crucially, the method never updates model parameters — it refines only the "execution harness" (the operational instructions the Actor follows), forming a recursive loop of execution, evidence-based diagnosis, guidance generation, and harness update.

The framework has three mechanisms. (1) A Hierarchical Memory Architecture pairs short-term working memory — a Condensed Progress Memory that summarizes completed subgoals, pending operations, and constraints, plus a Detailed Execution Memory that appends every observation/state/action/feedback record — with long-term experiential memory split into Task-Specific Memory (outcomes, diagnoses, guidance per task) and Cross-Task Memory (generalized operational principles). (2) Evidence-based Experience Reflection assigns each failure diagnosis to exactly one of five operational categories: Contact & Pose Control, Motion & Coordination, Task Protocol & Completion, Spatial Grounding, and State Tracking & Recovery. (3) Recursive Harness Self-Improvement converts the diagnosis into candidate guidance ΔG_k, integrates it into the harness H_{k+1}, and lets the next attempt's evidence drive further refinement.

The Actor's action interface has five command types — move, rotate, point, gripper, home — whose target poses are resolved to joint configurations by inverse kinematics under joint-limit and collision constraints.

## Key points

- Zero-shot results with a frozen GPT-6 Astra backbone: 82.4% success on RoboTwin 2.0 Clean-to-Randomized (vs 53.2% for RoboDawn with one demonstration, 75.5% for the strongest WAM PrismWAM, 58.4% for the best HarnessVLA variant); 94.0% average on RoboSPA fine-grained spatial reasoning at the hardest level L5 (vs 65.6% for RoboDawn, with CVR 24.0%→98.0%), holding 94–99% across difficulty levels L1–L5; 59.5% SR / 74.9% progress on RoboSPA long-horizon planning L5 (vs 41.4% / 56.4% for RoboDawn); 94.4% average on RMBench's nine memory-dependent tasks, with 100% on five of them (best baseline MaP-WAM 83.3%).
- Real-world evaluation on a Piper arm: 96.7% average over three tasks (90.0% arrangement, 100% placement, 100% stacking) vs 40.0% for π0.5.
- Recursive refinement compounds: over harness rounds 0→4, success rises 59.2→82.4% (RoboTwin 2.0), 78.2→94.0% (RoboSPA-Spatial), 43.0→59.5% (RoboSPA-Long), 71.7→94.4% (RMBench), with diminishing gains in later rounds.
- Ablations on RoboSPA-Long L5: removing short-term working memory hurts most (SR 59.5→32.1%, PS 74.9→49.5%); without the Reflector SR 43.0%, without long-term experiential memory 48.5%, without evidence grounding 52.3%.
- Reflection outcome distribution: Contact & Pose Control 34.5%, Motion & Coordination 22.7%, Task Protocol & Completion 21.8%, State Tracking & Recovery 10.9%, Spatial Grounding 10.1%.
- Backbone matters more than reasoning effort: GPT-6 Astra scores 59.5–61.1% SR on RoboSPA-Long regardless of effort level; the strongest open model (Qwen3.8-Max) reaches only 29.0% SR / 50.4% PS.
- Protocol: 45 interaction rounds per episode, 10 episodes per task (20 on RMBench), success rate plus progress score reported on long-horizon tasks.

## Details

Each Actor step samples an action from the frozen policy conditioned on the task instruction, current observation and proprioceptive state, the harness, and working memory; trajectories τ_k = {(o, s, a, f)} are the evidence passed to the Reflector. After each attempt the Reflector produces diagnosis d_k = R_φ(g, τ_k, r_k, H_k, E_k), generates candidate guidance ΔG_k = Generate(d_k, H_k, E_k), and updates the harness H_{k+1} = Update(H_k, ΔG_k, E_k), which retains effective guidance and resolves inconsistencies using accumulated experience. Long-term memory updates are E^s_{k+1}(i) = Update(E^s_k(i), r_k, d_k, ΔG_k) per task and E^x_{k+1} = Generalize(E^x_k, E^s_{k+1}(i)) across tasks. RoboTwin 2.0 covers 50 tasks under C2R; RoboSPA has 24 spatial (evaluated L1–L5) and 32 long-horizon procedural tasks at L5; RMBench has 9 memory-dependent tasks.
