# Breath-Dx: A Time Series Classification Benchmark for Disease Screening with eNose

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15348)
**Published**: 2026-11-11

## Overview

Electronic nose (eNose) devices offer a portable, non-invasive way to screen disease from exhaled breath, but the field lacks systematic benchmarks evaluated under realistic conditions — especially sensor drift, a known but rarely quantified failure mode. Existing studies typically report single methods on small private datasets (often under 100 samples) with inconsistent protocols, making results non-comparable. Breath-Dx (IEEE ICDM 2026 Best Paper Runner-Up, Applied Track) introduces the first comprehensive benchmark of time series classification for breath-based disease screening, built on Scent of Health (S-OH), the largest public clinical eNose collection to date: 1,234 patients across nine diagnostic groups, each providing a 17-channel multivariate time series with explicit temporal train/test splits that account for sensor drift.

Ten methods spanning four families (feature-based tabular learners, convolution-based models, a graph-based model, and time series architectures) were evaluated. All methods degrade significantly under temporal validation — a 0.05-0.12 AUC drop versus random cross-validation — confirming that drift embeds a strong temporal signal. The best methods reach ROC AUC of 0.74-0.76 for some lung conditions while others remain near chance. Recurrent architectures are the most consistent across diseases; specialized time series methods achieve the highest peak results. The S-OH dataset, temporal splits, evaluation protocols, and code are released for reproducible research.

## Key points

- S-OH dataset: 1,234 patients, nine diagnostic groups, 17-channel multivariate time series per patient, explicit temporal splits simulating sensor drift; released publicly with code and evaluation protocols.
- Temporal (drift-aware) validation costs every method 0.05-0.12 AUC versus random cross-validation — drift is a first-class evaluation axis, not an afterthought.
- Best results: lung cancer (C34), hepatitis (B18), and COPD (J44) exceed or approach AUC 0.70. InceptionTime leads lung cancer (AUC 0.746), Graphormer leads COPD (AUC 0.767), CatBoost + Stats_5 leads hepatitis (AUC 0.698).
- Diabetes (E11) and respiratory tuberculosis (A15) stay near chance (AUC 0.45-0.55) across all methods — the breath signal is weak, confounded, or beyond current sensor limits for these conditions.
- LogisticRegression baseline remains competitive on several tasks (e.g., K29 AUC 0.718, B18 0.687, A15 0.688), warning against assuming deep models win by default.
- Among feature-based ensembles, Stats_5 features consistently beat kinetic LogFit_2/LogFit_4 feature sets across most models.
- Demographic confounders matter: breath signals add independent diagnostic value for some conditions but little beyond age for others.

## Details

- Evaluation: ROC AUC (mean ± std) over nine binary classification tasks (Z00, E11, K29, K76, B18, C34, N18, J44, A15), drift-aware temporal splits; demographic analysis and drift quantification are included in the protocol.
- Method families: feature-based (CatBoost, XGBoost, HGBoost, RandomForest + LogFit/Stats features), convolution-based (CNN/ResNet18 + Polynomial), graph-based (Graphormer), and time series (LSTM, InceptionTime, TS2Vec), plus a LogisticRegression baseline.
- No single winner: method selection depends on clinical priorities (peak AUC vs. cross-disease consistency vs. robustness to drift).
- Limitations: performance ceiling for most conditions is far from clinically deployable; drift remains a critical deployment barrier; some tasks are near chance with current sensors.
