# Calpha: Toward Native Biomolecular Intelligence in a General-Purpose Language Model

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.20986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20986) · **Published**: 2026-11-16
**Authors**: Yong Liu, Zhanpeng Shi, Peicong Lin, Zhenyi Zhong, Yizhou Dang, Wendi Wu, Haoling Li, Shuangjia Zheng

## Overview

Biomolecular foundation models can predict the structures of proteins and their complexes accurately, while large language models reason in natural language — but the two capabilities remain separate. The structure models are not part of a general-purpose LLM, and the LLM's reasoning does not enter the structure prediction. Calpha aims to close that gap with a single general model that folds proteins and complexes, understands protein sequence and structure, and lets language reasoning influence the structures it predicts.

This report covers the first step toward that goal: a general-purpose LLM that outputs single-chain protein structures. Qwen3.5-9B reads a sequence enriched with ESM2 residue features, and it can either respond in text or emit a special token that invokes a pretrained SimpleFold structure head. The head receives information about the protein only through Qwen's residue states — no separate structural encoder feeds it directly.

The authors compare this LLM-driven folding against a baseline in which ESM2-3B encodes the sequence and a fine-tuned SimpleFold-3B head predicts the coordinates. They caution that the two systems differ not only in architecture but also in trainable weights, training duration and crop length, so the comparison is not a clean ablation.

## Key points

- Calpha's goal is one general model that folds proteins and complexes, understands protein sequence and structure, and lets language reasoning influence predicted coordinates; this report covers only single-chain folding as the first step.
- Qwen3.5-9B consumes a protein sequence enriched with ESM2 residue features and either answers in text or emits a token that triggers a pretrained SimpleFold structure head.
- The SimpleFold head receives protein information only through Qwen's residue states, making the LLM's internal representations the sole structural input.
- Under a fixed folding request, the head achieves mean TM-scores of 0.808 on the FoldBench-334 development set, 0.635 on CASP16 domains and 0.900 on a CAMEO-style PDB set.
- Against the ESM2-3B + fine-tuned SimpleFold-3B baseline: for chains of 100-299 residues, all three paired confidence intervals include zero; at 300-600 residues, mean differences are -0.044, -0.160 and -0.025, respectively.
- Four English request types triggered folding behavior on every evaluated example in a separate test.
- Protein training reduced accuracy on a 350-item MMLU-Pro subset from 77.4% to 70.3%.
- Complex prediction, structure understanding and the effect of language reasoning on predicted coordinates remain future work.

## Details

Method. The system pairs Qwen3.5-9B with a pretrained SimpleFold structure head. Input sequences are enriched with ESM2 residue features before being read by the LLM. During generation, the model either produces ordinary text or emits a dedicated invocation token; that token hands off to the SimpleFold head, which predicts coordinates using only the residue states produced by Qwen — it has no independent access to the raw sequence or to ESM2 embeddings beyond what flows through the LLM. The Calpha framework targets a unified model that can both converse about biomolecules and fold them, with language reasoning intended to shape structural output.

Evaluation. Folding quality is measured by mean TM-score on FoldBench-334 (development set), CASP16 domains, and a CAMEO-style PDB set, all under a fixed folding request. The comparison baseline encodes the sequence with ESM2-3B and predicts coordinates with a fine-tuned SimpleFold-3B head. The authors stress the systems are not matched on trainable weights, training duration or crop length, limiting how cleanly the gap can be attributed. By residue-length band, 100-299 residue chains show no significant difference (all three paired confidence intervals include zero), while 300-600 residue chains favor the baseline with mean differences of -0.044, -0.160 and -0.025 across the three sets.

Beyond folding, the authors test instruction following and general capability. Four English request types triggered the folding behavior on every evaluated example. However, adding protein training reduced accuracy on a 350-item MMLU-Pro subset from 77.4% to 70.3%, indicating a measurable cost to general reasoning capability under the current recipe.

Takeaway. An LLM can output single-chain protein structures from its own residue states, but current comparisons show lower long-chain scores and a reduced MMLU-Pro subset accuracy. Complex prediction, structure understanding, and the influence of language reasoning on predicted coordinates are explicitly left as future work.
