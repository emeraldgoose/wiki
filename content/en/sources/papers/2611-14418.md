# Precision on Demand: Escalating Weight Bits at Uncertain Decoding Steps in a Small Local Model

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.14418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14418) · **Published**: 2026-11-10
**Authors**: Mohammed Nayeem Khan, Logesh L, Aliyas Ahmad Misri

## Overview

Local decoding typically commits to a single weight precision for the entire generation. This companion study to the same authors' bits-versus-permutations paper explores a different allocation: keep 8-bit weights in memory, decode every step using only their top 4 bits, and redo a step at 8 bits only when the low-precision output distribution looks uncertain — signaled by entropy above a threshold or a small top-two margin. Quantization is simulated, the model is Qwen3-0.6B, the benchmark is GSM8K, and all costs are counted in one unit: weight bytes read per token.

The replay analysis is encouraging. Over 36,724 replayed steps, the entropy of the low-precision pass ranks argmax flips (steps where the 4-bit pass would disagree with the 8-bit pass on the top token) with an AUC of 0.93. At a 15% escalation budget, the entropy gate catches 78% of flips and the margin gate catches 94%, against the 15% expected from a random gate of equal rate — an expectation the authors compute rather than run.

The live picture is more reserved. On 150 live problems, all-high decoding scores 64.0% and all-low 55.3% (McNemar p = 0.053). An entropy gate with a 16% escalation rate scores 62.7% while reading 0.87 times the weight bytes of all-high decoding. But its paired difference is -1.3 points (-8.0 to +5.3) against all-high and +7.3 points (+0.0 to +14.7) against all-low (p = 0.080), so the live data do not statistically separate the gate from either extreme. In the same cost model, a verified-speculation scheme — checking the low-pass step and redoing it at full precision — reaches zero KL divergence at a similar byte count. The authors list what was not run: a live random control, a thinking-mode phase rule, a second model, and live KL measurement.

## Key points

- Mechanism: store 8-bit weights, decode each step with the top 4 bits, and escalate the step to 8-bit weights only when the low-precision distribution is uncertain — entropy above a threshold or a small top-two margin.
- Setup: simulated quantization; Qwen3-0.6B; GSM8K; cost measured as weight bytes read per token.
- Replay diagnostics: 36,724 replayed steps; low-pass entropy ranks argmax flips with AUC 0.93.
- Gate quality at a 15% escalation budget: entropy gate catches 78% of flips, margin gate 94%, versus 15% expected from a random gate of equal rate (computed expectation, not a run).
- Live results (150 problems): all-high 64.0%, all-low 55.3% (McNemar p = 0.053); entropy-gated decoding with 16% escalation scores 62.7% at 0.87x the all-high weight bytes.
- Statistical verdict: the gate's paired difference is -1.3 points (-8.0 to +5.3) versus all-high and +7.3 points (+0.0 to +14.7) versus all-low (p = 0.080) — the live data do not separate the gate from either end.
- Verified speculation: in the same cost model, verifying the low-pass step (and redoing at 8 bits on mismatch) reaches zero KL divergence at similar bytes.
- Stated gaps: no live random control, no thinking-mode phase rule, no second model, no live KL were run.

## Details

### The escalation design

The scheme treats weight precision as a per-step resource rather than a global constant. Memory always holds the 8-bit weights; the 4-bit pass reads only the top bits, so its per-token byte cost is roughly half. When the low-precision distribution is confident, the step stands; when entropy is high or the top two logits are nearly tied, the step is recomputed with full 8-bit weights, paying the escalation on a minority of steps. The gates are cheap statistics of the low pass itself — no extra forward passes are needed to decide whether to escalate.

### What the two phases establish

The replay phase establishes that uncertainty signals computed on the cheap pass are predictive of precision-induced disagreement (AUC 0.93), and that a 15% escalation budget can capture most flips (78% entropy, 94% margin) — far above the random-gate expectation of 15%. The live phase then asks whether this translates into accuracy at a byte budget: a 62.7% score at 0.87x the all-high bytes is close to all-high accuracy (64.0%) and clearly above all-low (55.3%), but the confidence intervals on the paired differences include zero, so neither a savings claim nor a quality claim survives statistics with this sample size. The zero-KL result of verified speculation marks the opposite end of the trade-off: paying similar bytes guarantees exact agreement with the full-precision distribution.
