# VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.08449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08449) · **Published**: 2026-11-05
**Authors**: Yu-Cheng Chou, Shihao Wang, Jin Huang, Chi-Pin Huang, Chiao-An Yang, Xianzhe Fan, Jing Wang, Zhiqi Li, Yuanguo Kuang, Ruijie Zheng, Johan Bjorck, Sifei Liu, Hang Su, Abhishek Badki, Lei Zhang, Alan Yuille, Linxi Fan, Jan Kautz, Zhiding Yu

## Overview

Physical AI models, including robotics and autonomous driving systems, can represent spatial goals and motion through visual plans rather than reasoning solely in language. These plans may include points, bounding boxes, and trajectories, but autoregressive vision-language models generate them one coordinate token at a time, making richer predictions increasingly costly.

VFlash addresses this through Flash Primitive Decoding. It exploits the known structure of visual primitives and uses a lightweight drafter to predict an entire primitive's coordinates in one forward pass. The layout adapts to primitive type, dimensionality, and length, supporting compact boxes and long variable-length trajectories.

Spatial accuracy does not require reproducing an autoregressive model's exact token sequence. VFlash trains directly on annotated primitives and accepts structurally valid predictions. Across 2D and 3D robot trajectory tasks it consistently reduces prediction errors relative to autoregressive decoding, with preliminary measurements indicating about 25 times faster generation for 3D point motion forecasting. A world action model conditioned on VFlash plans achieves 70.2% average success across four MolmoSpaces task suites versus 62.6% for the baseline.

## Key points

- Enables fast decoding of structured visual plans.
- Plans use points, boxes, and trajectories rather than only language.
- Generates a full primitive in one forward pass.
- Adapts to type, dimensionality, and length of primitives.
- Trains on annotated primitives rather than exact token sequences.
- Reduces errors and improves downstream visual-planning success.

## Details

The paper provides qualification for visual planning in robotics and autonomous driving. Approximate speedup is based on preliminary single-rollout measurements.
