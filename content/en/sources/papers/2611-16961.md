# The Value of Thinking: Learning When to Reason in Hybrid Decision Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.16961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16961) · **Published**: 2026-11-12
**Authors**: Zhengxu Yu, Ke Xu, Meng Fang, Haitham Ammar, Weilin Luo, Jun Wang

## Overview

Existing adaptive-reasoning methods shorten the explicit reasoning a model writes or skip it by switching modes, but either way the answer is still written out token by token. This paper explores a different paradigm, zero-or-T thinking: a single model either answers from the implicit thinking inside one forward pass, generating no token at all, or performs full autoregressive reasoning at a cost of T tokens when that is worth paying. The model thus decides whether autoregressive thinking is needed at all, rather than how much to think, and a question costs either nothing or the full price of a reasoning pass.

The paradigm is realized in a hybrid decision model (HDM). One frozen backbone serves both modes: the fast-thinking mode produces a decision directly from its implicit thinking through a readout layer, generating no token, while the slow-thinking mode is the backbone's own chain-of-thought reasoning inherited unchanged. Between them sits a learned self-routing block of a few hundred kilobytes that reads the hidden state of one transformer layer plus eight statistics of the answer the fast pass already produced, so the routing choice costs no extra forward pass and no token. The block estimates the value of thinking — the utility the slow mode is expected to add over the fast one — and the slow mode runs only where that value exceeds a price lambda set per request. One trained block therefore covers the whole cost-accuracy curve: varying lambda moves the operating point along it without retraining.

The authors stress that confidence is no substitute for this estimate. Confidence reports how sure the fast answer is, which is a different question from whether the slow mode would answer differently; the two come apart in both directions, since thinking sometimes spoils an answer the fast mode had right and adds nothing where both modes agree.

Where no per-decision label exists, as in episodic agentic tasks, the paper introduces Calibrated Decision Policy Optimisation (CDPO), a reinforcement-learning recipe that trains the routing block from episode-level returns discounted by the escalations spent. It is evaluated with two backbones, Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.8-27B, a 4,096-token thinking budget, and SGLang serving, across MMLU-Pro, AGIEval, GPQA-Diamond, JevBench, BANKING77, ALFWorld, and MiniWoB.

## Key points

- On JevBench's 231 public items, HDM-4B scores 0.857 against Jev's 0.851 (+0.7%) at a median 0.179 s per item against Jev's 0.652 s (72.5% lower); HDM-27B reaches 0.987 at 2.49 s.
- The router transfers to question types it never trained on: JevBench types its items as 139 choice, 74 noul (yes-or-no), and 18 score, and at lambda 0 the block escalates noul items at 0.784 against choice items at 0.417, although it trained on multiple choice only.
- BANKING77, a 20-way intent task over 300 items, is where thinking does not pay: always thinking reaches 0.913 at 135.3 tokens per item, the fast mode alone reaches 0.923 at none, and the router follows the cheaper mode, reaching 0.920 at lambda 0.05 for 1.1 tokens per item.
- On AGIEval, HDM-27B trades 1.5 accuracy points for 37% fewer thinking tokens; on GPQA-Diamond the block evaluates all 198 questions.
- On the agentic venue MiniWoB (27 held-out click tasks), HDM reaches 0.469 against always-thinking's 0.519, giving up 0.05 of reward to cut thinking tokens by 80.1% and wall-clock by 67.4%.
- On ALFWorld (134 held-out unseen games), the block at lambda 0 reaches 0.560 between the fast floor of 0.463 and the always-think ceiling of 0.642 — 54% of the available gain for 48% of the thinking tokens.
- Against direct routing rules at fixed accuracy, reaching 90% of always-thinking's performance costs 103 tokens per question on the 27B, versus 112 for RouteLLM, 138 for a confidence threshold, and 591 for always thinking; on the 4B, HDM spends 792 against RouteLLM's 822, while a published switching rule costs more than escalating at random (352 vs 213 tokens).
- Training data comes from eleven of MMLU-Pro's fourteen subject categories (1,154 of 1,500 questions) with three categories (346 questions) held out, so every reported number measures transfer to unseen subjects; the thinking budget is truncated on 36.1% of 4B questions and 14.9% of 27B questions, making those token counts and accuracies lower bounds.
- The routing objective formalizes that a correct answer is worth less the longer it takes: utility is discounted by an exponential in the tokens generated, and the slow mode runs only when the value of thinking exceeds lambda — that is, when thinking raises the chance of a correct answer by a factor of at least exp of lambda.
- Training draws lambda per example from the range -0.2 to 0.5, so one block serves any request-time price, including prices above the training range in evaluation sweeps.

## Details

### One frozen backbone, one small block

HDM encodes the prompt with the fast-thinking mode and reads an answer at the readout position, producing on the same pass the hidden state the block consumes. The block reads that hidden state and eight statistics of the answer — the quantities the fast pass has already computed — and returns a distribution over the four joint outcomes of whether the fast and slow modes would each be correct, so it estimates both modes' utility before either has run. Escalated questions continue autoregressively from the state the fast pass computed. The typed input schema lets the same backbone and block serve a choice among candidates, a binary judgement, a graded score, or free-form generation.

### The price of thinking

The reward a mode earns is its correctness under an exponential discount at rate rho on the tokens it generates: each generated token multiplies utility by e to the minus rho, and a wrong answer is worth zero at every price. The user sets lambda per request, turning routing into a simple threshold on the predicted value of thinking; no price appears in the training loss, so one fitted block serves every lambda.

### CDPO and what routing buys

For non-episodic tasks the block trains by self-labelled supervised learning on the four-outcome targets observed by running both modes. Episodic tasks such as ALFWorld and MiniWoB have no per-decision label, so CDPO trains the block from the return an episode earns, discounted by the escalations it spent. The evaluation shows the payoff over both endpoints and published System One models: near-Jev accuracy at a fraction of the latency on JevBench, cheaper-than-always-thinking operation on BANKING77 where thinking actively hurts, and double-digit token savings on agentic benchmarks while retaining most of the reward.
