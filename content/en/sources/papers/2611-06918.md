# From Pixels to Actions: Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.06918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06918) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Weichen Fan, Haiwen Diao, Penghao Wu, Siyuan Hu, Ziqi Huang, Tianqi Liu, Mingze Sun, Ziyi Wang, Zhongang Cai, Hanming Deng, Quan Wang, Lei Yang, Lewei Lu, Fangzhou Hong, Zhaoxi Chen, Dahua Lin, Ziwei Liu

## Overview

Vision-language-action models are typically built on the premise that perception is first compressed by visual encoders, and action reasoning begins only afterwards. Yet manipulation often hinges on precisely the visual signals most vulnerable to this abstraction, such as local geometry, boundaries, depth, and subtle state changes. The paper asks whether action policies should inherit a fixed visual endpoint or directly access evolving visual computation. It introduces NEO-VLA, a structurally encoder-free VLA framework. Images are tokenized by lightweight patch convolutional layers and evolve jointly with language throughout the backbone, allowing a flow-matching action expert to draw from instruction-conditioned visual representations at different depths. This simple change reveals a consistent pattern: native architectures outperform encoder-based counterparts under controlled comparisons; full fine-tuning reduces but does not erase the gap, and additional scale alone cannot recover it. More strikingly, layer-wise probing shows that action-critical signals often peak well before the final visual representation. Across diverse manipulation settings and distribution shifts, NEO-VLA consequently achieves stronger and more robust control. Together, these results suggest that embodied control benefits from access to visual representations across the computational hierarchy, rather than a fixed encoder output.

## Key points

- Standard VLAs compress perception via visual encoders before action reasoning, losing fine-grained signals.
- Manipulation often depends on local geometry, boundaries, depth, and subtle state changes.
- NEO-VLA is encoder-free: patch convolution tokenizes images and visual features evolve with language through the backbone.
- Action expert can draw from instruction-conditioned visual reps at multiple depths.
- Native architectures outperform encoder-based ones even after fine-tuning/scale; action-critical signals often peak before final layer.

## Details

Evaluation covers diverse manipulation tasks and distribution shifts. Probing analyses link performance to representational depth. Full architecture and benchmark details are in the linked source.
