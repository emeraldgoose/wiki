# When Morphs Look Better: Face Image Quality as a Training-Free Signal for Morphing Attack Detection

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.00074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00074) · **Published**: 2026-10-15
**Authors**: Hao Peng, Fu Bo, Mahmoud Elshirbeny, Guray Ozgur

## Overview

Face image quality assessment (FIQA) estimates the utility of a face image for automated face recognition and has been proposed as a morphing attack detection signal requiring no morph-specific training. This paper evaluates 17 FIQA methods from all current FIQA categories on 20 morphing attack sets from FRLL and FEI, including five diffusion-based attack sets. Each quality score is used directly as a decision score. Unlike prior work that inverted a quality measure when it lowered error, the study fixes score direction in advance from each method's definition.

## Key points

- FIQA methods are tested without morph-specific training.
- Seventeen methods are compared across FIQA categories.
- Twenty morphing attack sets come from two datasets.
- Five additional attack sets use diffusion-based morphing.
- Quality scores are used directly as decision scores.
- Score direction is fixed rather than post-hoc optimized.

## Details

The study treats face image quality as a possible proxy for manipulated identity strength. By avoiding training on morph examples, it tests a simpler detection concept. The benchmark includes a broad range of attacks and quality methods, and fixes decision directions before evaluation. This removes a source of optimistic reporting that can arise when direction is chosen after seeing labels. The results clarify when quality behaves as a reliable morphing attack indicator.
