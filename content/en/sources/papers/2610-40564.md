# Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Yuda Song

## Overview

Foundation models are typically trained in stages: pretraining, midtraining, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL), with changes in both data and learning algorithms. This paper shows the algorithmic separation is not fundamental. By deriving suitable RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions, it places all these stages under a single likelihood-based training algorithm called unified training. This perspective turns the foundation-model training lifecycle into a problem of choosing which data to train on, in what proportions, and when, with conventional staged training as a special case. The paper instantiates the framework in a unified training system that mixes static data and RL generations within a single trainer. Across controlled and real-world experiments, unified training improves the accuracy-compute tradeoff and task coverage while preserving broader capabilities, and provides a stronger starting point for continual learning. The work lays groundwork for a unified science of foundation-model training, connecting mathematical principles, training infrastructure, and capability development.

## Key points

- Pretraining, midtraining, SFT, and RL are often algorithmically separated.
- RL objectives are derived as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated targets.
- All stages are placed under one likelihood-based training algorithm.
- Unified training mixes static data and RL generations in one trainer.
- It improves accuracy-compute tradeoff and task coverage.
- It preserves broader capabilities and strengthens continual-learning starts.
- Staged training becomes a special case of data scheduling.

## Details

The unification moves the central design question to data selection, proportion, and timing rather than algorithm choice. The instantiated system demonstrates this in both controlled and real-world settings.
