# Reflex: Visually Grounded Reactive Humanoid Control

**Source**: Jia et al., arXiv, 2026-10-08

**Lead**: Catching a thrown box with a humanoid couples three hard problems: whole-body control (foot placement, impact absorption, balance), sub-second reaction to object dynamics under latency, and perception from a moving egocentric camera. Reflex splits them into three stages connected by a compact 8-D object-dynamics latent: PPO whole-body control from privileged state, latent dynamics learning under delay/dropout, then visual grounding from RGB-D history with the controller frozen. Simulation reaches 85.7% from egocentric RGB-D (vs 87.9% privileged); a real Unitree G1 catches 65% and 40% of human throws at 2.5 m and 5 m.

## Background

Prior catching systems dodge at least one of the three challenges: fixed-base arms skip balance, humanoid goalkeepers/catchers rely on motion capture or marked objects instead of onboard vision. Quasi-static loco-manipulation (lifting boxes at rest) lets the robot choose contact timing; catching removes that freedom—the body must be in place when the box arrives (~1 s flight, ~69 ms median camera-to-control latency).

Reflex's decomposition gives each capability a direct training signal and keeps expensive rendering out of RL: Stage 2 PPO runs ~28k steps/s rendering-free vs ~1.5k with RGB-D in the loop (59M steps: ~35 min instead of ~11 h).

## Three-Stage Method

**Stage 1 — whole-body control**: PPO trains a state-based 29-DoF policy (512–256–128 MLP, asymmetric critic) with approach/hold shaping, sustained-catch bonus, retargeted human motion tracking + AMP style reward, and fall/torso penalties. Throws, contact, and dynamics randomized.

**Stage 2 — object dynamics latent**: a privileged encoder compresses a delayed, partially observed 26-D box observation (position, velocity, extents, orientation, age, visibility) into an 8-D bounded latent. Action-matching behavior cloning against the Stage 1 teacher initializes the latent pathway (visible samples weighted 5×, 5 DAgger rounds), then joint encoder/decoder PPO refines it under 20–60 ms latency randomization. Probes show the latent encodes delayed position/velocity ($R^2$ 0.90–0.99) and partly compensates observation age—not size or orientation.

**Stage 3 — visual grounding**: a CNN predicts a box-localization heatmap from RGB-D; a GRU fuses 8 geometric features + 9-D ego-motion over a 25-frame history to regress the fixed Stage 2 latent (+ auxiliary position/velocity loss). Controller frozen; DAgger-style aggregation on visual-controller rollouts; color augmentation for appearance robustness. Downstream, a supervisor hands the retained box to a SONIC walking controller for carry + handover.

## Experiments

**Setup**: Isaac Lab simulation (200 Hz physics, 50 Hz control); Unitree G1, 29 DoF, torso RGB-D (192×144). Training throws 2.5–5.0 m; eval 640 throws/condition from 3.0–3.5 m. Catch = chest-region support, wrists flanking, low speed for 0.6 s. Real robot: 20 trials/condition, human-thrown boxes.

**Benchmarks**: Catch/interception rates across box size, weight, distance, color, lateral offset, added delay; ablations of reward, initialization, visual inputs; real-robot trials; detector+Kalman baselines.

**Results**:

| Result | Number |
|--------|--------|
| Simulation overall (6 visual controllers) | 85.7% (3290/3840) vs 87.9% privileged latent — vision preserves nearly all performance |
| Real robot (regular box) | 65% / 40% / 15% at 2.5 / 5 / 7 m = 76% / 59% of human success (85.0% / 68.3%) |
| Temporal history ablation | Removing it costs 35.6pp centered, 39.8pp at 0.6 m offset — the single biggest factor |
| Ego-motion ablation | Removing it costs 10.4pp / 22.6pp; latent regression beats full-state regression by ~19pp |
| Action-matching init | +5.2pp over position/velocity weight mapping |
| Latency randomization | +5–11pp at every tested delay; holds 83.0% at +40 ms, 72.7% at +60 ms |
| Color augmentation | Blue/black boxes 60.2%/61.2% → 88.0%/88.8% |
| Payloads / sizes (visual) | 0.5–2 kg: 77.7–91.2%; small 25.5% / large 39.7% (often intercepted but not retained — size-blind controller) |
| vs detection + Kalman | Visual latent wins all 13 in-range conditions (+5.0–15.6pp) over same-detector state pipeline |
| Downstream demo | 65% catch → 35% carry → 30% handover → 25% return (20 trials) |

Main limitation: long-range grounding—5 m throws are faster than any training throw and outside Stage 3 data (visual 43.0% vs privileged 79.7%); adding far throws to Stage 3 raises sim 5 m success to 75.8% (hardware not yet tested).

## Relevance to Software Engineers

- **Decompose perception from control**: training a compact latent interface with privileged state first, then regressing it from sensors with the controller frozen, cut rendering cost ~19×—a reusable recipe for vision-based robot policies
- **History and ego-motion are load-bearing**: single-frame perception lost ~36–40pp; if your onboard camera moves with the robot, feed ego-motion explicitly or pay ~10–23pp
- **Randomize what hardware will do to you**: latency randomization during training bought 5–11pp at deployment-like delays; measure your real pipeline latency (here 69 ms median) and train inside it
- **Distinguish interception from retention**: the controller reached off-size boxes (58–86% interception) but couldn't hold them—metric design that separates perception, interception, and stabilization failures diagnoses faster
- **Cheap robustness via augmentation**: color augmentation alone fixed the worst appearance cases (60% → 88%) with the decoder untouched
