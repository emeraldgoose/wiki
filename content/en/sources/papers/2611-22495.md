# Motion-Aware Reward Modeling for Instruction-Guided Video Editing

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.22495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22495) · **Published**: 2026-11-17 (v1)
**Authors**: Li Li, Xiangbo Gao, Bo Ni, Samyadeep Basu, Viet Dac Lai, Jing Shi, Seunghyun Yoon, Gang Wu, Yu Wang, Thien Huu Nguyen, Tyler Derr, Franck Dernoncourt, Zhengzhong Tu, Yue Zhao

## Overview

Instruction-guided video editing must apply the requested change while preserving the source content — changing a person's clothing should not make them stop walking. Reward models used to optimize editing systems therefore need to assess both edit quality and motion preservation, but temporal consistency alone is not enough: adjacent frames can become more similar precisely because motion was removed. The authors study this failure, which they term *motion collapse*, using a controlled motion ladder built from real edits by repeating frames over progressively longer intervals, ending in a fully frozen clip. On edits from VE-Bench, the composite reward they evaluate ranks a motion-suppressed candidate above the original edit in 97% of cases, and removing its temporal-consistency component substantially reduces that preference. Learned video rewards and a VLM judge also struggle with mild suppression, while human raters overwhelmingly prefer the original edits. The mismatch also shows up under optimization: both latent optimization and reward-guided fine-tuning (adapted from DRaFT) can raise reward while reducing motion, even as measured prompt adherence improves.

## Key points

- Diagnosis: a linear probe over the base preference model's representations separates original edits from fully frozen clips but performs near chance on mildly suppressed variants — the backbone is blind to subtle motion loss, motivating an explicit inter-frame dynamics input.
- Method: a motion-aware reward model that combines an RGB frame-difference stream (tanh-normalized differences of the edited video) with source and edited-video features, fused at block 4 of a Qwen3-VL-32B backbone and adapted with LoRA.
- Training: pairwise preference training contrasting original edits against both motion-suppressed and fabricated-motion negatives (teaching rejection of motion degradation without simply rewarding more movement), plus a distillation anchor that retains the base model's quality preferences.
- Results: on the VE-Bench motion ladder the model selects the original edit in every evaluated case (rate of selecting motion-suppressed candidates drops from 97% to 0%) and reaches a Spearman correlation of 0.57 with human quality ratings.
- Robustness and use: it detects suppression patterns absent from training, and when used for reward-guided fine-tuning on two editing pipelines it keeps motion close to its initial level.
- Contributions: (1) diagnosing motion collapse via controlled temporal perturbations, human preference comparisons, and reward-optimization experiments; (2) the motion-aware reward model with explicit dynamics, hard negatives, and distillation; (3) evaluation of robustness to unseen suppression, alignment with human judgments, and motion preservation under reward-guided fine-tuning.

## Details

The motion ladder perturbs each generated edit by frame repetition up to a fully frozen clip. Evaluation compares reward rankings against human ratings on unmodified outputs and under optimization. Related evaluation work distinguishes motion smoothness from dynamic degree (VBench), identifies inflated quality scores for low-dynamic videos (DEVIL), and provides source-relative trajectory fidelity (IVEBench); reward-side related methods include EditReward/EditScore for image edits, VideoScore/VideoScore2/VideoReward/VisionReward for video quality, VIVA's Edit-GRPO (CLIP + PickScore), DenseDPO's motion-aligned pairs, and LongCat-Video's multi-reward counteraction of reward-induced static outputs.
