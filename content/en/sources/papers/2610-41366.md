# What We Keep of What We Forget: Replacement Operators and Routing Semantics in Training-Free MoE Expert Pruning

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.41366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41366) · **Published**: 2026-10-30
**Authors**: Ezza Ali, Sehrish Bibi, Momina Moetesum, Faisal Shafait

## Overview

Training-free expert pruning makes two intertwined decisions: which experts to remove, and what the layer computes when its router still selects a removed expert. Published methods usually change both at once. This paper holds the removal mask fixed and compares replacement operators from three families across MoE and router configurations using paired, multiplicity-corrected tests.

A key implicit choice is the survivors' gate scale. On routers trained without top-k renormalization, renormalizing survivors' gates can cost 0.8-8.5 points and flip whether rerouting beats MoNE's mean-output replacement. On routers trained with renormalization, the trained scale is better at 50% pruning. The paper also introduces the tilted novice, a closed-form low-rank map of the pruned expert's input whose rank-0 case recovers MoNE's constant; at 50% pruning it adds only 1.2-1.8% parameters and improves accuracy on several models, with stability across calibration draws and quantization.

No single operator dominates. Trained-scale rerouting is best on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, and a partial copy of each pruned expert is statistically comparable to the tilted novice on two models. The paper further shows evaluation choices alter conclusions and proposes a five-item reporting checklist.

## Key points

- Expert pruning couples the choice of which experts to remove with the replacement behavior for removed experts.
- Under fixed masks, renormalizing survivors' gates can significantly hurt when routers were not trained with top-k renormalization.
- The tilted novice adds a low-rank map of the pruned expert and beats MoNE's constant in several settings.
- The tilted novice is stable across calibration draws and largely resilient to low-bit quantization.
- No operator dominates across all tested models and routers.
- Mask calibration domain matters more than resampling, and benchmark choice affects conclusions.
- A five-item reporting checklist is proposed.

## Details

Reported experiments span OLMoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Moonlight, and Granite, comparing operators with matched statistical tests. The conclusions depend on router training, pruning ratio, and evaluation suite, suggesting pruning studies should report replacement operator, gate scaling, mask calibration, and benchmark coverage consistently.
