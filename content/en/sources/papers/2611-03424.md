# IdeaTaste: Do LLMs Share the Research Taste of Human Researchers?

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03424) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Shanshan Zhong, Chen Xu, Jingjie Ning, Zhengzhong Liu, Aviral Kumar, Chenyan Xiong

## Overview

Research taste is the judgment of what to read, which problems to pursue, and which methods to try, and it increasingly shapes AI scientist work. IdeaTaste is a benchmark that scores an LLM by how closely its research proposals from a literature snapshot frozen at 2025 match work that the same communities published in 2026. Experiments span six language models and four research areas from established to emerging fields, covering more than 90,000 proposals. The central finding is that LLM agents fall short of human-like research taste.

## Key points

- Research taste covers problem and method selection.
- It is less understood than execution ability.
- IdeaTaste evaluates proposals against future publications.
- The benchmark freezes literature at 2025.
- It tests six models across four research areas.
- It covers more than 90,000 proposals.

## Details

The benchmark measures taste by comparing generated research directions to later human publications in the same area. This framing makes research judgment concrete rather than relying on subjective ratings. It includes established and emerging fields to test whether models can identify promising directions outside mainstream topics. The large-scale proposal count supports robust comparison across models. Overall, the work identifies gaps between LLM research proposals and human community priorities.
