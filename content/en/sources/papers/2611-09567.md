# TurtLES: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Turbulent 3D Neural PDE Surrogates

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.09567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09567) · **Published**: 2026-11-06
**Authors**: Jarno Platenburg, Armand Kassaï Koupaï, Paola Cinnella, Patrick Gallinari

## Overview

Machine-learning surrogates increasingly replace expensive computational fluid dynamics solvers, but existing benchmarks rarely combine what realistic turbulent flow demands: time-resolved three-dimensional dynamics, dense irregular discretizations, long rollout horizons, and an active energy cascade driven by vortex stretching. Regular-grid datasets stay idealized or coarse; industrial-scale irregular-mesh datasets tend to be steady-state or time-averaged. TurtLES fills this gap with a 14 TB dataset of 3D turbulent cylinder wakes in the shear-layer transition regime, computed with high-fidelity implicit large-eddy simulations (iLES).

The dataset contains 400 trajectories of 400 time steps each with 3–11 million irregular points per frame, spanning systematic variations in cross-sectional geometry, Reynolds number, and angle of attack. Trajectories come from an automated pipeline built on the Flux Reconstruction scheme in the open-source GPU solver PyFR: each 2D Gmsh mesh is extruded over 30 spanwise layers, elements hold 75–125 solution points, and each case keeps 2.9M–10.5M unique points within the stored wake region, with pressure, density, and three velocity components stored per node alongside per-case wake-probe power spectra.

The paper then benchmarks four neural operators — UPT, GINO, Transolver++, and CALM-PDE (with ECHO scaling) — on next-frame prediction and 400-step autoregressive rollouts under a shared budget of 100,000 steps, roughly 20 hours on one H100 GPU. The results expose complementary failure modes: short-horizon error, long-horizon instability, loss of high-frequency turbulent energy, and insufficient computational acceleration at this mesh scale.

## Key points

- TurtLES provides 400 iLES trajectories of 400 time steps each with 3–11 million points per frame, totaling 14 TB of 3D turbulent cylinder-wake data.
- The parameter space covers super-elliptical geometries with aspect ratios from 1/3 to 3 (exponent giving diamond, elliptical, rounded-rectangle, and rectangular shapes), Reynolds numbers 1000–3900, and angles of attack 0–45 degrees, sampled by a 400-point Sobol sequence and split 320/40/40 into train/validation/test.
- On next-frame prediction, CALM-PDE is the most accurate baseline with relative L2 error 6.80×10^-2 and VRMSE 7.26×10^-2; Transolver++ and GINO score VRMSE 1.06 and 1.21 — no better than predicting the per-frame spatial mean.
- Long-horizon stability separates the field: over the final window (steps 301–399) CALM-PDE's VRMSE grows only from 0.461 to 0.789, UPT drifts to 5.12, and Transolver++ reaches 31.24 (GINO 33.12).
- Spectral diagnostics show pointwise error is not enough: CALM-PDE has near-zero log-power discrepancy at low and intermediate wavenumbers, UPT loses small-scale energy at high wavenumbers, and Transolver++ overpredicts spectral energy, indicating spurious small-scale content.
- Full-mesh inference remains expensive: one trajectory costs 835 GPU-seconds for UPT and 4097 for GINO versus 13160 for the four-GPU solver run, so net speedups range from roughly 3× for GINO to 16× for UPT — far below the orders of magnitude often reported on smaller problems.
- To lower the entry barrier, mini-TurtLES defines standardized subsets reduced in tiers down to 0.8 TB plus a 5 GB debug subset.

## Details

### Dataset construction and validation

Each case places a super-elliptical cylinder at an angle of attack in a 3D domain with an inlet, three outlets, and periodic side-walls, meshed automatically with Gmsh. Collection starts only after the initial transient washes out, measured by a convergence statistic comparing the last three temporal quarters of wake-probe signals at 4D, 6.5D, and 9.5D behind the object; then 400 snapshots are stored at fixed intervals chosen to preserve the inertial range. The circular case's 400-step window captures four vortex-shedding cycles. The solver is validated against four reference cases — a circular cylinder at Reynolds numbers 1000 and 3900, a square cylinder at 2000, and an elongated rectangle at 3000 — agreeing well with published DNS and experimental data.

### Benchmark protocol

Models train on all 320 trajectories to predict the temporal residual between consecutive frames, conditioned on the Reynolds number, with MSE loss on subsampled points; optimization uses AdamW, learning rate 10^-4, 5,000 warmup steps, then cosine decay. Inference rolls out 400 steps from a single initial frame, predicting all nodes in chunks. Evaluation reports relative L2 error, variance-scaled RMSE (VRMSE), spanwise spectral errors over low, mid, and high wavenumber bands, and GPU-seconds per trajectory; a persistence baseline shows that matching the time-averaged spectrum alone is insufficient, so spectral agreement must be read together with field-level and rollout errors.

### Results and limitations

CALM-PDE is the strongest surrogate, stable across the whole rollout and most spectrally faithful; UPT is fastest but drifts; Transolver++ and GINO accumulate dominating errors. The authors are explicit about scope: one physical regime (shear-layer-transition cylinder wakes), an iLES reference rather than DNS or experiment, temporal subsampling that discards some high-frequency content, and short windows that prevent converged statistics such as Reynolds stresses. The four baselines are a starting point, and building the fuller account is exactly the role the benchmark is meant to serve.
