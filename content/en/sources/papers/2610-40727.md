# VoxLock: Target-Speaker Extraction for Voice Agents Without an Enrollment Step

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40727) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Arbaz Siddiqui

## Overview

A voice agent's speech recognizer may transcribe everyone near the microphone, including bystanders and background speakers. VoxLock targets the caller in such settings without requiring a separate enrollment recording. Instead, it uses a voice sample cut from the call itself.

Target-speaker extraction normally requires an enrollment recording and reports research-style metrics, both of which limit direct use in voice agents. VoxLock fine-tunes a public causal target-speaker extraction model on one GPU and evaluates recognizer output against commercial and open models on multiple benchmark windows.

## Key points

- Addresses caller isolation for voice agents with no enrollment step.
- Uses a voice sample extracted from the call itself.
- Fine-tunes a public causal target-speaker extraction model with limited compute.
- Evaluates recognizer transcription, not only extraction metrics.
- Tests on a commercial benchmark and AMI windows, including a frozen pre-scoring set.
- Aims to make target-speaker extraction practical for voice agents.

## Details

Standard noise suppression does not solve the problem because it preserves speech from any speaker. Target-speaker extraction is related but usually depends on enrollment audio and offline-style evaluation. VoxLock closes that gap by adapting an existing public model to causal, call-derived enrollment.

The work is framed as a single-GPU engineering result and focuses on whether transcription quality improves when only the target speaker is passed to the recognizer.
