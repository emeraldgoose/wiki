# Learning from Fleet Experience: Collective Experience Learning across 96 Robots

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25736) · Yu et al., Oct 19 2026 · arXiv:2610.25736
**Date**: 2026-10-19

**Lead**: This paper proposes Collective Experience Learning (CEL), a paradigm where robots pool execution experience to improve a shared policy without collecting additional expert demonstrations. The FERT algorithm uses failure-guided retrieval of successful trajectories for supervised updates, and FATS provides fault-tolerant asynchronous coordination. Across five real-world manipulation tasks with up to 96 robots, policies reach 93.3–99.2% success rates; on multi-stage phone packaging, 96 robots raise success from 79.2% to 98.3%. Fault-related downtime is only 1.3% of total robot time.

## Overview

Robot foundation models provide initial capabilities but do not guarantee reliable execution, especially for multi-stage tasks with deformable objects or precise alignment. Real-world execution generates valuable experience, yet traditional improvement requires costly expert demonstrations or corrective actions. CEL closes the loop by using the fleet's own autonomous executions as supervision.

## Method: CEL, FERT, FATS

FERT has four steps: asynchronous experience collection (binary outcome labels only), experience encoding (visual + proprioceptive embeddings), failure-guided retrieval using slope-constrained DTW to match failure contexts to successful segments, and prioritized fine-tuning that increases sampling priority for retrieved intervals. FATS coordinates execution, cloud inference and optimization asynchronously with local fault recovery via HotSwarm and pooled inference via SwarmLink.

## Key results

- Phone packaging: 79.2% → 98.3% with 96 robots (fixed collection time per robot).
- Overall success rates: 93.3% to 99.2% across evaluated tasks at largest fleet size.
- Average success improves with larger fleets under fixed per-robot collection time.
- 30 fault incidents on 22 robots; only 1.3% total robot time lost to faults.
- No new expert demonstrations required after initial policy initialization.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Self-supervised fleet improvement is transferable to distributed inference systems: use failure signals to retrieve relevant success patterns for targeted updates.
- Asynchronous training with fault tolerance (FATS) applies to any distributed ML pipeline with heterogeneous worker reliability.
- The 96-robot scale and fixed-time scaling result demonstrates that data volume, not expert time, can drive improvement.
