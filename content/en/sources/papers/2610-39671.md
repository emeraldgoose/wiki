# Incentivizing Self-Learning in Large Language Models via Reinforcement Learning

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.39671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39671) · **Published**: 2026-10-29

## Overview

LLMs combine parametric knowledge and retrieval-augmented generation to solve knowledge-intensive tasks, but solving problems in unfamiliar domains requires tightly coupled knowledge acquisition and reasoning — more than factual retrieval and aggregation, and more than learning from a preassembled context. The paper studies self-learning: autonomously retrieving, studying, and applying knowledge from long-form reference materials while a task unfolds, where an attempted derivation may reveal a missing prerequisite and a newly encountered principle may reshape the strategy. The model itself must decide what to study from static reference materials and how to weave that knowledge into a valid derivation.

The probe is a textbook-based mathematical problem-solving task. An end-to-end pipeline parses advanced mathematics textbooks into chapter-indexed text, separates exercises from exposition, and strips exercise solutions from the model-visible material, yielding 5,183 proof problems from 158 textbooks with reference solutions withheld. The model must navigate the exposition, learn the relevant principles, and construct proofs for exercises. A new BookNavigator interface supports this human-inspired study process — inspecting the table of contents, locating relevant sections, and reading their contents as reasoning unfolds — and an LLM judge scores the final proof against the withheld solution.

Training uses GRPO augmented with two fine-grained rewards that target the complementary failure modes of agentic study. An annealed exploration bonus, added to correct trajectories with at least one successful tool call and decayed on a cosine schedule with a positive floor, sustains textbook access as problem-solving ability improves. Round-level tool credit uses a frozen copy of the initial policy as a judge that labels each non-terminal round useful, neutral, or harmful — without seeing the reference solution — and redistributes the trajectory advantage across rounds while preserving the token-weighted mean.

The results reverse the protocol's starting position. Before RL, Self-Learning is the weakest of five protocols (51.5% overall, below No-Book at 55.7% and every fixed-retrieval baseline); after RL it is the strongest at 79.5% (+28.0 points), beating RL-trained No-Book by 11.0 points and the best baseline, Top-k Sections, by 6.4, while reaching 85.2% on unseen textbooks and 86.7% on unseen subjects. The gains are not source memorization — removing the provenance source section and its neighbors leaves 74.6% — and they transfer to BrowseComp-Plus (+1.7 points, the only protocol above base) and CL-bench (+2.3) under those benchmarks' original inference setups.

## Key points

- The central reversal: before training, the self-study protocol scores 51.5% overall versus 55.7% for No-Book and 62.2% for Top-k Sections; after RL it leads every protocol with 79.5% and the largest gain (+28.0 points).
- Fixed-retrieval baselines after RL: No-Book 68.5, Top-k Sections 73.1, Long-Context 72.2, Oracle Section 71.0 — all below Self-Learning's 79.5%.
- Generalization holds out of domain: 85.2% on unseen textbooks (+4.2 over the strongest baseline) and 86.7% on unseen subjects (+5.4).
- Gains are not memorization: RL also strengthens unaided reasoning (No-Book 55.7% to 71.7%), removing the source section plus four neighbors on each side costs only 4.9 points (79.5% to 74.6%), a topic-matched substitute textbook gives 75.2%, and a count-matched non-source removal control leaves 79.8%.
- Exploration bonus design: added only to correct trajectories with at least one successful tool call, scaled by a cosine-annealed coefficient with a positive floor, so early exploration is strongly encouraged while a small incentive for textbook access persists late in training.
- Tool credit design: a frozen copy of the initial policy judges each non-terminal round as useful (relevant new information, clarifying an unresolved condition, a meaningful navigation lead), neutral, or harmful (redundant or irrelevant retrieval, unjustified repetition of failed requests, protocol violations); round advantages are redistributed as A + alpha|A|(c − c-bar) over unmasked tokens.
- Ablation shows the two rewards are complementary: correctness-only RL collapses exploration (tool-using trajectories fall from 36.9% to 6.7%, calls from 1.5 to 0.3 per trajectory) while still reaching 74.9%; tool credit adds +3.3 in-domain but moves OOD splits little; the exploration bonus restores tool use (79.9% of trajectories) and adds +2.0/+2.9 on the OOD splits; combined, every split peaks (65.5 ID-Book, 85.2 OOD-Book, 86.7 OOD-Subject, 79.5 overall).
- Cross-task transfer is selective and real: BrowseComp-Plus 50.2 to 51.9 (+1.7, with every other RL protocol landing below base, No-Book at −1.6) and CL-bench 14.8 to 17.1 (+2.3), driven by Domain Knowledge Reasoning (+4.5) and Empirical Discovery and Simulation (+5.0) while Procedural Task Execution slips 0.6.
- Transfer was measured under each benchmark's original setup — BM25 retrieval for BrowseComp-Plus, context-only generation for CL-bench — so the gains reflect the learned strategy rather than the BookNavigator interface.

## Details

- Dataset construction: 5,183 proof problems from 158 advanced mathematics textbooks, chapter-indexed; exercises are separated from exposition and their solutions removed from everything the model can see; the judge evaluates the final proof against the withheld reference solution.
- BookNavigator supports three study actions within a round: inspecting the table of contents, locating relevant sections, and reading section contents while reasoning proceeds; the policy alternates textbook study and problem solving.
- The tool judge withholds the reference solution and the final correctness verdict, and scores the terminal round as neutral by construction; because the judge is a frozen copy of the initial policy, the +3.3 in-domain gain from tool credit requires no stronger model.
- Evaluation splits: ID-Book (held-out problems from training textbooks), OOD-Book (unseen textbooks), OOD-Subject (unseen subjects); Self-Learning's per-split gains are +31.5, +20.0, and +29.9 points.
- Base model: Intern-S2-Preview-35B; policy optimization augments GRPO with the annealed bonus and round-level credit, with the redistribution strength alpha controlling how strongly round quality shifts token-level advantages.
- The combined reward achieves stronger generalization without maximizing tool-call frequency — the exploration-bonus variant averages 5.9 calls per trajectory versus 0.3 for correctness-only RL — supporting complementary roles for exploration incentives and process-level credit.
