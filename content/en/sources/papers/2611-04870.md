# PrimeSim: A Generalist World Model Simulator from Large-Scale Cross-Embodiment Demonstrations

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04870) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Travis Davies, Wentao Ma, Lihao Guo, Tengfei Ma, Xiaowen Chang, Jiafeng Xu, Shilong Wang, Yiqi Deng, Shaowen He, Xiaoqi Li, Qian Zhu, Ran Cheng, Hao Dong

## Overview

A robot world model that serves as a simulator must do more than generate plausible videos: it must faithfully follow commanded actions, maintain consistency across camera views, and sustain coherent long-horizon rollouts. PrimeSim is an action-conditioned multiview world model simulator addressing these challenges through progressive midtraining and posttraining. Starting from a pretrained video generator, it midtrains on 30,110 hours of robot demonstrations spanning 22 platforms. To improve action following, it introduces a unified cross-embodiment action representation and frame-aligned dual-path conditioning. To maintain multiview consistency, it uses view-aware interleaved attention with explicit camera identities. A fast-video, slow-memory posttraining framework transforms the bidirectional video generator into an efficient autoregressive simulator.

## Key points

- PrimeSim is action-conditioned and multiview rather than video-only.
- Midtraining uses 30,110 hours across 22 robot platforms.
- The unified action representation supports cross-embodiment training.
- Dual-path conditioning strengthens action-frame correspondence.
- View-aware interleaved attention supports camera consistency.
- It runs at 10.1 FPS and supports long-horizon rollouts.

## Details

PrimeSim remains stable over long-horizon rollouts on rigid and deformable manipulation tasks and transfers to held-out platforms within 100 to 200 gradient steps where the pretrained backbone does not. Against five open-source action-conditioned world models it leads four of five WorldSimProbe suites and posts the best overall score, while fine-grained local calibration still trails. Rollouts are faithful enough to support GRPO-based policy optimization. These results depend on the training platforms, view configurations, and low-level control interfaces encountered downstream.
