# Signed but Steerable: Measuring and Closing the Text-Vector Integrity Gap in Offline RAG

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.06498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06498) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Georgios Feretzakis, Alexandros Papaspyridis

## Overview

Offline retrieval-augmented generation on edge devices is often updated with signed knowledge packs that carry text and precomputed embedding vectors. A signature proves publisher identity, but not that the vectors encode the text. This work studies that text-vector integrity gap.

A verifier that recomputes vectors and accepts them within cosine tolerance concedes a predictable share of queries to an attacker controlling the vectors. The hijack rate follows the distribution of angular retrieval margins, called the margin law. Margins are small across five corpora and two embedding models, leaving some queries exposed even under tight thresholds. On a Raspberry Pi 5 with a local language model, such an attack lowers correct answers from 0.75 to 0.39. Compressed vector audit also fares poorly: tampered vectors passing an audit can rank the attacker's document first for a large fraction of targets.

The paper proposes verifying quantization codes with ambiguity decided by the verifier, plus a calibrated guard band and canonicalization. This accepts honest vectors and removes adversarial freedom. It also documents non-bit-reproducible floating-point and dynamic INT8 inference pipelines and reports negative results on abstention, context pruning, and semantic screening.

## Key points

- Defines the text-vector integrity gap in signed offline RAG packs.
- Shows the hijack rate equals the distribution of angular retrieval margins.
- Finds small margins expose non-negligible query fractions.
- Demonstrates severe answer degradation on edge hardware.
- Shows compressed-vector audits can be bypassed by tampered vectors.
- Proposes code verification with guard band and canonicalization as a closure.

## Details

The 20-page paper reports seven tables and two figures, and releases code and data. It covers five corpora, two embedding models, and a FAISS product-quantization index over 57,638 documents.
