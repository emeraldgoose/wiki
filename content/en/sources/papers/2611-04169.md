# Length Is Cheap, Competition Is Not: Attention Predicts Context Rot in Retrieval

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04169) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Yinuo Liu

## Overview

Why do language models fail on long inputs? The common answer is length. This work argues that, for retrieval, length is cheap and competition is not. Across five open models from 0.5B to 3B parameters and four families, neutral filler costs little: accuracy stays at 0.97 to 1.00 up to 32K tokens on one model. Statements in the evidence format at the same positions drop accuracy to 0.16 to 0.33. A single rival statement that gives the queried key a contradictory value multiplies the loss substantially, and more rivals add further loss. The key internal predictor is attention mass remaining on the evidence; across 126,473 trials it explains up to 58 percent of loss variance while log length explains at most 8 percent.

## Key points

- Neutral filler in retrieval causes little accuracy loss even at long lengths.
- Evidence-shaped distractors reduce accuracy far more than neutral filler.
- A single rival value statement multiplies loss by 9 to 110.
- Additional rival statements add further loss.
- Remaining attention mass on the evidence strongly tracks failure.
- The masked-filler protocol leaves strong length effects fail in retrieval controls.

## Details

The paper distinguishes retrieval context rot from masked-filler phenomena, and tests 126,473 trials. Masked filler can reproduce length effects on MMLU, yet in retrieval visible filler causes no accuracy loss while masking costs 45 points at 32K tokens. The released probe and trial-level measurements support diagnosing attention competition, but thresholds depend on model family, size, retrieval task, and display conditions. The conclusion is that in retrieval, context rot is primarily a competition for attention.
