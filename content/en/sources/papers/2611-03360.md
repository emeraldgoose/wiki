# Every Pixel Is an Option: A System One Model for Medical Ultrasound Biometry

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03360) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Moein Heidari, Junbo Rao, Yudan Chen, Yasamin Medghalchi, River Jiang, Wenjin Chen, David J. Foran, Jasmine Grewal, Ilker Hacihaliloglu

## Overview

Conventional ultrasound landmarks are often measurements constrained by manual caliper placement, which introduces interoperator variability. Kev-Bio proposes a vision-language model guided by clinical measurement questions for ultrasound landmark detection. Rather than directly predicting a coordinate, it uses decision representations of the language model to generate queries for each caliper endpoint. Those queries are matched against shared dense ultrasound features, producing spatial probability maps from which landmark coordinates and clinical measurements are derived. The model is trained end-to-end using both localization and clinical measurement losses.

## Key points

- The model addresses interoperator variability in ultrasound biometry.
- Clinical measurement questions guide landmark detection instead of separate heads.
- It is inspired by System One decision models rather than long reasoning chains.
- Query representations match against dense features to form spatial probability maps.
- It explicitly models localization ambiguity rather than predicting coordinates directly.
- End-to-end training uses localization and clinical measurement losses.

## Details

On the nine-view FU/GU Biometry 2026 dataset, Kev-Bio achieves a mean radial error of 22.50 pixels and a clinical measurement mean absolute error of 15.38, outperforming compared baselines in overall performance. These results demonstrate effectiveness across different anatomical views. Clinical quality depends on the specific acquisition protocol, landmark definition, and anatomical set, so deployment should validate performance on local ultrasound devices and populations.
