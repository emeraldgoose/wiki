# JVT: Just Video Transformer Makes Pixel-Space Video Diffusion Simple

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.18549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18549) · **Published**: 2026-11-13
**Authors**: Jianjun Zhou, Yihan Hu, Yichong Wang, Xuanang Chen, Yian Wang, Zihao Zhao, Huiwen Zhang, Fengyun Rao, Yang Yang, Yao Feng, Yanfei Shen, Jiang Bian

## Overview

Direct diffusion modeling of high-dimensional raw video is hard, and existing pixel-space methods often lean on specialized architectures. JVT (Just Video Transformer) is a deliberately simple, large-patch DiT-style Transformer operating directly on pixels — no pretrained autoencoder, no auxiliary detailers, no cascaded stages, no U-Net skip connections. The question it answers is whether the simple transformer recipe that won image generation also wins video generation, without the simplifications that usually follow.

The authors systematically study three design levers: video patch embeddings (patch size trade-offs), register tokens (extra learnable tokens to stabilize global reasoning), and adaptive layer normalization (conditional modulation). Their findings balance generation quality and computational efficiency, yielding a straightforward recipe rather than a bag of tricks.

The resulting JVT design is competitive with latent diffusion baselines in both class-conditioned and unconditional settings. Just as importantly, the pixel-space formulation naturally supports joint generation of pixel-aligned modalities. As a demonstration, the authors train a camera-conditioned RGB-disparity generator: alongside the RGB frame it predicts a disparity map aligned to the same pixel grid, which improves camera following relative to RGB-only baselines and yields explicit scene geometry at inference with no extra reconstruction model.

## Key points

- Simplest recipe that works: a large-patch DiT-style Transformer operating in pixel space, no autoencoder, no auxiliary detailers, no cascaded stages, no U-Net skips.
- Design study: video patch embeddings, register tokens, and adaptive layer normalization are examined systematically to balance quality and efficiency.
- Competitive with latent diffusion: JVT matches latent-diffusion baselines in both class-conditioned and unconditional video generation.
- Joint-modal generation falls out: the pixel-space formulation naturally supports pixel-aligned outputs; the paper demonstrates camera-conditioned RGB-disparity generation.
- Camera following + geometry: adding the disparity head improves camera adherence vs RGB-only baselines and produces explicit scene geometry without a separate reconstruction model.
- Case for simple DiTs: results show plain transformers can handle pixel-space video diffusion at scale, offering practical guidance for future research.

## Details

The design study is the informative part of the paper. Patch embeddings are the obvious lever: larger patches cut token count and attention cost but lose fine detail, while small patches drown the model in tokens. The authors find a large-patch operating point that works because video has strong spatiotemporal redundancy. Register tokens — a handful of learnable embeddings appended to the sequence — are borrowed from vision transformers' trick of absorbing global noise into dedicated slots, improving stability without affecting content tokens. Adaptive layer normalization (adaLN) with class/camera conditioning modulates the network per token group, keeping the architecture close to vanilla DiT while adding the needed conditioning.

The unconditional and class-conditioned runs mirror standard DiT training on open video datasets. JVT ships no autoencoder; the diffusion process happens directly on RGB (and in the joint task, disparity). This is the main departure from mainstream video diffusion, which nearly always works in a latent autoencoder space to keep tokens manageable. The claim is that with the right patch size and normalization, latent becomes unnecessary.

The joint RGB-disparity result is the most practical consequence. Because disparity is aligned to the RGB pixel grid, a single model can generate both in one forward pass and one loss. The camera conditioning is a per-frame camera pose embedding. Compared with an RGB-only baseline given the same camera trajectory, JVT's camera-conditioned variant follows the trajectory more tightly, and the disparity output gives downstream consumers an explicit geometry signal — useful for editing, view synthesis, and reconstruction without a separate depth network.

Limitations are honest: pixel-space training is still compute-hungry in absolute terms, results are strongest at moderate resolutions, and the recipe is not yet shown at frontier scale. But as a existence proof that simple transformers can do pixel-space video diffusion competitively, and as a recipe for joint pixel-aligned modality generation, JVT makes a clear case.
