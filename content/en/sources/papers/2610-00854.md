# Are Frontier VLM Agents Ready to Be Robot Generalists? An Empirical Study with the Embodied Agent Arena

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.00854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00854) · **Published**: 2026-10-23
**Authors**: Haojian Huang, Pukun Zhao, Zexi Li, Yehang Zhang, Yangkai Wei, Wenqian Li, Han Yang, Kaiwen Zhou, Ying-Cong Chen, Yinchuan Li

## Overview

A robot generalist must interpret a scene, act, and satisfy every condition of a task. This study asks how far frontier vision-language models meet those demands with the Embodied Agent Arena. The benchmark evaluates ten VLM agents across Geometry, Spatial Reasoning, Affordance, Task Planning, and Manipulation. Its 1,000 cases combine 32 established sources with GeoProbe, a 168-case benchmark for camera, motion, depth, and scale estimation. A minimal harness leaves task decisions to the model through source-native interfaces. Closed-weight models lead all five domains, while open-weight VLMs show uneven, task-dependent embodied competence.

## Key points

- The benchmark evaluates ten VLM agents on five embodied capability domains.
- It contains 1,000 cases drawn from 32 sources plus the GeoProbe suite.
- Geometry, spatial reasoning, affordance, task planning, and manipulation are tested separately.
- A minimal harness reduces benchmark-specific scaffolding.
- Closed-weight models lead all evaluated domains.
- Open-weight models show broader but inconsistent embodied capability.

## Details

The study contributes benchmark construction, evaluation protocols, and task requirement analyses across appendix materials. Results depend on the minimal harness and selected interfaces, so open-weight models may improve under different prompting or tooling. Fine-grained per-domain profiles matter: high performance in one domain does not imply robust generalization. The findings guide model selection for embodied deployments and identify weak spots needing targeted data or methods.
