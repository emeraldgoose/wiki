# GPBam: A Retrieval-Aware German Public Law Exam Benchmark

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.18981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18981) · **Published**: 2026-11-19
**Authors**: Abdullah Al Zubaer, Lorenz Wendlinger, Simon Alexander Nonn, Michael Granitzer, Jelena Mitrović

## Overview

Legal reasoning in civil-law systems is not rule-lookup. The German First State Examination asks candidates to produce a structured opinion (Gutachten): identify the issues and governing norms, state each norm's requirements, apply them to the facts, and argue to a conclusion, repeating that structure across sub-issues. A correct final verdict can therefore hide broken intermediate analysis, which is why label-based formats such as multiple choice or short answer do not measure what the exam actually tests. GPBam (German Public Law Bar-style Exam) is built around exactly that document: 81 public-law examination cases with reference solutions, drawn from a state-examination preparation course, paired with a recitation task over 200 statutory provisions.

Because the cases come from private exam preparation material and are broad but rarely repeating, the authors keep them non-public to limit contamination, and report no evidence of exact-solution memorisation in the models they evaluate. Each reference solution cites the norms it relies on, so those citations form a retrieval gold set over the very instances the essays are graded on. That lets GPBam score retrieval and long-form generation jointly, making it a benchmark for legal retrieval as well as legal writing, and lets the authors ask how much retrieval quality a legal opinion actually needs. The work is an extended version of a paper accepted at JURIX 2026 (39th International Conference on Legal Knowledge and Information Systems).

## Key points

- 81 public-law state-exam-style cases with full reference solutions, plus a recitation task over 200 statutory provisions.
- Reference solutions average roughly 7,080 words against ~247-word mean reference answers in comparable open-ended benchmarks (the paper compares against LEXam).
- Reference norms double as a retrieval gold set, enabling joint retrieval + long-form generation scoring on the same instances.
- Across 32 open-weight and proprietary models evaluated without retrieval, essay scores span 4.81 to 76.42 out of 100.
- Verbatim statute recall (measured by the recitation task) correlates with essay quality at ρ = 0.92 over 32 models, strongly tracking prior knowledge.

## Details

Method. GPBam grades one complete Gutachten per case — a table of contents followed by a full assessment answering every question raised in the fact pattern, with no study-imposed output-token cap — rather than isolated sub-problems. Reference norms cited in the solutions define the retrieval gold set for each case. The evaluation separates two questions: how well models write the opinion from parametric knowledge alone, and how much retrieval changes that. The retrieval side uses a ladder of progressively richer retrieval configurations (query rewriting/diversification, dense+lexical fusion, re-ranking) over a German statutory corpus, and a statute-recitation task measures verbatim recall of 200 provisions.

Results. Without retrieval, 32 models score between 4.81 and 76.42 out of 100 — a wide capability spread, with essay quality strongly predicted by verbatim statute recall (ρ = 0.92). Supplying the law helps only modestly: across seven models and eight retrieval configurations, retrieval performance varies nearly threefold, yet buys at most about 4.1 essay points and shows nothing significant for three of the seven. An oracle retriever with 91% gold-norm recall improves scores by only 2–6 points. The picture that emerges is that retrieval quality is not the binding constraint on German public-law opinions — prior legal knowledge, reflected in statute recall, tracks performance far more strongly.
