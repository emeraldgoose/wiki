# A Broader Look into MLLM's Retrieval Heads

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.32411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32411) · **Published**: 2026-10-23
**Authors**: Jia Wang, Yuqing Li, Jiangnan Li, Zheng Lin, Mo Yu

## Overview

Locating relevant evidence in long inputs is a core capability of multimodal large language models. Prior studies attribute this capability to retrieval heads, a sparse set of attention heads that transfer information from context to output, but analyses have been confined to limited modalities and corresponding models. This paper asks whether the retrieval mechanism expands as models gain input modalities. Taking audio-visual input as a newly added modality, it constructs paired inputs where the same answer is either spoken or shown on screen. With evidence content fixed, retrieval heads remain sparse in the new modality and are largely shared across modalities. Across 18 language, vision-language, and omni-modal checkpoints, omni-modal models largely preserve the retrieval heads of their backbones. Ablating these heads eliminates nearly all audio-visual retrieval, showing that a tiny set of fewer than 2% of all heads is critical; ablating the shared heads also degrades document understanding, spatial perception, and visual mathematics. The paper then turns retrieval heads into an optimization target by training their attention with a ranking objective, raising Qwen3-VL-8B page Recall@1 on MMDocIR by 6.03 points and improving document retrieval across three model families. Functional attention heads can thus serve both as a lens on multimodal models and as a target for improving them.

## Key points

- Retrieval heads are sparse and largely shared across modalities.
- 18 checkpoints across language, vision-language, and omni-modal models were analyzed.
- Ablating retrieval heads eliminates nearly all audio-visual retrieval.
- Shared heads account for fewer than 2% of all heads but affect document, spatial, and math tasks.
- Ranking-objective training raised Qwen3-VL-8B Recall@1 by 6.03 points on MMDocIR.

## Details

By fixing evidence content while changing its presentation modality, the study isolates modality effects from content effects. The result is that adding a modality does not create a separate large head set; instead a small shared set supports multiple capabilities. Training those heads with a ranking objective converts an interpretability finding into a practical retrieval improvement across model families.
