# LoopFill: Pipelined Multi-Token Speculative Decoding for Looped Transformers

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.19649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19649) · **Published**: 2026-11-14
**Authors**: Siqi Zhu, Suozhi Huang, Yi Zhao, Jiaxuan You

## Overview

Looped Transformers improve parameter efficiency by repeatedly applying the same weight-shared block of layers, usually structured as a prelude, the recurrent block applied T times, and a coda. During autoregressive decoding, this recurrence is also their weakness: every token requires T sequential passes through the block, and at small batch sizes each pass is memory-bound, leaving most compute idle. Pipelined self-speculative methods such as LoopSpec and WaveFront Decoding exploit the depth dimension by reading a draft token from a shallow-loop hidden state and verifying it at full depth, but each new token can only enter the pipeline once the current one reaches the drafting depth, bounding the speedup by roughly T/d and forcing restarts on rejection.

LoopFill addresses the sequence-dimension underfill of that pipeline. It is a training-free speculative decoding algorithm for looped LLMs that combines two draft sources in one draft tree: multi-token suffix drafts retrieved from local (the current request's prompt and generated tokens) and global (outputs of completed requests) suffix trees, and shallow-loop predictions from the model itself. Because both drafters contribute to the same tree, a suffix lookup can immediately extend a shallow prediction — the paper's example pairs the shallow proposal "to" with a suffix-retrieved continuation such as "join Lean" — and several future tokens can enter the pipeline together instead of one per d loop steps.

The shared block scores the highest-path-score draft candidates at different loop depths in a single batched pass, and full-depth verification preserves the model's autoregressive output distribution. Across seven benchmarks, LoopFill delivers an average speedup over greedy autoregressive decoding of 5.85x on Ouro-2.6B and 5.13x on Huginn-3.5B, exceeding LoopAhead, the strongest baseline, by 40% on Ouro-2.6B and 25% on Huginn-3.5B.

## Key points
- LoopFill is training-free: it reuses the looped model's own shallow-loop predictions and retrieval from suffix trees, with no additional drafting model or fine-tuning.
- Two draft sources are merged into a single tree: suffix drafts from a local tree (current request) and a global tree (completed requests), matching every suffix up to 64 tokens long, and shallow-loop predictions proposed from the hidden state once a node reaches a drafting depth d < T.
- Each suffix match builds a small extension tree scored by the product of next-token frequencies along paths, capped at min(Q, p) tokens; the candidate subtree with the largest sum of node scores is merged into the draft tree.
- Draft nodes are ranked by path score — the product of edge weights from the root — and the root plus the top N nodes are kept per request; retrieval weights combine match fractions with a scaling factor c, and shallow predictions assign model probabilities to the top k children (zero to the rest).
- On Ouro-2.6B, the top-1 token after one of T=4 loops matches the full-depth greedy token at 88.8% of positions, and after two loops at 95.6% (Figure 1 of the paper).
- Average speedup over greedy autoregressive decoding is 5.85x on Ouro-2.6B and 5.13x on Huginn-3.5B across seven benchmarks.
- Speedups exceed LoopAhead, the strongest baseline, by 40% on Ouro-2.6B and 25% on Huginn-3.5B.

## Details

### Method
Looped models consist of a prelude, a weight-shared block applied T times, and a coda. LoopFill builds on the inductive bias that shallow-loop predictions agree closely with full-depth outputs, and on suffix trees that index prior text. At the root, suffix retrieval proposes candidate next tokens from both trees; when a drafting node reaches depth d, the LM head is applied to its hidden state and its top-k tokens are proposed as children. Both sources feed one tree, so a suffix match can extend a shallow draft and vice versa. Nodes are scored by path score s(v) = s(parent) * w(v), where retrieval edges carry w(v) = min{1, c*q(v)} with q(v) the fraction of matches continuing with v, and shallow-prediction edges carry model probabilities for the top k children. After any shallow prediction or root change, nodes are re-ranked and the root plus the top N are kept; a parent always ranks ahead of its children, so kept nodes keep their full path to the root. When suffix retrieval extends the shallow draft, several future tokens can be in flight at once, filling the pipeline bubbles that limit depth-only speculation.

### Results
Across seven benchmarks, LoopFill averages 5.85x speedup over greedy autoregressive decoding on Ouro-2.6B and 5.13x on Huginn-3.5B. These speedups beat LoopAhead, the strongest baseline, by 40% on Ouro-2.6B and 25% on Huginn-3.5B. The underlying shallow-vs-full agreement on Ouro-2.6B is 88.8% top-1 match after one loop and 95.6% after two loops, which is what makes the shallow-drafting signal reliable enough to combine with retrieval. Full-depth verification means the emitted tokens follow the original model's autoregressive distribution, so the speedup does not come at the cost of changed outputs.
