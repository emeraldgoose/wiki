# MedJev: A System One Vision-Language Decision Model for Fast and Effective Medical Image Understanding

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20992)
**Published**: 2026-11-16

## Overview

Medical image understanding with vision-language models (VLMs) usually works by autoregressively generating reasoning and answer tokens. That generation cost scales with response length, and the answer still has to be parsed out of free-form text, which adds latency and avoidable decision errors. MedJev instead maps a medical image, a question, and a set of candidate answers directly to a decision in a single multimodal forward pass, with no autoregressive decoding at all. For speed, it removes the upper language layers and the generative output pathway while keeping the full vision pathway and all visual tokens.

On this shortened backbone, a decision head scores each candidate by pretrained query–key matching and refines those scores with a lightweight multilayer perceptron (MLP) residual, coupling the pretrained matching geometry with task-specific nonlinear discrimination. Built on Qwen3.5-4B, MedJev-4B improves both accuracy and speed over the original model on each of four medical image classification and visual question answering benchmarks, with an absolute accuracy gain of 7.93% and a 6.04× end-to-end speedup. Against 20 cutting-edge 4B–9B general and medical VLMs, MedJev-4B is state of the art in both performance and inference speed, indicating that direct multimodal decision making is a viable route to fast and effective medical image understanding. Code: https://github.com/zfkarl/MedJev.

## Key points

- Benchmark gains: most accurate model on all four benchmarks (HAM10000, PneumoniaMNIST, MedThinkVQA, PMC-VQA) and fastest on three of them and on average; against Qwen3.5-4B (same vision encoder, connector, and first language layers) it gains 7.93% average accuracy at 6.04× faster decision latency.
- Generative latency spans two orders of magnitude across the four benchmarks; on MedThinkVQA, Qwen3.5-9B spends 20,947.7 ms per case vs MedJev-4B at 250.7 ms. Free-text failures (context overflow on LLaVA-Med-v1.5-7B / LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna-7B, unparseable option letters on HAM10000) cannot occur because a MedJev prediction is a candidate index.
- Ablation: depth reduction from 32 → J=23 language layers costs no accuracy (mean rises 68.31%→68.70% under query–key head, 68.93%→69.06% under residual decision head) while cutting ~16 ms; QKH alone reaches 68.70%, plain MLP scorer 68.83%, and the zero-initialized residual decision head (RDH, 1.97M added parameters, 1.4 ms over QKH) reaches the best mean of 69.06%.
- Statistical significance: 10,000× paired bootstrap resampling — gains on HAM10000 (+9.99%), MedThinkVQA (+15.56%) and PMC-VQA (+4.73%) clear zero; PneumoniaMNIST (+1.44%, interval [-0.80, 3.69]) does not. Disagreement splits (MedJev-only correct: 5,615 PMC-VQA, 221 MedThinkVQA; Qwen3.5-4B-only: 4,035, 109) show the gains rest on flipped decisions.
- Robustness: permuting candidate order under three seeds drops the unweighted mean only 69.06%→68.10%; fixed label vocabularies lose nothing on PneumoniaMNIST and 1.6% on HAM10000, while free-form options lose 13.1% (MedThinkVQA) and 9.4% (PMC-VQA). t-SNE shows training collapses per-benchmark decision-marker clusters (intra/inter ratio 2.36 → 1.36) into one region shared by candidate sets of 2–7 answers.
- Outperforms released decision models (d1-3B, Visual Jev-4B, OmniMed-Jev-4B) on the four-benchmark average.

## Details

- Method: language-side depth reduction keeps the pretrained vision pathway and connector intact; the decision head reads a decision marker position and scores each candidate via pretrained query–key matching, with a zero-initialized MLP residual refining the score. Training uses the same four benchmarks' training splits; inference is a single forward pass with a fixed visual budget, so every case fits the input window.
- Setting: four benchmarks — HAM10000 (dermoscopy, 7 classes), PneumoniaMNIST (chest X-ray), MedThinkVQA (multi-image medical reasoning VQA), PMC-VQA (biomedical figure VQA). Backbone Qwen3.5-4B; compared against 20 general and medical VLMs at 4B–9B scale.
