# ZipTok3D: High-Fidelity 3D Tokenization with Compact Token Prefixes

Source: Mingda Lin and 9 other authors, arXiv:2609.01740, Sep 1, 2026.

**Lead**: 3D generation pipelines first tokenize object geometry into latent sequences, and sequence length directly sets downstream generative-modeling cost. Existing 3D tokenizers either anchor tokens to spatial regions or compress geometry into a fixed set of global tokens — and both degrade sharply when squeezed to just a few tokens. ZipTok3D instead organizes geometry into **progressively informative global-token prefixes** and unfolds them through **iterative decoding**. During training, nested dropout randomly truncates the latent sequence so every retained prefix must reconstruct the whole object, concentrating essential geometry in the leading tokens; a parameter-shared Transformer block then refines the spatial representation over repeated passes. With the same token width, one ZipTok3D token matches the 32-token COD-VAE baseline on ShapeNet and four tokens match it on TRELLIS — **32x and 8x shorter sequences** at comparable quality.

## Why few-token 3D reconstruction is hard

With a moderate token budget, a global tokenizer can spread complementary geometric detail across many latent vectors. Shrink the budget to one or two tokens and each token must summarize a far larger share of the object, forcing global structure and fine detail to compete for tiny latent capacity. Fixed-budget tokenizers (VecSet, COD-VAE) resolve that competition only implicitly through the final reconstruction loss, with no explicit decision about what to preserve first. ZipTok3D separates the problem into two inference-time controls: retained **prefix length K** (how much latent sequence survives) and **refinement depth L** (how much decoder computation unfolds it).

## Nested prefixes plus shared refinement

Training samples a retained length from exponentially spaced budgets {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128} out of a 128-token, 512-wide latent sequence, masks the suffix from selection and refinement, and requires the prefix alone to reconstruct the full shape. Because every shorter prefix is contained in every longer one, leading tokens are forced to carry object-wide geometry while later tokens add residual detail. Decoding reuses a single six-layer Transformer block for L passes (five in the main results) with intermediate supervision plus intra-loop self-distillation, so decoding depth grows with no step-specific parameters and no separate generative sampling stage.

## What the results show

On ShapeNet, the **1-token** setting reaches the same rounded CD and F1 as COD-VAE-32 and trails by only 0.3 IoU points; on the more diverse TRELLIS split, **4 tokens** match COD-VAE-32 on query IoU and beat it on CD and F1:

| Setting | ShapeNet IoU / CD / F1 | TRELLIS IoU / CD / F1 |
|---|---|---|
| COD-VAE-32 | 97.1 / 0.012 / 97.8 | 75.25 / 0.0172 / 95.67 |
| **ZipTok3D-1** | **96.8 / 0.012 / 97.8** | 75.18 / 0.0168 / 95.81 |
| **ZipTok3D-4** | 96.9 / 0.012 / 97.9 | **75.31 / 0.0166 / 95.92** |

By contrast COD-VAE collapses at 2 tokens (77.7 IoU on ShapeNet), proving the gain is not just "fewer tokens". A two-token code also drives class-conditioned generation close to COD-VAE-32 with a 16x shorter stage-2 sequence and higher sampling throughput. Ablations confirm prefix training and shared refinement are the primary contributors, with intermediate supervision a smaller bonus.

## Caveats

All operating points per dataset come from a single checkpoint and one training seed, so run-to-run variance is unreported; the bootstrap CIs cover evaluation-object variation only. The IoU edge over COD-VAE-32 on TRELLIS (+0.06) has a CI crossing zero — the CD/F1 wins are the statistically firm part. Efficiency numbers are single-GPU (H20) protocol-specific. The paper evaluates reconstruction and class-conditioned generation, not text-to-3D prompting.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- If your 3D pipeline's diffusion/autoregressive stage chokes on long latent sequences, the tokenizer — not the generator — may be the compression point worth fixing.
- Nested dropout is a cheap, general trick: train one model decodable at many budgets instead of one model per budget.
- Shared-weight iterative refinement buys depth without parameters; the cost is latency (2.65ms vs 0.98ms per shape at matched settings), so budget K and L jointly against your SLO.
- Gains concentrate in the extreme low-token regime — longer prefixes mostly compensate for shallow decoding, so profile before adding tokens.
