# FastFrames: Video Generation at Interactive Latency

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23226)
**Published**: 2026-11-17

## Overview

Autoregressive video diffusion models generate frames in response to new observations and inputs, making them a natural medium for interactive visual experiences. But existing models can be orders of magnitude too slow for fluid interaction. FastFrames is a family of fast AR video diffusion models that reaches interactive latency without compromising quality. Profiling a reference 1.3B model finds that dominated costs lie not in diffusion denoising but in operations treated as fixed implementation details — VAE decoding (a single decode step costs as much as 3.5 DiT passes) and KV-cache refreshes — which together account for over half of end-to-end latency.

FastFrames challenges the assumption that these components are indispensable. The VAE decoder is replaced with a ConvNeXt head that operates only at low spatial resolution followed by a single unpatchifying linear layer (147× lower decode latency, same quality, and cheap enough for end-to-end pixel-space training with perceptual/GAN losses, bypassing the VAE reconstruction bottleneck). The extra DiT evaluation used to refine the KV cache is replaced with a lightweight network (10× lower cache-update latency without measurable degradation). System-level, hardware-aware optimizations target the rest of the critical path. Project page: https://apple-aiml-research.github.io/ml-fastframes/.

## Key points

- Latency: FastFrames-S (1.3B, B200) reaches 36 ms/frame (27.8 RPS) matching Wan 2.1-based CF++ quality while ~8× faster; on 8 GPUs 14 ms (71.4 RPS). FastFrames-L (14B) reaches 45 ms (22.2 RPS) on an 8× B200 node, with better visual quality/realism; on a single GPU 158 ms (6.33 RPS).
- Target: interactive regime ≤ 50 ms per frame (≥ 20 RPS, the delay-detection threshold for seamless interaction). No baseline meets it — the fastest baseline, CF++, is 272 ms (3.68 RPS), 5.4× short; Krea-Realtime 14B is over 10× slower than FastFrames-L.
- Interactive applications: techniques transfer directly to off-the-shelf camera-conditioned (LingBot 1.3B: 842 → 52 ms, 16× on 8 GPUs) and physics-conditioned (RealWonder 1.3B: 1141 → 51 ms, 22×) models, both approximately reaching the interactive target; LingBot 14B 1373 → 233 ms (5.9×).
- Both variants use two-step diffusion sampling, generating one latent frame per autoregressive step; FastFrames-S initializes from the Causal Forcing++ 1.3B checkpoint (DiT frozen, trains the pixel decoder + KV refinement network), FastFrames-L converts Wan 2.1 14B to AR and distills with DMD.
- VBench scores match the corresponding same-backbone baselines; the higher visual quality of -S over CF++ is attributed to end-to-end pixel-space adversarial training.

## Details

- Profiling-driven redesign of the inference critical path: replaced VAE decode (ConvNeXt low-res head + linear unpatchify, bit-level quality parity), replaced KV-cache DiT refresh (lightweight network, 10×), end-to-end pixel-space training (perceptual + GAN losses), system-level hardware-aware optimizations (attention/serving, B200 tuning).
- Evaluation: VBench (same prompts/seeds, ~480p, B200 unless noted); latency measured at steady state with full KV cache (AR) or native clip length (bidirectional); RPS = 1/latency, one response per generation unit.
