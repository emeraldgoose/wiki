# WorldReward: Reward Modeling for Camera-Conditioned World Models

Source: Yibin Wang and 15 other authors, arXiv:2609.03952, Sep 3, 2026.

**Lead**: Camera-conditioned world models generate interactive videos where commanded actions should produce the expected scene changes while appearance, geometry, and temporal dynamics stay coherent. Existing rewards assess these separately: geometry rewards estimate trajectory execution but cannot judge visual quality; image rewards measure frame quality but miss action execution and dynamics. **WorldReward** is a VLM-based pairwise preference reward model unifying both. Key insight: judging a whole long video against its full action sequence creates a long noisy context where short-lived local evidence gets diluted — so it decomposes paired videos into **action-aligned chunks**, organizes each chunk into structured visual evidence, and aggregates chunk decisions by voting into separate action and visual-quality preferences.

## Training data and benchmark

Training uses a large-scale reasoning-augmented preference dataset: structured judgments from a frontier VLM, refined through tool-based agent auditing plus targeted human review. Evaluation introduces **WorldReward-Bench**, a human-annotated benchmark measuring reward-model agreement with human preferences on three axes:

| Dimension | WorldReward vs. GPT-5.5 margin |
|---|---|
| Action consistency | **+3.42 pp** (highest agreement overall) |
| Appearance quality | **+1.45 pp** |
| Motion quality | **+3.56 pp** |

Used for RL post-training of **HY-WorldPlay 1.5**, it consistently improves both action execution and visual quality across short- to long-term horizons.

## Caveats

This summary is based on the arXiv abstract and metadata; per-chunk ablations, dataset size, and benchmark sample counts were not verified against the full text and are omitted. Margins over GPT-5.5 are agreement-rate differences on the authors' own bench — independent replication is pending.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Long-horizon judging needs decomposition: chunk by action boundaries and vote, instead of stuffing the whole trajectory into one context.
- One reward model can cover both instruction-following (action consistency) and quality (appearance/motion) if the aggregation keeps the axes separate.
- Frontier-VLM judgments + agent auditing + spot human review is a practical pipeline for preference data at scales humans cannot label directly.
- A reward model is only as trustworthy as its human-agreement benchmark — build that benchmark first, on your own distribution.
