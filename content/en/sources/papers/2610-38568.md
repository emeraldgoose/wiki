# A-RSI: Adaptive Task Refresh for Zero-Data Multimodal Self-Improvement

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.38568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38568) · **Published**: 2026-10-28
**Authors**: Yu Liu, Youxing Li, Yuedong Tan, Zhiwei Yang, Zihan Su, Hanqi Feng, Shaojie Zhang, Libin Liao, Zihao Wan, Chenxu Wu, Cong Cao, Fangfang Yuan, Yanbing Liu, Ziheng Chen, Pei Fu, Radu Timofte, Zongwei Wu, Nicu Sebe, Barnabas Poczos, Jian Luan

## Overview

Recursive self-improvement requires models not only to acquire useful training experience but also to decide when current experience ceases to be useful. Existing methods often focus on what experience to generate next while paying less attention to when fresh experience should be introduced, and many construct training targets through self-consistency or majority voting so supervision depends on the model's own uncertain predictions. A-RSI addresses these two questions separately: training dynamics determine when to acquire new experience, while answers to new tasks remain independently checkable. It monitors reward variation and accuracy in training logs to refresh a task pool when its learning signal declines, without consulting validation or test scores. A static library of model-authored programs produces multimodal questions and derives their reference answers; the task-generation contract is specified, chosen properties of reward and refresh rules are automatically verified, and generated answers are separately audited. The training loop uses no external training examples or human-written answer labels. On the primary eleven-benchmark suite, A-RSI raised the performance average from 57.77 to 61.05 with 800 fresh tasks.

## Key points

- Timing of fresh experience is separated from selecting what to generate.
- Task-pool refresh uses reward variation and accuracy from training logs only.
- No validation or test scores are consulted for refresh.
- Model-authored programs generate multimodal questions and reference answers.
- Reward and refresh rules are automatically verified; answers are separately audited.
- No external training examples or human-written answer labels are used.
- Average performance rose from 57.77 to 61.05 with 800 fresh tasks.

## Details

The framework makes task generation and supervision checkable rather than self-consistent: programs define tasks and derive answers independently of the model's uncertain predictions. Refresh is triggered by decline in training-level learning signal, keeping test data untouched.
