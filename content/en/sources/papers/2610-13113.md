# Continually Post-training Small Models via Synthetic Self-distillation

**Source**: Koh et al., arXiv, 2026-10-09

**Lead**: Continual post-training must add new capabilities without forgetting old ones, and self-distillation fine-tuning (SDFT) does this well — but only for large models with strong in-context abilities. The authors trace SDFT's success to in-distribution learning, show small models cannot self-rewrite well enough, and propose Synthetic Self-Distillation (SynthSelf): a minimally larger same-family model rewrites demonstrations into the student's distribution, filtered by rejection sampling. It wins acquisition across all tested domains and scales.

## Background

SDFT feeds demonstrations in-context to the model itself and distills on-policy toward its self-rewrite, preserving the native distribution. That mechanism collapses at small scale: self-rewrite accuracy falls toward 60% and chain-of-thought traces get dropped. Small models matter disproportionately — on-device, low-latency, draft/verifier, and collaborative deployments all depend on them.

The paper's diagnostic contribution comes first: across 23 math datasets, post-training distribution shift correlates strongly with forgetting (Spearman ρ = −0.85), and within a fixed corpus, lower-shift targets monotonically improve both acquisition and retention. In-distribution learning, not any particular trick, is the key to continual learning.

## Synthetic Self-Distillation (SynthSelf)

Two departures from SDFT that jointly deliver in-distribution + high-quality supervision:

1. **Off-policy supervision with rejection sampling.** Rewrites are generated offline into a dataset, so each can be checked (extracted answer must match gold) and bad ones discarded — impossible under on-policy training. Small models are especially quality-sensitive, making this filter critical.
2. **Minimally larger same-family aligner.** Formalized as: minimize student NLL on rewrites subject to beating gold-SFT divergence and base-model quality. Same-family scaling (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B/14B for 0.5–3B students) uniquely satisfies both — cross-family giants (Qwen3.5-27B, Gemma-4-31B) give higher raw accuracy but worse distributional fit and worse downstream acquisition. Bonus: 62.9–76.9% less data-generation FLOPs than the large alternatives.

Pipeline: greedy rewrite per demonstration → keep only answer-matching rewrites → plain SFT on the filtered set. Tuning-free, zero new hyperparameters (baselines were hyperparameter-tuned against it).

## Experiments

**Setup**: Base models Qwen2.5-Instruct 0.5B/1.5B/3B (chosen to avoid contamination); aligners Qwen2.5-7B (SS-1Δ) and 14B (SS-2Δ). Baselines: SFT, SDFT, DFT, ASFT, plus off-/on-policy distillation with the identical aligner (isolating the rewrite-prompt effect).

**Benchmarks**: 4 acquisition domains — science (SciKnowEval Chemistry), 4-subject science mixture, tool-use (ToolAlpaca), medicine (MedMCQA) — and 14 retention benchmarks (instruction following, commonsense, knowledge, code, math).

**Results** (acquisition %, averaged over 3 scales; retention avg):

| Domain | Base | SFT | SDFT | SynthSelf | Δ vs off-/on-policy distill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Science | 28.9 | 63.5 | 52.7 | **66.8** | +19.7 / +35.5 |
| Science mixture | 32.4 | 55.1 | 42.2 | **57.3** | +9.0 / +18.7 |
| Tool-use | 33.3 | 54.6 | 44.7 | **55.7–60.1** | +5.8 / +3.1–12.0 |
| Medicine | 42.3 | 35.4 | 36.8 | **45.9** | ≈ par (aligner already knows it) |
| Overall acquisition | 34.2 | 52.2 | 44.1 | **56.4** | +5.7–8.5 / +9.3–16.9 |
| Overall retention | 42.8 | 40.5 | 41.5 | **41.7–41.8** | ties best baseline |

Further analyses: in-distribution aligners beat stronger OOD ones (+2.5/+4.6 acquisition pts); SynthSelf beats prompt-matched SDFT by +18.96 pts acquisition / +1.68 retention (prompt is not the confound); rejection sampling helps both SynthSelf and off-policy distillation; training on the smaller filtered set still wins, so quality — not quantity — drives the gain.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

- **Small-model post-training recipe**: when fine-tuning on-device/draft models, rewrite gold data through a one-size-up sibling model and rejection-sample — cheaper and better than distilling from the biggest teacher available.
- **Distribution-fit metric**: student NLL on candidate-teacher outputs predicts downstream success better than teacher benchmark scores — use it for teacher selection before any training run.
- **Quality over quantity**: a 17% smaller filtered dataset still wins — budget inference compute for pass@k coverage + filtering rather than more raw epochs.
- **Retention is free-ish**: the same in-distribution mechanism that boosts acquisition preserves prior capabilities, so one pipeline covers both continual-learning axes without extra regularizers.
