# Learning the Task or Learning the Verifier? Rethinking Criteria Exposure in Embodied Self-Improvement

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.08318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08318) · **Published**: 2026-11-05
**Authors**: Wenbo Huang, Jianxin Bi, Shunyu Yang, Ruiyang Si, Rui Ni, Qiang Wang, Shulong Jiang, Yintianrun Zhang, Feixiang Ruan, Kewei Zhang, Ye Huang, Shukai Gong, Daquan Zhou

## Overview

Embodied coding agents that write robot-control programs are increasingly compared on simulation benchmarks, and several recent self-improvement systems hand the agent the benchmark's success criteria — a practice the paper names Criteria Exposure (CE). Those criteria were written to verify imitation-learned policies and often demand more than the instruction states, so a high verifier score may not mean the instructed task was done. The paper asks: when an agent reads the criteria, does it write code that better performs the task, or code that merely overfits the verifier?

Controlled experiments run a self-improvement loop in RoboDojo with and without CE on 25 tasks: 16 criteria-limited tasks, where criteria and instruction disagree on some trajectories, and 9 faithful tasks. Success-only learning doubles test success from 24.6% to 47.8%, and exposure adds 14.2 points under the original criteria (62.0%) — but the entire gain comes from the criteria-limited tasks, where exposure leads 51.6% to 27.8%; on faithful tasks the settings are level (83.3% vs. 80.6%).

The decisive test re-judges the same episodes with instruction-faithful criteria, rewritten to verify only what the instruction asks. The exposure advantage vanishes: 45.0% versus 47.2%, within noise. Skills acquired under exposure also transfer poorly to new RoboDojo tasks and to LIBERO-PRO and RoboCasa365, and about a third of the exposed agent's memory entries cite the criteria code while none of the success-only entries do.

## Key points

- Under the original criteria, exposure lifts test success on the 16 criteria-limited tasks from 27.8% to 51.6%, but on the 9 faithful tasks the settings are level (83.3% vs. 80.6%) — exposure pays off only where criteria demand more than the instruction.
- Re-judged by instruction-faithful criteria, the same libraries score 45.0% (w/ CE) versus 47.2% (w/o CE); the exposed library itself falls from 57.0% to 45.0% under the rewritten criteria.
- Each library scores best under the criteria its learner read: the library trained on faithful criteria solves 65.0% of episodes under them but 13.5% under the original ones, and on press_by_number the exposed library solves all 20 layouts under the original criteria and none under the faithful ones.
- Success-only learning alone doubles success from 24.6% to 47.8% across all 25 tasks; exposure adds 14.2 points to 62.0%.
- A manual review of RoboDojo found 20 criteria-limited tasks with 26 variants across seven disagreement types — hidden rules, hard thresholds, timing and irreversible events, criteria defects, extra end states, loose criteria, and contradictions — with hidden rules and hard thresholds most common; similar cases exist in LIBERO, RoboCasa365, and RoboTwin 2.0.
- The exposure lead does not transfer: on 10 new RoboDojo tasks the success-only library reaches 80/200 successes versus 71/200 for the exposed one (empty library 65/200), and on LIBERO-PRO and RoboCasa365 the RoboDojo libraries add little (48/60 and 46/60 vs. 45/60; 24/100 and 24/100 vs. 22/100).
- Exposure changes what the learner writes down: about a third of its memory entries cite the criteria code, none of the success-only entries do, and the success-only learner's rules track physical causes instead.

## Details

### Criteria-limited tasks and instruction-faithful criteria

A task is criteria-limited when criteria and instruction disagree on some trajectory: RoboDojo's press_by_number requires a button protocol (blue pressed twice between two red groups) the instruction never states, a LIBERO task asks to stack a bowl on the front bowl while its criteria require the back one, and RoboTwin 2.0's shake_bottle_horizontally succeeds once the bottle is lifted. For each such task the authors rewrite only the criteria code, never the instruction: hidden rules removed or relaxed, defects fixed, hard thresholds relaxed, loose criteria tightened where the simulator can detect the difference. Every flipped verdict was hand-checked, with no misjudgment found.

### Experimental protocol

The loop runs five rounds on five development layouts per task, rolls back underperforming rounds, and tests each library on 20 layouts against an empty-library control. The settings differ only in the evidence channel: the w/o CE learner sees success flags, the w/ CE learner also reads criteria code, score, and stages. Transfer is measured by distilling ten learned-task libraries into one per setting and applying them to ten unseen RoboDojo tasks and to LIBERO-PRO and RoboCasa365 with aligned primitives.

### Implications and limitations

The recommendation: measure embodied self-improvement without exposure by default and report success under instruction-faithful criteria alongside, since a real-world robot has no criteria code — only the part of a gain that survives a criteria change should be expected to transfer. Benchmarks should state in the instruction whatever their criteria verify (counts, order, end states, arm resets) and release versioned criteria compared against instructions. Limitations: criteria were rewritten and re-judged on RoboDojo only; instruction-faithful criteria are the authors' own proxy for the instruction; and the transfer study covers three benchmarks with aligned primitives.
