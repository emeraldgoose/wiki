# After One-Click Video: The Inflow of Service-Type Content, the Failure of the Recommendation Pool Mechanism, and the BoLe System

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.37695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37695) · **Published**: 2026-10-24
**Authors**: Bo Zou

## Overview

In September 2026 a new large language model made program-generated explainer videos very popular, and one-click video content appeared in large amounts on many platforms. On the surface this is increased individual creative power, but at a deeper level it changes the structure of the content industry. The argument rests on two observations. First, this wave is service-type content: people watch it to meet a need rather than to enjoy the content itself, while work-type content depends on people who have something to say; AI speeds up both, but anyone can make service content whereas work content stays limited by its creators, so the ratio between the two becomes unbalanced. Second, free platforms select content through layers of trial viewing, consuming user attention; total attention is fixed, and the unbalanced ratio dilutes good content. A simplified mathematical model shows that the recommendation pool mechanism fails once new content grows past a certain multiple. The paper proposes one possible way out: machines and entry rules judge whether service-type content passes the bar, while trusted people judge work-type content. This is called the BoLe system, and the paper closes with a brief outlook on the two content types.

## Key points

- One-click AI video production increased sharply in late 2026.
- Service-type content meets a need, while work-type content depends on creators with something to say.
- AI speeds both, but service content can be produced by anyone.
- Platforms consume fixed user attention through trial-viewing layers.
- A simplified model predicts recommendation-pool failure past a content multiple.
- The BoLe system has machines and entry rules screen service content and trusted reviewers screen work content.

## Details

The distinction between service-type and work-type content drives the capacity imbalance, not production speed alone. Platform selection layers compete for fixed attention, and the resulting dilution is modeled rather than empirically measured. The BoLe proposal separates screening responsibility by content type.
