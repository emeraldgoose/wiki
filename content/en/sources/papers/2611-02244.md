# Can AI Reviewers See Your Efforts to Improve Your Paper? A Large-Scale Study of Paper Revision Trajectories

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.02244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02244) · **Published**: 2026-11-01
**Authors**: Yaochen Wang, Chenguang Wang, Ming Li, Haomin Zhuang, Xinyue Zeng, Han Chen, Kwesi Adu Cobbina, Dang Nguyen, Dianqi Li, Dawei Zhou, Tianyi Zhou

## Overview

AI reviewers are increasingly used to assess scientific manuscripts, yet it remains unclear whether their judgments reflect the improvements authors make through revision. PaperTrajectory-2026, a dataset of 6,959 paper trajectories across five AI conferences, annotates changes along 14 dimensions and records separate judgments of overall quality. Its annotations show that major improvements emphasize empirical evidence and framing, while later revisions tend toward incremental improvement. Using independent assessments from eight AI reviewers, the authors examine whether annotated major improvements receive higher scores, which revision dimensions are associated with score changes, and whether user opinions can outweigh observed revision gains. The findings show that substantive revisions are not consistently reflected in review scores and that observed scoring gains can be vulnerable to user framing.

## Key points

- Major improvements tend to emphasize empirical evidence and framing rather than routine polishing.
- Most AI reviewers leave scores unchanged on most major improvements in the studied trajectories.
- Agreement across reviewers on the direction of score changes is weak.
- Revision dimensions show some reviewer-specific associations but limited predictive discrimination.
- A brief negative user opinion can erase or reverse an advantage gained by revision.
- The results argue for manuscript-evidence-linked assessment that remains stable absent scientific-evidence change.

## Details

The dataset covers 6,959 trajectories across five AI conferences and 43 pages of analysis. The study compares eight AI reviewers and annotates 14 revision dimensions, including separate judgments of overall quality. Reviewer-specific variation is substantial, so the reported associations should be read as evidence of instability rather than a transferable score rule. The authors also discuss implications for stable assessment interfaces, evidence-grounded judgments, and limitations of current automated review.
