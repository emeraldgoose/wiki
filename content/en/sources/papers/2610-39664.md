# EditAgent: Rethinking Agentic Image Editing

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.39664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39664) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Haiyang Xu

## Overview

Agentic image editors decompose complex requests into steps, but when a step fails, they mostly retry with the same narrow set of tools and a judge that scores each output by eye directly. Such agents are bounded by two spaces: the action space of what the agent can do and the observation space of what its judge can see. On the authors' expert-annotated benchmark, a general-purpose editor yielded no usable result in over 40% of cases, and no judge was reliable across edit types. EditAgent expands both spaces: it routes each step to general editors and specialized methods, and attaches a verification record to each candidate. With these records, off-the-shelf VLM judges outperform finetuned reward models. Candidates are generated in parallel and judged in one listwise call; experts prefer this to sequential refinement in 78.5% of decided comparisons, at no more than half the judge calls. EditAgent improves two open-weight editors on three benchmarks and serves as a data engine for training stronger editors.

## Key points

- Existing agents fail over 40% of cases on the expert benchmark.
- No single judge is reliable across all edit types.
- EditAgent routes steps to general and specialized methods.
- Verification records expand the judge's observation space.
- VLM judges with records outperform finetuned reward models.
- Parallel generation plus one listwise judgment is preferred in 78.5% of comparisons.
- It uses at most half the judge calls versus sequential refinement.

## Details

Candidate generation is parallel rather than sequentially refined, reducing judge calls while expert preference favors the listwise process. The expanded action and observation spaces let the agent recover from failures more reliably, and the collected data can train stronger editors.
