# Efficient Streaming Audio-Visual Target Speaker Extraction for Real-World Acoustic Scenes

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.32749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32749) · **Published**: 2026-10-23
**Authors**: Wendi Sang, Kai Li, Yifan Li, Jianqiang Huang, Xiaolin Hu

## Overview

Streaming audio-visual target speaker extraction (AVTSE) is essential for latency-sensitive applications. Current causal systems face two coupled gaps: benchmarks typically fix the speaker count at two and ignore near-/far-field interference, reverberation, and sudden noise of real scenes; and high-performance causal architectures are too heavy for edge deployment, while lightweight alternatives reclaim capacity by repeatedly invoking a small separator.

To address both, the authors release RealSSA, a realistic AVTSE benchmark with simulated and real-recorded evaluation, and propose Falcon, a single-pass causal extractor with an encoder-space speaker contrastive loss.

## Key points

- Releases RealSSA (RealSSA-Sim and RealSSA-Real), covering 1-5 dynamic near-field interfering speakers plus 10-15 far-field interferers in simulated scenes.
- Proposes Falcon, performing multi-scale time-frequency separation in a single encoder-decoder pass.
- Falcon's contrastive loss suppresses near-field leakage at zero inference cost and transfers across backbones.
- Achieves state-of-the-art on RealSSA-Sim, LRS2-2Mix, LRS3-2Mix, and VoxCeleb2-2Mix.
- Versus causal AV-TFGridNet: 91.9% fewer parameters, 20.2x less computation, 11.1x lower GPU latency.

## Details

The contribution is both evaluation and efficiency. RealSSA broadens AVTSE testing to realistic dynamic scenes, while Falcon avoids repeated separator calls by doing one encoder-decoder pass and adding a contrastive speaker loss that costs nothing at inference.
