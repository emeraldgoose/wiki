# No Channels Left Behind: Understanding Diffusion Acceleration through Residual Gates

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40914) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Junha Park, Jaemyung Yu, Jaehui Hwang, Jong-Seok Lee, Dongyoon Han

## Overview

Diffusion transformer acceleration methods such as REPA and dispersive regularization are usually explained by additional supervision, spatial-structure priors, or improved feature geometry. This paper revisits that explanation and finds a striking internal change: the AdaLN-Zero residual gate participates much more strongly after acceleration, changing from near-zero values to active levels.

The authors study DiT- and SiT-style blocks, where timestep and class embeddings generate modulation vectors for transformer branches. They perturb the gate and observe that accelerated models are especially sensitive to disruption, indicating that gating is not a minor detail.

## Key points

- Diffusion acceleration methods increase activation of the AdaLN-Zero residual gate.
- The residual gate moves from near-zero participation to substantially active levels.
- Accelerated models are more vulnerable to gate perturbation at test time.
- Removing or disrupting gating matters even for the unaccelerated baseline.
- A Radial objective, a simple teacher-free hidden-feature norm objective, is introduced.
- Radial requires no pretrained teacher and no pairwise feature comparisons.
- Residual gating is argued to track generation quality under controlled sweeps.

## Details

The paper connects channel participation to generation quality and argues that existing acceleration objectives reorganize the residual gating pathway. Rather than focusing only on explicitly targeted representations, it reframes acceleration in terms of how signals pass through transformer blocks.

Overall, the work suggests that residual gates should be studied alongside representation quality when understanding diffusion training acceleration.
