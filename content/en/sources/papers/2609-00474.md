# Exploring Collaboration between a language and a non-language agent

Source: Harini S I and 5 other authors, arXiv:2609.00474, Sep 2, 2026. Accepted at EMNLP 2026.

**Lead**: LLMs are increasingly deployed as orchestrators coordinating specialized subagents through natural language — but in domains like game playing and robotics, the strongest available agents are *not* language models, so integrating them requires **verbalization**: compressing rich continuous representations into sparse text summaries at every step. This paper asks whether verbalization is a bottleneck and answers with **LLAMIA-Bench**, six collaborative chess tasks across three facets (behavioral imitation, state assessment, natural-language explanation), each unsolvable by either the LLM or the chess engine alone. Its fix is **latent state internalization**: projecting the subagent's continuous representations directly into the LLM's token stream as learned state tokens, with dynamic re-encoding as actions advance the environment. The gap between internalization and verbalization — the **verbalization debt** — widens through training and persists from 4B to 14B parameters, while a single 14B **LLAMIA** model matches or exceeds task specialists and frontier models including GPT-5.1 with tool access, and generalizes out-of-distribution where task-specific finetunes collapse.

## Why verbalization should be suspect

Every orchestrator–tool interaction today passes through text: the subagent's state gets summarized into words, the LLM reasons, and the loop repeats. That text bottleneck discards most of the subagent's representation at exactly the moment precision matters. Chess is the ideal testbed because the non-language expert (the engine) is superhuman yet genuinely inarticulate — its knowledge lives in continuous state, not sentences — so any collaboration gain or loss can be attributed to the interface rather than to a weak partner.

## Latent state internalization

Instead of translating state into words, internalization projects the subagent's continuous representations directly into the LLM's token stream as **learned state tokens**, re-encoded dynamically as actions advance the environment state. The LLM then attends over live subagent state the way it attends over words — no summarization step, no information thrown away at the boundary.

## What the results show

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| Benchmark | 6 chess tasks × 3 facets (imitation, assessment, explanation); neither partner can solve them alone |
| Verbalization debt | Internalization-vs-verbalized gap widens through training, persists at 4B through 14B scale |
| Single 14B LLAMIA | Matches or exceeds task specialists and GPT-5.1 with tool access on all tasks |
| Out-of-distribution | Generalizes where task-specific finetunes collapse |

The two load-bearing claims: the debt is structural (scaling the LLM from 4B to 14B does not close it), and the fix preserves generality (one model, all tasks, plus OOD robustness that specialists lack).

## Caveats

This summary is abstract-based; effect sizes, per-task breakdowns, and training costs live in the full text. All collaboration evidence comes from chess — a discrete, fully-observed domain — so the size of verbalization debt in noisy robotics settings is unshown. "Matches GPT-5.1 with tool access" is on these benchmark tasks, not a general capability claim.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- If your orchestrator talks to specialist models or sensors via text summaries, you may be paying verbalization debt: benchmark the text interface against passing richer representations before scaling the orchestrator LLM.
- The debt-widens-with-training finding is the actionable one — more training on a lossy interface widens the gap rather than closing it, so interface design comes before training budget.
- One generalist beating per-task specialists plus OOD robustness is the payoff shape to look for when you invest in representation-level integration.
