# OpenFlyV2: An onboard vision-language-action model for aerial perception and navigation

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.39287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39287) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Junli Liu, Zhongrui You, Chenhui Li, Zhen Li, Yuyang Tian, Qizhen Chen, Delin Qu, Haoming Song, Dong Wang, Xuelong Li, Zhigang Wang, Bin Zhao

## Overview

Human cognition flexibly draws on a shared perceptual foundation to understand environments, attend to referenced entities, reason about spatial relationships, and act. Endowing aerial robots with an analogous functional organization remains challenging because existing systems are often specialized for individual capabilities, dependent on offboard computation, or unable to sustain closed-loop operation on resource-constrained platforms. OpenFlyV2 is a unified onboard vision-language-action architecture that uses a shared vision-language representation to support semantic understanding, language-grounded perception, spatial reasoning, and continuous aerial control within one model. A compact action expert converts the shared multimodal context into short-horizon flight waypoints, while motion-aware temporal memory and action priors reduce the computational cost of closed-loop execution. The authors constructed a simulation-based dataset comprising approximately 70,000 navigation trajectories and 60,000 vision-language instruction-response pairs. OpenFlyV2 generates 12-waypoint action chunks entirely onboard at 10 Hz on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX. It transfers zero-shot from simulation to physical quadrotor flight, achieving a 48% navigation success rate across four real-world environments without real-world aerial training data. The results demonstrate that broad vision-language competence and continuous closed-loop flight can coexist in one model on a resource-constrained aerial platform.

## Key points

- One shared vision-language representation supports perception, reasoning, and control.
- A compact action expert produces short-horizon waypoints.
- Motion-aware temporal memory and action priors reduce closed-loop cost.
- The dataset has about 70,000 trajectories and 60,000 instruction-response pairs.
- The model runs 12-waypoint chunks onboard at 10 Hz on Jetson Orin NX.
- It achieves 48% real-world navigation success across four environments.
- The transfer is zero-shot from simulation without real aerial training data.

## Details

The onboard design avoids offboard dependency, combining semantic and spatial competence with continuous control. Efficiency comes from compact action generation and memory/action priors rather than separate specialized modules.
