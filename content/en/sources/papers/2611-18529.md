# eDIAR: Training-Free Open-Speaker Streaming Diarization for Long Meetings and Large Archives via e-means

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.18529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18529) · **Published**: 2026-11-13
**Authors**: Kyudan Jung, Jungwoo Park, Yeongmin Kim, Minseong Kwon, Seongmin Moon, Sungju Park, Kangwook Lee

## Overview

Meetings run up to an hour and nobody knows in advance how many people will speak. Neural diarizers with a fixed number of output slots — Streaming Sortformer, Nemotron-3 — are accurate and stream online, but they simply cannot name more speakers than they have slots. eDIAR removes that limit without retraining. The slot head keeps deciding who is active and when speech overlaps, and speaker embeddings are clustered inside a single slot — using the e-means algorithm — only after every slot has been used.

In the streaming system, re-clustering is confined to a bounded window, labels are revised for a few seconds, and identities are anchored to the slots of the head. The result has no limit on the number of speakers and stays accurate when speakers outnumber the slots, exactly where fixed-slot models break down. On meetings that grow to an hour it stays far more accurate than Streaming Sortformer, which accumulates errors, and its cost per update does not grow with the meeting length.

The same recipe turns a four-slot Streaming Sortformer into an open-speaker system. Across recordings, e-means also makes it affordable to link speakers of a large archive frame by frame — a task that arises when conversational corpora are prepared — and it reaches the clustering quality of Lloyd's k-means several times faster. The method needs little tuning: e-means runs with its default recipe from a few hundred to more than a million embeddings.

## Key points

- The problem is structural: fixed-slot diarizers (Streaming Sortformer, Nemotron-3) cannot label more speakers than they have output slots, regardless of accuracy.
- Training-free fix: keep the slot head for activity and overlap detection, then cluster embeddings inside a single slot with e-means once all slots are in use — no retraining of the underlying model.
- Streaming with bounded revision: re-clustering is limited to a window, labels are revised for a few seconds, and identities are anchored back to slot decisions, so the loop remains online.
- Accuracy where fixed-slot fails: when speakers exceed slots, eDIAR stays accurate; over hour-long meetings it is far more accurate than Streaming Sortformer, which accumulates error.
- Cost per update is flat with meeting length — the bounded window keeps runtime independent of how long the meeting has run.
- Archive use: e-means links speakers frame-by-frame across large recordings at Lloyd's k-means-level quality, several times faster, from a few hundred to over a million embeddings.

## Details

The architecture separates two jobs that fixed-slot models conflate. The slot head answers "who is speaking now?" and "are two people overlapping?" — a cheap, streaming classification that existing models already do well. The second job is naming: mapping those slot outputs to actual speaker identities. eDIAR handles naming only when the slot head runs out of capacity: up to the slot count, the head's own slots serve as identities; beyond that, the system re-clusters the embedding pool with e-means to split and merge identities as new speakers appear.

e-means is the core clustering primitive. Unlike Lloyd's k-means, which requires knowing k and iterating over all points each step, e-means is an incremental algorithm designed for streaming contours: it maintains a small summary per cluster and updates it as embeddings arrive, keeping the number of clusters adaptive. The paper shows it matches Lloyd's clustering quality while being several times faster, and that its default recipe works across an embedding range of four orders of magnitude — a few hundred to more than a million — without tuning.

The streaming loop is where the systems care shows. Re-clustering is prohibited from reaching back over the whole meeting; it operates on a bounded window. Labels near the boundary are revised for a few seconds after new evidence arrives, then frozen. Identities are anchored to the slot head's output so a speaker who goes quiet and returns keeps the same identity. Together these prevent drift and keep per-update cost constant.

Evaluation covers long meetings (up to an hour) and archive-scale linking. On meetings, eDIAR remains accurate where Streaming Sortformer degrades with accumulated error; on archives, e-means provides the clustering engine needed to link speakers across recordings cheaply enough to be practical for corpus preparation. The authors stress that no part of the pipeline requires fine-tuning — the method is a drop-in around existing models.
