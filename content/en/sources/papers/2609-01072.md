# Let Confidence Change, Not the Prediction: Prediction-Preserving Repair for Post-hoc Calibration

Source: Daehwan Kim, Haejun Chung, Ikbeom Jang, arXiv:2609.01072, Sep 2, 2026.

**Lead**: Post-hoc calibration corrects reported confidence, but a multiclass calibrator can also change the associated top-1 prediction — and accuracy only captures the *net* effect on correctness, not how often predictions flip. The paper names the missing metric, **Top-1 Prediction Change Rate (TPCR)**, and proposes **CORD** (Calibrator-Output Repair for Top-1 Decision Preservation), the first post-fit adapter imposing exact prediction preservation by repairing the full calibrated probability vector. From the original and calibrated outputs alone, CORD sets the mass on the original top-1 class and distributes the remainder over the other classes via the calibrated conditional distribution, so the repaired vector's argmax recovers the original prediction; on the calibration split it further coordinates the repaired masses to retain the calibrated outputs' mean mass on original predictions whenever attainable. Across CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-1K, CORD attains **zero TPCR by construction** and lowers mean **ECE, NLL, and Brier** relative to direct calibrated outputs in every dataset, with paired gains persisting under distribution shift and across calibration-set sizes.

## The problem accuracy hides

A calibrator that fixes confidence on 100 examples while flipping 5 correct predictions into wrong ones (and 5 wrong into right) shows zero accuracy change — yet it silently overrode the underlying model's decisions 10% of the time. TPCR measures that flip frequency directly, and the paper's point is that deployment safety cares about it: calibration should change *confidence*, not *decisions*.

## How CORD repairs without refitting

CORD touches neither the fitted calibrator nor its direct output, fits no additional supervised map, and needs no tuned hyperparameter. It works purely from the two output vectors: keep the original top-1 as argmax by construction (assigning its mass appropriately), allocate the rest according to the calibrated conditional distribution, and — where attainable — preserve the calibrated outputs' mean mass on original predictions as measured on the calibration split. Preservation becomes a post-processing guarantee rather than a constraint baked into calibrator fitting.

## What the results show

| Setting | Result |
|---|---|
| TPCR (all datasets) | Zero, by construction |
| Mean ECE, NLL, Brier vs direct outputs | Lower in every dataset (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-1K) |
| Robustness | Paired gains persist under distribution shift and across calibration-set sizes |

Notably, preservation costs nothing here — calibration quality *improves* on all three metrics rather than trading off against the constraint.

## Caveats

This summary is abstract-based; magnitudes of the ECE/NLL/Brier gains, shift protocols, and calibrator types tested live in the full text (code is at github.com/labhai/CORD). Zero TPCR is a guarantee about the repair, not about correctness — a wrong original prediction stays wrong, by design. Results are on image classification; LLM-style sequence calibration is out of scope.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- If you ship calibrated confidence scores (thresholding, abstention, human review queues), check TPCR, not just accuracy: your calibrator may be flipping decisions you already validated.
- CORD's repair-only design means you can retrofit preservation onto an existing calibrated pipeline with no refitting and no hyperparameters — low adoption cost.
- The lesson generalizes: separate *fitting the calibrator* from *enforcing decision constraints*, and enforce the latter as output repair.
