# Muon Makes the First Mixture-of-Experts Layer Harder to Balance

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.37914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37914) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Marcos Hernanz

## Overview

Many open mixture-of-experts language models train with Muon rather than AdamW and balance load via expert routing biases. Recipes disagree on whether Muon should also train the router. This study measures how the optimizer changes the span of these biases in 55M and 108M active parameter models. The span grows when all tokens favor the same experts and thus indicates how hard a layer is to balance. At learning rates tuned on shorter runs, Muon raised the span of the first MoE layer at every seed, roughly doubling it on average, while other layers changed much less. The rise came from Muon on the rest of the network, whichever optimizer trained the router. Inputs to the first router shared a direction, and shortly after warmup its busiest expert averaged four to seven times its fair share. Subtracting the batch mean from these inputs removed this overload in the first 3,000 steps while barely changing the loss. Halving Muon's learning rate removed most of the rise and slightly improved loss on the smaller model.

## Key points

- Bias span measures layer balancing difficulty.
- Muon notably increases first-layer bias span.
- The observed effect comes from Muon on the non-router network.
- First-router inputs shared a direction in affected runs.
- Centering batch mean inputs removes much overload with minimal loss change.
- Halving Muon's learning rate can mitigate much of the rise.

## Details

Across two small MoE scales and multiple seeds, training the router with Muon or moving it to AdamW both showed load shift dynamics during warmup, but keeping it on Muon is favored at this scale. Results are limited to models of 55M-108M active parameters, short-run-tuned learning rates, and load balancing with routing bias updates. Generalization to larger sparser MoE systems should be tested before recommendation.
