# AI Reviews Are Biased Towards the Appendix

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.17811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17811) · **Published**: 2026-11-13

## Overview

As LLMs become a routine part of peer review, the weaknesses they emit increasingly shape the reviews humans submit. This paper asks a narrow but consequential question: which parts of a paper do LLM-generated reviews actually criticize? Analyzing 2,000 papers from ACL, EMNLP, COLM and ICLR, the authors show that the latest GPT and Claude models draw about 50% or more of their review weaknesses from the appendix. The behavior holds across all three access methods studied — API, coding agents, and the web chat interface.

In contrast, human reviewers draw only 8.4% of their weaknesses from the appendix, and those weaknesses primarily concern presentation and content related to a paper's core idea. LLMs, by contrast, cite weaknesses deep in the appendix about fine-grained technical details that are not central to the paper's contributions. The authors argue this bias is new, emerging mainly with the latest, more capable models, and that it may already be leaking into human reviews.

## Key findings

- **2,000 papers analyzed**: 800 from recent conferences (ACL 2026, EMNLP 2026, COLM 2026, ICLR 2027) and 1,200 from ICLR 2021–2026, using four widely used review models: GPT-6-Astra, GPT-6.1-Sol, Claude-Opus-5.5 and Claude-Sonnet-5.5.
- **Newer models are appendix-heavy**: GPT-6-Astra and GPT-6.1-Sol draw around 60–70% of weaknesses from appendix-related material, versus less than 30% for the earlier GPT-5; the gap holds for every conference studied.
- **Access method does not fix it**: The appendix bias persists across API, coding-agent (e.g. Codex-style) and web chat access paths.
- **Absolute appendix-only shares**: GPT draws 17.33% of its weaknesses from the appendix alone (vs 10.16% for Claude), with no link to the main body.
- **Humans differ qualitatively**: Human ICLR appendix weaknesses center on presentation and core-idea content, while LLM appendix weaknesses are deep, fine-grained technical points.
- **Spillover into human reviews**: Human ICLR reviews cited roughly 6–10% appendix weaknesses for years; the share began rising after GPT-5 and nearly tripled to 27.60% in ICLR 2027 reviews posted shortly after GPT-6-Astra and Claude-Opus-5.5.
- More than half of weaknesses from recent-conference LLM reviews involve appendix-related content in almost all model–conference combinations.

## Details

The methodology assigns each generated weakness to either the main body or the appendix using a two-step pipeline: reviews are generated first with a prompt kept close to real-world usage, then a separate model call attributes each weakness to its source section. Separating attribution from generation avoids contaminating the prompt with extra instructions. A weakness counts as appendix-related if any part of its supporting evidence comes from the appendix, whether alone (W4-type) or combined with the main body (W2-type). For human reviews, ICLR public reviews from 2021–2027 were analyzed in aggregate.

The authors flag several limitations: attribution itself is done by an LLM (mitigated by a "None when uncertain" instruction and manual spot checks); compared human and LLM reviews come from different papers and years; the rise of appendix-related human criticism is a temporal correlation, not proof of LLM causation; the study measures where criticism comes from, not whether it is correct; and numerical review scores were not analyzed.

Code is publicly available at https://github.com/cincynlp/review_appendix . Authors are Saptarshi Ghosh, Mohammad Saim and Tianyu Jiang (University of Cincinnati).
