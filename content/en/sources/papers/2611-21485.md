# Lost in the Hallway: Benchmarking Persistent Spatial Memory Across Revisits

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.21485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21485) · **Published**: 2026-11-28 · **Original**: 2026-11-16 (v1)
**Authors**: Henry Zheng, Weibo Gong, Yang Yue, Rui Huang, Yang Zhang, Gao Huang

## Overview

Persistent spatial understanding is what lets an embodied agent operate reliably in environments it repeatedly encounters: recognizing a revisited place despite viewpoint changes, telling it apart from similar-looking rooms, and updating its memory when the environment changes. Existing evaluations tend to leave the physical identity of a place across separated visits implicit, so a model can score well without ever proving it remembers which room it is actually in. This paper makes that identity explicit with ReSpaceBench, a benchmark of 3,244 questions over 590 synthetic and real-home scenes, organized into 9 tasks spanning room association, persistent state tracking, and downstream reasoning.

Visits are defined by physical room entry in controlled walkthroughs, and the benchmark deliberately includes same-type room distractors (several rooms of the same category that look alike) and cross-session object changes, so a system must track physical place identity rather than surface appearance. The authors evaluate 32 models and 3 agentic video-understanding frameworks and find substantial limitations: the strongest evaluated zero-shot model reaches 50.5% overall accuracy and 52.2% on a 450-question subset, far below the 90.1% achieved by human annotators, and the agentic frameworks offer no overall improvement over direct inference with the same base model.

## Key points

- **Benchmark design**: 3,244 questions, 590 scenes (synthetic plus real homes), 9 tasks; visits defined by physical room entry, with same-type room distractors and cross-session object changes built in.
- **Zero-shot ceiling**: strongest zero-shot model 50.5% overall / 52.2% on the 450-question subset vs. 90.1% human; agentic video-understanding frameworks do not improve over direct inference with the same base model.
- **Failure modes**: analysis of agent execution traces shows evidence omissions and incorrect cross-visit room associations as major failure modes — iterative evidence seeking alone does not ensure consistent spatial memory.
- **Familiarity vs. identity gap**: further analyses expose a gap between recognizing a familiar room and identifying the correct visited room; annotation-guided sampling shows intervening visits and transitions must be preserved.
- **Training data**: RS-30K, a separate synthetic training set with no template, scene, route, or video overlap with the benchmark; fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on it raises overall accuracy from 35.5% to 60.4%, with the largest gains in association tasks and improvements extending to real-life scenes.

## Details

The benchmark separates three capabilities that are often conflated: recognizing that a place is familiar, distinguishing the visited room from similar rooms, and tracking how objects in it changed between visits. Room association tasks test matching across visits; persistent state tracking tests whether object-level changes across sessions are noticed; downstream reasoning tasks test whether the tracked memory is actually used. The 450-question subset is a harder, curated slice on which even the strongest model reaches only 52.2%.

The evaluation of 32 models plus 3 agentic frameworks shows that letting an agent iteratively gather evidence does not close the gap: trace analysis attributes failures to omitted evidence and wrong cross-visit room associations, i.e., the agent looks but does not consistently remember which visit it is in. Human annotators reach 90.1%, indicating the benchmark is far from saturated and the deficit is in the models' memory mechanisms rather than in question ambiguity.

The companion training dataset RS-30K is generated from synthetic sources with no template, scene, route, or video overlap with ReSpaceBench, avoiding train-test leakage. Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on RS-30K lifts overall accuracy from 35.5% to 60.4%; gains concentrate in association tasks and transfer to real-life scenes, suggesting the synthetic data teaches transferable place-identity reasoning rather than scene-specific shortcuts.
