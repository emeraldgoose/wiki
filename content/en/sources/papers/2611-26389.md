# ViDecide: Teaching Video Diffusion Models to Reason and Plan Step by Step

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.26389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26389) · **Published**: 2026-11-19 (v1)
**Authors**: Kaiwen Zhang, Yiming Gao, Zeqi Xiao, Xintao Wang, Pengfei Wan, Kun Gai, Xingang Pan

## Overview

Leading video generation models produce realistic clips but still violate physical and logical constraints in multi-step reasoning and planning, even after large-scale reasoning data or diffusion RL. ViDecide attributes this to the conventional training paradigm, which treats a solution as a fixed-length clip rather than a chain of decisions. The framework instead trains video diffusion models to predict state transitions causally — each generated frame depends on preceding visual states, aligning generation with the causal order of decisions (typically K=1 state per step for rule-based games, larger K for longer-lasting states such as manipulation) — and to decide when to stop via a learned visual EOS token, analogous to a language-model EOS, so the trajectory adapts its length instead of a fixed horizon.

A decision-focused training schedule makes flow-matching training distinguish task-relevant state transitions from background, and Corrective Fine-tuning (CFT) corrects failed decisions by mapping their noise to the model's own successful continuations — stable improvement without policy-gradient optimization. Across abstract games, planning tasks, and a new multi-step robot-planning benchmark (RoboTwin-Plan on RoboTwin 2.0 dual-arm block planning), ViDecide reaches an average strict success rate of 83.8%, far above 23.3% for standard fine-tuning and 67.8% for the strongest prior method; on RoboTwin-Plan it solves 78.0% vs 63.9% for the prior best, and the decision-focused schedule alone lifts Wan2.2's default schedule from 33.3% to 73.9% on those robot videos. It also generalizes to larger puzzles and unseen layouts, suggesting transferable rules rather than memorized solutions.

## Key points

- Decision-centric formulation: a solution is a chain of decisions, not a fixed-length clip; video diffusion models generate state transitions in causal order (K frames per decision step).
- Adaptive termination via a learned visual EOS: generated after task completion and learned through the video objective itself, no separate termination predictor; generation stops when detected.
- Decision-focused training schedule: makes flow matching concentrate on task-relevant transitions; on RoboTwin-Plan robot videos it raises success from 33.3% (Wan2.2 default schedule) to 73.9%.
- Corrective Fine-tuning (CFT): post-training for autoregressive video diffusion that maps failed decisions' noise to the model's own successful continuations; stable gains without policy-gradient RL.
- Average strict success 83.8% across four test sets vs 23.3% standard fine-tuning and 67.8% strongest prior method; RoboTwin-Plan (new dual-arm multi-step block-planning benchmark in RoboTwin 2.0): 78.0% vs 63.9%.
- Generalizes to larger puzzles and unseen layouts beyond the training range, indicating learned transferable rules.

## Details

The framework has three components: causal generation (each frame conditioned on preceding visual states), adaptive termination (visual EOS), and the decision-focused schedule. CFT is applied as a post-training stage on autoregressive video diffusion, using the model's own successful continuations as correction targets for failed decisions, which avoids the instability of policy-gradient optimization. Experiments cover abstract games, planning tasks, and RoboTwin-Plan (multi-step block planning for a dual-arm robot in RoboTwin 2.0), with strict success as the metric and generalization probes on larger puzzles and unseen layouts. Baselines include standard supervised fine-tuning and the strongest prior diffusion-RL/reasoning method.
