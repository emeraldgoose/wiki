# Multi-Branch Transformer

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19800)
**Published**: 2026-11-15

## Overview

Transformers are usually scaled by widening blocks or stacking them deeper. This paper explores a complementary axis — branching, the parallel composition of full Transformer blocks. The Multi-Branch Transformer (MBT) processes the input through shared initial layers, executes independently parameterized branches of sequential blocks in parallel, and fuses their outputs before shared final layers. Three variants are studied at matched training compute: Wide-Shallow (preserves block width), Narrow-Deep (preserves sequential depth), and Balanced (interpolates).

Across controlled pretraining experiments at four mixture-of-experts scales (0.8B–2.1B total parameters), 2-branch Wide-Shallow MBTs consistently outperform sequential Transformers under matched parameter and training-compute budgets, and Balanced MBTs improve further. Quality is retained as branches increase from 2 to 3 or 4. For long-context inference, Joint-KV constructs a shared KV cache from branch representations at each depth, cutting full-model KV-cache storage by up to 34% and delivering up to 51.4% higher decoding throughput on a single H200 GPU.

## Key points

- 2-branch Wide-Shallow MBTs beat sequential Transformers at matched parameter and compute budgets across all four MoE scales (0.8B–2.1B).
- Balanced MBTs further improve over Wide-Shallow; quality holds as branches grow from 2 to 3–4.
- Joint-KV (shared KV cache built from branch representations) reduces full-model KV-cache storage by up to 34% and raises single-H200 decoding throughput by up to 51.4%.
- Fitted scaling curves indicate standard Transformers need up to 1.26× as much training body compute to match the final recipe's validation loss.
- Ablations: gains go beyond width/depth changes; Joint-KV is an effective KV-sharing strategy; robust across fusion functions; branches work best in the middle layers.

## Details

- Architecture: shared initial layers → parallel independently parameterized sequential-block branches → fused outputs → shared final layers; evaluated as Wide-Shallow, Narrow-Deep, and Balanced variants.
- Joint-KV: shared KV cache from branch representations at each depth; supported by an inference engine exploiting branch grouping and shared-cache reuse.
- Evaluation: controlled pretraining at four MoE scales (0.8B/1.xB/2.1B ladder), validation perplexity vs. decoding throughput on one H200 (16K input tokens, 64 requests); scaling-law fits for 2-branch and multi-branch MBTs and for Joint-KV configurations.
- Ablations: width/depth controls, KV sharing across branches vs. Independent-KV (cache-matched), fusion mechanisms, branch placement (middle layers best).
- Caveats: experiments are MoE pretraining at ≤2.1B scale; inference gains are reported on a single H200 under the stated benchmark protocol; downstream suites are limited (appendix A.3).
