# Terminal-style Mathematical Modeling Bench: The First Agentic and Verifiable Benchmark for Mathematical Modeling

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.08237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08237) · **Published**: 2026-11-05
**Authors**: Xiaojun Wu, Cehao Yang, Xuhui Jiang, Chengjin Xu, Jia Li, Jian Guo

## Overview

Mathematical modeling transforms an open-ended real-world problem into assumptions, a model, a solution, and a defended recommendation, but answers matter only if they are close to a credible reference and physically feasible. Existing benchmarks often score solver fragments or grade reports with an LLM judge, leaving numerical answers insufficiently checked.

TB-MathModel makes 18 real contest-style modeling problems agentic, verifiable, and terminal. Each task ships a verbatim statement, official attachments, and figures in a containerized terminal. Agents return one JSON artifact scored on the numeric core of the final question against a reproduction of the Outstanding-award paper.

Two design choices distinguish the benchmark. Direction-aware, one-sided scoring never penalizes beating the anchor. Two-layer feasibility verification combines a programmatic gate over six decidable invariant forms with 624 Lean 4 obligations, hard-gating infeasible answers. Each task is scored against a panel of human outstanding papers rather than one. Across seven multimodal entries, the strongest reaches 64.58% multi-reference O-Eval, while a low-cost open model reaches 59.81% at a fraction of cost.

## Key points

- Turns complete modeling problems into agentic terminal tasks.
- Provides verbatim statements, attachments, figures, and artifact scoring.
- Scores numeric answers against multiple outstanding papers.
- Uses direction-aware one-sided scoring to reward exceeding anchors.
- Verifies physical feasibility with programmatic and Lean 4 gates.
- Enables reproducible machine-checkable evaluation of long-horizon modeling competence.

## Details

The benchmark covers 18 real CUMCM and MCM problems from 2023 to 2025. The 20-page paper includes four figures and fourteen tables. Its source code is released.
