# TouchScale: 500 Hours of Human Vision and Touch for Visual-Tactile Learning

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10288)
**Date**: 2026-10-07

## Overview

Large-scale egocentric video datasets have driven progress in human activity understanding, but video alone cannot capture the contact forces and pressure distributions that characterize physical interaction. TouchScale addresses this gap with 500 hours of synchronized egocentric video and high-resolution tactile sensing, collected across diverse manipulation tasks. The dataset spans 15 institutions and includes synchronized RGB, depth, IMU, and tactile data from wearable sensors, enabling visual-tactile representation learning, tactile prediction from vision, and sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation.

## Background

Visual-tactile learning aims to predict tactile signals (contact location, pressure distribution, force) from visual input, enabling robots to "feel" through cameras. Prior datasets (ActionSense, FEEL) are limited in scale (<50 hours), sensor diversity, or task coverage. TouchScale scales by 10x while adding multi-modal synchronization and real-world robotic evaluation.

## Methodology

Data collection used a wearable rig: RGB-D camera, 6-DOF IMU, and a high-resolution tactile glove (Taxim) capturing 3D force distributions at 100Hz. 50 participants performed 120 manipulation tasks (cooking, assembly, tool use, object rearrangement) in naturalistic environments. The dataset provides:
- Synchronized multimodal streams (RGB, depth, IMU, tactile)
- 3D hand/object pose annotations
- Contact segmentation masks
- Benchmark splits for tactile prediction, representation learning, and policy learning

Models evaluated include tactile-from-vision predictors (PressureVision++, ViT-based) and visual-tactile representation learners (contrastive, masked autoencoding). Policies trained on TouchScale representations show improved sim-to-real transfer on contact-rich manipulation tasks.

## Key Findings

- **Scale matters**: Representations pre-trained on TouchScale outperform those from smaller datasets on downstream tactile prediction and manipulation.
- **Tactile prediction**: Vision-to-tactile models achieve reasonable contact localization but struggle with fine-grained pressure distribution.
- **Robotic transfer**: Policies using TouchScale pre-trained encoders succeed on real-world insertion, pouring, and tool-use tasks where vision-only baselines fail.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

TouchScale provides a benchmark for multimodal representation learning at scale. Engineers building robotic perception stacks can use it to pre-train visual-tactile encoders, evaluate tactile prediction models, or study sim-to-real gaps. The dataset is publicly released with standardized splits and evaluation protocols.
