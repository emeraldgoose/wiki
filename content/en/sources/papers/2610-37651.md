# GEPA-Curator: Curated Learning for Self Improving Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.37651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37651) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Zack Yu, Nicholas Lee, Minjae Lee, Lakshya Agarwal, Luca Manolache, Wonjun Kang, Matei Zaharia, Michael W. Mahoney, Kurt Keutzer, Sehoon Kim, Amir Gholami

## Overview

LLM agents increasingly run multi-turn agentic workloads with tools and external environments. Reflective prompt optimizers such as GEPA learn from failed rollouts, but agentic failures are noisy: they may come from prompt weaknesses, harness limitations, stochastic execution, flaky environments, or noisy judges rather than the prompt itself. A Reflector may therefore misattribute a failure and make harmful prompt edits.

GEPA-Curator is introduced as an agentic data Curator that sits on top of existing prompt optimizers. It has two roles: task curation, selecting tasks likely to provide clean signal and discarding noisy or faulty ones; and selective retrieval, reusing prior successful trajectories as guidance when needed. It is evaluated on WebArena websites, ALFWorld, AppWorld, and tau-bench.

## Key points

- Reflective optimizers struggle because not every failed rollout reveals a genuine prompt weakness.
- GEPA-Curator curates minibatches for cleaner signal instead of choosing tasks randomly.
- It selectively retrieves prior successful trajectories to improve mutation quality.
- It matches or exceeds evaluated baselines across four WebArena sites, ALFWorld, AppWorld, and tau-bench.
- Reported gains reach up to 20.7% over GEPA and 21.2% over ACE.
- The benefits of curation are described as extending across optimizer models, not just one tuned configuration.

## Details

The paper motivates the method with examples where a bad outcome can come from a broken environment, such as a purchase button not working, rather than from the agent's prompt. Validation sets can guard against bad mutations, but curation at the minibatch level targets the source of failure attribution more directly.

GEPA-Curator does not replace GEPA or similar optimizers; it runs as a harness on top of them. Its curation role filters potentially misleading tasks before reflection, while selective retrieval brings in earlier successful trajectories when they can help guide a mutation. Together these mechanisms aim to make reflected prompt changes more reliable in multi-turn, tool-mediated settings.
