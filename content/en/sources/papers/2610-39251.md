# Where to Learn and How Far to Roll Out: Efficient Adaptive Training for GUI Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.39251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39251) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Yuxiang Chai, Quyu Kong, Panrong Tong, Steven Hoi, Yue Wang

## Overview

Trajectory-level Group Relative Policy Optimization is common for multi-step GUI agents, but repeatedly rolling out complete trajectories from the task root can be inefficient. Task-level rewards give coarse supervision over which intermediate decisions need improvement, while mastered prefixes are repeatedly re-executed before reaching difficult states. The paper introduces a two-stage adaptive training framework that jointly addresses where to learn and how far to roll out. Reusable intermediate checkpoints are built from successful expert trajectories, and action disagreement selects informative pivots. Stage I performs one-step reinforcement learning at pivots to improve uncertain local decisions. Stage II progressively expands policy responsibility through adaptive local, suffix, and root training as task progress grows. Recoverable checkpoints enable training from intermediate states without replaying preceding trajectories.

## Key points

- Reusable intermediate checkpoints avoid replaying mastered prefixes.
- Action disagreement identifies uncertain local decisions.
- Stage I performs one-step RL at selected pivots.
- Stage II expands through local, suffix, and root training.
- Recovery from intermediate states improves training efficiency.
- Environment interaction is reduced while task performance improves.

## Details

Across MobileGym and CUA-Gym-Hub, the method reduces environment interaction by 56-61 percent relative to vanilla trajectory-level GRPO while improving task performance. The results demonstrate that jointly allocating training across states and rollout ranges can substantially improve GUI-agent RL efficiency. However, checkpoint construction depends on successful expert trajectories and selected pivots, and benefits may differ on noisy environments, broad action spaces, or tasks with weak task-level rewards.
