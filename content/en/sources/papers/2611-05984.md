# On the Design of Hyper-Connections: Simplification, Stability, and Scalability

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05984) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Xiangdong Zhang, Xiaohan Qin, Sunan Zou, Tuo Dai, Bizhao Shi, Zhuo Xia, Yebin Yang, Yizhou Chen, Yuliang Liu, Yu Cheng, Junchi Yan

## Overview

Hyper-Connections (HC) replace the single residual stream of a transformer with several parallel streams plus learned read, write, and mixing operations, and the manifold-constrained variant mHC has been adopted in frontier language models. The paper starts from an efficiency puzzle: with an efficient implementation, mHC adds 7%–27% end-to-end training time over a vanilla residual baseline despite contributing less than 1% additional FLOPs. The cost is not compute but memory traffic, because HC-style mappings read and write an expanded residual state of n streams of width C at every sublayer.

Ablating the three components on a 15B-A1B MoE backbone, the authors find two effective interaction groups: learned read/write gating without mixing, and unconstrained mixing with fixed gates. Either alone matches mHC quality, while Sinkhorn constraints remove much of mixing's benefit (56.2 unconstrained versus 55.0 with Sinkhorn). Because unconstrained mixing risks cumulative amplification of earlier contributions, the paper settles on learned read/write gating with identity transitions — a minimal effective structure it names SimpleHC.

The second half targets execution. Without mixing, each earlier output reaches a later input through a scalar coefficient, so scalar corrections reconstruct inputs exactly without materializing intermediate expanded states; gates for groups of K=4 sublayers are generated from one shared group-entry state, enabling batched reads and a single write-back. Specialized fused kernels turn this into measured efficiency gains up to width C=6144.

## Key points

- mHC adds 7%–27% training time over a vanilla baseline despite adding less than 1% FLOPs, with repeated access to the expanded residual state identified as the main overhead.
- At the largest tested width (C=6144), SimpleHC cuts extra end-to-end training time from about 7.0% for fused mHC and identityHC to 2.4% — eliminating about two-thirds — while the alternative xHC sits at 27.7%.
- SimpleHC halves residual-state memory traffic from 18C to 9C per sublayer relative to fused mHC and cuts stored activation memory by approximately 31%.
- Shared memory access with K=4 reduces full-state reads from 8 to 2 and writes from 4 to 1 per group; K=2 and K=4 match identityHC loss while K=8 slightly increases it.
- Quality holds at scale: on 30B-A2B MoE models SimpleHC averages 63.1 over fourteen benchmarks versus 62.9 for mHC and 62.8 for identityHC, with higher scores on MMLU, MATH, and C3.
- Both design pieces are load-bearing: removing scalar corrections raises loss from 1.660 to 1.705, and reusing one read/write gate pair across the group lowers the average score from 55.0 to 53.5 at K=4.
- Training is smoother and more stable: the maximum 1,000-step rolling gradient-norm standard deviation after warmup is 0.032 for SimpleHC versus 0.206 for mHC and 0.248 for identityHC, and under stress at 10× the reference learning rate on 230B-A6B models, mHC shows gradient spikes near 1.9×10^6 while SimpleHC and identityHC remain bounded.

## Details

### Two effective interaction groups

On the 15B-A1B model, learned read/write gates without mixing score 55.4, unconstrained mixing with fixed gates 56.0, and full unconstrained mixing under the mHC parameterization 56.2 versus 55.0 with Sinkhorn and 55.4 with identity transitions. Fixing either learned gate lowers the score significantly, supporting read/write gating as one group. Full-state gate inputs also matter: restricting read or write gates to stream-local inputs raises loss by 0.001 or 0.003.

### Shared memory access and kernels

Scalar corrections exploit the additive update so each preceding output contributes through the inner product of its write gate with the later read gate, precomputed at group entry, avoiding intermediate state writes. Layer-specific gates for all K=4 sublayers are generated jointly from the group-entry state, batching base reads into one scan with a single group-boundary write-back. At n=K=4 the traffic is 9C per token per sublayer versus 18C for fused mHC and 34C for naive mHC. Predictors trained on the frozen 15B model recover later write gates with mean absolute error 0.0103 versus 0.4086 for random gates, showing the shared state suffices.

### Scaling and stability

Across widths from C=1024 to C=6144, SimpleHC has the lowest overhead under both efficient and PyTorch implementations; at the smallest scale it is 6.3% versus 10.1% for identityHC and 26.8% for mHC. On the 30B-A2B model SimpleHC matches mHC loss (both 1.561) and slightly exceeds the benchmark average, though it trails on GSM8K and HumanEval. The stress test attributes the stability gains to removing residual mixing: mHC's spikes accompany loss spikes and growing activation variances, while both simplified variants stay bounded. Evidence covers MoE models up to 30B-A2B and width C=6144, trained with Muon on mixed English–Chinese text, code, and math at sequence length 8192.
