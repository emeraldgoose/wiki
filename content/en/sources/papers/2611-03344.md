# FLAMIA: FLAsh MIcroscaling Attention for Fast and Causally Safe Training on Blackwell

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03344) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Fedor Velikonivtsev, Mikhail Koltakov, Denis Kuznedelev

## Overview

Attention remains a substantial cost in Transformer training, especially at long context. NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs provide high-throughput low-precision matrix multiplication, but exploiting it in attention requires addressing quantization overhead, causal information leakage through shared scales, and numerical inconsistencies between forward and backward passes. FLAMIA provides attention kernels for Blackwell with MXFP8 and mixed NVFP4/FP8 training paths. Its design combines token-block scaling, scale-swizzling to prevent causal leakage, and dual-scale cross alignment for consistent quantization across orientations.

## Key points

- Attention cost is significant at long context.
- Blackwell supports high-throughput low-precision math.
- Quantization overhead must be managed.
- Shared scales can leak causal information.
- Forward and backward passes need numerical consistency.
- FLAMIA targets these issues with specialized kernels.

## Details

The paper focuses on making low-precision attention both fast and correct. Token-block scaling reuses quantized operands across matrix orientations to reduce overhead. Scale-swizzling prevents causal leakage that would otherwise violate autoregressive validity. Dual-scale cross alignment keeps quantization consistent across forward and backward directions. Together these mechanisms enable MXFP8 and mixed NVFP4/FP8 training on Blackwell without sacrificing causal safety.
