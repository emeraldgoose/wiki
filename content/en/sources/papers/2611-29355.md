# Polytropos: What Do Vision-Language Model Descriptions Make of a Cultural Object?

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.29355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29355) · **Published**: 2026-11-22 (v1)
**Authors**: Yuechen Jiang, Enze Zhang, Yuyan Wang, Mohsinul Kabir, Chenying Lin, Sijia Li, Qianqian Xie, Stavroula Golfomitsou, Konstantinos Arvanitis, Sophia Ananiadou

## Overview

Vision-language models have become a new kind of describer: their written descriptions now shape how people and other models know cultures. But descriptions of culture tend to turn particular things into types, and what a description loses reveals what its describer lacks. Polytropos ("many-turning") operationalizes this: take each description of a cultural object, regenerate it as an image with a fixed generator, and ask whether the reconstruction is still recognised as the object's recorded culture and as the object itself. The benchmark uses 374 objects from the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the Getty (stratified by region, type, and language eligibility via an 8-step multilingual screening protocol), 1,870 machine descriptions from five VLMs (Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, GPT-4.1-mini, LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B, LLaVA-OneVision-7B, Mistral-Small-4), and 230 museum-authored curator descriptions as the human baseline — 2,100 description conditions in total, all run through the same generator and the same image-only culture recogniser (LLaVA-OneVision-7B selected on held-out artefacts) with fixed scoring denominators and 20,000-draw stratified bootstrap intervals.

## Key points

- Primary result: culture-recovery accuracy of generated images is 54.55%-59.09% across the five models, all above the 52.14% of the original photographs (generator-side recognition screening confirms the generator itself does not inflate culture recognition). By model: Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite 54.55 (lowest), GPT-4.1-mini 54.78, Mistral-Small-4 57.75, LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B 59.09 (highest).
- But paired analysis: 16%-21% of objects change recognition outcome between original photograph and reconstruction (net change positive for models, i.e. reconstructions gain more than they lose; curators lose a similar share but gain less, so models have net-positive while humans are net-negative or flat).
- Specific-object identification collapses: only 0.49% of reconstructions are identified as the specific object vs 2.67% of original photographs — the description pipeline preserves culture typicality, not object identity.
- Controls: image-only culture recogniser (no text) with reference-compatibility judge; original-photograph control isolates generator error; Generation-Feasibility Filtering removed 49 of 2,100 conditions (97.67% feasibility); human-baseline cohort H = 230 objects with curator descriptions, same generator/recogniser/scoring.
- Error analysis: six quantitative error classes (nonrecognition, wrong-culture, wrong-type, reference incompatibility, etc.) with stratified analyses by region and type; qualitative case studies (votive painting with lion and text, landscape album, calligraphy title-linked emphasis, disputed wall painting, goldfish in bottle, bird-and-flower print material attribution, natural stone patterns) show descriptions "give many faces" — each typical of the culture, almost none the object itself; American work read through European styles, title-linked thematic emphasis, material misattribution.
- Metrics and diagnostics: fixed-denominator accuracy (Eq. 5), paired outcome changes, nonrecognition-conditioned accuracy, claimed vs locally verified identification, culture agreement by region/type, paired changes relative to photographs (Appendix F/G); recogniser selection protocol on published outputs and held-out artefacts (Appendix F.5).
- Ethics and release (Appendix B): synthetic images must be marked; screening selects recognisers by image-recognition reliability; record-level provenance; responsible reuse of collection material; no claims of cultural authority — the benchmark measures description fidelity, not interpretive truth. A Getty reconstruction pilot (Appendix I) validated the pipeline against the museum's own object records.

## Details

Section 3 formulates the task (description -> generated image -> recognition vs original photograph), the dataset (Met + Getty corpora, eligibility filtering and sampling, 230-object paired human-baseline cohort, provenance fields; Appendix D statistics), and the evaluation protocol (targets, human baseline, image generation wrapper, image-only recogniser, accuracy/uncertainty, controls; Appendices C/F prompts and metric definitions). Section 4 reports recogniser selection (4.1), main results (4.2: per-model accuracy, paired changes, generator-side screening, feasibility), and error analysis/case studies (4.3, Appendices G/H). Section 5 concludes; Appendix A states limitations (collection and reference scope, pipeline dependence, screening); Appendix B covers ethical considerations and release practice. Dataset and code are released with the paper.
