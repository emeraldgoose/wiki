# TailSteer: Trajectory-Tail Steering for Drift-Resistant Multi-turn Image Editing

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.06741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06741) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Ruikang Li, Zixuan Chen, Jiarui Wu, Mingde Yao, Yiyuan Zhang, Yujin Wang, Hanming Deng, Lewei Lu, Tianfan Xue

## Overview

Strong single-turn image editing does not guarantee reliable multi-turn interaction: reused edited outputs accumulate small unintended changes into severe visual drift. Existing approaches often rely on multi-turn training data or inference-time mechanisms that can alter pretrained priors or become difficult to scale to foundation models.

TailSteer improves multi-turn robustness as a final-stage training framework while preserving capabilities learned earlier. It keeps early denoising dynamics close to those of the base model and concentrates preservation corrections near the trajectory tail, where fine-grained deviations can be suppressed.

Corrections are learned from the editor's own on-policy denoising rollouts, using unchanged source content as a preservation reference without curated ground-truth editing pairs. Across FLUX, SenseNova, and Qwen families, TailSteer substantially reduces cumulative drift over 15 to 20 turns while largely preserving base-model text-to-image and single-image editing performance.

## Key points

- Targets long-horizon visual drift in multi-turn image editing.
- Requires no curated ground-truth editing pairs.
- Keeps early denoising behavior close to the base model.
- Applies preservation corrections near the trajectory tail.
- Learns corrections from on-policy denoising rollouts.
- Reduces cumulative drift over 15 to 20 editing turns while preserving benchmarks.

## Details

The 16-page paper includes nine figures and a project page. It positions long-horizon editing robustness as unlockable after earlier capability training, not as relearning existing capabilities.
