# Weaken to Strengthen: Weakening-Induced On-Policy Self-Distillation for Large Reasoning Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.38681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38681) · **Published**: 2026-10-28
**Authors**: Yecheng Wu

## Overview

Self-distillation can improve reasoning models when no suitable external teacher is available, but common approaches rely on privileged information to construct a stronger self-teacher, limiting use when only unlabeled prompts are available. Weakening-Induced On-Policy Self-Distillation (WISD) instead constructs a weaker counterpart of the original model and uses the contrast between the two as a training signal without requiring privileged information. Two ways to build the weaker model are explored: low-bit quantization and magnitude pruning. WISD was evaluated across diverse architectures and scales on mathematical and coding reasoning tasks. Across benchmarks, quantization and pruning improved average accuracy over original models by 4.0 and 5.5 percentage points respectively, achieving state-of-the-art performance even without privileged information. Notably, pruning raised Qwen3.5-4B accuracy from 57.7% to 70.5% on HMMT 2025 and from 39.1% to 56.7% on LiveCodeBench v6. Quantization raised Qwen3.6-35B-A3B accuracy from 68.5% to 74.6% and from 61.8% to 69.5% on the same benchmarks. These results establish WISD as a novel and effective self-distillation framework for large reasoning models.

## Key points

- WISD needs no privileged information or external teacher.
- It constructs a weaker model instead of a stronger self-teacher.
- Low-bit quantization and magnitude pruning were compared.
- Quantization improved average accuracy by 4.0 points; pruning by 5.5 points.
- Pruning raised Qwen3.5-4B from 57.7% to 70.5% on HMMT 2025.
- Quantization raised Qwen3.6-35B-A3B from 68.5% to 74.6% on HMMT 2025.

## Details

By contrasting the strong original model with a systematically weakened version, WISD obtains an on-policy self-distillation signal from unlabeled prompts alone. The tested weakening mechanisms are quantization and pruning, with pruning generally showing larger gains on the reported benchmarks.
