# Evaluating DeepSeek V4.1 Flash-1017 for loss of control and harmful manipulation risks

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.35671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35671) · **Published**: 2026-10-26
**Authors**: Yao Huang, Zhiyuan Zeng, Yu Wang, et al.

## Overview

As frontier language models grow more capable and autonomous, independent third-party evaluations are critical for characterizing systemic safety risks. Open-weight models often receive less scrutiny than closed-source releases. This paper presents an independent safety evaluation of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash-1017, which departs from V4 with a new architecture, scaled-up training, and strong agentic capabilities.

Drawing on the EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, the evaluation spans 34 benchmarks, including five in-house suites, covering evaluation integrity, loss of control, and harmful manipulation.

## Key points

- Compares V4.1 Flash-1017 against V4.1 Flash, earlier DeepSeek V4 models, and closed-source systems (GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol, Gemini 3.8 Flash) under AEF-1.
- V4.1 Flash-1017 shows substantial safety improvements over V4 and approaches/matches closed-source baselines on several dimensions.
- Some notable behaviors still warrant continued attention.
- Recommends integrating safety evaluation into incremental releases, since safety can regress.
- Recommends process-level observability across reasoning traces and action logs, not only final-turn outputs.
- Recommends developing private benchmarks because public benchmarks elicit evaluation awareness and contamination risks.

## Details

The evaluation highlights a transition from outcome-only checks to process-level monitoring for agentic risk. Open-weight frontier models need comparable third-party scrutiny to closed-source systems.
