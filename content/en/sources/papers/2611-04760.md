# Koopman Generative Operators for Efficient Probabilistic Time-Series Forecasting

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04760) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Raz Marshanski, Liran Nochumsohn, Mayank Jauhari, Boris N. Oreshkin, Omri Azencot

## Overview

Probabilistic time-series forecasting needs models that capture structured temporal dynamics, expressive uncertainty, and efficient inference. Existing approaches fall short in at least one respect: latent dynamical models impose structure but rely on restrictive generation mechanisms, while modern generative methods such as diffusion and flow matching are flexible but require iterative, expensive sampling. The paper introduces the Koopman Generative Operator (KGO), which conceptualizes prediction as the evolution of structured uncertainty. It integrates three components: Koopman Patch Embedding (KoPE) for temporally consistent latent trajectory extrapolation; Koopman Flow Matching (KoFM), which enables fast single-step generation through a closed-form matrix exponential in Koopman latent space, bypassing iterative sampling; and an Adaptive Uncertainty Gate (AUG) that provides probabilistic forecasts by adapting uncertainty per variable and horizon, corresponding to a learned decomposition of aleatoric uncertainty without manual tuning. KGO achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on 13 of 17 ProbTS benchmarks in CRPS and 16 of 17 in NMAE. By eliminating iterative sampling, it delivers at least a 25-fold reduction in inference time compared with iterative generative models. The results position KGO as a practical framework that advances the accuracy-efficiency trade-off with leading accuracy and fast single-step generation.

## Key points

- Traditional structuring restricts generation; diffusion/flow matching is flexible but iterative.
- KGO treats forecasting as evolution of structured uncertainty.
- KoPE extrapolates trajectories, KoFM gives single-step generation via matrix exponential, AUG adapts uncertainty per variable/horizon.
- State-of-the-art on 13/17 CRPS and 16/17 NMAE ProbTS benchmarks.
- At least 25x faster inference than iterative generative models.

## Details

Adaptive uncertainty adapts per-variable and per-horizon without manual tuning. Efficiency and accuracy tradeoffs, architecture details, and full benchmarks are in the linked source.
