# Near-Zero Token Embeddings Silently Stall Training in Gated Sequence Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04449) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Xin Liu, Boyuan Sun, Yuxin Wang, Haoran Song, Mingjian Zhao, Mengyu Liu

## Overview

Among surveyed open-weight repositories, reachable input embeddings with norms below 10^-6 appear in 52.6 percent of untied gated and hybrid checkpoints, compared with none of weight-tied models. Such near-zero rows can arise during pretraining when unseen rare or template tokens receive zero input gradients while AdamW weight decay shrinks untied embeddings. In gated sequence models such as Mamba, Gated DeltaNet, and xLSTM, a near-zero token can amplify input-embedding gradients by more than 30 orders of magnitude. At zero input, current-token readout factors vanish, suppressing historical-state readout; then residual RMS values can fall below the square root of the RMSNorm floor across consecutive layers, causing Pre-RMSNorm to amplify backward gradients by up to 10^3 per layer. In Telugu continual pretraining of Nemotron-3-Nano-4B, this mechanism discards updates on roughly 20 percent of steps and leaves triggered rows frozen.

## Key points

- Near-zero embeddings are common in untied gated and hybrid checkpoints.
- Weight-tied models show this failure mode in none of the surveyed cases.
- Rare or template tokens may receive zero input gradients.
- Weight decay can then shrink untied embeddings toward zero.
- Near-zero tokens amplify gradients enormously in gated models.
- Gradient overflow can zero out the whole batch update in affected configurations.

## Details

The proposed remedies are GroupClip for released checkpoints and GateFloor for pretraining. GroupClip decouples input-embedding clipping via a 64-bit or rescaled norm; its rescaled implementation eliminates update loss and heals triggered rows in one step with negligible overhead. GateFloor uses SiLU(z)+delta with delta between 0.01 and 0.1, keeping the gate positive at zero input. Experiments up to 370M parameters reduce near-zero gradients by 28 orders of magnitude while maintaining comparable perplexity. The findings are specific to tested configurations and should be replicated before broad application.
