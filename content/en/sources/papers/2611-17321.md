# BrickFlow: Connectivity-Guided Brick Reconstruction

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.17321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17321) · **Published**: 2026-11-12
**Authors**: Peter Kulits, Cordelia Schmid

## Overview

Given a box of LEGO parts and a picture on the front — no instructions — infer the 6D pose of every piece. Unlike monocular 3D reconstruction, this inventory-conditioned assembly task must satisfy discrete, connector-defined physical constraints: every piece must land in a precisely compatible position and orientation, the visible image reveals only the outside, and the internal structure (which bricks support which, what fills unseen spaces) must be inferred jointly over thousands of parts that connect in diverse ways. Peter Kulits and Cordelia Schmid (Inria/ENS, MPI Tübingen) turn this into a generative modeling problem.

The authors first learn a part-set-conditioned assembly prior by training an SE(3) flow model on procedurally generated assemblies, built with a typed connector system over LDraw parts by starting from random pieces and iteratively placing new ones through compatible connectors. These assemblies are not semantically meaningful; they are constraint-rich samples from the physical grammar of LEGO, and pretraining on them amortizes search over connector-valid configurations into a reusable structural prior. The prior is then adapted to image-conditioned reconstruction: a smaller set of procedural assemblies is rendered into structure–image pairs, features from a frozen visual encoder are injected via cross-attention, and the flow model is finetuned using about 1% as much data as pretraining.

Because a learned flow captures global structure but does not guarantee precise connectivity — small translational or rotational errors leave intended connections slightly misaligned — the paper adds connectivity-field guidance: an analytic test-time energy over poses and connector geometry that is zero exactly on connected assemblies, applied as in diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) with the field as negative log-likelihood, correcting poses in late steps along its gradient at the model's predicted final assembly.

## Key points

- Validity scales with model size: on the BrickNet evaluation set, unguided image-conditioned DiT-S/B/L reach 19.4%, 26.1%, and 30.5% validity, and connectivity-field guidance adds 4.6–5.6 points at every size.
- Pretraining matters structurally: training the image-conditioned model from random initialization without the pretrained hand-off drops BrickNet validity from 19.4% to 2.3% even though pose error (MM) improves.
- Image conditioning reduces pose error from 58.9 to 31.0 MM (LDU) on BrickNet (pile-trained DiT-L → image-conditioned DiT-L), and finetuning on 50% or 25% of the renders only moderately lowers validity (19.4% → 18.1% / 16.2%) — evidence for the 1%-data adaptation claim.
- Guidance roughly halves the number of disconnected components for the largest assemblies (removing 16% of components for the smallest and about half beyond 80 parts) and grows the largest component from 51 to 66 parts for 96–100-part assemblies, while changing pose error by at most 0.3 LDU.
- ProcBrick, whose assemblies are on average twice as large, follows the same trends at lower validity.
- Honest limitation: validity falls with part count — below 1% beyond roughly 50 parts for single samples and beyond 85 parts for the best of 100 samples, since one detached part or a single collision invalidates a whole assembly.

## Details

Evaluation covers inventory-conditioned assembly and image-conditioned reconstruction, scoring pose accuracy (mean matching in LDraw units) alongside assembly validity and connection validity, with collisions counted separately. Appendices give training settings, the snapping procedure, connectivity-field details, guidance hyperparameters, full results, inference times, and ablations. Models and data are released for research use at kulits.github.io/BrickFlow. This is the v1 (12 November 2026) HF-listed submission.
