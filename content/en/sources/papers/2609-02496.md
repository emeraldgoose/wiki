# Debias-SparseGPT: Bias-Aware Pruning for Large Language Models

Source: Irina Proskurina and 3 other authors, arXiv:2609.02496, Sep 2, 2026. Accepted to EMNLP 2026.

**Lead**: Pruning and quantization make LLM deployment cheap — but recent work shows weight sparsification like SparseGPT can *amplify* existing biases, with outputs varying sharply on persona cues in the prompt. **Debias-SparseGPT** is a post-training pruning method that bakes representational debiasing into the pruning objective through a second-order term defined over demographically contrasting inputs. Validated across a range of generative LLMs at 25%, 50%, and structured 2:4 sparsity, it consistently reduces pruning-induced bias versus SparseGPT while preserving perplexity and zero-shot accuracy. Under the harshest 2:4 pattern, enriching the calibration set with long-context, content-rich examples further improves both downstream performance and fairness. The net claim: a better bias–performance trade-off at full sparse-model efficiency, with code released.

## How the debiasing works

Standard SparseGPT minimizes layer-wise reconstruction error on a calibration set, blind to whose representations it distorts. Debias-SparseGPT adds a second-order correction computed over demographically contrasting input pairs, penalizing pruning choices that push representations apart along demographic axes. It stays post-training — no retraining, just a smarter calibration-time objective — so it slots into existing compression pipelines wherever SparseGPT runs today.

## The calibration-set finding

The secondary result is practically the most actionable: at 2:4 structured sparsity, where quality degrades most aggressively, augmenting calibration with long-context, content-rich examples lifts *both* downstream accuracy and fairness. Calibration data composition is an under-appreciated lever — the same sparsity budget yields different bias outcomes depending on what the pruner sees during calibration.

## Caveats

This summary is abstract-based: the bias metrics, persona-cue evaluation protocol, model list, and the magnitude of bias reduction are all in the full text. "Consistently reduces" is qualitative at abstract level — check the paper's tables for whether any model/sparsity cell regresses. Fairness gains measured on persona-cue benchmarks may not transfer to all bias dimensions; scope your claims to what was measured.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- If you ship pruned or quantized LLMs, bias-test the *compressed* artifact, not just the base model — compression can amplify bias your base evals missed.
- Debias-SparseGPT is a drop-in calibration-time change; evaluate it wherever SparseGPT is already in your pipeline before accepting bias regressions as the price of sparsity.
- Audit your calibration set: long-context, content-rich examples helped most where sparsity hurt most — calibration composition is a free lever.
- Structured 2:4 sparsity (the hardware-friendly kind) is also the bias-hostile kind; budget extra fairness eval there.
