# Restoring Diversity in Distilled Image and Video Generators by Probing Activations

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.18550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18550) · **Published**: 2026-11-13
**Authors**: Furkan Horoz, Ozgur Kara, Kaan Oktay, Adil Kaan Akan, Atakan Kara, Anand Bhattad, James Matthew Rehg

## Overview

Distilling diffusion-based image and video generators into few-step student models cuts sampling latency dramatically, but the students exhibit pronounced diversity collapse: different random seeds produce strikingly similar samples for the same prompt. This paper asks where in the student's internal representation that lost variation disappears, and whether the diagnosis can guide a targeted repair rather than a full re-distillation.

Probing activations block by block under identical inputs, the authors find a consistent pattern across every model family tested: the student's total activation variance matches or even exceeds the teacher's, but it is concentrated into far fewer directions at one identifiable intermediate block during the initial denoising step. Diversity collapse therefore stems from a misallocation of activation variance, not from an inherent loss of generative capacity.

Guided by that finding, the authors introduce ReDiverse, two complementary interventions applied at the identified block. ReDiverse-FT fine-tunes lightweight LoRA adapters (active only at the first denoising step) to regress student activations onto the teacher's. ReDiverse-TF requires no training: it steers activations along precomputed teacher principal directions with random coefficients scaled by the teacher's spread along each direction. Both can be used independently or combined.

## Key points

- Distilled students keep the teacher's total activation variance but compress it into substantially fewer directions at an identifiable intermediate block at the first denoising step — output metrics cannot localize this, but activation probing can.
- ReDiverse-FT inserts LoRA modules (rank 32, attention + FFN, blocks up to and including the identified block ℓ) and regresses first-step student activations toward the teacher's τ-th-step activations; the LoRA is active only during the first step.
- ReDiverse-TF is training-free: it injects perturbations along the top K=8 teacher principal directions (estimated from N=25 teacher runs) with random coefficients scaled by the teacher's directional spread.
- Evaluated on four teacher–student pairs across image (SDXL, SD3) and video (Wan 2.1, MiniMax-H3) generators, all using publicly released 4-step checkpoints, ReDiverse restores most of the diversity gap while preserving generation quality (CLIPScore changes under 0.5 points; subject/background consistency stay close to the teacher).
- On SDXL and SD3, ReDiverse achieves the highest diversity among 4-step methods on every metric; it even exceeds the teacher on LPIPS (SDXL) and Vendi (SD3), and beats Noise Optimization on all diversity metrics except Vendi for SD3.
- On video, ReDiverse matches or exceeds the teacher on Wan 2.1 diversity metrics except V-JEPA 2, and approaches (surpassing on ViCLIP diversity) the teacher on MiniMax-H3; DFD reduces diversity on Wan 2.1 and barely changes MiniMax-H3.
- A user study (and a Gemini 3.1 Pro VLM judge) shows raters prefer ReDiverse over every 4-step method on diversity overall and per aspect, rate it on par with — even slightly above — the teacher, and prefer it in prompt following against every method.
- ReDiverse also mitigates within-image diversity collapse (e.g., identical-looking vases in "a photo of two vases"), consistent with the directional-collapse diagnosis.

## Details

The probing methodology measures, at each block, the total activation variance across seeds and its participation ratio (how broadly variance is spread across directions). In early blocks the student tracks the teacher closely; deeper down, total variance equals or exceeds the teacher's but is restricted to far fewer directions in a particular block, cascading downstream. The collapsed block ℓ differs per pair (e.g., block 17/12/28/45 for SDXL/SD3/Wan 2.1/H3, with teacher target steps τ = 7/9/8/16), and the same collapse separates students distilled from one teacher under different recipes (Appendix C).

ReDiverse-FT trains on just 100 DiffusionDB captions times M=16 noises for 200 iterations (batch 32) with a single recipe across all four pairs — no re-distillation and no real video data required. ReDiverse-TF estimates teacher directions once per prompt and injects them at the first step only. Baselines include Noise Optimization (test-time noise search, reported as an upper bound), DP-DMD (needs a full distillation run), and DFD (needs real videos and post-training); ReDiverse-FT needs only a short fine-tune and ReDiverse-TF needs none.

Headline results (Tab. 4): SDXL student 59.4 DINO / 5.02 Vendi → ReDiverse 65.0 / 7.03 (teacher 66.3 / 7.66); SD3 student 48.4 / 3.78 → ReDiverse 60.1 / 6.24 (teacher 61.6 / 6.02 — ReDiverse exceeds the teacher on Vendi). Wan 2.1 student 31.6 DINO / 22.6 ViCLIP-div → ReDiverse 52.2 / 40.0 (teacher 51.6 / 36.2); MiniMax-H3 student 36.5 / 28.4 → ReDiverse 50.9 / 38.6 (teacher 52.5 / 37.2 — ReDiverse surpasses the teacher on ViCLIP diversity). Quality is largely preserved: CLIPScore moves less than 0.5 points from the student, and ImageReward/PickScore stay close to the teacher on SDXL.

Ablations (Tab. 5, SD3) confirm the diagnosis is causal rather than correlational: regressing at the identified block (or all blocks) recovers the most diversity, earlier/later blocks help less, and regressing at the first denoising step beats regressing at later steps. Combining ReDiverse-FT and ReDiverse-TF gives the largest gains. The paper notes limitations: interventions act only at the first step, depend on teacher access at inference (TF) or a short fine-tune (FT), and are evaluated on four publicly released 4-step pairs.
