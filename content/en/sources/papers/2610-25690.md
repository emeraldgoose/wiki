# ScaleNav: Scalable Internet Video Pretraining for Language-Conditioned Local Navigation

Source: Yihui Yang, Yuanhong Yu, Jiawei Wang, Zhongrui Yu, Jia Shi, Kun Zhan, Ruizhen Hu, Xiaowei Zhou, Yu Liu, Sida Peng. arXiv:2610.25690, Oct 19, 2026.

**Lead**: Language-conditioned local navigation asks a robot to reach a target described in text and visible in its first observation. End-to-end policies need diverse data, but real-robot data is expensive to collect and simulated data leaves a sim-to-real gap. ScaleNav sidesteps both by pretraining visual dynamics on internet egocentric video — millions of hours of people already walking toward things they name — and then using those learned dynamics to generate robot actions. Reported success rates are 86.5% on a single-view adaptation of Short-Horizon OVON and 94.9% on the authors' HabitatGS-LN benchmark, with deployment on a real quadruped.

## Why internet video

Simulators give you unlimited rollouts but they encode a renderer, not a world. Real robots give you the right physics but the data collection cost scales linearly with fleet size. Egocentric video from the internet sits in between: it is real, it is enormous, and it is already labelled by language, because people narrate what they are looking at.

## Three-stage training

ScaleNav is a unified world-action framework trained in three stages:

1. **Instruction-conditioned video pretraining** — the model learns how observations evolve as a camera approaches an instructed target, across many real scenes.
2. **Action alignment** — action-labelled simulation data teaches the model to emit executable action chunks rather than just predict video.
3. **Joint fine-tuning** — all trainable components are optimised together for navigation.

The design intent is that pretraining supplies visual world knowledge that is expensive to simulate, while action alignment supplies the control interface that pretraining alone lacks.

## Results and what it means for engineers

ScaleNav beats evaluated baselines on both benchmarks and transfers to a real quadruped, which is the part that usually breaks. The generalisable pattern is worth noting: use passive, plentiful, language-aligned video for representation learning, then bridge to actuation with a small labelled slice, rather than trying to scale actuated data collection. For SW engineers the pipeline shape — pretrain on abundant passive data, align to actions, jointly fine-tune — is the same shape used in many production VLA systems.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Data strategy: passive-first pretraining changes what you collect and what you pay for.
- The world-model/action split maps onto separate modules you can version and test independently.
- Real-robot transfer is the acceptance criterion, not a simulation score.
