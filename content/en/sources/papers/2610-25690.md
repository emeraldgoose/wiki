# ScaleNav: Scalable Internet Video Pretraining for Language-Conditioned Local Navigation

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25690) · Yang et al., Oct 19 2026 · arXiv:2610.25690
**Date**: 2026-10-19

**Lead**: This paper introduces ScaleNav, a unified framework for language-conditioned local navigation that learns from scalable Internet egocentric videos. By combining action-free video pretraining with simulated action-label alignment, ScaleNav achieves 94.9% success rate on HabitatGS-LN and 86.5% on Short-Horizon OVON. It addresses the cost of real-robot data collection and the sim-to-real gap through three-stage training: instruction-conditioned video pretraining, action alignment, and joint fine-tuning. The approach demonstrates that diverse real-world navigation experience can be extracted from publicly available video sources without manual action labeling.

## Overview

Language-conditioned local navigation requires a robot to navigate to a visible, text-described target and stop at the correct location. Existing policies suffer from costly real-robot data collection and sim-to-real gaps in simulated environments. ScaleNav proposes using Internet egocentric videos as a scalable training source by learning goal-directed visual dynamics from these videos and applying them to robot action generation through a three-stage pipeline.

## Three-stage training

Stage 1 performs instruction-conditioned video pretraining on Internet egocentric videos to learn how observations evolve as the camera approaches instructed targets across diverse scenes. Stage 2 uses action-labeled simulated data for action alignment, training the model to predict executable action chunks. Stage 3 performs joint fine-tuning of all trainable components together. The full model achieves 94.9% success rate (SR) and 86.1% success weighted by path length (SPL) on HabitatGS-LN, compared with 90.4% SR without video pretraining.

## Key results

- Full ScaleNav: 94.9% SR / 86.1% SPL on HabitatGS-LN; 86.5% SR on Short-Horizon OVON adaptation.
- Without video pretraining: 90.4% SR (shows value of Internet video data).
- Without action alignment: 93.6% SR / 85.1% SPL (alignment contributes ~1.3 SR points).
- Without video replay: 94.3% SR / 84.2% SPL (replay improves SPL by 1.9 points).
- Joint training on videos + simulated data without separate pretraining: 90.4% SR / 83.1% SPL — confirming that the three-stage separation is critical.

## Caveats

Results come from arXiv abstract and paper structure; full dataset details, latency measurements, and per-environment breakdowns require the original paper. The approach depends on the diversity and quality of Internet egocentric videos, which may not cover all navigation domains.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Pretraining from unstructured public video (Internet egocentric) is a scalable alternative to labeled simulation data — a pattern transferable to any domain with abundant unlabeled visual data and limited labeled actions.
- The three-stage separation (pretraining → alignment → joint tuning) is a clean architecture for combining unlabeled and labeled sources; adopt it when your data sources have different cost structures.
- The 86.5% / 94.9% benchmarks provide concrete targets for navigation agent evaluation; compare your internal metrics against these rather than arbitrary accuracy.
