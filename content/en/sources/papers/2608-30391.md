# Using Grounded Theory for Agent Behavior Analysis at Scale

Source: Zhuoran Lu and 7 other authors, arXiv:2608.30391, Aug 31, 2026. EMNLP 2026 Findings.

**Lead**: Understanding what agents actually do requires methods that scale to thousands of trajectories and surface *new* patterns in long, unfamiliar tasks — exactly where pre-built classifiers fall short. This paper imports **grounded theory**, a six-decade-old qualitative method from the social sciences with a principled saturation criterion and an auditable trail from data to theory, into agent trajectory analysis. It introduces **AutoTraceGT**, the first multi-agent pipeline that automates grounded theory on agent trajectories, iteratively performing open, axial, and theoretical coding until saturation and producing a behavioral taxonomy tailored to each task. Across six trajectory corpora, AutoTraceGT codebooks recover **73–91 percent** of the failure modes in human-annotated taxonomies while surfacing additional patterns those taxonomies miss. Used as a deductive feature space, the codebook outperforms zero-shot and few-shot LLM baselines on downstream failure prediction.

## Why pre-built classifiers fall short

A classifier can only find what it was built to look for. In long, unfamiliar agent tasks the interesting behaviors are often the ones nobody pre-registered — novel failure modes, unexpected strategies, interaction patterns that only emerge at scale. Manual qualitative analysis catches these but does not scale to thousands of trajectories. The paper's move is to take a method designed for exactly this situation — grounded theory, where theory is built bottom-up from data with an explicit stopping rule (saturation) — and automate it.

## AutoTraceGT: coding until saturation

AutoTraceGT is a multi-agent pipeline that mirrors the classic grounded-theory sequence: **open coding** (label what you see in the trajectories), **axial coding** (relate codes to each other), and **theoretical coding** (integrate them into a narrative), iterating until the saturation criterion says there is nothing new left to find. The output is a behavioral taxonomy tailored to each task, plus an auditable trail from raw trajectories to theory — something a black-box classifier never gives you.

## What the results show

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| Failure-mode recovery vs human taxonomies (six corpora) | 73–91% |
| Patterns beyond human taxonomies | Found (qualitative) |
| Downstream failure prediction with codebook features | Beats zero-shot and few-shot LLM baselines |
| Theoretical narrative | Aligns with prior expert accounts |

Two results matter most. First, the high recovery rate plus extra patterns means the automated method reproduces most of what humans found *and* adds coverage — the emergent narrative still agrees with expert accounts, so the extras look like signal rather than noise. Second, the codebook works as a **deductive feature space**: coding new trajectories with it predicts failures better than asking an LLM zero- or few-shot.

## Caveats

This summary is abstract-based; the 33-page full text will carry the details that matter (the saturation operationalization, the six corpora, baseline prompts). The 73–91% range is recovery *against human taxonomies*, which are themselves imperfect ground truth — recovering 100% would arguably mean overfitting to human blind spots. And "outperforms LLM baselines" is relative to zero-shot and few-shot prompting, not to a fine-tuned failure classifier.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- If you operate agents in production, your incident taxonomy is probably incomplete. A saturation-driven coding pass over real trajectories is a principled way to find the failure modes your dashboards never named.
- The auditable trail from data to theory matters for the same reason runbooks matter: when a new failure appears, you can trace which evidence produced which category.
- Consider the codebook-as-features trick: a cheap coded representation of trajectories can beat raw LLM judgement on downstream prediction.
- Saturation gives you a stopping rule for analysis effort — "keep looking until nothing new appears" beats "look at 50 traces and hope."
