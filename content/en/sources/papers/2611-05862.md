# CARTBench: Rubric-Based Evaluation of Shopping Advice for Complex and Ambiguous Requests with Timelines

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05862) · **Published**: 2026-11-04

## Overview

LLM shopping assistants now recommend products in multi-turn conversations, but real shopping needs are complex, ambiguous, and perishable: an expired gift deadline, a one-bag limit, an unknown hospital list. Existing shopping benchmarks do not explicitly report timeline inputs, and none credits replies that keep unconfirmed preferences open — so an evaluation cannot tell whether advice follows a need that has already expired, nor separate a lucky guess from a justified conditional answer. CARTBench targets CART: Complex and Ambiguous Requests with Timelines.

Two obstacles shaped the design. Data scarcity: real commercial data is hard to access, and identifying complex or ambiguous needs in confidential conversations requires costly annotation — so cases are curated from real commercial shopping needs via stratified sampling, yielding 169 representative cases across 17 retail domains and 8 intents, each pairing timestamped dialogue (which retains changed needs and unanswered questions) with 1,603 cached product records from an internal search platform. Difficulty standardizing criteria: a shared checklist cannot capture case-specific needs, while case-by-case criteria drift without a common standard — so experts distilled four Ideal Response Principles from excellent replies manually selected from successfully completed orders, and instantiated them as case-specific rubrics. The frozen experimental rubrics contain 1,328 expert-annotated criteria in three tiers: Core (required behavior), Excellence (high-quality advice beyond it), and Safety (case-specific risks). Models receive the principles but not the rubrics, through Principle-Guided CART Prompting.

The headline result is an understanding-to-execution gap. In the first full-suite evaluation under frozen rubrics — 7 flagship commercial API models (including GPT-6.1-Sol and Claude-Opus-5) plus three 35B+ open-weight models — three of ten models exceed 90% on Core, yet none exceeds 45% on Excellence. Goal alignment (97.6–99.2% among commercial models) outruns requirement adherence and uncertainty preservation; no model passes every required check; and the open-weight tier trails badly on Core (68.9–80.3%). Judging is reliable: 13,239 of 13,280 judge labels (99.69%) agree with blinded expert review.

Failure analysis shows where the gap lives. Unsupported product claims (F7), unjustified suitability leaps from stated attributes (F8), and premature resolution of unknowns (F2) recur in every model; context-state drift (F1) is minor overall but the most frequent diagnosed mode in task-switch cases; and in decisive-unknown scenarios evidence and fit errors outnumber uncertainty collapse. Most striking, among replies that pass all resolved need and evidence checks, 13.0% still fail a plan check — a shopping feasibility mirage.

## Key points

- Benchmark composition: 169 cases, 17 retail domains, 8 intents, timestamped dialogues, 1,603 product records, and 1,328 frozen criteria across Core/Excellence/Safety; dates and intervals distinguish active, expired, and unconfirmed historical needs.
- Four Ideal Response Principles distilled by experts from excellent replies on successfully completed orders: address the current purchase decision; follow active needs and preserve unknowns; ground claims and explain trade-offs; give clear and useful decision help.
- Core results: Qwen3.8-Max 95.4% and GPT-6.1-Sol 95.9%, DeepSeek-V4-Pro 91.0% exceed 90%; Claude-Opus-5 89.0, Kimi-K3 89.3, MiniMax-M3 88.8, MiMo-V2.6-Pro 87.0; open-weight Qwen3.5 models span 68.9–80.3%.
- Excellence collapses the field: best is Claude-Opus-5 at 39.8%, then Kimi-K3 and Qwen3.8-Max at 37.2, GPT-6.1-Sol at 29.6 — none within reach of Core-level performance; open-weight models sit at 18.9–25.4%.
- The gap decomposes by capability: goal alignment (GA) is near-saturated for commercial models (97.6–99.2%) while uncertainty preservation (UP) trails (87.5–96.2%) and drops to 65.7–78.6% for open-weight models; GA beats RA for all 10 models and UP for 9.
- Constraints scenarios have the lowest Excellence rate for 7 of 10 models — recommendations that must reconcile interacting constraints or dependent choices are the hardest to make excellent.
- Safety behaves differently per model: GPT-6.1-Sol passes 100.0% of case-specific risk checks while MiniMax-M3 (82.2) and the open-weight tier (60.0–84.4) lag.
- Shared failure modes in every model: F7 unsupported product claims, F8 unjustified user-specific suitability guarantees, F2 unknowns treated as settled — grounded advice needs both supported product facts and justified links to user needs.
- Scenario structure matters: F1 context-state drift is the most frequent diagnosed mode in task-switch cases (continuity does not guarantee state tracking), and decisive-unknown scenarios surface more F7/F8 than F2 — bounding claims matters as much as preserving unknowns.
- Feasibility mirage: among replies passing all resolved goal, requirement, uncertainty, and evidence checks, 13.0% still fail a plan check (95% interval 8.1–18.4), so requirement satisfaction does not imply a workable recommendation.

## Details

- Cost and scale: one complete 169-case evaluation costs under US$24 per API model for inference and judging at official list prices, enabling inexpensive large-scale model selection; three repeated evaluations produce the reported mean ± SD.
- Product evidence is fixed across models: 1,603 cached records (titles, available prices, specifications, descriptions) from an internal product search platform, with missing fields left missing and retrieval conferring neither user endorsement nor suitability.
- Rubric design: each of the 1,328 criteria checks one observable behavior and accepts supported alternatives; experts reviewed the frozen set; Excellence rewards comparisons, priorities, and conditional advice beyond Core's minimum obligations.
- Judging protocol: rubric-based judging under Principle-Guided CART Prompting; one expert independently reviewed outcomes blinded (99.69% agreement, 13,239/13,280 labels); reported scores use expert-confirmed labels.
- Diagnosis scope: each failed Core response–criterion instance receives one primary AI-assisted diagnosis; Excellence and Safety failures are excluded and uncertain codes remain in the denominator; scenario rates weight models and cases equally.
- Coverage note (limitations): common scenarios such as gifting and travel clothing are covered, but specialized needs (museum-grade historical costumes, polar-expedition equipment) and adaptation across purchase–return–replacement cycles remain open.
- Code and data: github.com/Da1yuqin/CARTBench.
