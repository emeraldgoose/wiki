# HiVe-Mem: Interleaving Horizontal Memory Expansion and Vertical Evidence Composition for Multimodal Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.12872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12872) · **Published**: 2026-11-09
**Authors**: Haozhen Zhang, Bohan Liu, Jianzhu Bao, Qingyuan Liu, Haodong Yue, Xiao Li, Zi Pong Lim, Wenya Wang

## Overview

Multimodal agents that persist over long interactions need memory that preserves and reuses what they saw and discussed. Existing systems compress interaction histories into fixed representations and retrieve them with semantic search; the authors argue this has two failure modes. Structures centered on abstract entities and relations miss cross-episode contextual affinities, and a single fixed representation cannot serve the heterogeneous evidence needs of different queries.

HiVe-Mem addresses the two axes separately. Horizontally, memory episodes become nodes in an undirected affinity graph whose edges reflect overlap between textual and visual anchors, weighted by inverse document frequency so distinctive shared cues count more than ubiquitous ones. Retrieval starts from a few semantically similar seed episodes and adds graph neighbors scored by query-relevant anchor overlap. Vertically, every retrieved memory keeps five evidence forms — raw interaction text, a compact summary, an image caption, the raw image, and a grounded visual view — and a lightweight trainable selector predicts, in one forward pass over concatenated query and summary embeddings, which forms each memory should contribute.

Across three multimodal memory benchmarks the design lifts task performance across base models while consuming roughly a tenth of the multimodal tokens of the most efficient competing baselines.

## Key points

- Evaluated on Mem-Gallery, WorldMemArena, and H2HMem with Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and GPT-5-mini backbones.
- Best average scores: F1/LJ of 32.32/57.96 with Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and 35.94/64.69 with GPT-5-mini; with GPT-5-mini the selector was transferred from the Qwen model without retraining yet still won all three benchmarks.
- Token efficiency: average consumption of 0.658M and 0.748M tokens versus 7.812M and 7.350M for the most efficient competing multimodal baselines — reductions of 91.6% and 89.8%.
- Ablations (LJ, Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct): removing horizontal expansion drops 57.96 to 56.51, removing visual anchors to 57.30, and removing IDF weighting to 56.55.
- The largest drop comes from summary-only vertical composition, which falls to 44.47; removing raw text and raw images yields 49.68 and 51.02.
- Latency: time to first token is 1.03 seconds on Mem-Gallery, substantially below multimodal systems that run memory operations at answer time.
- Hyperparameters: best graph degree K_edge is 4, and the best retrieval split is 5:2 between semantic seeds and graph expansion at a total of 7 memories.
- Selector behavior: visual-dominant queries still select raw images for only 35.2% of retrieved memories, showing compressed evidence often suffices even when the query involves vision.

## Details

### Horizontal expansion

The affinity graph connects episodes whose textual and visual anchors overlap, including cross-modal bridges where a concept appears as text in one episode and as an image in another. IDF weighting emphasizes distinctive shared cues, and each node keeps at most K_edge edges chosen greedily by edge weight. Expansion candidates are ranked by IDF-weighted cosine similarity between the query and the anchors they share with the seeds, so the added memories are both related to what was retrieved and relevant to the question.

### Vertical composition and budget control

The selector takes the concatenated embeddings of the query and each memory's compact summary and jointly predicts evidence-specific selection probabilities in a single pass. A budget parameter lambda shifts the operating point: as it grows, raw-history selection drops sharply and compositions become more compact, while raw images stay rare but dominate consumed tokens when kept — a contrast between selection frequency and per-selection cost that motivates optimizing utility and consumption jointly.

### What the ablations say

Summary-only memory loses more than 13 LJ points relative to the full system, evidence that compact summaries alone cannot answer many multimodal queries, and every evidence form contributes: even removing captions or grounded views costs performance. The learned policy also generalizes across backbones, since the GPT-5-mini results reuse the Qwen-trained selector directly, suggesting evidence-selection behavior is a transferable skill rather than a model-specific artifact.
