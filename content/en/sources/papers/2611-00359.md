# Humanized but Still True? Auditing Facts and Detection in AI-Text Humanizers

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.00359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00359) · **Published**: 2026-10-30
**Authors**: Ziyuan Wang, Genliang Zhu, Xiaobo Chen

## Overview

AI "humanizers" rewrite machine-generated text so that detectors classify it as human-written. Public leaderboards such as the AI Humanizer Benchmark score these services on detector evasion and on embedding-similarity measures of meaning — neither of which says whether checkable details survive. A changed number or a reworded quotation can leave the surface meaning nearly intact while making the text false. The paper audits unit survival instead: a rule-based program extracts numbers and dates, person and organization names, and quoted spans from each source and checks whether each unit survives in the rewrite, validated by two AI annotators at precision 0.84–1.00 and recall 0.93–0.95.

Two text families are studied. The commercial side uses the October 2026 cycle of a public humanizer leaderboard: 33 AI-written source texts and 363 outputs from 11 tools written by six source models, each scored by seven commercial detectors. The open side applies an open 12B humanizer, in four released quantizations, to fact-dense U.S. federal agency prose. Detection is then tested on matched federal controls where human originals, AI-styled drafts, and humanized rewrites cover the same content.

The headline results cut both ways. Humanizers do mangle checkable units — every commercial tool altered at least one in 35–65% of eligible documents — yet unit retention and detector evasion were not clearly related, and a general-purpose decision model called Jev detects humanized text far better than dedicated detectors.

## Key points
- All 11 commercial tools changed or dropped at least one audited unit in 35–65% of the October 2026 documents containing one; the September cycle gave a similar 19–50%.
- Quoted spans suffer most: no tool kept more than 4 of the 15 quoted-span units verbatim and 10 tools reworded at least 12; number losses ranged from 2/56 to 13/56 and name losses from 0/23 to 8/23.
- The result is not an artifact of quotes: counting numbers and names only, 11–58% of documents still had an alteration, and 10 of 11 tools stayed at or above 20% (hix-bypass fell to 11%).
- The open 12B humanizer kept at least 97% of numbers and names on federal text (retention 0.964–0.972 across the four files) yet reworded 12–14 of the 18 quoted spans, or 67–78%, against its own prompt.
- Across tools, retention and evasion correlate only weakly and unreliably (Spearman ρ = −0.46, 95% CI −0.65 to 0.08); the two weakest evaders, with mean human probabilities of 0.13 and 0.19, still altered facts in 55% and 45% of documents.
- The four released files of the open model, two of them low-bit QAT files, did not show the pre-registered monotone trade-off: evasion stayed at 0.73–0.75 while retention moved by only 0.008 between bf16 and IQ2_XS-QAT.
- Asked a single zero-shot question, the decision model Jev separated human originals from 12B rewrites on 92 federal passages with AUROC 0.864 (0.827–0.902), 0.18–0.22 higher than Binoculars, Fast-DetectGPT, and the adversarially trained RADAR.
- At a 5% false-positive rate Jev flagged 57% of rewrites versus 5–20% for the other detectors; at the 0.5 threshold it called 24% of human originals AI.
- On AI-styled drafts by 2026 models, both perplexity-based detectors scored below chance (AUROC 0.37 and 0.39), also with line breaks removed; the whole study cost about US$0.65.

## Details

### Text sets and checker
The federal matched-control set combines all 54 modern passages of the GoHumanize test split (39 AI-styled drafts by GPT-4o-mini, 15 by DeepSeek-Chat) with 38 NEW2026 passages from 2026 NIST news and Federal Reserve speeches — 92 passages from 68 articles. Quoted spans are 15 of 94 units per commercial source set and 18 of 643 units in the federal set. Expanding or contracting a negation counts as a change, which suits verbatim quotation, and the checker, per-item judgments, and a script recomputing the main tables are released.

### What humanizers actually change
Most quoted spans are not reported speech: of the 15 commercial spans, only 6 attribute statements to named people in two news articles, while the rest are sample lines for asking a manager for a promotion, placeholder achievements in a cover-letter template, a term, and rhetorical questions — rewording them need not make the text false. On the federal side, 13 of 18 quoted spans are attributed statements, two are passages quoted from official documents, and three are terms or mottos. In one development example a Q3 rewrite turned "Champlain Towers South" into "Champlian Towers South", a building-name slip that the fact categories do not count.

### Fidelity versus evasion
The correlation between keeping units and evading detectors was negative but not reliable (ρ = −0.46, CI −0.65 to 0.08). Within the open model, retention went 0.972, 0.967, 0.964, and 0.964 from bf16 to IQ2_XS-QAT while evasion (the share of rewrites below a 5%-false-positive threshold, averaged over four detectors) stayed at 0.73, 0.73, 0.75, and 0.74. The pre-registered monotone condition failed, and the data rule out a retention loss above about two percentage points — not a smaller one. The two low-bit files were refined by quantization-aware training to reproduce the bf16 model, so bit width alone is not isolated.

### Detection results
Jev reached AUROC 0.943 on human-versus-draft and 0.864 on human-versus-rewrite, while Fast-DetectGPT managed 0.688 and Binoculars less on rewrites, and RADAR, though strongest of the four on drafts at 0.717, also dropped after humanization. Jev's probabilities were only moderately calibrated (ECE 0.129, median P(ai) 0.37 on human originals). The margin is setting-specific: in a 30-text development pilot on commercial outputs without matched human controls, Binoculars (0.80) and Fast-DetectGPT (0.84) matched Jev (0.78). A rerun of the 552 federal texts on an RTX A6000 reproduced every score of the main A40 run exactly.
