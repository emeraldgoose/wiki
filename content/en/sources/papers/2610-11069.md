# Clinician Use of Language Models Diverges from How the Models Are Evaluated

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11069)
**Date**: 2026-10-08

## Overview

This large-scale survey of 6,342 clinicians across 35 medical specialties maps real-world clinical LLM usage (Real-World Clinical Questions, or RCQs) against the tasks used in public LLM benchmarks. The study finds a significant divergence: clinical use is dominated by documentation (31%) and knowledge retrieval (28%), while diagnosis—the focus of many medical LLM benchmarks—accounts for only 4% of RCQs. High-stakes clinical questions are concentrated in specific specialties and are partly identifiable from question text alone. Over a third of RCQs cannot be answered well as posed, due to ambiguity, missing context, or requiring judgment beyond current LLM capabilities. The authors introduce RCQ-Map, a taxonomy of clinical task and intent categories, and show that public benchmarks (MedQA, PubMedQA, MMLU-Medical) sample a different workload from real clinical practice.

## Background

Medical LLM evaluation has centered on multiple-choice exam questions (USMLE-style) and diagnostic vignettes. However, clinicians use LLMs as daily assistants for writing notes, summarizing records, looking up guidelines, and drafting messages—tasks that are open-ended, context-dependent, and often low-stakes. This mismatch means benchmark performance may not predict clinical utility.

## Methodology

The authors surveyed clinicians at NYU Langone Health and affiliated institutions (Oct 2024–Jan 2025), collecting 6,342 RCQs with metadata (specialty, role, urgency, patient context). Each RCQ was coded for task category (documentation, knowledge retrieval, diagnosis, communication, planning, other) and intent (information seeking, generation, verification, analysis). A subset was evaluated against GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet for answer quality. Benchmark overlap was assessed by mapping MedQA, PubMedQA, and MMLU-Medical items to RCQ-Map categories.

## Key Findings

- **Workload mismatch**: Documentation + knowledge retrieval = 59% of RCQs; diagnosis = 4%. Benchmarks overweight diagnosis and underweight documentation.
- **Specialty variation**: Emergency medicine and primary care generate more high-stakes RCQs; psychiatry and pathology generate more knowledge-retrieval RCQs.
- **Answerability**: 36% of RCQs are poorly posed for LLM response (ambiguous, missing context, require institutional knowledge).
- **Role differences**: Attending physicians ask more diagnostic questions; residents and APPs ask more documentation questions.
- **Identifiability**: High-stakes RCQs can be detected from text features (mention of specific patient, urgency markers, clinical terminology density) with AUC 0.82.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

For teams building clinical AI tools, this work underscores that evaluation must reflect real clinical workflows—not just exam benchmarks. Documentation assistance, summarization, and guideline retrieval are higher-impact targets than diagnostic reasoning. The RCQ-Map taxonomy provides a framework for building clinically representative eval sets.
