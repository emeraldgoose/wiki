# RoboColosseum: Evaluating Physical Intelligence at Scale In-The-Wild

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.02497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02497) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Tan Sang Nguyen, Yi Ru Wang, Shailesh Shailesh, Gang Yang, Jiamo Hu, Jianuo Jiang, Haoquan Fang, Zhongzheng Ren, Zhongyi Luo, Ian Chong, Aaron Tung, Santiago Pravisani, Minghui Lin, Yao Mo, Ishneet Singh, Benjamin Tee, Harold Soh, Siddhartha Srinivasa, Dieter Fox, David Hsu, Haoming Song, Maxime Ellerbach, Thomas Wolf, Pepijn Kooijmans, Ruijie He, Michael Cho, Jiafei Duan

## Overview

General-purpose robot policies must work across robots, tasks, and environments, yet the variation that makes physical trials informative also makes them difficult to reproduce. RoboColosseum is a distributed evaluation framework that combines unified policy serving with standardized protocols across a network of locally operated robots in diverse environments. The initial study evaluates five vision-language-action policies and three frontier AI agents across four embodiments: SO-101, Franka, Bimanual-YAM, and a G1 humanoid. An open track compares models without task-specific adaptation in blinded, pairwise trials under matched local conditions, while a fine-tune track measures adaptation to 20 tasks when each policy receives 100 demonstrations per task.

## Key points

- The framework combines unified policy serving with standardized local protocols.
- It spans SO-101, Franka, Bimanual-YAM, and G1 Humanoid embodiments.
- Five VLA policies and three frontier agents are compared at distributed physical sites.
- The open track compares untuned models in blinded pairwise trials.
- Fine-tuning produces different leading policies on different embodiments.
- Policy rankings disagree strongly across embodiment-specific task suites.

## Details

Across 5,699 pairwise open-track comparisons at 124 sites, frontier agents rival specialized VLA policies, while all systems struggle to complete open-world tasks reliably. After fine-tuning, GR00T N1.7 leads on SO-101 and pi0.5 leads on Franka and Bimanual-YAM. Open-track rankings poorly predict relative fine-tuned performance, with substantial rank reversals. Partial-progress measurement reveals differences that task success alone misses; on the G1 Humanoid no policy completes any task, yet average progress ranges from 13.3 to 23.2 percent. Rankings stabilize as trials accumulate, and active matchmaking achieves comparable agreement with fewer physical trials.
