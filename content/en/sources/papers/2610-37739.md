# OpenFrontiers: A Collection of Open Research Problems and a Benchmark for Scientific Rediscovery

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.37739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37739) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Jiarui Yao

## Overview

Large language models are increasingly capable of research-level problem solving, but the corresponding problem collections and evaluations are limited. OpenFrontiers addresses both needs with a cross-disciplinary resource that combines a structured corpus of open research problems with a retrospective benchmark of recently resolved mathematics problems.

The corpus records problem statements, context, progress, and related information for 12,151 problems. The benchmark contains 200 problems, 110 resolved in 2026 and 90 still open, each with a Lean 4 statement checked by automated probes and two independent reviews. An HQ-50 subset adds quality filtering. An evaluation protocol grades formal proofs using two independent checkers in an isolated sandbox under closed-book, frozen-retrieval, and live-web conditions.

Evaluating seven LLM agents on HQ-50 under a standardized budget shows that closed-book rediscovery is rare, at most 7 of 40 resolved problems. Live-web success mostly reflects retrieval of published solutions. The linked corpus, benchmark, and grading protocol provide a foundation for measuring research-level rediscovery.

## Key points

- Combines a structured corpus of open research problems with a formally graded rediscovery benchmark.
- Records 12,151 problems with statements, context, and progress.
- Provides a benchmark of 200 mathematics problems with Lean 4 statements and independent reviews.
- HQ-50 filters higher-quality problems for focused evaluation.
- Grades formal proofs with two independent checkers under multiple retrieval conditions.
- Closed-book rediscovery by LLM agents is rare; live-web results largely trace retrievable solutions.

## Details

The resource links broad problem collection to a formally evaluable subset with documented solutions. The reported experimental scope uses seven LLM agents and a standardized budget, and the benchmark distinguishes resolved and still-open problems. This makes OpenFrontiers useful for studying progress toward solving currently open scientific problems.
