# Regularized Score Centering

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21791)
**Published**: 2026-11-16

## Overview

Asynchronous reinforcement learning raises training throughput, but stale-policy data and numerical mismatch between training and inference can destabilize learning. Score centering (SC) stabilizes off-policy learning by removing the effect of reward baselines on the expected update, even without full importance sampling (IS). Empirically, full IS produces extreme gradients and SC significantly reduces their magnitude, yet SC still fails when the learner moves far from the behavior policy that generated the data.

Regularized Score Centering (RSC) adds a reverse Kullback–Leibler (KL) penalty encouraging the learner's next-token distribution to stay close to the behavior policy's next-token distribution. RSC works with or without truncated importance sampling (TIS), and the theoretical analysis shows KL regularization improves convergence guarantees. Dense and mixture-of-experts models were evaluated on mathematical reasoning and agentic tasks — online shopping, tool use, games, and fully asynchronous software engineering. Project page: https://biechi.github.io/blog/rsc/.

## Key points

- RSC with TIS substantially improves sample efficiency on Crafter and Craftax (higher training rewards for the same number of sampled responses, advantage persisting through training).
- Final evaluations: top performance across all six agentic tasks. Vs IS-PPOClip (strongest non-RSC baseline on Crafter/Craftax/BabyAI): +52% on Crafter, +16% on Craftax, 9.9× its score on BabyAI. Vs SC+TIS: +43% on WebShop. AppWorld mean evaluation score +14.3% over both SC and SC+TIS. TextWorld Cooking near-maximum scores.
- WebShop 74.80, TextWorld 99.95, per Table 1 (mean across three seeds); gains concentrated where actions change subsequent states and rewards — agentic tasks with stale policies.
- Failure modes identified: SC fails under top-k estimation and under agentic/highly stale settings; RSC's reverse KL (reference = behavior policy, refreshed as it updates) limits policy drift; with TIS the combination caps token importance weights while controlling the distribution mismatch SC cannot fix.
- Software engineering results extend the gains to fully asynchronous training pipelines where policy lag varies over time (generation latency, environment interaction, queueing), resembling production RL workloads.

## Details

- Method: RSC = SC + reverse KL (+ optional TIS). The KL penalty compares learner vs behavior next-token distributions at each history; both the score center and the KL penalty are conditional expectations estimated from behavior samples (32 auxiliary samples in the SWE experiments), requiring no additional rollouts.
- Baselines: IS, sequence-level IS/GSPO-style, MIS, TIS, IS-PPOClip, SC, SC+TIS; models: dense and MoE on math reasoning plus WebShop, AppWorld, BabyAI, TextWorld Cooking, Crafter, Craftax, and fully asynchronous SWE training.
