# Where Does Alignment Emerge and What Does It Mean? A Closer Look at the Platonic Representation Hypothesis

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.12869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12869) · **Published**: 2026-11-09
**Authors**: Julia Herbst, Simon Roschmann, Quentin Bouniot, Zeynep Akata

## Overview

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) claims that sufficiently capable vision and language models, trained on different modalities but exposed to the same underlying world, converge toward similar representation spaces. Two recent critiques complicated the picture: Koepke et al. showed that the original performance-alignment trend does not extend to newer language model families, and Gröger et al. showed that representation similarity scores are confounded by model depth and width, so raw scores need calibrated baselines before they mean anything. Prior PRH studies also reported only the maximum similarity across all layer pairs of full representations, hiding where alignment actually lives. The authors revisit the hypothesis along three axes: where alignment emerges across layers and representation dimensions, how caption characteristics modulate the capability-alignment relationship, and what alignment magnitudes mean once compared against explicit baselines.

On the first axis, they map mutual k-NN alignment between every layer pair instead of taking the maximum. Cross-modal alignment between unimodal vision and language models emerges mainly in later layers, once abstract semantics have formed, peaking at a mutual k-NN score of 0.143 between DINOv3 layer 21 and Qwen3-8B layer 30 on WIT — and the final layer is never the most aligned one. Within modality the picture is far stronger: vision models form block-diagonal regions above 0.3 (peaking at 0.538), and a generative LM and a text embedding model reach roughly 0.5 across many early-to-middle layer pairs. Compression experiments with PCA show the shared structure survives aggressive dimensionality reduction: cross-modal alignment is largely captured within a subspace of about 100 dimensions, against original language representation widths of 1,536 to 5,120.

On the second axis, they re-test whether stronger language models align more with vision models. Using five-shot MMLU accuracy against alignment with DINOv3, the correlation is weak to moderate on the short, noisy captions of CC3M (Pearson r = 0.26, not significant) and WIT (r = 0.46), but rises to r = 0.66 (p = 0.001) on DOCCI's long, fine-grained human captions — and survives calibration. Crucially the trend persists even when DOCCI captions are summarized to 10% of their length (r = 0.59), so verbosity alone is not the explanation; sufficiently clean and visually informative captions are what reveal the shared structure. The trend generalizes to ARC Challenge (r = 0.73) but not to GSM8K, whose mathematical reasoning is less grounded in world knowledge shared with vision — alignment tracks semantic capability, not capability in general.

On the third axis, they place unimodal cross-modal alignment against three references: randomly initialized models, explicitly aligned vision-language models, and same-modality model pairs. On COCO and DOCCI the unimodal pairs exceed 0.3 mutual k-NN, surpassing CLIP and SigLIP 2 though remaining below Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B, the multimodal retrieval state of the art. Yet all cross-modal scores stay far below the ~0.5 observed between models of the same modality. The pragmatic reading: unimodal models already carry partially shared, low-dimensional world structure that lightweight alignment methods exploit, but "platonic" convergence between modalities remains far from within-modality similarity.

## Key points

- Scope: 3 unimodal vision encoders (DINOv2 ViT-L, DINOv3 ViT-L, MAE ViT-L), 21 generative language models from the BLOOMZ, Llama, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma, and DeepSeek families, 2 text embedding models (Qwen3-Embedding-8B, NV-Embed-v2), and 3 explicitly aligned VLM references (CLIP ViT-L/14, SigLIP 2 So400M, Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B).
- Datasets span caption regimes: WIT (short, noisy Wikipedia captions), CC3M with synthetic DreamLIP captions, COCO (concise human captions), and DOCCI (long, fine-grained human captions); DOCCI captions are additionally summarized by Qwen3-8B to 10/25/50/75% of original length to isolate verbosity.
- Metric: mutual k-nearest-neighbor overlap (k = 10, N = 1024 image-caption pairs) in the original formulation and in the calibrated variant of Gröger et al.; CLS tokens for vision, mean-pooled L2-normalized representations for language; earlier work found scores stable when the relative neighborhood size k/N is preserved. Everything runs on a single 80 GB A100.
- Layer-wise result: cross-modal alignment peaks in late layers (0.143, DINOv3 layer 21 vs Qwen3-8B layer 30 on WIT); final layers are never the most aligned; within-modality alignment is much higher (vision-vision peak 0.538; Qwen3-8B vs NV-Embed-v2 about 0.5).
- Dimensionality result: after PCA, cross-modal alignment is largely retained within ~100 dimensions (original language widths 1,536-5,120), so the shared structure is localized in depth and concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace.
- Capability-alignment trend on MMLU (5-shot, vs DINOv3, maximally aligned layers): r = 0.26 on CC3M (p = 0.25), r = 0.46 on WIT (p = 0.03), r = 0.66 on DOCCI (p = 0.001, unchanged under calibrated k-NN), and still r = 0.59 (p = 0.005) with DOCCI compressed to 10% length.
- ARC Challenge (4-shot) correlates even more strongly (r = 0.73, p = 0.0002); GSM8K shows no significant correlation — cross-modal alignment is associated with semantic, world-knowledge capabilities rather than being a universal capability proxy.
- Baseline context: on COCO and DOCCI unimodal pairs reach mutual k-NN above 0.3, beating CLIP and SigLIP 2; Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B scores highest overall, showing that strong multimodal retrieval and high neighborhood agreement are compatible; same-modality pairs sit near 0.5, far above any cross-modal score.
- For VLMs, final joint-embedding representations versus max-across-layer-pairs yield nearly identical scores, and shortening DOCCI captions lowers absolute alignment but preserves the model-pair ordering.
- Code and evaluation artifacts: github.com/ExplainableML/prh-closer-look.

## Details

### Experimental setup

Alignment is measured with mutual k-NN overlap: for each dataset 1,024 image-caption pairs are sampled, the k = 10 nearest images in vision space are compared with the k = 10 nearest captions in language space, using CLS tokens for vision models and mean-pooled, L2-normalized representations for language models. VLM text encoders with 77- or 64-token limits (CLIP, SigLIP 2) are handled by chunking captions and averaging chunk embeddings. Language-model capability comes from the LM Evaluation Harness: five-shot MMLU, four-shot ARC Challenge, five-shot GSM8K exact match. The capability-alignment analysis follows Koepke et al.'s protocol, sampling 1,024 pairs across five runs (the original WIT subset of Huh et al. has only 1,024 samples, hence a single run there).

### Where alignment emerges

Layer-wise heatmaps on WIT show cross-modal alignment concentrated in later layers, peaking at 0.143 between DINOv3 layer 21 and Qwen3-8B layer 30, while final-layer representations never reach the maximum and can be far from it. Vision-vision alignment forms three broad block-diagonal regions above 0.3 with a late-layer peak of 0.538, and the generative-to-embedding language pair (Qwen3-8B vs NV-Embed-v2) holds about 0.5 over many early-to-middle layer pairs. PCA compression shows the cross-modal signal is largely contained in roughly 100 principal dimensions of spaces that are 1,536-5,120 wide, i.e., alignment is localized in depth and low-dimensional in width.

### Does higher alignment indicate stronger performance?

Maximally aligned layers give MMLU correlations of r = 0.26 (CC3M, p = 0.25), r = 0.46 (WIT, p = 0.03), and r = 0.66 (DOCCI, p = 0.001), with the DOCCI result unchanged under calibrated mutual k-NN. Compressing DOCCI captions to 10% of their length keeps the trend (r = 0.59, p = 0.005), ruling out verbosity as the driver: clean, visually informative captions are what expose shared structure. ARC Challenge aligns at r = 0.73 (p = 0.0002), also after calibration; GSM8K does not correlate significantly, so cross-modal alignment should be read as a signature of semantic world knowledge, not of reasoning or capability in general.

### How to interpret alignment magnitudes

Four regimes are compared: randomly initialized models, unimodal vision-language pairs (the object of study), explicitly aligned VLMs (cross-modal reference), and same-modality pairs (upper reference). On COCO and DOCCI, unimodal pairs exceed 0.3 and surpass CLIP and SigLIP 2, but stay below Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B, which posts the highest cross-modal alignment — consistent with its state-of-the-art multimodal retrieval results. Same-modality pairs remain near 0.5, far above every cross-modal score, so unimodal convergence is real but partial. Shortening DOCCI captions reduces absolute scores without changing the ordering; DINOv2/DINOv3 paired with Qwen3-Embedding-8B or NV-Embed-v2 keep beating CLIP and SigLIP 2 at every caption length.

### Limitations

Conclusions are limited to the evaluated models, datasets, and metrics; only cross-modal alignment between unimodal vision and language encoders is studied, and generalization to other modalities is open. Mutual k-NN captures one notion of alignment, and its absolute values depend on hyperparameters, representation extraction, and evaluation data; the authors mitigate this by repeating findings with the calibrated metric and interpreting raw scores against a wide baseline set.
