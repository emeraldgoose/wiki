# VibeVoice-ASR-Streaming Technical Report

Source: Yujie Tu and 13 other authors, arXiv:2609.02812, Sep 2, 2026.

**Lead**: Traditional speaker-attributed ASR splits transcription and speaker diarization into two stages; recent end-to-end models like VibeVoice-ASR unified them but stayed offline, missing the low-latency needs of voice assistants and agents. **VibeVoice-ASR-Streaming** is one of the first LLM-based end-to-end approaches to *streaming* speaker-attributed ASR: it interleaves fixed-size audio chunks, a small lookahead, and previous text so the model emits "who said what" as speech arrives, with no separate diarization stage. The 7B model posts the lowest average WER/CER across five evaluation sets and best or tied-best speaker attribution on **12 of 13** evaluation settings. Both 1.5B and 7B weights plus inference code are released.

## How streaming works here

The design tension is context vs latency: streaming recognizers starve without future context, but waiting for it kills responsiveness. The paper's answer is a three-part interleave — fixed audio chunks for bounded latency, a small lookahead slice for coarticulation and speaker-turn cues, and previously emitted text as linguistic memory. Because attribution and transcription share one LLM, speaker labels benefit from language context (who plausibly says what next) that a standalone diarizer never sees.

## What the results show

Breadth is the claim: five transcription sets led on average WER/CER, and near-sweep (12/13) on attribution settings. A single model covering both 1.5B (edge-friendly) and 7B (accuracy) sizes with released weights and inference code makes this immediately testable — the technical-report format signals engineering artifact over theoretical novelty, which is exactly what integrators want.

## Caveats

This summary is abstract-based: chunk sizes, lookahead duration, emission latency numbers, the five/split evaluation sets, and the one attribution setting it does not win are all in the full report. "Lowest average" can hide per-set regressions — check per-set tables before choosing the 1.5B vs 7B operating point. Real-world streaming adds VAD, endpointing, and network jitter the paper's benchmarks may not include.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Building a voice agent? A unified streaming model removes the ASR→diarization handoff — one less stage to align, time-skew, and operate.
- The chunk/lookahead/history pattern is reusable: bounded chunk + small lookahead + text memory is a template for streaming any audio-LLM feature.
- Evaluate streaming ASR on joint metrics (words *and* speaker labels over time), not WER alone — attribution errors are the user-visible failure.
- Start from the released 1.5B for latency budgets and 7B for accuracy ceilings; measure emission latency on your hardware before committing.
- The 12/13 near-sweep still leaves one weak setting — find which one matches your acoustic conditions before deploying.
