# TPHC: Improving Hyper-Connections with Tensor-Parallel Grouped Updates

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23026)
**Published**: 2026-11-17

## Overview

Hyper-Connections (HC) expand the residual stream of a Transformer into N parallel streams, and manifold-constrained HC (mHC) stabilizes them at scale. But mHC benefits little from more than four streams, partly because each sublayer writes a single output to all streams, limiting how differently they can be updated. TPHC integrates tensor-parallel (TP) shards with HC streams to promote greater independence across streams: a separate mixture of the residual streams is routed to each TP shard, and the individual shard outputs are kept separate during write-back, so each stream receives a different mixture of those outputs.

This makes residual-stream updates more expressive without adding parameters or FLOPs to the attention or FFN sublayers. With native support for stream sharding under TP, TPHC requires only 9% more training time than mHC on an 8B model at four streams; going from four to sixteen streams raises training time by only 19%. On a 1.1B model trained on 100B tokens, TPHC reduces validation loss by 0.7–1.0% and raises average zero-shot accuracy across six commonsense reasoning benchmarks by 0.3–0.7 percentage points relative to mHC at matched stream counts N ∈ {4, 8, 16}. Code: https://github.com/wuyou2002/TPHC.

## Key points

- Main results (1.1B, 100B tokens): at N=4, TPHC4 val loss 2.0819/2.0838 vs mHC4 2.0967/2.0985 (and mHC-TFA4 2.0904/2.0923); at N=8, TPHC8 2.0689/2.0706 vs mHC8 2.0907/2.0924 (mHC-TFA8 2.0841/2.0859); TPHC also leads or matches at N=16. Average six-benchmark zero-shot: TPHC in the 48.9–49.3 range across stream counts.
- Efficiency: only 9% more training time than mHC at four streams (8B model, TP); scaling to sixteen streams costs a further 19%; no added parameters or FLOPs in attention/FFN — overhead lives in the stream routing and sparse-update scheme.
- Method: per-TP-shard read mixtures, separate write-back (each stream gets its own mixture of shard outputs), sparse update with doc-aware scheduling, dedicated initialization, and a TP-native implementation (replicated and sharded stream variants).
- Why mHC saturates: each sublayer writes one output to all streams, so streams cannot diverge in what they receive; TPHC's grouped updates restore per-stream differentiation without touching sublayer compute.
- Ablations cover grouped read/write, read-only, write-only and update variants plus stream diversity analysis (Sec. 4.3–4.4).

## Details

- Setup: 1.1B model trained on 100B tokens; benchmarks HS, OBQA, WinoGrande, ARC-c, ARC-e, PIQA (avg); matched stream counts N ∈ {4, 8, 16}; baselines Llama, mHC and mHC-TFA (a stronger HC baseline from prior work).
- Implementation: batched group computation, functional scatter, initialization details, full-update variant, numerics (Appendix A); update schedule details (Appendix B); training FLOPs/parameter overhead (Appendix C); algorithm (Appendix D).
