# Rethinking in Spikes: Mitigating Hallucinations in MDLMs with Step-Aware Decoding

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.12901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12901) · **Published**: 2026-10-14
**Authors**: Zhongxing Xu, Zhonghua Wang, Zhe Qian, Shiyan Su, Ming Hu, Xiaocheng Zou, Wei Feng, Mingquan Lin, Yuyin Zhou, Yifan Peng, Hamid Rezatofighi, Zongyuan Ge

## Overview

Multimodal diffusion language models (MDLMs) generate text by iteratively denoising a masked sequence, which gives them strong global modeling capabilities but a different failure profile from autoregressive models. The natural expectation is that predictive uncertainty should fall steadily as more tokens are committed and the context grows complete. The paper's analysis confirms the overall decline — average mask entropy decreases across denoising — but finds the decrease is not monotonic: a small number of steps show sharp local entropy increases that break the trend of their temporal window. These are the entropy spikes, and they are where hallucinations live.

The statistical case is specific. A step is flagged as a spike when its stepwise entropy change exceeds the μ + σ bound from its calibration window; roughly 80% of hallucinated tokens fall within this spike region. Spikes concentrate in the early-to-middle stages of the trajectory — about 71% occur in the first half of steps on LLaDA-V and about 62% on Dream-VL — precisely when much of the sequence is still masked and context is limited. A perturbation experiment sharpens the causal picture: replacing low-confidence remasking with random remasking at 10% of randomly chosen steps causes a clear performance drop, while applying the same perturbation only at spike steps changes almost nothing. At spike steps, confidence signals lose their discriminative power, so discrete commitment is weakly grounded.

StepRefine turns the diagnosis into a plug-and-play decoding strategy built on a step-aware switch between two modes. At normal steps the model denoises discretely as usual. At spike steps it activates latent refinement: masked positions are embedded as probability-weighted mixtures of candidate tokens, which implicitly preserves diverse reasoning hypotheses, and multiple refinement rounds let the candidate semantics interact with the surrounding context before any discrete commitment. A refinement controller monitors distributional stability and terminates the loop once the mean KL between successive rounds drops below a threshold, returning the model to standard denoising. Crucially, refinement passes are counted against the total denoising budget, so the forward-pass count — and thus the cost — stays fixed.

## Key points

- Diagnosis: entropy spikes are rare steps whose entropy change exceeds the μ + σ local bound; they carry about 80% of hallucinated tokens and cluster in the early-to-middle decoding stage (71% in the first 50% of steps on LLaDA-V, 62% on Dream-VL).
- Confidence fails at spikes: random remasking applied at 10% random steps clearly degrades three datasets, while the same perturbation at spike steps is negligible — evidence that confidence-based commitment is unreliable exactly at spikes.
- Mechanism: at spike steps, masked positions receive probability-weighted embeddings rather than hard tokens, so competing hypotheses keep interacting with the context across refinement rounds; the refined distribution then enters standard discrete decoding and remasking.
- Bounded cost: if k refinement passes are used at step t, the next iteration begins at index t−1−k, so total forward passes equal the ordinary denoising budget (L = 256, T = 256 in all experiments); the stability threshold ε is 0.01.
- Backbones and benchmarks: four MDLMs (LLaDA-V-8B, LLaDA-O-8B, Dream-VL-7B, LaViDa-L-8B) across general reasoning (VStar, MMVP, MMEval-Pro, VMCBench, RealWorldQA), hallucination (MMHalu, Bingo, POPE), mathematical (MathVision, MathVista), and scientific (MMK12 Physics/Chemistry) categories, against training-free decoding baselines CoTA, ReMix, and WINO.
- Results: on LLaDA-V-8B, +2.8% average general-reasoning accuracy over the baseline, matching or beating the competing decoders on every general-reasoning benchmark, plus +2.5% on MMHalu and +4.4% on Bingo; every backbone gains, with average improvements from +2.3% to +2.8%.
- Robustness: MMHalu gains of +3.7% to +4.5% across three remasking strategies, +1.8% to +4.8% across two generation lengths with three step budgets, and consistent improvement at every block length (largest +4.7% on LLaDA-O-8B at block length 32) — no per-setting tuning.
- Acceleration compatibility: plugged into Fast-dLLM, dLLM-Cache, and dKV-Cache, StepRefine largely maintains their speedups while adding +2.9% on VStar and +1.6% on MMHalu, with throughput preserved or slightly increased.

## Details

### Detecting spikes in a falling-entropy trajectory

The detector computes the mean entropy over masked positions at each denoising step and compares the stepwise change against a calibration window's statistics. The μ + σ rule is deliberately simple: spikes are defined relative to each sequence's own local trend, not an absolute level, so the detector transfers across models and domains without tuning. The token-level scatter shows hallucinated tokens concentrating above the bound, which is what licenses treating spike detection as a hallucination precursor rather than a curiosity of the entropy curve.

### What happens inside a refinement round

Within a spike step, the model runs additional forward passes over soft embeddings derived from the previous round's predicted distributions. As rounds proceed, the plotted per-position distributions sharpen — entropy of the predicted distribution falls — and the prediction converges on the candidate that is finally committed. The qualitative trajectory shows the point of the design: a single greedy commit is least reliable exactly where uncertainty spikes, and feeding the soft mixture back into the model lets low-probability-but-correct candidates recover before the hard decision. The controller's KL criterion prevents the loop from running to the budget ceiling when stability arrives early.

### Where the budget argument matters

Because refinement passes consume the same global budget as denoising steps, StepRefine's accuracy gains do not come from extra compute: the total number of forward passes is identical to the baseline's. This also explains the clean interaction with acceleration methods — cache and feature-reuse schedules apply unchanged to ordinary steps, and refinement simply spends budgeted passes on embedding-space operations. The robustness sweeps at tight step budgets confirm that the gains survive when total compute is constrained.

### Scope and provenance

The claims cover four backbones, four benchmark categories, three training-free decoding baselines, three acceleration stacks, and multiple remasking/length/budget configurations, all training-free and plug-and-play.
