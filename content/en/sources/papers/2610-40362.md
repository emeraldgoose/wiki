# Your LR Schedule May Not Be Principled: Effective Learning Rate Scheduling in Weight-Normalized Training

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40362) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Qifan Yu, Xinyu Ma, Xingyan Bin, Di He

## Overview

Weight-normalized (WN) training is emerging as a compelling alternative to conventional weight decay (WD), with recent studies reporting substantial pretraining loss gains. This work shows those gains are driven primarily by the induced effective learning rate (ELR) trajectory rather than by normalization alone. Matching ELR trajectories largely collapses the training loss dynamics of WN and WD, so schedule design should target ELR rather than the nominal learning rate (LR) alone.

## Key points

- The training gains of weight normalization come from the induced ELR trajectory, not normalization itself.
- ELR is the principled object for learning rate schedule design.
- AIR (Affine Inverse-square-Root decay) combines a theoretically derived 1/sqrt(t) decay with an affine adjustment to satisfy prescribed peak and terminal LRs over a finite horizon.
- Late-stage ELR scheduling is a key source of gains; a shared late schedule attenuates the effect of earlier LR choices.
- Experiments on 128 B-token Mixture-of-Experts pretraining show AIR outperforms cosine and other schedules in training loss, downstream performance, and hyperparameter robustness.
- Applying the same ELR trajectory to WD reproduces the corresponding dynamics, extending benefits beyond fixed-norm training and establishing ELR as the principled scheduling object.

## Details

The analysis demonstrates that schedule design should target ELR rather than nominal LR alone. AIR reduces terminal training loss by approximately 0.01 relative to cosine at matched cumulative LR. Results hold across training loss, downstream performance, and hyperparameter robustness in large-scale MoE pretraining.
