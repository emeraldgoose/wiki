# Your LR Schedule May Not Be Principled: Effective Learning Rate Scheduling in Weight-Normalized Training

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40362) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Qifan Yu

## Overview

Weight-normalized (WN) training is emerging as an alternative to conventional weight decay, with recent studies reporting substantial pretraining loss gains. This paper shows those gains are driven primarily by the induced effective learning rate (ELR) trajectory rather than by normalization alone. Matching ELR trajectories largely collapses the training loss dynamics of WN and WD. Therefore, schedule design should target ELR rather than the nominal learning rate alone. Building on this principle, the paper proposes AIR (Affine Inverse-square-Root decay), which combines a theoretically derived 1/sqrt(t) decay with an affine adjustment to satisfy prescribed peak and terminal learning rates over a finite training horizon. It further identifies late-stage ELR scheduling as a key source of gains, because a shared late schedule attenuates the effect of earlier LR choices. Experiments on 128 B-token Mixture-of-Experts pretraining show AIR outperforms cosine and other candidate schedules in training loss, downstream performance, and hyperparameter robustness, reducing terminal training loss by approximately 0.01 relative to cosine at matched cumulative learning rate. Applying the same ELR trajectory to WD reproduces the corresponding training dynamics, showing benefits extend beyond fixed-norm training and establishing ELR as the principled scheduling object.

## Key points

- WN gains are driven by effective learning rate trajectory, not normalization alone.
- Matching ELR collapses WN and WD training loss dynamics.
- AIR combines 1/sqrt(t) decay with an affine adjustment.
- AIR satisfies prescribed peak and terminal learning rates.
- Late-stage ELR scheduling is a key source of gains.
- AIR reduced terminal training loss by about 0.01 versus cosine.
- The ELR principle extends beyond fixed-norm training.

## Details

The schedule is finite-horizon aware: an affine term enforces both peak and terminal learning rates while the inverse-square-root backbone provides the decay. Reproducing the ELR trajectory in weight-decay training shows the effect is transferable.
