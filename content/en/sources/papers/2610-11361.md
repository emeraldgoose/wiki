# SepGen: Multi-Stem Audio-Video Separation and Generation in a Single Model

**Source**: Dahan et al., arXiv, 2026-10-08

**Lead**: Joint audio-video generators can synthesize synchronized video and soundtrack, but emit the entire scene as a single audio mix. SepGen adds source separation to a pretrained generator, enabling individual stem control—each sound source can be positioned, moved, or replaced in the 4D scene without retraining the generator.

## Background

Recent joint audio-video generators (HaCohen et al., 2026; Low et al., 2025) produce video with synchronized sound, but output a single mixed waveform where individual sources (footsteps, dialogue, ambient noise) are baked together. This prevents post-hoc control: you cannot move a sound source to match a new camera angle, isolate dialogue for editing, or replace background music. Traditional source separation operates on fixed recordings, not generative outputs.

## Method: SepGen

SepGen takes a **frozen pretrained joint audio-video generator** and adds a lightweight **separation head** that decomposes the generated audio mix into individual stems conditioned on the video. The key insight: the generator already knows the video-to-audio mapping; separation just makes the latent sources explicit.

Architecture:
- **Generator** (frozen): Pretrained joint audio-video model (e.g., AudioVideoGen)
- **Separation head** (trained): Transformer-based mask estimator that predicts per-source masks in the latent audio space
- **Training objective**: Reconstruction loss on separated stems + adversarial loss for perceptual quality
- **Inference**: Single forward pass generates video + separated stems simultaneously

No generator retraining, no paired stem annotations needed—the separation head learns from the generator's own outputs using self-supervision.

## Results

On VGGSound and Landscape benchmarks:
- **Separation quality**: SI-SDRi +8.2 dB over mixture, competitive with supervised separators
- **Generation fidelity**: FVD within 2% of the frozen generator (separation head adds negligible degradation)
- **Controllability**: Demo applications—source repositioning, stem replacement, audio-guided video editing—all work in real time

## Relevance to Software Engineers

SepGen demonstrates a **compositional design pattern** for multimodal models: instead of training monolithic models for every capability, attach lightweight heads to frozen foundation models. This enables:
- **Incremental capability addition**: Separation, localization, editing as plug-ins
- **Compute efficiency**: One generator serves multiple downstream tasks
- **Deployment flexibility**: Swap separation heads without regenerating video

The approach generalizes to any joint generative model where the output mixes latent factors that users may want to disentangle (e.g., multi-object video, multi-speaker TTS).
