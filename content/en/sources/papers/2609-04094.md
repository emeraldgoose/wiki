# DRACO: Fine-Grained Credit Assignment with Dynamic Rubrics for Long-Horizon Agent Training

Source: Shubham Gandhi and 3 other authors, arXiv:2609.04094, Sep 3, 2026.

**Lead**: RL from verifiable rewards works when a programmatic checker exists, but most long-horizon agent domains have none — the **outcome-blind** setting with no ground-truth success signal. Multi-criteria rubrics can supply the reward, but scoring them once per trajectory yields a single scalar spread over tens of steps: a poor credit-assignment signal. **DRACO (Distributing Rubric-based Advantage for Credit Optimization)** generates rubrics dynamically during training to track the policy's evolving capability, scores them once per completed trajectory, and **redistributes that judgment over the responsible steps** into differentiated per-step GRPO advantages. The redistribution is closed-form — no trained attribution module.

## What the results show

| Benchmark | Result |
|---|---|
| AppWorld | **+15.9 points over the base model**, +5.3 over GRPO trained with a sparse ground-truth reward — despite using no verifiers at all |
| Tau-Bench (out-of-domain) | **+5.3 over base** without a frontier judge, beating both ground-truth-reward training and other rubric-based settings |

Beating ground-truth-reward training without any verifiers is the striking claim: a dense, well-assigned proxy signal can outperform a sparse perfect one.

## Caveats

This summary is based on the arXiv abstract and metadata; the redistribution formula, rubric-generation procedure, and training budgets were not verified against the full text and are omitted. AppWorld/Tau-Bench point gains are reported deltas under the authors' setup — judge and base-model choices bound how far they generalize.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- For long-horizon agents without checkers, the bottleneck is credit assignment, not reward existence — a trajectory-level score smeared uniformly over steps teaches the wrong lessons.
- Dynamic rubrics that track current capability beat fixed ones: what counts as progress changes as the policy improves.
- Prefer closed-form attribution over learned attribution modules when possible — one less thing to train, debug, and drift.
- Dense proxy > sparse truth is a real possibility in RL; don't assume ground-truth rewards are the ceiling if they are too sparse to learn from.
