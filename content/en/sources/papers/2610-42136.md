# ReLift: Dense 3D Tracking as Lifted Trajectory Repair

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.42136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42136) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Assaf Singer, Maroun Khriesh, Noam Rotstein, Yoni Kasten, Or Litany

## Overview

Dense 3D tracking from monocular video requires establishing correspondences, estimating depth, and inferring trajectories during occlusion. Existing methods typically learn all three jointly, which is demanding given limited 4D supervision.

ReLift leverages already strong pretrained 2D trackers and geometry models. It takes 2D correspondences and depth estimates, lifts them into 3D trajectories, and trains a corrector to repair those lifted trajectories. This avoids tying training inputs to the errors of specific estimators.

## Key points

- Repairs lifted 3D trajectories rather than learning tracking and geometry jointly.
- Uses strong 2D correspondences and pretrained depth/geometry.
- Separates correspondence, geometry, and occlusion inference.
- Trains a corrector on simulated lifts rather than estimator-specific errors.
- Shows provider transfer with multiple trackers and geometry models.
- Targets dense 3D point tracking from monocular video.

## Details

A joint tracker-geometry model must solve correspondence, scene geometry, and occlusion simultaneously. ReLift instead combines existing 2D correspondences with depth and camera estimates, then repairs the resulting 3D path.

The method is designed to transfer across providers without retraining. The paper evaluates the same corrector with different 2D trackers and geometry estimators, indicating robustness to the underlying components.
