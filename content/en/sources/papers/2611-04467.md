# Is Forgetting Unified? Exploring Concept Unlearning in Unified Multimodal Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04467) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Zhongkai Xue, Junkai Chen, Mingcong Lei, Rumo Zhang, Yixiao Liu, Zhimin Sun, Shu Wu, Qiang Liu, Lihua Zhang, Liang Wang

## Overview

Unified multimodal models integrate image generation and visual understanding. Existing single-pathway unlearning methods are often assumed sufficient to suppress a target concept across both pathways, but this paper studies cross-pathway concept unlearning. The goal is to prevent a model from both generating images of a target concept and identifying it in input images while preserving other capabilities. UUBench covers 30 target concepts and 60 semantically related neighboring concepts. Experiments on representative unified multimodal models show that generation-side editors strongly suppress target generation but yield limited forgetting in visual understanding, while understanding-side baselines struggle to achieve substantial forgetting even in their own pathway. Direct composition of these methods also yields limited joint forgetting.

## Key points

- The benchmark covers 30 target concepts and 60 neighboring concepts.
- Single-pathway unlearning is insufficient for joint suppression.
- Generation-side editors suppress generation well but leave understanding largely intact.
- Understanding-side baselines struggle to forget even on their own pathway.
- Composing pathway-specific methods does not achieve strong joint forgetting.
- UniForget improves joint forgetting on two representative unified models.

## Details

UniForget achieves joint forgetting rates of 80.0 percent on BAGEL-7B-MoT and 92.7 percent on Janus-Pro-7B, outperforming representative baselines. The results reveal trade-offs between joint forgetting and capability preservation. Evaluating unlearning over one pathway can therefore overestimate concept suppression in integrated systems, and defense claims should account for generation-understanding overlap explicitly.
