# Learning Representations to Guide Pixel Diffusion Transformers

**Source**: Pan et al., arXiv, 2026-10-09

**Lead**: Visual representations are built for understanding, but what makes one useful for generation is unclear. The authors show that in pixel diffusion transformers, stronger aligned semantics decay during pixel synthesis and specialized pixel decoders merely redistribute modeling without helping. Their fix, Representation Guidance (RG), forms a compact bottleneck representation and explicitly forces the decoder to use it, reaching FID 1.55 on ImageNet 256x256 with a plain Transformer.

## Background

Modern vision encoders transfer well to recognition, so generative models increasingly borrow their features — either as latent spaces or as alignment targets for intermediate layers (e.g., REPA). But generation differs from recognition: it must preserve global semantics and spatial structure all the way down to every pixel.

The paper studies pixel diffusion transformers (pDiTs), which synthesize directly from raw pixels, using the plain-Transformer JiT baseline as a clean testbed. Two findings motivate the method:

- **Better representation quality does not imply better generation.** Aligning a JiT-B layer with DINOv2 improves semantics at that layer, but the gains vanish in later layers as the network specializes toward pixel prediction. REPA-4 speeds early training yet finishes worse than baseline (FID 4.80 vs. 4.56 at 200 epochs); deeper alignment is worse still (REPA-6: 5.38, REPA-8: 8.33).
- **Architectural separation redistributes rather than improves.** Pixel-specialized decoders (PixelDiT, DiP, DeCo) change what the encoder retains — global semantics vs. spatial structure — but bring no consistent generation gain while adding parameters and FLOPs (PixelDiT matches vanilla FID 4.26 vs. 4.17 with 55% more parameters).

## Representation Guidance (RG)

RG keeps the plain Transformer and works on the representation interface with two complementary pieces:

1. **Compact formation via bottleneck.** The encoder output passes through a low-dimensional linear bottleneck ($d = 128 \ll D$), creating an implicit latent space that forces generation-relevant information into a concise representation. The decoder sees the encoder only through this representation (as token-wise adaLN conditioning) and has its own patch embedding plus a few blocks (default 8 encoder / 4 decoder blocks).
2. **Active exploitation via guided target.** During training the representation is randomly dropped (prob. 0.1) to obtain a representation-free prediction from the same network. The offset between the two decoder predictions modifies the flow-matching target:

$$v_{RG} = v + (s-1)\, sg(\Delta v_{rep}), \quad \Delta v_{rep} = v_{\theta}(z_t, t, r) - v_{\theta}(z_t, t, \emptyset)$$

with RG scale $s = 1.4$ ($s = 1$ recovers plain flow matching). The offset is computed with the EMA teacher, applied for $t \in (0.3, 1]$ after a 20-epoch warm-up, and combined with a REPA alignment loss on an intermediate encoder layer.

At convergence this amplifies the representation's contribution to the predicted velocity, sharpening the learned distribution toward samples consistent with it — a training-time analogue of classifier-free guidance, but driven by the model's own learned representation rather than a class label.

## Experiments

**Setup**: ImageNet-1K class-conditional generation at 256x256 and 512x512 (AdamW, batch 1024, logit-normal timesteps, 50-step Heun sampling with CFG); text-to-image variant RG-T2I-L/16 built on MiniT2I blocks with a frozen FLAN-T5-Large encoder, pretrained on CC12M and fine-tuned on a 120K mixture.

**Benchmarks**: FID/IS/precision/recall on ImageNet; GenEval (553 prompts) and DPG-Bench (1,065 prompts) for text-to-image.

**Results**:

| Model | Params | Benchmark | Score |
|-------|--------|-----------|-------|
| RG-XL/16 | 725M | ImageNet 256x256 FID | **1.55** (best pixel-space) |
| RG-L/16 | 459M | ImageNet 256x256 FID | 1.79 (beats 2B JiT-G at 1.82) |
| RG-XL/16 | 725M | ImageNet 512x512 FID | **1.74** (best pixel-space) |
| RG-T2I-L/16 | 912+341M | GenEval / DPG-Bench | **0.89** / **87.1** |

RG-T2I beats larger latent models (SD3 8B: 0.68/84.1, FLUX.1-dev 12B: 0.67/83.8) and its same-recipe MiniT2I baseline (+0.01 GenEval, +1.2 DPG-Bench). PCA visualization confirms RG keeps clear semantic contours in encoder layers while high-frequency detail dominates decoder layers — functional specialization that REPA alone loses.

**Ablations** (B/16, 200 epochs): bottleneck width matters little at 200 epochs but $d = 128$ wins at 600; alignment at layer 3 beats layers 6/8; 8/4 encoder-decoder split is best.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

- **Efficiency pattern**: a 459M RG model beats 2B-parameter baselines — representation shaping can substitute for raw scale in diffusion serving costs.
- **Training-time guidance**: RG adds two gradient-free EMA forward passes during training and zero inference cost, a cheap trick portable to other flow-matching pipelines.
- **Debugging representations**: the PCA layer-wise analysis (semantic encoder vs. high-frequency decoder) is a reusable recipe for checking whether alignment losses actually survive to the output.
- **Plain-architecture scaling**: no custom pixel decoder kernels needed — standard Transformer blocks throughout, which simplifies deployment on existing inference stacks.
