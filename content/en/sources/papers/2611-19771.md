# JEPA4R: Embodied JEPA World Models with 4D Point Planning

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19771)
**Date**: 2026-11-15

## Overview

Joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) world models predict future embeddings rather than pixels and plan in latent space. Two problems remain for robot manipulation: 2D image observations leave the metric 3D geometry that manipulation acts on implicit and entangled with appearance, and what these models imagine stays largely invisible, since they are mostly judged in simulation by short-horizon latent planning.

JEPA4R addresses both. It is a JEPA world model that observes a scene as a point cloud from a single depth camera, predicts and plans inside that point cloud's latent space, and acts through a policy head attached to its frozen latents — a setup the authors call 4D point planning. A decoder attached to the frozen world model can render what it imagines as 4D point clouds over a rollout, making the model's internal predictions inspectable rather than opaque.

The model is trained only in a digital twin, yet JEPA4R and its policy heads transfer zero-shot to a real robot arm equipped with both a parallel gripper and a dexterous hand.

## Key points

- JEPA world model over single-depth-camera point clouds instead of 2D pixels, keeping metric geometry explicit.
- Plans in latent space and acts through a policy head on frozen latents ("4D point planning").
- A decoder on the frozen world model visualizes imagined rollouts as 4D point clouds, with a mean point error within 1.1 cm over 200 steps.
- Under OGBench and CortexBench policy-learning protocols, JEPA4R leads pixel-based JEPA models on OGBench Cube-Single, Cube-Double, Cube-Triple and Scene.
- Ranks first on MetaWorld (98.4%) and Adroit Relocate (88.0%), above the runner-up R3M (96.0% and 65.3% respectively).
- Trained only in a digital twin; runs zero-shot on a real arm with both a gripper and a dexterous hand.

## Details

**Method.** Following LeWM, JEPA4R trains on offline trajectories of observations and actions cut into four-frame clips spaced five environment steps apart; each action block concatenates the five actions between consecutive frames. An encoder with a projector maps each observation to a 192-d latent (the pixel variant uses a ViT-tiny trained from scratch on 224x224 images; the point-cloud encoder is a PointNet variant in the style of DexArt). The predictor is a six-block transformer conditioned on action embeddings via AdaLN-zero, attending causally over at most three past latents. Training minimizes a latent prediction loss plus SIGReg (lambda = 0.09, no stop-gradient or EMA target encoder) to prevent collapse. JEPA4R's default observation is a 1,200-point cloud sampled from one fixed depth camera (treated as a time-of-flight sensor), cropped to a workspace box, voxel-merged at 5 mm, and normalized by a fixed affine map shared with the real arm; each point carries xyz and rgb. Planning uses the cross-entropy method to search action-block sequences whose final predicted latent is closest to a goal latent, but the main evaluation asks how well frozen latents support policy learning rather than short-horizon search.

**Results.** The decoder on the frozen world model shows imagined rollouts as 4D point clouds with mean point error within 1.1 cm over 200 steps. Under the policy-learning protocols of OGBench and CortexBench, JEPA4R leads pixel JEPA models on OGBench Cube-Single, Cube-Double, Cube-Triple and Scene, and ranks first on MetaWorld and Adroit Relocate with 98.4% and 88.0%, above the runner-up R3M at 96.0% and 65.3%. Trained only in a digital twin, JEPA4R and its policy heads run zero-shot on the real arm with both a gripper and a dexterous hand.
