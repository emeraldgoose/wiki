# DiLE: Discrimination-Aware Latent-Space Learning for Universal Multimodal Embeddings

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.25419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25419) · **Published**: 2026-11-19 (v1)
**Authors**: Ben Kang, Junda Wang, Yuxuan Yuan, Hang Li, Yuhan Liu, Xin Chen, Wanting Geng, Tong Lin, Pei Fu, Dong Wang, Jian Luan, Huchuan Lu

## Overview

Multimodal large language models have improved input understanding in universal multimodal embedding models, but that understanding does not guarantee the embeddings preserve the evidence needed to distinguish relevant items from confusable alternatives. DiLE (Discrimination-Aware Latent-Space Learning) addresses this by combining task guidance with on-policy retrieval feedback so the compact latent representation retains discriminative evidence.

The method has three parts. Task-guided Latent Space Learning (TLSL): a few learnable queries, steered by a reasoning instruction, extract task-relevant information from each input into a compact latent space, and auxiliary answer-generation supervision shapes the representations for both task understanding and retrieval. Shared–Private Expert Adaptation (SPEA): shared experts learn cross-task common knowledge while private experts capture task-specific distinctions, covering task-specific discriminative needs across modalities. On-Policy Hard-negative Distillation (OPHD): the student retrieves confusable candidates itself, and a Qwen3-VL-8B reranker teacher provides a fine-grained relevance distribution over them; a reverse-KL objective aligns the student's embedding-based similarity distribution with the teacher's judgments, and candidates are periodically refreshed from updated student snapshots so supervision tracks the student's evolving confusions.

Across four model configurations spanning two backbone generations, DiLE achieves state-of-the-art on MMEB-v2, UVRB, and MRMR. On UVRB's 16 datasets (textual/composed/visual tasks across coarse/fine-grained/long-context domains), DiLE with Qwen3-VL-4B averages 56.2 vs 52.9 for the previous best, and with Qwen3-VL-2B 49.6 vs 44.7 — gains spread across all task, domain, and sub-domain groups.

## Key points

- Motivation: MLLM-based embedding models understand inputs but can lose the evidence that separates relevant items from confusable hard negatives.
- TLSL: a small set of learnable latent queries, guided by a reasoning instruction, pools task-relevant information into a single retrieval vector; auxiliary answer-generation supervision adds an understanding signal.
- SPEA: shared experts encode cross-task knowledge, private experts encode task-specific distinctions — balancing knowledge sharing with per-task discriminative focus.
- OPHD: on-policy hard-negative distillation — the student picks confusable candidates via its own retrieval, a Qwen3-VL-8B reranker gives a fine-grained relevance distribution, and reverse KL aligns the student's similarity distribution to it; candidates refresh from updated student snapshots.
- SOTA on MMEB-v2, UVRB, MRMR across four configurations (two backbone generations, e.g., Qwen3-VL-2B/4B): UVRB average 56.2 (Qwen3-VL-4B) and 49.6 (2B) vs 52.9 and 44.7 for the prior best.
- Unlike ranking-score or margin losses (CPRD-style) or iterative distillation, OPHD aligns the student's full similarity distribution with teacher relevance via reverse KL on student-selected hard negatives.

## Details

DiLE learns a single-vector encoder in two stages (TLSL + SPEA, then OPHD). The retrieval vector comes from pooling the latent queries' hidden states. Prior approaches to hard negatives include ranking scores/margins, CPRD's relative ordering of informative hard negatives, iterative distillation with an updated retriever, and Rabtriever's token-level reverse-KL distillation from a generative reranker; OPHD adapts on-policy distillation to retrieval by letting the student's own retrieval policy determine which negatives are learned from. Evaluation covers MMEB-v2, UVRB (16 datasets across 3 tasks × 3 domains × 3 sub-domains), and MRMR, with results reported per group to show the gains are not confined to one modality or task type.
