# Reducing Representational Interference in Retrieval Embeddings with Trainable Prefix Tokens

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.07298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07298) · **Published**: 2026-11-05
**Authors**: Seongtae Hong, Youngjoon Jang, Jungseob Lee, Chanjun Park, Heuiseok Lim

## Overview

Embedding models compress variable-length text into fixed-size vectors, but compression can entangle semantic content with residual token-level information, including artifacts from attention sink behavior. This work proposes prepending trainable `[DUMP]` tokens to the input during embedding model training.

The method keeps architecture, pooling, and training loss unchanged. The prefix tokens optimize through the standard contrastive retrieval objective and interact with regular tokens during encoding, reducing influence of residual or uninformative token-level signals on the final retrieval representation.

Experiments on standard retrieval and long-context benchmarks show consistent improvements over finetuned baselines across encoder and decoder embedding models. Further analysis indicates `[DUMP]` tokens alter token-level activation and attention patterns associated with low-information tokens, suggesting trainable prefix tokens provide a lightweight way to improve retrieval representations.

## Key points

- Addresses representational interference in fixed-size retrieval embeddings.
- Prepends trainable `[DUMP]` tokens without changing architecture or loss.
- Optimizes prefix tokens through standard contrastive retrieval training.
- Reduces influence of residual and uninformative token-level signals.
- Improves standard retrieval and long-context benchmark results.
- Shows altered activations and attention patterns for low-information tokens.

## Details

The paper is accepted at CIKM 2026 and focuses on compatibility with existing embedding model architectures. It emphasizes lightweight improvement without additional inference changes.
