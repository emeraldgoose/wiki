# CRISP: Cliff-awaRe Input-adaptive Sparse Prefilling with Structural-Mass-Motivated Routing

Source: Huu Huy Nguyen and 6 other authors, arXiv:2609.01925, Sep 1, 2026. Accepted to EMNLP 2026 (Main Conference).

**Lead**: The prefilling phase of long-context LLM inference scales quadratically, and fixed-pattern or offline-profiled sparse attention cannot adapt to input-dependent attention structure. Dynamic routers like FlexPrefill route heads in real time but rely on an indirect Jensen-Shannon Divergence proxy (with its own matmul + KL overhead) and cumulative coverage thresholds that ignore the post-softmax mass hierarchy. CRISP fixes both: a structural proxy **C_struct** that reads routing directly off mass at sink/recency positions, reproducing JSD decisions on 94.0% of Llama heads at zero extra cost, and a **sink-aware threshold** grounded in the noise floor. Across InfiniteBench, RULER, and LongBench on two model families, CRISP is the strongest sparse method overall — matching or beating dense attention on retrieval-heavy benchmarks with up to **+28.0pp recovery** and **5.30x attention speedup at 512k tokens**.

## JSD routing is indirect and expensive

FlexPrefill sends each head down a Vertical-Slash path (concentrated heads) or Pooled-Estimation path (diffuse heads), deciding via JSD between a pooled attention estimate and the per-query mean. That pooled estimate exists only for the comparison — it costs an extra matmul, a second softmax, and two log/reduction passes per head. CRISP observes that concentrated heads in current architectures park their mass at structurally predictable spots (attention sinks + local recency window), so mass at those anchor positions is itself the routing signal. C_struct is a constant-time slice reduction over the proxy attention map already computed for index selection, agreeing with JSD on 94.0% (Llama-3.1-8B) and 88.1% (Qwen2.5-7B) of heads.

## The mass cliff defeats cumulative thresholds

Post-softmax mass forms a rigid hierarchy: architectural sinks (sometimes >90% of mass), task-relevant signal blocks, and near-zero background noise thinning as O(1/n). A cumulative coverage threshold gamma cannot navigate the cliff between signal and noise — it either terminates inside the sink (**sink-only collapse**, selecting zero semantic tokens) or crosses into noise and collects **O(n) background tokens**. Tuning gamma cannot escape: pushing FlexPrefill from 0.95 to 0.97 buys almost no accuracy at severe latency cost. CRISP's sink-aware rule instead sets the threshold at alpha times the mean background mass over non-anchor blocks (alpha=1.0 is a calibration-free default), so the budget emerges from task complexity rather than a coverage target.

## What the results show

CRISP alpha=1.0 matches or exceeds exact dense attention on retrieval-heavy InfiniteBench (Llama 48.7 vs 48.6; Qwen 28.7 vs 24.0) and recovers large retrieval gaps over FlexPrefill:

| Gain over FlexPrefill | Llama | Qwen |
|---|---|---|
| KV retrieval (InfiniteBench) | +17.8pp | — |
| Passkey (InfiniteBench) | 0.0pp | +28.0pp |
| Passage retrieval EN / ZH (LongBench) | +12.50 / +3.16pp | +13.50 / +4.50pp |

Latency scales as the theory predicts: all three CRISP configs overtake FlexPrefill beyond 131k tokens, reaching 5.17x/5.30x/5.40x over FlashAttention at 512k on a single H100. Ablations show the two fixes interact — C_struct routing alone regresses on Llama because it exposes more heads to the broken gamma selection, and only the full combination is stable across architectures.

## Caveats

Evidence spans two 7–8B model families; C_struct exploits attention sinks, so it breaks on sink-suppressing architectures (e.g. gated attention) and the paper is explicit about this. Llama RULER regresses slightly (-0.40pp) since aggregation tasks need broad mid-range coverage the conservative threshold can under-select. Below ~64k tokens sparse prefilling loses to dense (routing overhead dominates), so gate it on context length. This covers prefilling only, not decode-time KV selection.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Serving long-context models? Below ~64k tokens, skip sparse prefilling entirely — routing overhead dominates; above 131k, CRISP-class methods compound their wins.
- A coverage target (top-p style gamma) over a heavy-tailed mass distribution silently collects O(n) noise — threshold against the noise floor instead.
- The cheapest routing signal is often structure you already compute; audit your per-head overhead before adding proxy matmuls.
- Retrieval-heavy vs aggregation-heavy workloads want different operating points (alpha=1.0 for accuracy, 1.25–1.5 for speed) — pick per workload, not per model.
