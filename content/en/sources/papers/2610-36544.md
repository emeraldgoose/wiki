# Monitor Accuracy Isn't the Answer: A Decision-Value Framework for Evaluating AI Agent Restart Monitors

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.36544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36544) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Siddharth Jain, Venkat N. Vedam

## Overview

Classification accuracy and true-positive and false-positive rates alone do not determine whether restarting an AI agent improves success. Using an exact alarm-to-action identity for an independent clean restart with zero monitoring overhead, the paper shows analytically that a monitor with fixed 90% accuracy can swing from a five-percentage-point loss to a 35-point gain in success rate, depending solely on the replacement attempt's residual-budget success probability. It then introduces two techniques for evaluating monitors and progress signals in decision terms. First, a decision-sufficiency criterion, a binary-action specialization of Blackwell's comparison of experiments, identifies which progress representations or context compressions preserve the information needed to choose optimally between restart and continue, independent of trace reconstruction fidelity. Second, a finite-sample, model-free decision audit uses simultaneous binomial intervals over existing agent traces, without additional model calls, to certify a preference for restart or continue under stated sampling assumptions, or to return abstain when evidence is insufficient. In a study of 194 externally evaluated model executions on 19 selected SymPy issues from SWE-bench Verified, with a retrospective audit of 130 traces, the authors report a 2.29-point apparent restart gain traced to a single surviving failed attempt, a 42.29-point loss on a second model, no positive replication of the initial gain on prospective task cohorts, and zero of 103 live comparisons meeting the certification bar. These results motivate evaluating agent monitors through recovery opportunity, decision value, and statistical support rather than classification performance alone.

## Key points

- Fixed classification accuracy can hide large swings in actual success rate: a 90%-accurate monitor ranged from a five-point loss to a 35-point gain depending on the replacement attempt's residual-budget success probability.
- A decision-sufficiency criterion, based on Blackwell's comparison of experiments, identifies which progress or context representations preserve the information needed for the continue-or-restart decision without requiring trace reconstruction.
- A finite-sample, model-free decision audit certifies restart or continue from existing traces using simultaneous binomial intervals, or abstains when evidence is insufficient.
- The empirical audit of 194 executions across 19 SymPy issues showed an apparent 2.29-point gain explained by one surviving failure, a 42.29-point loss on another model, no positive replication, and no certified live comparison out of 103.

## Details

The reported experiments cover 194 externally evaluated model executions on 19 selected SymPy issues from SWE-bench Verified, with a retrospective audit of 130 traces. The paper separates monitor accuracy from recovery opportunity and decision value, arguing that statistical support should be reported alongside classification metrics.
