# The SIMPLE Law for Energy Cost of LLM Inference

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03330) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Hiari Pizzini Cavagna, Andrea Proia, Daniele Cesarini, Zeynep Kiziltan, Andrea Bartolini

## Overview

Generative AI is billed per token, yet tokens are not equally expensive. Each token enters the KV-cache and raises the cost of those that follow, so energy grows non-linearly along a sequence whose tokens look identical. No analytical model states what a token costs, and the most accurate state-of-the-art model is a fit rather than a law because it absorbs architecture into fitted coefficients requiring per-LLM fitting. The paper derives closed-form expressions of the computational and memory-access complexity of LLM inference in terms of architectural hyperparameters, covering MHA and GQA, and compares analytical models of increasing granularity. From these it obtains SIMPLE (Inference Model to Predict LLMs Energy), a four-parameter law separating prefill from decode and decode into KV-cache cost and next-token computation. Over traces of 13 dense LLMs from six families on NVIDIA H100 GPUs, SIMPLE attains R^2 = 0.969 and 11.97% MAPE, 3.1-3.5x below the state of the art, without per-LLM calibration. The law also explains behavior: prefill energy grows quadratically with hidden dimension, aggressive GQA reduces per-token energy up to 3x relative to MHA, reasoning token budgets push output length beyond the energy minimum causing super-linear growth, Intelligence per Joule peaks at an intermediate budget and falls up to 3.14x over a 4.0x reasoning range, and agentic serving raises energy per request 13.0x with prefix caching removing most prefill cost (-93%) but leaving the KV-cache term, so cached agentic requests still cost 4.8x more.

## Key points

- Token cost is not uniform because each token increases KV-cache overhead for later tokens.
- SIMPLE is a four-parameter analytic law separating prefill, decode, KV-cache cost, and next-token computation.
- It covers MHA/GQA and avoids per-LLM calibration while improving error over prior fits.
- Architectural implications: larger models suffer more on long inputs; aggressive GQA cuts per-token energy up to 3x.
- Reasoning budgets can reduce Intelligence per Joule, and agentic serving raises energy 13x even after prefix caching.

## Details

The study evaluates 13 dense LLMs from six families on NVIDIA H100 GPUs. It provides both prefill and decode terms. Limitations and extended traces are described in the linked paper.
