# Centered Ablation: Why Last-Layer Directional Ablation Under-Fires or Collapses, and a Closed-Form Fix

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20148)
**Date**: 2026-11-19

## Overview

Directional ablation — estimate a "behaviour direction" in hidden space from the difference between the mean activations of two prompt classes, then remove each hidden state's component along it at inference time — is the standard, gradient-free way to switch a behaviour such as refusal off in a chat model. Its track record, however, is erratic: on some models the edit barely fires (Llama-3.2-3B still opens 86% of answers to harmful requests with a refusal after ablation), while on others the output collapses into repetition (Gemma-2-9B degenerates into "Here's the truth: Here Here Here…" on half of prompts). This paper explains both failure modes from first principles and proposes a cheap, closed-form repair.

The key insight is that the one site where the edit has an exact closed form — the residual stream after the last decoder block, which feeds only the final normalization and the unembedding — turns ablation into a cosine-gated logit bias. The bias is always the direction's vocabulary footprint, which decides *which* tokens move; the gate, a cosine measured from the origin, decides *how hard* the edit fires. Because last-layer states share a large common mode far from the origin, a direction that overlaps that common mode acquires a "benign floor" in the gate: it fires on ordinary text and under-fires on its intended targets.

Centered Ablation (CA) fixes this by projecting the common mode out of the direction in closed form before applying it. Across eight models, CA raises attack success by 29 and 46 points where the standard recipe under-fires, cuts collapse on Gemma-2-9B from 50% to 1%, and preserves 12 to 40 points more benign answers on high-overlap models — 39 to 46 more than the affine offset of prior work (ACE). The authors also derive two pre-generation numbers that predict which benefit (faithfulness or attack success) an unseen model will see, plus a collapse index that marks when text degenerates, and they show that two standard safety judges score collapsed text in opposite directions, which can fabricate a 46-point effect.

## Key points

- At the last residual stream, directional ablation is *exactly* a logit bias: a fixed vocabulary footprint (the unembedding applied to the direction) scaled by a single scalar gate equal to the cosine between the hidden state and the direction.
- Selectivity lives entirely in the gate — a state is untouched iff its gate is zero; neither the ablation strength nor the footprint protects benign text.
- Last-layer states share a large common mode far from the origin, so a DIM (difference-in-means) direction that overlaps it gets a benign-floor gate: it fires everywhere and under-fires on targets.
- Centered Ablation (CA) removes the common-mode component from the direction in closed form — no retraining, no gradient steps, applicable at inference as a logit processor.
- A collapse index beta compares the footprint's effect on logits against the spread of the model's own logits; beta well below 1 mildly perturbs token ranking, beta around or above 1 lets the footprint dominate and text degenerates.
- Two numbers computed before generation predict, on held-out models, whether ablating that direction yields faithfulness gains or attack-success gains.
- The authors demonstrate that two standard safety judges score collapsed (degenerate) text in opposite directions, which can manufacture an apparent 46-point effect; collapse must be measured, not just judged.

## Details

**Method.** The paper restricts the edit to the residual stream after the last decoder block. There, RMSNorm forgets the length of h and the unembedding (with the final norm gain) is linear, so for a unit direction u and strength s the steered logits are, up to a positive scalar that never changes the argmax, the original logits minus s·sqrt(d)·kappa(h)·f, where kappa(h) = cos(angle(h, u)) is the gate and f = W_U·G·u is the direction's fixed footprint over the vocabulary. Selectivity is therefore a pure function of the gate. Because last-layer hidden states share a large common mode displaced from the origin, the standard difference-in-means direction u_DIM = unit(mu+ − mu−) inherits overlap with that mode, producing a benign floor in the gate (fires on harmless text) and attenuating movement on the target class (under-firing); where the gate is large on benign states, the footprint can dominate the logit spread and the model emits the same few tokens repeatedly (collapse). CA re-centres the direction by projecting the common mode out of it in closed form — the paper also analyzes the alternative route of shifting the origin instead of centring the direction — and gives diagnostics, including the beta collapse index, for when the operator fires, and for what last-layer ablation provably cannot do.

**Results.** Across 8 models, CA raises attack success by 29 and 46 points where the standard refusal-ablation recipe under-fires; on Gemma-2-9B it cuts collapse from 50% to 1%; and on high-overlap models it preserves 12 to 40 points more benign answers, exceeding the affine concept editing (ACE) offset baseline by 39 to 46 points. Two statistics computed before any generation is produced predict, on models not used in the analysis, whether a model will gain faithfulness or gain attack success, and the collapse index identifies degenerate outputs. The paper also reports that two standard safety judges move in opposite directions on collapsed text, which can fake a 46-point improvement — an evaluation-pitfall finding for the safety-editing literature.
