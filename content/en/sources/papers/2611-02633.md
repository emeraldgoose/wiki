# Measuring AI Agents as Teammates with Collaborative Intelligence

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.02633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02633) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Sean Wu, Aneesh Pappu, Jiaru Zou, Yubin Kim, Yuncong Mao, Hongjian Zhou, Junchi Yu, Jieneng Chen, James Zou

## Overview

Progress in AI is often measured by what a single model can do alone, but teams of AI agents are beginning to make breakthroughs in science, medicine, mathematics, and software engineering. As AI works in teams of agents and people, collaboration becomes a core part of intelligence. Drawing on research that has measured collaboration in human teams for decades, the paper introduces the Collaborative Intelligence Benchmark. It contains 240 tasks across 8 dimensions of teamwork, including refusing unsafe behavior and social calibration.

## Key points

- Single-model metrics dominate AI evaluation.
- Agent teams are becoming important in complex domains.
- Collaboration should be measured as intelligence.
- The benchmark is inspired by human team research.
- It includes 240 tasks.
- It spans 8 teamwork dimensions.

## Details

The benchmark moves beyond individual capability by testing whether an agent contributes appropriately to a team. Tasks cover dimensions such as communication, trust, safety, and coordination. Refusing unsafe requests and calibrating to teammates are treated as measurable behaviors. The aim is to assess collaborative intelligence as a first-class capability rather than an emergent side effect.
