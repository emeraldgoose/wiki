# Instruction Capacity: Behavior and Degradation of Language Models under Controlled Load

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.36095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36095) · **Published**: 2026-10-26
**Authors**: Hennadii Kutomanov

## Overview

The quality with which language models follow instructions falls as load grows, yet there is still no quantity expressing a model's limit in units of load with an uncertainty. This work proposes an instrument where load consists of rules replacing single-token words in meaningless text. Each instruction is atomic, rules are independent by construction, and outcomes are determined by a computable reference answer.

Every occurrence becomes a Bernoulli trial with a known position and rule number. The statistical model measures how replacement probability depends on text length, word position, replacement density, rule count, and rule number, then describes it with smooth laws and restores a reference level independent of coding choice.

## Key points

- Defines instruction capacity: the number of rules a model holds at a given share of right replacements, with uncertainty.
- Measurement of GPT-4o mini (2,958 requests, 642,430 trials) gave regular laws for every load factor and answer collapse.
- Instruction capacity about 37-38 rules at 0.95 share of right replacements; about 18-19 rules at full replacement density.
- Code, measurement data, and passport are open; every passport number is recomputed from published points.
- Provides a model passport serving as calibration for a future meter of instruction load in prompts.

## Details

The method turns instruction following into controlled Bernoulli trials to isolate how load degrades performance. By defining capacity in rules with uncertainty, it gives a comparable quantity across models and prompt designs.
