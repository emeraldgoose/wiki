# Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed Points

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.06833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833) · **Published**: 2026-10-05
**Authors**: Benhao Huang, Chufan Shi, Junlin Chen, Shicheng Wen, Zhengzhong Liu, Eric Xing, Xuezhe Ma

## Overview

Every recurrence of a looped language model adds a cost somewhere: activations to store during training, keys and values to cache during decoding, compute to prefill a prompt, and a replay to score each rollout in reinforcement learning. The fixed-point equation z* = F(z*; x) involves only the endpoint, not the path taken to reach it, so once a model's recurrent states are near a fixed point the endpoint can stand in for the whole trajectory. This paper argues that the looped-model literature has been using two consequences of that fact without naming them: truncated backpropagation is what shapes the fixed points that terminal KV sharing depends on, while fixed-depth training with full backpropagation through time does not. Two further shortcuts follow from the same argument: a distilled student can predict the recurrent endpoint during prefill, and rollout states saved at the endpoint can score and update an RL policy without replaying the loops.

Whether fixed points form at all is decided by training, and two components do that shaping. The first is the depth prior, the distribution over training depths. Because tokens in a sequence converge at different depths, the sampled depth decides whether supervision sees the converged prefix context that terminal KV sharing supplies at inference. Training at one fixed depth predicts best at that depth but breaks sharing; Huginn's fixed broad Poisson-log-normal prior supports sharing but dilutes supervision at the target depth more than sharing actually requires. The authors instead learn the prior from prediction feedback, pulling supervision toward depths that predict well, and add an entropy term that keeps it broad enough for sharing. The second component is input injection, which adds the input to the recurrent state at every iteration. Huginn and Parcae leave the state's component along the input unconstrained, so depending on its sign that component amplifies or cancels the injection; the paper's orthogonal injection projects the carried-over state off the input so that every recurrence receives the input at the same strength.

The empirical work spans three scales from 100M to 1.6B parameters. Fixed-depth training turns out to be the worst case for cache sharing: at 1.6B, sharing drops its GSM8K accuracy from 50.6 to 21.2, and it loses over half its GSM8K accuracy at the medium and large scales. With tolerant fixed points the picture reverses: sharing cuts the KV cache 3x at five recurrences, from twelve banks to four, while keeping the entropy-regularized learned-prior models within 0.4 points of their full-cache downstream average. The learned prior lowers validation perplexity by 0.7-1.8% relative to the fixed PLN-5 prior for at most 1.6% more training FLOPs and raises the downstream average at every scale, and the entropy term is essential at the largest scale, where dropping it costs 11.45 points of GSM8K. Orthogonal injection has the lowest validation perplexity and the highest downstream average at every scale against DEQ-QKV, Huginn-Linear and Parcae-Decay, lowering validation perplexity over the strongest baseline by 0.3-1.4%. At 1.6B parameters the learned prior with a 3x smaller KV cache even matches the full-cache downstream average of fixed-depth training.

The remaining costs fall to the two new shortcuts, both of which trade accuracy for speed. Distilled prefill trains a two-block student on a quarter of the teacher's pretraining tokens and is up to 1.79x faster on 8K prompts, trailing the teacher's downstream average by 1.0, 2.3 and 4.5 points at the small, medium and large scales; at matched latency it beats simply stopping the teacher early. Rollout-state reuse halves the scoring and backward time of an RL update, 1.99x on GSM8K and 2.02x on MBPP+, while staying within the run-to-run variation of full backpropagation through time. The authors are explicit about the limits: everything is trained at up to 1.6B parameters with one seed per configuration, and the distilled-prefill gap widens with scale, so the techniques are demonstrated rather than settled.

## Key points

- Fixed points explain the existing shortcuts. Truncated backpropagation alone shapes the fixed points that terminal KV sharing relies on; fixed-depth training with full backpropagation through time does not. At 1.6B parameters, sharing drops that model's GSM8K accuracy from 50.6 to 21.2, and at M and L it loses over half its GSM8K accuracy relative to the full cache.
- Terminal KV sharing pays off with tolerant fixed points. Sharing keeps only each prefix token's terminal KV, cutting the cache 3x at five recurrences (four banks instead of twelve), and keeps the entropy-regularized learned-prior models within 0.4 points of their full-cache downstream average.
- Learned depth prior. With terminal sharing it lowers validation perplexity over the fixed PLN-5 prior by 0.7-1.8% for at most 1.6% more training FLOPs, raises the downstream average at every scale, and is highest at S and L, where it also leads all three generation tasks. The entropy term is essential at L: without it GSM8K falls from 47.92 to 36.47. Its downstream-average lead over a parameter-matched Untied 4 grows from 2.2 points at S to 5.0 at M and 8.9 at L, while its validation perplexity stays 13.6%, 8.9% and 6.3% above Untied 12, whose logical depth it matches with 3x fewer parameters and cache.
- Orthogonal input injection. Against DEQ-QKV, Huginn-Linear and Parcae-Decay, all trained with PLN-5, OrthoInj has the lowest validation perplexity and the highest downstream average at every scale and ends training with the lowest loss; over the strongest baseline, Parcae-Decay, it lowers validation perplexity by 0.3-1.4%. Removing the component of the carryover along the input keeps the injection strength constant across recurrences and keeps the projected carryover contractive.
- Distilled prefill. A student initialized from the teacher's recurrent core and trained on a quarter of its pretraining tokens replaces the loops during prefill. It is up to 1.79x faster on 8K prompts (1.5-1.8x across scales) and trails the teacher's downstream average by 1.0 (S), 2.3 (M) and 4.5 (L) points; at matched latency it is 1-4% faster than teacher prefill stopped at two recurrences yet 0.5-0.9 points higher on the downstream average. Truncating the teacher to three recurrences stays within 1.6 points but saves only 21-29% of latency against 35-44% for the distilled student, and a single recurrence is 12-23% faster than the student while losing 2.7-3.5x as much accuracy.
- Rollout-state reuse in RL. Fine-tuning the 1.6B learned-prior model with Dr. GRPO at R = 6, reuse applies Neumann gradients to saved rollout states instead of replaying the loops. Scoring and backward time per update falls by 1.99x on GSM8K and 2.02x on MBPP+. All four update methods lift GSM8K pass@1 from 40.6% to 60-63% and MBPP+ pass@1 from 30.9% to 37-41%, and the GSM8K gains transfer to MATH500, which RL never trains on, by 3.9-5.4 points. Reuse and the recomputation control agree to whole-model gradient cosines of 0.997-0.999, so their pass@1 gaps (1.6 points on GSM8K, 0.5 on MATH500, 2.3 on MBPP+) measure run-to-run variation; truncating to Neumann-3 keeps GSM8K pass@1 within 0.35 points.
- Convergence is heterogeneous. Tokenwise relative change with a 2% threshold shows tokens converging at different depths, not in causal order, and convergence depth correlates only weakly with position (Spearman correlation 0.14) - which is why the sampled training depth matters for whether supervision sees converged context.

## Details

The models are built from dense Llama Transformer blocks in a Huginn layout: one prelude block, a two-block recurrent core and one coda block, written 1+2xR+1 because R recurrences give a logical depth of 2+2R blocks. Three scales are trained on a weighted multilingual mixture that includes TxT360, tokenized with the Jais64k tokenizer: S at 100M parameters and 21.5B tokens, M at 400M and 85.9B, L at 1.6B and 343.6B. Optimization is AdamW with a warmup-stable-decay schedule, 5% linear warmup then 10% cosine decay to one tenth of the peak learning rate, swept per scale. Two feedforward anchors are used: Untied 4 stacks four distinct blocks to match the looped models' physical depth, parameters and cache size, and Untied 12 stacks twelve distinct blocks to match the logical depth of five recurrences with three times the non-embedding parameters and KV cache. Perplexity is reported on MixVal and WikiText-103; downstream accuracy covers LAMBADA, HellaSwag, PIQA, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, OpenBookQA and SciQ; generation uses GSM8K, DROP and MBPP+. Unless stated otherwise everything is evaluated at R = 5 with terminal KV sharing, which caches four KV banks instead of twelve. Priors are compared with mean depth 5, the learned ones capped at R_max = 64 and started from the fixed PLN-5 prior. The RL study fine-tunes the L learned-prior model with Dr. GRPO at R = 6, the depth with its best GSM8K accuracy: 400 GSM8K training questions evaluated on 500 GSM8K test questions and MATH500, and 272 MBPP+ training tasks evaluated on 100 held-out tasks, with 16 sampled responses per training prompt.

Methodologically the paper is a study of endpoint gradients. Full backpropagation through time differentiates through all R recurrences, so its cost grows with depth; when the recurrent states are near a stable fixed point, implicit differentiation gives the equilibrium sensitivity D* = (I - J*)^-1 B*, whose truncated Neumann series D_b = sum over k < b of J*^k B* has a bias that decays geometrically in b. Implicit gradients of that kind destabilize training when applied from initialization, which is why implicit models warm up with unrolled backpropagation, whereas truncated backpropagation through time differentiates through only the last b recurrences and is the exact gradient of a well-defined objective from any state, recovering the fixed-point gradient as the visited states converge. The learned prior replaces the Poisson-log-normal depth sampler with a categorical distribution p_phi = softmax(phi) over depths, updated with a stop-gradient advantage built from the model's detached cross-entropy, an EMA baseline and a rescaling that keeps the entropy coefficient lambda_H and the mean-depth penalty lambda_m comparable across reward designs; the mean depth stays near five while the entropy term spreads probability mass across depths. Orthogonal injection defines zeta = Q_q (Lambda h) + q with Q_q = I - q q^T / (||q||^2 + epsilon), so for q not equal to zero and with the stabilizer set to zero the component of zeta along q is exactly q at every recurrence, including at a fixed point (a positive stabilizer makes this approximate), while the orthogonal part evolves freely and the projected carryover stays contractive. For distillation, the student replaces the iterated recurrent core during prefill: from the prelude output it predicts the depth-R endpoint, one teacher recurrence and the coda are applied to that prediction, and each attention layer's KV bank is kept. The student is initialized from the teacher's recurrent core and trained with a normalized hidden-state regression term plus a next-token KL term; decoding is unchanged, with the teacher still running at R = 5 with terminal KV sharing. Teacher initialization matters, adding 0.7 (S) and 1.1 (M) downstream points and lifting medium-scale MBPP+ from 2.50 to 6.12, and the KL term lifts a randomly initialized student's medium-scale MBPP+ from 0.25 to 2.50.

The stated scope is deliberately narrow. All models are trained at up to 1.6B parameters with one seed per configuration, so run-to-run variation is handled by comparing gaps against a recomputation control rather than by replication, and the ablations do not exhaust the design space. Larger scales and other configurations are untested, and the distilled-prefill gap to the full teacher grows with scale, which the authors attribute to a fixed distillation budget rather than a fundamental limit. They also flag further work on better distillation objectives and on allocating the distillation token budget, on student architectures such as mixture-of-experts layers, and on whether the distilled student could serve as a standalone model or as a drafter for speculative decoding. The feasibility of these techniques in real-world deployments is left open.
