# Re-Search: Dissecting LLM Ideation Through a Two-Player Questioning Game

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.14487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14487) · **Published**: 2026-11-10
**Authors**: Muhan Gao, Hangxiao Zhu, Xinqi Su, Yu Zhang, Daniel Khashabi, Kuan-Hao Huang

## Overview

Research ideation is iterative — researchers refine ideas step by step against feedback — but existing evaluations of LLM ideation score only the final proposal, obscuring the search process that produced it. That makes it impossible to see how a model explores the space of research ideas, or what limits its progress. Re-Search isolates the information-seeking process by adapting Twenty Questions, the classic cognitive-science paradigm, to scientific ideation: one player (the inquisitor) receives only the research problem of a published paper and must rediscover the paper's core idea by asking up to 100 yes-or-no questions; the other player (the answerer) has read the full paper and answers Yes, No, or Unknown. Every 10 questions the inquisitor writes down its current best guess as an idea card, and a panel of judges scores it against the paper's actual idea, producing a step-by-step recovery curve.

The measurement design is careful where it matters most. The benchmark is built from 200 ICML 2026 papers with three games per paper; six recent LLMs serve as inquisitors (Gemma-4-31B, GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Preview, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5.4 — newer models excluded to avoid knowledge contamination); the answerer is fixed to Gemma-4-31B, chosen for its 94.0% answer accuracy (versus 86.2% and 78.4% for the alternatives) and its low 1.1% rate of flipping Yes and No. Recovery scores come from three judge models averaged per card. Because the game separates what the model knows (its questions) from what it can verify (oracle answers), ideation is measured independently of execution.

The headline pattern is that ideation is decided early. GPT-5.4 leads by a wide margin, followed by Gemma-4-31B and Claude Sonnet 4.5, with the remaining three scoring about two thirds of GPT-5.4. But all models share the shape: most progress happens within the first 30 of 100 questions, after which three models gain little, Gemma and Sonnet climb slowly, and only GPT-5.4 keeps improving consistently. A model's score at question 30 largely predicts its final score — so the paper's real question is why models stall with 70 questions left.

## Key points

- Two-player protocol: inquisitor asks up to T=100 yes-or-no questions using only the problem statement; a fixed answerer (Gemma-4-31B, 94.0% accuracy, 1.1% Yes/No flips) replies from the full paper; an idea card every 10 questions is scored 1–10 by a panel of three judges, averaged into the recovery score.
- Early-decision finding: nearly all progress happens within the first 30 questions; the question-30 score largely predicts the final score, and most models plateau while GPT-5.4 alone keeps climbing.
- Knowledge is not the bottleneck: in a closed-book probe on 100 papers, GPT-5.4 and GPT-OSS-120B each listed 30 candidate ideas; on the 24 papers where both listed the core idea beforehand, GPT-5.4 still scored 2.28 points higher — knowledge closes barely 10% of the gap.
- Question-pool intervention: when each round's question is picked from candidates proposed by four different models (GPT-5.4, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GPT-OSS-120B), all four pickers finish between 5.19 and 5.34 — weak models gain over a point over their solo baseline — and GPT-OSS-120B picks its own questions 30% of the time and GPT-5.4's only 34%, so the gain is not blind copying.
- Both steps matter, unequally: best-of-4 self-sampling lifts GPT-OSS-120B only from 4.16 to 4.57 (below 5.20 with a four-model pool), so the candidates themselves matter; random selection from the four-model pool drops the score to 4.43, so selection matters too; but swapping which model selects barely changes final scores — the gap comes mainly from the candidates each model proposes.
- Hypothesis-space intervention: giving models a survey of candidate directions that includes the paper's core idea lets weaker models zero in within a few questions, with the weaker model gaining more; for GPT-5.4 all four survey conditions end close together.
- Trace analysis: ideation traces show hill-climbing failing in two shapes — over-exploiting one direction (circling in a basin, as DeepSeek-V4-Flash does in the paper's opening example) or over-exploring too many directions — with weaker models locking into an unproductive pattern early.
- Design principle: since models can judge candidates they cannot produce, enriching autoresearch search with externally sourced ideas and questions may beat improving any single model's generation ability.
- Information bound: binary feedback yields at most T·log2 3 bits over the game (with the three-answer alphabet), bounding information gained through questioning conditional on the problem — a formal cap on what more questions alone can buy.

## Details

### What the game isolates

The three capabilities Re-Search demands — proposing candidate ideas, asking informative questions, and hill-climbing along promising directions while abandoning dead ends — are exactly the moves of ideation, stripped of experiment execution. Because the answerer knows the paper and the inquisitor cannot see it, every score change is attributable to information the inquisitor extracted through its questions. The 10-question card cadence turns a single opaque outcome into a recovery curve whose shape (early surge, plateau, basin-circling) is itself the data. The judges see the problem, the card, and the paper's idea, and the mean of three ratings smooths single-judge noise.

### Why the early plateau is a generation failure

The paper rules out the obvious suspects in turn. Knowledge: the closed-book probe shows that even when a weak model already lists the core idea, it underperforms a strong model in the game — knowing is not enough. Question quality: the four-model pool experiment separates proposing from judging, and the result is asymmetric — weak models select nearly as well as strong ones (swapping selectors barely moves scores) but the pool's value vanishes when the weak model also proposes all candidates itself (best-of-4 gives 4.57 versus 5.20). Hypothesis space: with a survey that names the idea, weak models converge quickly, which they cannot do from their own generation. Together the interventions locate the bottleneck in generation — of questions and of hypotheses — rather than judgment.

### Trace shapes and what they suggest

The qualitative analysis of recovery curves and question histories identifies two failure modes. Over-exploitation locks onto one direction and circles a basin, re-asking paraphrases of the same question; over-exploration jumps across unrelated directions without committing, so no basin is ever deepened. Both are visible in the recovery curves as flat lines after question 30, and both are consistent with the generation bottleneck: a model that cannot invent a genuinely new question or hypothesis can neither escape its basin nor deepen one. GPT-5.4's continued climb suggests it retains some ability to generate novel probes, which is why external candidates help weak models far more than it.

### Scope and provenance

The benchmark covers 200 ICML 2026 papers, six inquisitor LLMs, a fixed answerer, three games per paper, and controlled interventions on questions and hypothesis spaces; this document is written from arXiv HTML v1 (submitted 2026-11-10). The authors frame the contribution as benchmark, early-decision finding, and a generation-not-judgment diagnosis with a direct design lever — external candidates — for autoresearch systems.
