# Autoregressive View Model

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.23417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23417) · **Published**: 2026-11-17 (v1; v2 2026-11-20)
**Authors**: Jensen, Zhou, Samuel Rota Bulo, Denis Rozumny, Duncan Zauss, Peter Hedman, Nikhil Keetha, Philip Torr, Christian Rupprecht, Andrea Vedaldi, Peter Kontschieder

## Overview

The goal is to generate a video of a complex 3D scene along a requested virtual camera trajectory from one or more images or videos of the scene: reconstruct observed regions, synthesize newly visible content as the camera moves, and stay consistent with both the inputs and the model's own earlier outputs on revisits. Reconstruction methods such as neural radiance fields and 3D Gaussian splatting fit an explicit 3D-consistent representation to the input views and need dense captures for faithful rendering across viewpoints; video generators can synthesize unseen content but may drift from inputs and earlier outputs as trajectories lengthen. The Autoregressive View Model (AVM) instead generates the scene as a stream of views, with no explicit 3D representation fitted.

## Key points

- Unified in-context interface: AVM conditions on images or videos to generate short clips along a requested camera path; the same interface handles reconstruction, interpolation, exploration, and revisits, with context ranging from a single image to dense image sets and synchronized video streams, supporting camera control, event prompting, and long-horizon revisits in static and dynamic scenes.
- Bounded active context: relevant views are retrieved from the inputs and from generated history (earlier clips' generated views), so the model conditions on a bounded active context rather than the full past.
- Training: a pretrained bidirectional video diffusion transformer is fine-tuned with flow matching in an N-in-1-out formulation; a causal model is then tuned on its own rollouts to improve photorealism, reduce denoising steps, and limit long-horizon drift.
- Scale: training scales to approximately 8M scenes and video clips.
- Evaluation: generation capacity and scene persistence are evaluated across benchmarks for camera control, text control, visual quality, dynamics, sparse and 4D reconstruction, and long-horizon consistency, showing that one in-context interface adapts to diverse input and output settings.

## Details

Section 1 motivates view-stream generation against NeRF/3DGS-style reconstruction (dense captures, fitted 3D representations) and pure video generation (long-horizon drift). The method centers on retrieval-based active context from prefill images/videos and generated history, the N-in-1-out flow-matching fine-tuning of the bidirectional video diffusion backbone, and causal rollout tuning.
