# Disen-Forcing: Disentangling Semantic Anchoring from Motion for Autoregressive Video Diffusion

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.02822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02822) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Zhengyang Yu, Akio Hayakawa, Masato Ishii, Qingtao Yu, Xuesong Li, Takashi Shibuya, Yuki Mitsufuji, Jing Zhang

## Overview

Autoregressive video diffusion models (AR-VDMs) such as Self-Forcing and Causal-Forcing generate video frame by frame and make real-time, long-horizon synthesis practical, but they suffer from exposure bias: each generated frame becomes conditioning for the next, so small errors accumulate and quality and semantic consistency decay once the inference horizon outruns the training horizon. The de facto stabilization heuristic is frame sink — keeping the initial frames pinned in the KV cache as persistent context for every attention head, directly inspired by attention sinks in streaming LLMs. It works: consistency recovers. But it also suppresses temporal dynamics, producing conservative, near-static motion.

This paper takes the frame-sink analogy apart. Analyzing temporal attention patterns in AR-VDMs, the authors find that initial frames do not act as a universal attention attractor the way sink tokens do in LLMs. Instead, semantic anchoring is concentrated in a sparse, stable subset of heads — anchor heads — while the majority of heads, working heads, predominantly capture local temporal dynamics. Masking the top-20% anchor heads during inference causes visible appearance drift even within the 5-second training horizon, while masking the same number of randomly chosen working heads causes none. The anchor/working partition is invariant to prompt and diffusion timestep, already present in the ODE-initialization checkpoint, preserved through distillation, and visible in the bidirectional teacher backbone — a structural property of the pretrained model, not an artifact of the AR pipeline.

The diagnosis turns frame sink from a fix into a pathology. Exposing every head to the pinned initial-frame context — clean references with minimal exposure bias, in a model distilled with a mode-seeking DMD objective — hands the student a shortcut to the low-dynamic, near-static modes of the teacher, since static outputs score higher on consistency metrics and static scenes abound in pretraining data. The proposed Disen-Forcing is a distillation-time plug-in that respects the head specialization: disentangled context routing gives each head type only its functional context, and a motion-disentangling regularization stops the remaining semantic-to-motion leakage through anchor heads.

## Key points

- Diagnosis: initial frames in AR-VDMs anchor long-range consistency through a sparse subset of heads, not a universal attention sink; the same vertical stripes of high initial-frame attention appear across all tested prompts and all four diffusion timesteps.
- Over-conditioning pathology: universal frame-sink exposure combined with the mode-seeking DMD objective drives the student toward near-static modes — at the 5 s training horizon itself, frame sink cuts dynamic degree from 65.00 to 37.00 for only marginal consistency gains.
- Disentangled context routing: anchor heads attend to [initial frames; current frame] and working heads to [local sliding window; current frame], substituting rather than expanding context so both head types keep the same budget; anchor heads are identified once by per-head average initial-frame attention thresholded at the 80th percentile over all 1,003 MovieGenBench prompts.
- Motion-disentangling regularization: using the cross-frame self-attention map as a differentiable motion proxy, a counterfactual pass replaces anchor heads' routed context with the local window, and a KL penalty (λ_reg = 0.002, layer 26) makes the motion proxy invariant to anchor-head intervention — closing the leakage routing alone cannot reach.
- Long-horizon results on Self-Forcing (VBench-Long, 200 MovieGenBench prompts, 30 s and 1 min at 16 fps): dynamic degree rises from frame sink's 35.66/35.83 to 73.12/73.95 while first-to-last imaging drift drops from the base model's 7.23/12.73 to 4.03/4.04.
- The same pattern on Causal-Forcing: dynamic degree 17.75/16.06 (frame sink) → 69.72/69.64, drift 9.38/12.96 (base) → 4.83/5.10 — improving motion and consistency simultaneously, the trade-off sink-based methods cannot balance.
- Ablation: context routing alone lifts dynamic degree from 34.69 to 52.56 while slightly reducing drift (4.40 → 4.08); the anchor threshold is U-shaped (p = 70 over-restricts motion to 48.22, p = 90 raises drift to 4.77), and random or layer-matched head sets perform worse than the identified anchors.
- A VLM-based human-preference judge prefers Disen-Forcing over frame sink across all five dimensions on both base models, guarding against rule-based metrics that favor near-static video.

## Details

### How the head specialization is established

The analysis computes, per head, the average attention mass on the initial frame across prompts, diffusion timesteps, and AR steps. The distribution is bimodal in effect: a small stable set of heads holds large values (anchors), the rest stay local (workers). Three experiments separate structure from artifact. The masking experiment shows causal relevance — anchors carry the appearance reference. The stability analysis shows the same partition across prompts and timesteps, and that it exists before distillation in the ODE-initialization model. And the teacher-backbone check finds a similar partition in the bidirectional model, locating the origin in pretraining rather than in the AR distillation pipeline. The anchor set is therefore computed once per base model and frozen.

### Why universal frame sink collapses motion

Under DMD the student is rewarded for landing on high-probability teacher modes. The sliding window accumulates self-rollout error, but pinned initial frames do not — so a student that can see them from every head can hedge toward the clean, static reference and score better on consistency than by committing to motion. Because pretraining video corpora contain many static scenes, these near-static modes remain teacher-probable, and the mode-seeking objective turns the availability of a clean static reference into an attractor. The over-conditioning is thus an interaction between an architectural choice (shared KV context for all heads) and the distillation objective, not a property of attention sinks per se.

### What routing and regularization each contribute

Routing removes the shortcut for the majority of heads but cannot control what anchor heads do with their privileged context — they can still pull global semantics into motion construction. The regularization targets exactly that residual: the counterfactual pass (anchor context swapped for the window, with VAE re-encoding of the intervention) yields a second motion proxy, and the KL penalty with stop-gradient on the counterfactual side forces the standard pass's motion field not to depend on anchor access. Routing recovers most of the motion (34.69 → 52.56 dynamic degree); regularization closes the remaining gap toward the 73-range results while keeping drift at routed levels.

### Evaluation setup and scope

Training distills Self-Forcing and Causal-Forcing (ODE-initialization checkpoints) with the DMD objective on VidProM, a 5-second horizon of 7 rollout steps of 3 latent frames each; evaluation generates 30-second and 1-minute videos on the 200-prompt MovieGenBench indices published by Rolling-Forcing, scored on six VBench-Long dimensions plus total, with drift measured as the mean absolute imaging-quality gap between first and last segments. The plug-in is distillation-time only: inference architecture, context budget, and the KV-cache layout are unchanged, so the method adds no deployment cost over frame sink.
