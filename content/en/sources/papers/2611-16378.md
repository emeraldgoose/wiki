# Evidence-Governed Adoption of Self-Generated Updates in Autonomous AI Agents

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16378)
**Published**: 2026-11-12

## Overview

EGE (Evidence-Governed Evolution) is a framework for governing the adoption of self-generated agent modifications (prompts, memories, tools, workflows, policies). It addresses why a plausible modification is not necessarily suitable for persistent adoption: candidate selection is adaptive, evaluators may be imperfect, and local gains can hide degradation against an earlier baseline.

## Key points

- **Separation of concerns**: candidate generation is separated from confirmatory adoption; frozen candidates are evaluated on fresh paired audits.
- **Prespecified primary objective**: meaningful improvement required, with declared capabilities protected by reference-relative non-inferiority tests.
- **Governance constraints** apply before bounded canary deployment.
- **Evaluator-calibrated simultaneous lower bound** construction for a countable sequence of adaptively generated candidates: combines candidate-wise Hoeffding bounds with independent calibration samples comparing the operational evaluator with a reference procedure.
- **Reference replacement** formalised through bridge tests; cumulative degradation bound along a versioned reference lineage; audit-planning condition relating effect-size margin, sample sizes, and acceptance probability.
- **Monte Carlo stress test** illustrates calibration under candidate-dependent evaluator optimism; it is a stylised statistical experiment, not an evaluation of a deployed agent.

## Details

The guarantees are conditional on bounded paired scores, fresh sampling, a valid reference-evaluation construct, and declared evaluation distributions. They do not establish universal agent safety or robustness to arbitrary distribution shift. The framework's core insight is that adaptive candidate selection biases naive estimates upward; simultaneous lower bounds with evaluator calibration restore valid thresholding for persistent adoption decisions.
