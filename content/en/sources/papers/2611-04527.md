# Future Representation Distillation for Imagination-free World Action Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04527) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Jaehyeong Jo, Sangwon Jang, Sihyun Yu, Seonghyeon Ye, Yekyung Nah, Hojung Jung, Kimin Lee, Sung Ju Hwang

## Overview

World Action Models use predictive knowledge from pretrained video diffusion models for robot control by forecasting future video. That forecasting is costly, and inaccurate visual predictions can compromise action prediction. DreamFree removes future-video generation by making the video diffusion model's predictive knowledge available through visual representations computed from the current observation.

DreamFree repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model as a predictive visual encoder through representation distillation. The visual encoder learns to predict a frozen video diffusion model's representation using only the current observation. An action module then extracts action-relevant information from that encoder to predict actions.

Across real-world and simulated tasks, DreamFree achieves higher success rates than state-of-the-art VLAs and WAMs while doing real-time closed-loop control at 11Hz with a 14B video diffusion backbone. The method also translates stronger video priors and additional video data into better control and enables learning new tasks from video demonstrations, including those from a different embodiment, without new action annotation.

## Key points

- Eliminates future video prediction from world action models.
- Uses representation distillation to turn a video diffusion model into a predictive visual encoder.
- Predicts a frozen teacher's representation from the current observation only.
- A separate action module extracts action-relevant information.
- Achieves higher success rates than state-of-the-art VLA and WAM baselines.
- Runs real-time closed-loop control at 11Hz with a 14B video diffusion backbone.

## Details

The evaluation shows the method can benefit from stronger video priors and extra video data, and can learn new robot tasks from demonstrations. Code and a project page are provided. The submission dates are 2026-11-03 and 2026-11-04.
