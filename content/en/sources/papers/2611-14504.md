# Emergent Biological Capabilities in a Foundation Model for Molecular Interactions

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.14504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14504) · **Published**: 2026-11-10
**Authors**: Adi Mashiach

## Overview

Foundation models trained self-supervised at scale develop emergent capabilities — tasks beyond their training objective — a principle demonstrated from language to protein structure (ESM-2) and genomes (Evo). Molecular interaction modeling has not joined that list: co-folding models predict 3D coordinates and little else beyond them, affinity prediction is bolted on as a supervised head, and independent benchmarks report substantial memorization with weak generalization to unseen ligands. No interaction model has shown capabilities beyond its explicit training objective without task-specific supervision.

Bonbon is a transformer trained self-supervised on approximately 3.2 trillion tokens of paired protein-ligand sequences — nothing else. No task labels, no structural coordinates, no binding geometries enter training. The scaling regime is deliberately data-heavy: a 1:2,000 parameter-to-token ratio, two orders of magnitude beyond the Chinchilla-optimal ratio for language, chosen because paired interaction data is informationally sparse — the difference between an agonist and an antagonist is distributed across millions of examples rather than concentrated in any sequence. From this single representation space, three zero-shot capabilities appear at hierarchical levels of resolution: functional (mechanism of action, including agonist/antagonist discrimination within one receptor), residue (binding-site localization and orthosteric/allosteric discrimination), and bond (covalent versus non-covalent engagement, with reactive-residue localization). All three generalize to out-of-distribution complexes.

The paper's scaling evidence is its sharpest claim. Six scales from 42M to 1.6B parameters were trained under identical conditions — same corpus sub-sampled to preserve the data mixture, same architecture family, optimizer, and warmup, single run per scale, no early stopping. Pretraining loss falls smoothly and monotonically from 6.94 to 1.44, yet every capability is absent at the five smaller scales and jumps sharply at 1.6B — measured on continuous metrics over non-overlapping protein sets, which answers the standard objection that emergence is an artifact of discontinuous evaluation. Loss-based scaling laws, the paper concludes, do not predict when zero-shot capabilities become available.

## Key points

- Mechanism of action (functional level): a 160-feature parameter-free signature from frozen representations yields 81.1% macro precision across six MoA classes (inhibitor, agonist, antagonist, activator, substrate, product) on a 107,027-pair OOD interaction set (molecules below 0.60 Tanimoto to training data).
- Within-receptor agonist/antagonist discrimination: holding the receptor constant across 28,126 interactions on 222 GPCRs (an ablation of the protein-identity shortcut), Bonbon reaches 85.5% balanced accuracy; no prior method has demonstrated zero-shot MoA prediction across protein families.
- Binding-site localization (residue level): attention weights correlate linearly with ligand proximity; about 25% of heads self-organize for spatial localization, giving F1 = 0.381 (ID) / 0.388 (OOD) across 1,176 co-crystal structures in nine protein families — including GPCRs (0.393 OOD) and nuclear receptors (0.398 OOD), where co-folding methods place ligands unreliably — and F1 = 0.497 on COACH420, beating all three supervised baselines while using sequence alone.
- Orthosteric/allosteric discrimination: AUROC 0.834 across 6,032 OOD complexes (141 OOD proteins) versus 0.589 for Morgan fingerprints; on the harder within-protein task (1,860 OOD complexes with both binding modes) AUROC 0.821, 0.788 on OOD membrane receptors; ablating the 25% site-detection heads drops the score to 0.751 versus 0.816±0.002 for random 25% ablations — a causal cost 4.5x larger.
- Covalent recognition (bond level): a 36-feature signature achieves AUROC 0.838 on 1,212 OOD pairs (237 proteins); among warhead-carrying molecules it separates true covalent binders (0.834) and among warhead-free molecules it still finds them (0.775), meaning the signal is not ligand electrophilicity; per-residue representations distinguish reactive from same-type non-reactive residues (serine 0.902, cysteine 0.817, lysine 0.701), and a two-step pipeline localizes the reactive residue at 71.8% Top-1 versus a 5.8% chance baseline.
- Controlled scaling: loss falls 6.94 → 1.44 smoothly; MoA macro precision sits at 0.567–0.608 across the five sub-1.6B scales then jumps to 0.811; orthosteric/allosteric AUROC 0.602–0.646 then 0.834; covalent AUROC 0.645–0.695 then 0.838; sub-threshold slopes are 0.005–0.012 per tenfold compute versus 0.437–0.780 for the final step.
- Standard benchmarks: state-of-the-art zero-shot binding prediction (MF-PCBA AUPR 0.081, EF0.5% 24.8; LIT-PCBA EF1% 11.4, more than double the previous best; LIT-PCBA-AF3 EF1% 16.6 versus 9.3 for the strongest co-folding baseline) and affinity prediction matching physics-based free energy perturbation (CASP16 r = 0.74, FEP+4 r = 0.78, OpenFE r = 0.69).
- Speed: ~2,500 pairs/s on-the-fly or ~42,500 pairs/s with cached representations on one H100 versus Boltz-2's 0.05 pairs/s — roughly five orders of magnitude; screening DrugBank against the human proteome (1.66B pairs) takes 11 hours versus an estimated ~1,100 GPU-years for Boltz-2.
- Prospective wet-lab validation: de novo compounds under synthetic-accessibility constraints ranked on nine disease targets across seven protein families (all test molecules < 0.60 Tanimoto to training data); up to 24 compounds per target synthesized with dose-response IC50 determination — 61.5% dose-response hit rate, confirmed inhibitors for all nine targets, IC50 down to single-digit nanomolar (JAK1: 3 nM without optimization).
- Novelty under control: 17 of 23 TYK2 hits, 18 of 19 MNK2 hits (0.269–0.492 Tanimoto to any known active), and 12 of 15 HDAC6 hits are structurally novel molecules — the HDAC6 hits lack hydroxamic acid entirely, the functional group present in every FDA-approved HDAC inhibitor but responsible for dose-limiting toxicity; on the two out-of-distribution targets (ELANE, VEGFR2 — no protein above 90% sequence identity in training) it still yields 2 and 10 confirmed inhibitors respectively.

## Details

### Why the data-heavy ratio is the point

Previously reported emergent foundation models converge on parameter-to-token ratios between 1:2 and 1:100. Bonbon's 1:2,000 reflects the sparsity of paired interaction data: interaction-level distinctions are statistical patterns over millions of pairs, not facts carried by single sequences. The allocation was found through internal experimentation across token budgets rather than a fixed-compute IsoFLOP sweep — deliberately, because an IsoFLOP sweep optimizes pretraining loss, which the scaling experiment shows does not predict capability availability. The scaling protocol holds everything but parameter count constant, so the sharp 1.6B jump is attributable to scale rather than data mixture or tuning.

### How the capabilities are measured without task tuning

All three capabilities are read out through parameter-free signatures — feature vectors and signs derived once from frozen representations and applied unchanged across scales and evaluations — which isolates capability emergence from any readout tuning. Each capability is evaluated on a non-overlapping protein set, and the controlled shortcut ablations are a recurring design: within-receptor agonist/antagonist discrimination holds the receptor constant; within-protein orthosteric/allosteric classification holds the protein constant; the warhead-free covalent subset holds ligand electrophilicity out. The head-ablation experiment ties the site-detection heads causally to orthosteric/allosteric discrimination.

### What the wet-lab panel is designed to separate

The nine targets form three progressively harder tests. TYK2, PRMT5, JAK1, and CDK1 have rich structure-activity literatures and test efficient novelty within established chemical space. HDAC6, MNK2, and MMP-9 have narrow inhibitor literatures and test escape from them — the HDAC6 result (12 novel non-hydroxamate inhibitors) directly addresses a two-decade toxicity bottleneck, the MNK2 result (18 novel molecules for a kinase with no approved inhibitor) is the strongest structural novelty in the panel, and the MMP-9 hits avoid the zinc-chelating warheads behind that family's dose-limiting cross-reactivity. ELANE and VEGFR2 are absent from training entirely and test out-of-distribution generalization. Across the panel, all tested compounds sit below 0.60 Tanimoto similarity to training data, so memorization cannot explain the hits.

### Scope and provenance

The claims rest on a single training run per scale (training-seed variability was not measured), continuous-metric evaluations on OOD sets, and prospective experiments whose full dose-response curves are in the appendix. The author positions the result as a paradigm extension — emergent capabilities in multimodal biology — with drug-discovery implications, while noting the loss-scaling insufficiency finding as the methodological takeaway for the field.
