# Amortizing Sequential Experimental Design in Reasoning LLMs

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.20832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20832) · **Published**: 2026-10-14
**Authors**: Anita Kriz, Antonin Berthon, Nicolás Astorga, Mihaela van der Schaar

## Overview

Large language models have extensive pretraining knowledge and strong reasoning capabilities, yet struggle to adaptively gather information in experimental design tasks. This work studies amortizing sequential Bayesian experimental design (BED) into an LLM by explicitly training the reasoning that guides action selection using information-gain rewards. It specifies the experimental generative model independently of the LLM's own beliefs, optimizing expected information gain under a chosen model.

## Key points

- Amortizes sequential BED into LLMs as a learned reasoning skill.
- Uses information-gain rewards to train information-gathering behavior.
- Allows generative model specification independent of LLM beliefs.
- Improves information acquisition and task performance on Twenty Questions.
- Outperforms inference- and training-time BED baselines at lower cost.
- Generalizes to unseen categories and transfers to Medi-Q clinical dialogue.

## Details

Information gathering can be learned as a generalizable reasoning skill, enabling cheaper and more effective sequential experimental design in LLMs.
