# K2-Horizon: A Frontier Fleet of Open Source Language Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.21003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21003) · **Published**: 2026-11-19
**Authors**: K2 Team (Zhengzhong Liu, Haonan Li, Xuezhe Ma, et al.)

## Overview

K2-Horizon is a fleet of six fully open language models, spanning a 0.9B dense model up to a 375B-A23B mixture-of-experts (MoE) flagship. They are built for general-purpose reasoning and agentic work across math and science, software engineering, deep research and office automation. According to the report, the models lead or are competitive with strong open models of their size on agentic and coding benchmarks.

On the modeling side, the team introduces Mixture-of-Value Attention (MoVA), which applies the MoE idea to attention value projections, and softplus attention gating, a more flexible, theoretically motivated gating function. Their 36B-A4B model uses both and nearly matches a 32B dense model trained on the same data while activating only about one-seventh of the parameters. They also present Uno, lossless diffusion adapters that speed up decoding by up to 2.2x without requiring a separate draft model.

The release follows the LLM360 definition of open source for transparency and reproducibility. Alongside the weights, the team publishes the full development history — intermediate checkpoints from every training stage, pre-training data, training logs, and all training, evaluation and infrastructure code — notably the production-scale xLLM framework for dense and MoE training and the RL360 reinforcement-learning stack. They also document the pipelines that built the data, not just the data itself; those pipelines produced roughly 10T synthetic pre-training tokens. Four of the six models share a single 22T-token pre-training corpus and recipe, which the authors use to study scaling behavior and sparsity in a controlled way. All artifacts are available at https://ifm.ai/k2/.

## Key points

- Six fully open models — 0.9B, 3.7B, 7B, 32B dense, 36B-A4B MoE (with MoVA) and 375B-A23B MoE flagship — target general reasoning and agentic workloads.
- Mixture-of-Value Attention (MoVA) applies MoE to attention value projections; softplus attention gating is a more flexible, theoretically motivated gate.
- The 36B-A4B model, using both techniques, nearly matches a 32B dense model trained on the same data with one-seventh the active parameters.
- Uno provides lossless diffusion adapters that speed up decoding by up to 2.2x with no separate draft model; Uno adapters ship for the 7B and 0.9B models.
- Release artifacts: 6 final models, 380 checkpoints, 23 quantized builds, 21.6TB of training data, 58 training runs and 17 code repositories.
- The flagship 375B-A23B was pre-trained on 15.1T tokens in two phases; the dense and MoVA models see 22T tokens, of which roughly 10T are synthetic.
- Mid-training continued on about 1.9T curated tokens, extending context from 32k to 128k and then 512k tokens.
- Post-training trains separate RL experts per domain (search, knowledge work, tool use, scientific reasoning, instruction following with abstention), merges them by weight averaging, then applies a final SFT round.
- Openness follows the LLM360 definition: intermediate checkpoints, data, logs and code (xLLM, RL360) are all released.

## Details

Method. Pre-training used 8k-token sequences of web text, code, mathematics, scientific literature and multilingual data, with roughly 10T synthetic tokens produced by the team's documented pipelines; learning rates followed a scaling rule fitted across model sizes, and the data mix was chosen by benchmark scores predicted at production scale, leading to a web-heavy mix. The flagship was trained on 15.1T tokens in two phases (the second with an updated data mix), while the dense and MoVA models saw 22T tokens; most runs held a constant learning rate, with checkpoint averaging used in place of a decay before mid-training. Mid-training extended the context window from 32k (stage 1) to 128k (stage 2) and 512k (stages 3–4), raising the RoPE base alongside, on about 1.9T carefully curated tokens heavy on long documents, reasoning, coding, instruction following and agent trajectories. Post-training differed per model size; for the flagship, five domain-specific RL experts (search, knowledge work, tool use, scientific reasoning, instruction following with abstention) were trained in sandboxed environments with verifier-scored outcomes, then merged into one model by weight merging, followed by a final SFT round on demonstrations to close remaining gaps such as unfamiliar agent harness formats.

Results. The report states the models lead or are competitive with strong open models of their size on agentic and coding benchmarks, and that the 36B-A4B MoVA model nearly matches a 32B dense model trained on the same data with one-seventh the active parameters. Uno adapters speed up decoding by up to 2.2x without a separate draft model. Four of the six models share the 22T-token corpus and recipe, showing consistent scaling behavior and enabling controlled comparisons of scale and sparsity.
