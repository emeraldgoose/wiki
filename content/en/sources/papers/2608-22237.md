# Read Less, Solve More: Token-Efficient Sparse Reading of External Content for AI Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2608.22237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22237) · **Published**: 2026-08-23

## Overview

Web-facing agents repeatedly access webpages, documents, repositories, and tool responses, yet reading interfaces usually expose entire objects when each decision needs only a bounded evidence slice. This over-reading is expensive and not benign: prior work reports external observations account for 60–80% of the tokens processed in agent trajectories, and paired executions show unmodified agents can consume up to 17.3 times more tokens without improving task quality. Excess content dilutes relevant evidence and can induce erroneous tool use, source confusion, and failures to stop, and whatever is admitted early tends to replay in many later model calls — the paper measures this as replay-weighted exposure, weighting each newly exposed slice by the number of subsequent calls that retain it. Annotation studies confirm how sparse the actual target is: supporting evidence covers a median 3.17% of source tokens per long-document QA question in LooGLE, and about 5.8% for annotated answerable Qasper questions.

Existing fixes each miss part of the problem. Long-context architectures and KV-cache compression reduce the cost of holding context but never decide which source content the model should see; retrieval, including iterative and agentic variants, can fetch passages on demand but provides no artifact-level contract for evolving read intents, explicit source coverage, bounded exposure, and full-view fallback. Two challenges block a general mechanism: information needs evolve as the agent works through subgoals, so each partial read must record its purpose and what remains uncovered; and sparse access is not always economical, since small objects or whole-view requests can make repeated partial reads costlier than direct access — the regime boundary is unknown before reading.

SparseRead is a reusable reading control layer that governs content admission before broad content enters the model context, without modifying the agent model or adding a generative compression model. Its Read Intent Protocol turns broad artifact access into a bounded, stateful process: the agent states per-access needs, the layer returns an EvidencePack with source-anchored evidence and explicit coverage, and the agent keeps the sufficiency and stopping decisions. Adaptive Reader Backends separate reading control from evidence acquisition through a unified Reader interface, combining artifact-specific location or processing methods with exposure forms selected by calibrated evidence reliability and estimated reading costs. The Budgeted Read Gate is an inference-time controller that bounds cumulative raw artifact exposure per episode, checks budgets before delivery, and retains a faithful full-view fallback — bypassing the sparse path entirely for tasks where it does not fit.

Across 30 settings (six models, including Claude Opus 5, times five scenarios), SparseRead reduces total tokens by 15.7–92.9% (median 61.4%) and wall time by 23.0–89.0% (median 53.7%) while preserving or improving task quality in 29 of 30 settings — the only regression is GLM-5.1 on AI for Science. On Web deep-search tasks all six models gain 5–14 percentage points while saving 31.0–43.0% of tokens. A six-method comparison and integrations with three agent frameworks (NanoBot, OpenCode, OpenClaw) support generality and portability.

## Key points

- Three designs do the work: the Read Intent Protocol makes per-access needs and source coverage explicit; Adaptive Reader Backends choose how evidence is located and exposed per artifact type; the Budgeted Read Gate bounds cumulative exposure per episode with a faithful full-view fallback.
- Useful evidence is genuinely sparse — a median 3.17% of source tokens per LooGLE question and about 5.8% on annotated Qasper items — so object-granularity reads are mostly over-read.
- Over-reading compounds: the replay-weighted exposure metric counts each exposed slice once per later call that retains it, and paired high-sparsity runs show full-read agents spend up to 17.3 times more tokens without a score gain.
- Headline results: tokens down 15.7–92.9% (median 61.4%) and wall time down 23.0–89.0% (median 53.7%) across 30 settings, with score preserved or improved in 29; long-context tasks save 76.5–92.9% of tokens and 67.1–89.0% of time.
- Web deep search is the quality-positive case: all six models gain 5–14 percentage points while cutting tokens 31.0–43.0% and time 23.0–37.0%; on AI for Science savings reach 82.7–89.1% of tokens.
- Against baselines in 15 settings, SparseRead preserves or improves score everywhere, uses the fewest tokens in six settings and the least time in nine, and takes both minima for all five models on multi-file diagnosis with score gains of 17.8–34.5 points.
- Baseline failures are informative: RAG Static is faster on all five Web settings yet scores below Naive in nine of 15; RAG Agent loses score in eight settings under equal iteration and timeout limits; ACON loses score in five and raises both costs in six; observation masking loses score in five.
- Portability: across NanoBot, OpenCode, and OpenClaw, all 18 settings cost fewer tokens and less time, with score preserved or improved in 15 and median token reductions of 65.0%, 65.2%, and 30.6%.
- Ablations validate each part: combining session and access intents cuts repeated reads from 4.8 to 1.5 per task (68.8%) and untraceable evidence from 28% to 8%; hybrid evidence location beats BM25 by 5.2 points at the same exposure; pre-delivery budget checks keep cumulative raw exposure within 1.62–1.93 times the full view, versus 2.10–2.33 times overshoot when checks run after delivery.

## Details

- Version note: v1 appeared 2026-08-23 and v2 (2026-11-07) is the current revision — this summary follows v2.
- Three distinct quantities underlie the design: processing scope (what the Reader computes over), model exposure (what enters the context), and evidence coverage (which intent slots have support). Reducing exposure does not require reducing processing scope, so a Reader can scan a whole table while exposing only the computed result — exact whole-object operations remain available.
- Reader location calibration: on 47 LooGLE documents and 741 questions, a hit requires at least 80% annotated evidence coverage; lexical matching reaches 39.5% hits at 780 median characters, BM25 60.1% at 1,497, and the hybrid 65.3% at 1,490, while widening BM25 windows raises exposure to 2,106 characters and lowers the hit rate to 56.7%.
- Gate economics: on DeepSeek-V4-Flash (five tasks, three repeats per cohort), checking budgets before delivery keeps all six cumulative-raw-exposure ratios within 1.62–1.93 times the full view with upgrades in five of 15 runs; checking after delivery lets three upgrades overshoot to 2.10–2.33 times.
- The gate also handles the other regime: for tasks unsuited to sparse reading it bypasses the sparse path and retains native tool use, avoiding protocol overhead — Appendix C quantifies the cost of forcing sparse reading there.
- Model coverage spans six models including Claude Opus 5, GLM-5.1, and DeepSeek-V4-Flash; Opus 5 itself saves 41.0–89.0% of tokens and 35.0–78.1% of time across all five scenarios, and 32.5–58.6% of tokens across the three agent frameworks.
- Integration reuses the protocol, Readers, and Gate through thin adapters with no model retraining; the three framework integrations show three small score decreases of 0.013–0.031 against median token cuts of 65.0%, 65.2%, and 30.6%.
