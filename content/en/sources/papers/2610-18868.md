# Tev: Scaling Long-Horizon Agentic Environments with Dense Progress Rewards

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.18868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18868) · **Published**: 2026-10-13
**Authors**: Wenbo Hu, Shraman Pramanick, Krishna Kumar Singh, Nanyun Peng, Kai-Wei Chang, Yong Jae Lee

## Overview

Long-horizon agents can work autonomously for hours or days on economically valuable tasks, which is a key capability for automated discovery and recursive self-improvement. Open-source models still struggle with these tasks because both suitable training data and a working training recipe are scarce. Long tasks are hard to curate and verify, and the reward signal is usually sparse: an agent may act correctly for many steps before anything can be judged.

Tev addresses both problems. The authors introduce **Tev-Gym**, a long-horizon agent training dataset with verifiable, dense, progressive rewards. It contains 1,170 verified task environments across seven domains.

## Latent-world-first environment construction

Tev-Gym is built from a *latent world* rather than from raw task traces. For each domain, the authors first specify the hidden task structure: the workflow, the expected state after each step, and the conditions for success. An agent-facing environment is then rendered from that specification step by step. Because the underlying world is explicit, the environment can be made progressively harder, and progress toward the goal can be measured rather than guessed.

## Dense rewards with PPRS

The paper turns that progress signal into a training reward through **Progress-Potential Reward Shaping (PPRS)**. Instead of waiting for a terminal success or failure, PPRS converts movement toward the goal into a dense per-step reward. This improves training efficiency and makes tasks trainable that would otherwise provide too little signal for reinforcement learning.

## Results

Training with this recipe produces **Tev-27B**. The authors report substantial gains on the Agents' Last Exam computing and math sciences domains (ALE-CM), reaching the level of GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 at Pass@5. The model also generalizes beyond its ALE-related training data: it outperforms GLM 5.2 and Gemini 3.7 Flash on Terminal-Bench 3.0 and 4.0 subsets, and reaches Claude Opus 4.8-level performance on middle-horizon tasks such as TB 2.1.

## Why it matters

The main contribution is not only a larger agent benchmark but a construction method: define a correct hidden world first, render controlled environments from it, and use progress through that world as a dense learning signal. That recipe makes long-horizon agent training more reproducible and less dependent on sparse end-of-episode rewards.
