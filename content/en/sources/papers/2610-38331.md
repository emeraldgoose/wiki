# Multi-Token Prediction Done Right

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.38331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38331) · **Published**: 2026-10-28
**Authors**: Jayden Teoh

## Overview

Multi-Token Prediction is now a common ingredient in language-model pretraining because it improves downstream performance and enables faster inference through speculative decoding. Standard MTP training has a flaw: a residual component of the MTP gradient interferes with both next-token and multi-token learning, increasingly so with scale. The MTP cross-entropy gradient decomposes exactly into a KL self-distillation term that pulls each MTP head toward the model's own next-token prediction distribution and improves speculative decoding, plus a residual with low signal-to-noise ratio that buries the useful KL signal. The paper introduces the KL gradient-recovery trick: when two distributions share a linear output head, the gradient of their KL divergence can be recovered exactly by combining their cross-entropy gradients through straight-through gradient routing. Unlike minimizing KL directly, this trick requires no vocabulary-sized logit matrices and is compatible with standard fused cross-entropy kernels. This enables Soft Multi-Token Prediction, a one-line code change that removes the noisy residual and backpropagates only the KL self-distillation signal by reusing existing NTP and MTP cross-entropy computations. Soft-MTP supports both DeepSeek- and Meta-style MTP, adds no parameters, and incurs no measurable overhead. From 167M to 1.2B parameters, Soft-MTP improves over standard MTP with lower NTP perplexity, faster speculative decoding, and higher zero-shot accuracy at every scale, lower MTP perplexity from 689M onward, and stronger math and code reasoning after fine-tuning at 1.2B. It also matches or exceeds direct KL divergence minimization on most metrics, making it effectively a free lunch for MTP optimization.

## Key points

- Standard MTP gradients contain a noisy residual that interferes with learning.
- The KL term pulls MTP heads toward the model's next-token distribution.
- The gradient-recovery trick combines cross-entropy gradients without vocabulary-sized logit matrices.
- Soft-MTP is a one-line change with no parameters or measurable overhead.
- It improves NTP perplexity, speculative decoding, and zero-shot accuracy at every tested scale.
- It supports both DeepSeek- and Meta-style MTP.

## Details

The trick relies on shared linear output heads and straight-through gradient routing. Instead of minimizing KL directly, Soft-MTP reuses existing NTP and MTP cross-entropy computations, so it fits standard fused kernels. At 1.2B scale it also improves math and code reasoning after fine-tuning.
