# AllocFlow: End-to-End Portfolio Trading Action Generation from Raw Market Data

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19937)
**Date**: 2026-11-19

## Overview

Real-world portfolio management is the problem of turning noisy market observations into coordinated, simultaneous allocations across many assets while respecting shared capital constraints. Forecasting models and alpha-discovery systems can surface candidate opportunities, but conventional pipelines split signal prediction from portfolio construction, so the learning objective is often misaligned with the allocation and execution goals that actually determine account performance. A forecast that is directionally correct can still lose money if the position is oversized, duplicates another exposure, or is too expensive to change.

AllocFlow addresses this by generating the entire portfolio decision in one shot. It is an end-to-end flow-matching framework that maps raw multi-asset market histories directly to joint portfolio allocations, rather than to per-asset signals that a separate optimizer then reconciles. A cross-asset flow Transformer, conditioned on temporal market representations, produces continuous, signed position weights for all instruments simultaneously, so cross-asset dependencies are modeled inside the generator itself.

Because learning continuous allocation policies from scratch is sample-inefficient and unstable, the authors use a two-stage training scheme. First, strategy-supervised imitation shapes a structured distribution of candidate portfolios from demonstrations. Second, a group-relative reinforcement learning stage refines the joint decisions using portfolio-level financial rewards, with a frozen reference to the policy for stability.

## Key points

- End-to-end generation: bars-to-weights inference with no separate forecasting-then-optimization stage.
- Cross-asset flow Transformer captures dependencies among positions and emits signed weights jointly across all instruments.
- Market context comes from a frozen Kronos financial foundation model used as a temporal representation encoder, not as a price predictor; its own forecasts reportedly lose money after fees, so AllocFlow learns trading decisions in the flow head.
- Two-stage training: strategy-supervised imitation, then group-relative RL with portfolio-level rewards and a frozen policy reference.
- On hourly cryptocurrency perpetual futures, RL refinement lifts the imitation policy from 27.79% to 51.38% annualized net return and from 0.955 to 4.043 Calmar ratio, surpassing the teacher strategy on every reported metric.
- AllocFlow reaches a Calmar ratio of 4.90 on six further months of unseen 2026 data without retraining.

## Details

**Method.** Each asset's normalized OHLCV history (plus derived amount) is tokenized into coarse and fine token IDs and passed through the frozen Kronos encoder; the mean of the last eight steps of each stream yields one coarse and one fine market token per asset. These token pairs are interleaved with a shared market token (2N+1 tokens for N assets) and fed into a DiT-style flow head. Starting from Gaussian noise, a market-conditioned velocity field evolves the action vector over flow time; four Euler steps are used at inference. The decoded weights are projected to remove the common component, enforcing dollar neutrality (weights sum to zero) before execution rounding, with a fixed scaling constant; the model still learns relative directions, sizes, and variable gross exposure. Current holdings enter the trading ledger rather than the policy input, with a closed-loop variant discussed as an extension.

**Results.** Evaluated on hourly cryptocurrency perpetual futures with after-cost trading performance over 2020-2025 (out-of-sample from the dashed marker in the paper's figure), the imitation policy achieves 27.79% annualized net return and a 0.955 Calmar ratio; after group-relative RL refinement these rise to 51.38% and 4.043 respectively, exceeding the teacher strategy on every reported metric. On six additional months of unseen 2026 data, AllocFlow holds a Calmar ratio of 4.90 without retraining. Ablations cover the action head, encoder representation, and training stages; the paper also analyzes when imitation shapes the action space and RL adds the value.
