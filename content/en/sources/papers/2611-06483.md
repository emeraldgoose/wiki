# What Does Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning Really Improve in LLM Agents?

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.06483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06483) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Kejian Zhu, Zhuoran Jin, Shangqing Tu, Tianyi Men, Yupu Hao, Jiachun Li, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao

## Overview

Multi-turn agentic reinforcement learning (RL) excels on long-horizon tasks, but its performance gains remain highly sensitive to interaction constraints. Evaluating across sampling breadth K and interaction depth T via Pass@(K,T), the paper shows that trajectory-level RL advantages depend on sufficient turn budgets, diminishing significantly when budgets tighten. In addition, agentic RL does not uniformly elevate single-step decision quality; rather, its strength concentrates in error recovery and long-horizon resilience. To mitigate deployment risks caused by this budget dependence, the authors provide a preliminary discussion on the benefits and limitations of process supervision signals and introduce BudgetMix RL as an alternative that varies interaction budgets during training. Experiments demonstrate that BudgetMix RL achieves robust performance across diverse execution settings.

## Key points

- Multi-turn agentic RL gains are sensitive to sampling breadth and interaction depth.
- Evaluated with Pass@(K,T), trajectory-level advantages diminish as turn budgets tighten.
- Agentic RL helps error recovery and long-horizon resilience more than single-step decision quality.
- Process supervision can mitigate budget dependence but has limitations.
- BudgetMix RL varies interaction budgets during training and is robust across execution settings.
