# Parsed, Plausible, Wrong: Output Format Controls Silent Errors in Small-Model Semantic Parsing

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17169)
**Published**: 2026-11-12

## Overview

A small fine-tuned parser that emits a well-formed but wrong tree fails silently. The authors ask whether output format — a free design choice — controls how often that happens. On the PIZZA ordering benchmark, sub-billion models (Qwen3.5-0.8B and LFM2.5-350M) are fine-tuned with LoRA on six lossless encodings of the same target tree (44.3 down to 10.9 tokens per order), including matched-length pairs that vary surface form at fixed length.

## Key points

- **Shortening alone never helps**, but writing the target in ordinary words does.
- **Word encoding** is the most accurate format on both models (87.5% vs 85.8% exact match for native s-expression on Qwen) while emitting **4.1x fewer tokens**; cuts the silent error rate **2.3x on Qwen** and **1.8x on LFM2**, separating at every pool size.
- **Error analysis** locates much of the gain in negation.
- **Off-menu input**: all runs learn to refuse (held out: 95.9-99.0% handled) and to apply in-utterance self-corrections.
- **Strict refusal has a price**: 2.1-10.1% of real, mostly polite, orders are refused.

## Details

The study ran 300 runs (5 training pool sizes, 5 seeds) with a hierarchical bootstrap and Holm correction fixed before results were read. Matched-length pairs isolate the effect of surface form from the effect of length. Key takeaway: for small semantic parsers, the cheapest reliability win is choosing a word-level output encoding, not compressing the tree format.
