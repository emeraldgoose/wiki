# UMM-JEPA: Generative Pretraining via Masked Next-Scale Representation Prediction for Unified Multimodal Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.18668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18668) · **Published**: 2026-10-26

## Overview

Unified multimodal large language models can support both visual understanding and generation in one system, yet generative training does not necessarily improve understanding. The authors argue this limitation may come from a mismatch between the representation targeted by generation and the perceptual representation used for understanding. Conventional generation objectives optimize synthesis-oriented spaces such as discrete visual codes, generative VAE latents, or pixel-level targets, while visual understanding relies on semantically organized perceptual states produced by a vision encoder and consumed by the language model. Generative supervision can therefore update shared parameters without directly shaping the perceptual state that understanding later reads.

UMM-JEPA reformulates generation as progressive prediction of continuous visual-understanding representations instead. The model builds a multi-scale hierarchy of representations from coarse to fine, and pixel rendering is decoupled from representation pretraining and attached only afterward. The design is grounded in a factorization analysis (progressive next-scale conditioning) and a masked-prediction analysis showing that masking forces recovery of target states from textual, spatial, and cross-scale context rather than trivial direct observation.

Controlled comparisons indicate that the representation-prediction objective accounts for most of the understanding improvement, while a data-matched control leaves group means nearly unchanged. UMM-JEPA reaches a 16-benchmark visual mean of 51.71, against 46.36 for the understanding-only Qwen-VL baseline, and the text mean also rises. Compared with discrete-code and latent-flow generation objectives, perceptual representation prediction yields stronger and more consistent transfer to understanding. Linear probes and visual-evidence interventions further reveal richer object and spatial structure and stronger use of image-specific information.

The takeaway is that generation can benefit understanding when it directly supervises the perceptual representations that understanding consumes — the objective, not merely the data or the parameter sharing, is what unlocks transfer.

## Key points

- Core idea: replace synthesis-oriented generation targets (discrete codes, VAE latents, pixels) with direct prediction of continuous visual-understanding representations, so the generative objective supervises the same perceptual states that understanding consumes.
- Multi-scale hierarchy: representations are built coarse-to-fine at 64/128/256/512 (4/16/64/256 visual tokens), each parent scale predicting its native children at the next scale, supervised against a frozen teacher.
- Masked next-scale prediction: visual states are randomly masked during training (including fully masked samples) so the model must recover targets from caption and cross-scale context; squared loss on ℓ2-normalized targets is optimized in practice as a cosine representation loss.
- Decoupled rendering: pixel rendering is split from representation pretraining and attached only afterward; the 512-scale enters the sequence only as a prediction target.
- Headline result: 16-benchmark visual mean of 51.71 vs 46.36 for the understanding-only Qwen-VL baseline; the text mean also rises.
- Ablations attribute most of the gain to the representation-prediction objective itself; a data-matched control leaves group means nearly unchanged.
- Perceptual prediction beats discrete-code and latent-flow generation objectives on transfer consistency, and linear probes plus visual-evidence interventions show richer object/spatial structure and stronger image-specific evidence use.
- Understanding advantage reportedly survives instruction tuning (per the paper's evaluation sections).

## Details

Method. Representation targets are frozen SigLIP2 (layer 23) features of the clean ground-truth image at resolutions 64/128/256/512, produced by the frozen teacher applied independently to clean images. Generation factorizes progressively: p(z64|c), p(z128|c,z64), p(z256|c,z64,z128), p(z512|c,z64,z128,z256), where c is the conditioning context (caption plus any visible states under masking). Each parent predicts its four native children at the next scale with a cosine loss against the frozen teacher, with the four scales equally weighted and normalized. Training is teacher-forced: 64/128/256 input spans come from the ground-truth image through the student visual pathway (encoder, merger, masking recipe), never from the model's own prior predictions; the shared backbone contextualizes them under cross-scale causal attention with bidirectional attention within each visual span, and a prediction head maps each parent hidden state to its four children's features. The 4-to-16-token transition is learned conditional prediction, not interpolation of a coarse grid. At inference, predicted states are recursively reused as context (ẑ64 → ẑ128 → ẑ256 → ẑ512) starting from the caption.

Theoretical framing. The paper derives a progressive factorization bound showing each finer scale is best predicted from previously available coarse states, and shows that under squared prediction loss the Bayes-optimal masked predictor is the conditional expectation of masked states given visible states and context — masking removes direct access to the target and requires recovery from available context. Because target representations are ℓ2-normalized, squared Euclidean distance is equivalent to cosine distance up to a constant, which is what is optimized in practice.

Results. UMM-JEPA reaches a 16-benchmark visual mean of 51.71 versus 46.36 for the understanding-only Qwen-VL baseline, and the text mean also improves. Controlled comparisons show the representation-prediction objective accounts for most of the understanding improvement, with a data-matched control leaving group means nearly unchanged. Perceptual representation prediction outperforms discrete-code and latent-flow generation objectives in transfer to understanding. Linear probes and visual-evidence interventions reveal richer object and spatial structure and stronger use of image-specific information, and the understanding advantage is reported to persist after instruction tuning.
