# Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.31670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31670) · **Published**: 2026-10-23
**Authors**: Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan, Kaiyu Hang, Chen Wei

## Overview

Robot foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, motivating low-cost offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. This work asks whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion?

To test this, the authors propose Sensorimotor Alignment (S^2), a training-free offline metric measuring how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion.

## Key points

- Introduces S^2, a training-free offline metric aligning sensory representations with motion geometry.
- S^2 correlates positively with downstream robot task success across model families, acting as a low-cost proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation.
- S^2 can identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action (VLA) models.
- Beyond correlation, S^2 offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs).
- Extends PRH to robotics, giving a low-cost, representation-level view that closed-loop success rates alone cannot reveal.

## Details

Existing proxies for embodied capability are expensive because they require closed-loop evaluation. S^2 operates offline and without training, using alignment to motion geometry as a stand-in for embodiment. Its positive correlation with task success suggests that sensory representations in capable models do converge toward the structure of physical motion.
