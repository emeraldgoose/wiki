# What Makes Good Data for Training Coding Agents? A Systematic Study on SWE-Gym

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.30207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30207) · **Published**: 2026-11-23 (v1)
**Authors**: Yikai Zhang, Mai Xu, Yunbei Zhang, Qirui Jin, Kenan Li, Yuwen Jia, Jinwei Yao, Xiangyu Zhang, Jielun Tan, Jin Pan, Zijian Jin, Xiao Sun

## Overview

Reinforcement learning trains coding agents on software tasks whose hidden tests decide the reward, but task collections typically check those tests only against the reference fix. This paper asks what makes a task good training data, focusing on the correctness of its reward labels, and audits SWE-Gym by executing suspected test errors with two LLM families. The central finding is that unsolved often means mislabelled: 74% of the tasks a 9B base model never solves have at least one test that rejects correct fixes (a false negative), versus 20% of tasks the model sometimes solves. The robust signal is not a pass rate — which shows whether a task yields reward, not whether the reward is right.

The authors build SWE-Gym-Verified by repairing or removing flawed tasks: every assertion is judged and a witness is executed (false negatives), wrong passing patches are confirmed by counterexample and tests hardened (false positives). Of 2,001 audited tasks whose assertions were judged, 1,091 (55%) receive a false-negative verdict; the pattern holds under dual-family confirmation (68% vs 13%) and under human labels (61% vs 21%). Trained at equal size and matched difficulty (Clean and Orig match at ~49% base-model pass rate), policies trained on audited tasks score 2.4 points higher on SWE-bench Verified and 2.7 on SWE-bench Pro over three seeds, whereas simply dropping never-solved tasks (Orig+Filter) shows no such gain. False positives make up 26.1% of distinct passing patches on clean tasks (plus 37.3% undetermined), yet adding tests that catch a quarter of them leaves held-out pass@1 unchanged.

## Key points

- 74% of never-solved SWE-Gym tasks are false-negative flawed (a test rejects correct fixes) vs 20% of sometimes-solved tasks; 55% of 2,001 judged tasks get an FN verdict overall.
- SWE-Gym-Verified releases 1,087 audited tasks, each verdict with executed evidence, plus training sets and the full audit pipeline.
- Clean (audited) training data beats Orig by +2.4 pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified and +2.7 on SWE-bench Pro at equal size/matched difficulty; dropping never-solved tasks alone gives no gain.
- False positives are 26.1% of passing patches on Clean tasks (19–36% at every pass count); hardening against them does not detectably change held-out pass@1.
- Never-solved FN-flawed tasks are 11.1% of Orig's groups but only 0.9% of gradient-carrying groups — they mostly receive reward 0 and waste compute.
- Repairs append a Requirements block stating the missing requirement without revealing the solution, and are accepted only if two frontier models find no leak and the task re-passes the audit (184 of 588 attempts).

## Details

Method (Sec. 3–4): a fix must pass fail-to-pass (F2P) tests without breaking pass-to-pass (P2P) tests; a task whose tests reject every reasonable patch gives all samples reward 0. False negatives are confirmed by execution witnesses from two LLM families (in 810 confirmed verdicts both families fail the same test); false positives by counterexamples, then hardening keeps only tests that pass with the gold patch and every non-FP patch (493 tests on 143 tasks). Experiments (Sec. 5): GRPO runs of 80 steps × 16 tasks × 8 samples over three seeds; evaluated on held-out Verified-397 and SWE-bench Pro sharing no repository with training; checkpoints selected on a 100-task dev split. RQ2 traces how label errors reach the training signal via 3,840 groups of eight samples; RQ4 shows hardening leaves scores unchanged. The release (Appendix A) includes audited tasks, verdict evidence, training sets, and pipeline code.
