# GigaSpeech 3: Scaling Multi-Dialect Arabic Speech Recognition with Weakly Labeled Data

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.02476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02476) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Junzhe Liu, Yujie Tu, Yifan Yang, Peng Wang, Haoran Wang, Guodong Lin, Leyan Liao, Chengquan Zhang, Zhifu Gao, Xiangang Li, Qinyuan Cheng, Xipeng Qiu, Wei-Qiang Zhang, Guoguo Chen, Kai Yu, Xie Chen

## Overview

Dialectal Arabic speech recognition remains constrained by the scarcity of dialect-resolved transcribed speech despite the abundance of web audio. GigaSpeech 3 introduces a corpus of 39,000 hours and 10 million weakly transcribed segments covering Algerian, Egyptian, Iraqi, Saudi, and Syrian Arabic. Starting from 60 hours of manual transcription per dialect, the authors train labeling models using speech-recognition-biased self-supervised learning and apply those models to dialect-matched web audio. Data selection and four rounds of noisy-student refinement for Saudi and Syrian Arabic yield the final corpus. Validation covers dialect-specific and multi-dialect recognition training and cross-domain evaluation.

## Key points

- The corpus covers five Arabic dialects rather than only Modern Standard Arabic.
- It combines 39,000 hours of audio with 10 million weakly transcribed segments.
- Labeling starts from only 60 hours of manual transcription per dialect.
- Noisy-student refinement is applied for Saudi and Syrian Arabic.
- Dialect-specific models outperform their labeling models on all five dialects.
- A multi-dialect model also supports Modern Standard Arabic using self-predicted dialect identities.

## Details

On the authors' benchmark, final dialect-specific models reach a macro-average word error rate of 23.86 percent, a 16.6 percent relative reduction from the strongest third-party system covering all five dialects. The multi-dialect model reaches 25.51 percent macro-average word error rate. Across eight conditions from three external corpora, a GigaSpeech 3-trained Zipformer achieves the lowest word error rate among six evaluated models in every condition, and adapting a Qwen3 speech recognizer on the corpus improves performance across all eight conditions. The corpus and recipes are released for further dialect-aware Arabic speech processing.
