# Unlocking Any-Order Generation in Pretrained Autoregressive Image Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.39293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39293) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Rishav Pramanik

## Overview

Autoregressive image generators are powerful but inflexible: training with a fixed raster-scan order hardcodes a single factorization of the image distribution into the model, leaving it unable to inpaint, outpaint, or edit at inference time. Existing solutions such as modern discrete-diffusion models train from scratch on a mixture of token orderings, costing roughly three times standard raster-scan training and sacrificing compatibility with the AR inference ecosystem including KV caching and vLLM. The paper argues this cost is unnecessary because a pretrained raster-scan AR model has already learned the joint distribution over image tokens; what it lacks is not knowledge but the ability to express that knowledge through non-sequential conditional pathways. The proposed recipe adapts pretrained raster-scan models into any-order generators with two components: future-aware positional embeddings that resolve a target-position identifiability problem at negligible overhead, and an ordering curriculum that traces a stable optimization path from the pretrained solution to a good any-order basin. Applied to four base models across two architectures and two scales (LlamaGen-L/XL, RAR-L/XL), the recipe substantially outperformed MaskGIT on ImageNet inpainting (FID 6.21 versus 10.44) and outpainting, was competitive with MAR-L, traded a 1.2-1.9 increase in class-conditional FID for this flexibility, and cost only 13-33% additional compute beyond pretraining while leaving the standard AR inference stack untouched.

## Key points

- Fixed raster-scan training prevents inpainting, outpainting, and editing at inference.
- Discrete-diffusion alternatives cost about 3x pretraining and break AR inference compatibility.
- The proposed recipe adapts existing pretrained AR models instead of retraining.
- Future-aware positional embeddings resolve target-position identifiability at negligible overhead.
- An ordering curriculum stabilizes optimization into an any-order basin.
- The recipe outperformed MaskGIT on ImageNet inpainting (FID 6.21 vs. 10.44).
- Extra compute is 13-33% beyond pretraining, and AR inference compatibility remains.

## Details

The key premise is that pretrained AR models already encode the joint distribution; only expression pathways are missing. Future-aware embeddings make target positions identifiable, while the curriculum provides a stable path from raster-scan pretraining to any-order capability.
