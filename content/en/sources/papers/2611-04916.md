# Behind Video Generation: Observations and Insights on Multimodal Understanding from Kling

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04916) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Kling Team (many contributors)

## Overview

Video generation has progressed from isolated clips to coherent short-form audiovisual sequences, and the bottleneck is shifting from rendering to semantic control. This report summarizes observations and insights on multimodal understanding gained from successive generations of Kling. It argues that generation quality is the projection of multimodal understanding through the entire data, conditioning, alignment, and evaluation chain, and examines this chain module by module. Kling-MLLM is a shared multimodal foundation model unifying image, video, audio, synchronized audio-video, and interleaved inputs, specialized for each downstream module.

## Key points

- Video generation quality depends on multimodal understanding across the whole chain.
- Captioning is treated as a semantic contract maximizing description density.
- Reference-grounded captioning makes reference bindings explicit.
- Prompt enhancement translates user intent into the caption distribution.
- A reliable text encoder comes from perceptual training rather than stronger language ability.
- Reward modeling pairs general critiques with fast multidimensional scoring.

## Details

The report describes captioning extensions, prompt-enhancement training, encoder specialization, reward-model design, and task-specific automatic evaluation. Basic prompt enhancement is derived from captions of compelling videos and refined with text-level and generator-in-the-loop reinforcement learning; agentic prompt enhancement refines story and retrieves compelling examples. Evaluation protocols are highlighted that expose scoring shortcuts and trace failures upstream. The lessons offer guidance rather than a self-contained training recipe, and practical use should account for the underlying products and data pipelines.
