# VideoMemBench: How Well Do MLLMs Remember in Video Conversations?

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40131) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Zhengdong Hu, Chao Wang, Kaituo Feng, Huanjin Yao, Mingfei Lu, Hehe Fan, Yi Yang

## Overview

Multimodal large language models can now process hour-long videos, making extended multi-turn dialogue over a single video a natural mode of use. Sustaining such a dialogue requires more than answering each question in isolation: the model must retrieve and reuse information established earlier in the conversation, both what it has seen and what it has said, an ability termed in-context video memory. Existing video QA benchmarks leave this ability unmeasured because their questions are mutually independent, never reference one another, and each is answerable from the video alone, so nothing requires carrying information across turns. VideoMemBench addresses this by pairing each sample with a single video and a long multi-turn dialogue whose turns are connected by typed cross-turn dependencies. An episodic dependency refers back to a visual episode described in an earlier turn and probes memory of what the model has seen, while an answer dependency builds on the model's own earlier answer and probes memory of what the model has said. Every dependency is verified for necessity, meaning the question cannot be answered without its earlier turns, and non-leakage, meaning the question does not restate the information from those turns. Construction uses three LLM roles, a planner, a generator, and a judger, with human expert review. VideoMemBench comprises 326 videos with 2,476 turns, of which 1,387 depend on earlier turns, including multi-parent and long-range callbacks. Among over 20 evaluated open- and closed-source MLLMs, Claude Opus 5 performed best with an overall score of only 45.7%, highlighting the challenge of in-context video memory.

## Key points

- In-context video memory means reusing both perceived video content and the model's own prior answers across turns.
- Existing video QA benchmarks do not measure memory because their questions are independent and answerable from the video alone.
- VideoMemBench uses typed cross-turn dependencies: episodic (memory of what was seen) and answer (memory of what was said).
- Each dependency is checked for necessity and non-leakage, built by planner/generator/judger LLM roles and human review.
- The benchmark has 326 videos, 2,476 turns, and 1,387 dependent turns, including multi-parent and long-range callbacks; best model achieved only 45.7%.

## Details

The work releases both dataset and evaluation. Results show that carrying information across turns remains difficult even for strong multimodal models. Complete construction details, baseline setup, and metric definitions are in the linked source.
