# On the Generalization Limitation of On-Policy Distillation for Post-RL Consolidation

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05609) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Yichen Wang, Yufa Zhou, Siqi Zhu, Haozheng Luo, Jingzhi Bao, Zhichao Liu, Zijun Wei

## Overview

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) extends on-policy distillation (OPD) to consolidate capabilities from multiple teachers. Although prior work reports near-perfect in-distribution (IND) distillation performance, the paper finds that this can mask incomplete capability consolidation beyond the supervised distribution. A preliminary synthetic reasoning study shows that OPD does not fully transfer teachers' generalization capabilities. The authors then scale the investigation to real-world tasks, evaluating extrapolative and compositional generalization across mathematical reasoning, coding, agent, and long-context capabilities with Qwen3 models. Across extrapolative settings, OPD and MOPD reach 96.04-102.73% of their teachers' IND scores. Yet their out-of-distribution (OOD) scores remain 2.45-16.25% below the base model's. Across three compositional settings, MOPD improves IND performance over the base model, but its performance on composed tasks as OOD remains 18.19-24.34% below that of the best teachers. To understand these gaps, the authors examine teacher capability and data coverage as two potential bottlenecks. Jointly alleviating both eliminates OOD forgetting in their experiments and suggests potential for generalization beyond the teacher. Relative to RL baselines, (M)OPD reduces OOD forgetting on average and exhibits smaller, more spectrally concentrated updates, plus greater shared update structure under MOPD. Motivated by these findings, they propose geometry-based patching and base-preserving replay to improve OOD performance while largely preserving IND performance. The work identifies a blind spot in capability consolidation via (M)OPD and highlights the need for algorithms that explicitly support OOD retention and generalization.

## Key points

- (M)OPD can show near-perfect IND scores while failing to consolidate capabilities beyond the supervised distribution.
- Extrapolative settings reach 96.04-102.73% of teacher IND scores but OOD remains 2.45-16.25% below the base model.
- In three compositional settings, MOPD improves IND but composed-task OOD stays 18.19-24.34% below best teachers.
- Teacher capability and data coverage act as bottlenecks; alleviating both eliminates OOD forgetting.
- Geometry-based patching and base-preserving replay aim to recover OOD performance with little IND loss.

## Details

Experiments use Qwen3 models across math, coding, agent, and long-context capabilities. Comparisons include RL baselines and update-geometry analysis. Full diagnostics and proposed method details are in the linked source.
