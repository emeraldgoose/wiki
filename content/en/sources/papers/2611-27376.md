# Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation of RL Experts: Toward Continual Post-Training

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.27376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27376) · **Published**: 2026-11-20 (v1; v2 2026-11-24)
**Authors**: Zhen Zhang, Changyi Yang, Xuehai He, Chengzhi Liu, Yutong Yin, Ziyue Liu, Zhuoran Yin, Yangmin Li, Zhihan Liu, Jisen Li, Mingyu Jin, Chuhan Li, Zhaotian Weng, Ruoyu He, Wenyao Gao, Jiaxin Deng, Chenwei Wu, Hui Shen, Wenhao Chai, Chenyang Zhao, Zhuofan Ying, Kieran Liu, Andrew Chen, Pony Ma, Zhaoran Wang, Alkesh Patel, Zhe Gan, Xin Eric Wang

## Overview

RL post-training is usually done one domain at a time, producing domain experts that must then be consolidated into a single model — and as new experts keep arriving, that consolidation becomes continual. Weight merging and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) must re-consolidate the whole model whenever an expert is added, apply one fixed mixture to every request, and can erode capabilities outside the target domains. The paper proposes Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation (CSPD): keep the pre-RL model frozen, distill each RL expert independently into a soft prompt, and compose experts by simply concatenating their prompts at inference, with no joint training and no weight updates.

The method has two parts. Each expert is distilled into a 256-vector soft prompt on its frozen pre-RL checkpoint with prefix-robust training (Muon optimizer, 200 updates), so prompts survive being concatenated in different orders. Composition then attaches all prompts at once (e.g. math-code-science-instruction order "MCSI"), and experts can be added or removed by prepending or deleting a prompt while the backbone and all other modules stay fixed.

## Key points

- Single-expert distillation: across 23 public pre-RL/post-RL pairs spanning math, LiveCodeBench code, SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1 agents, with 7B–35B dense and MoE models, the prompted student recovers at least 90% of the teacher's gain in 22 of 23 settings and matches or exceeds the teacher in 15; e.g. DeepSWE's student goes 24.90% → 45.65% on SWE-bench Verified vs 44.05% for its teacher. Lowest recovery is 88.08% for MiMo-7B on code, distilled without the expert's original training data.
- Prompt length and supervision: 32 vectors already reach 90.0% recovery vs 93.1% for 256 vectors on GPT-OSS math; training on responses only 69% of which are correct still recovers 90.5% vs 96.1% for correct-only responses, so verifier filtering is not strictly required.
- Composition: on the M2RL four-domain setup (AIME24 / LiveCodeBench v5 / GPQA-Diamond / IFBench, student = M2RL-SFT on Qwen3-4B-Base), concatenated CSPD prompts raise the average from 50.41 to 67.83 (107.5% recovery), matching MOPD (67.79) and beating expert TA merge (67.27), TIES (67.47), LoRA-distill+TA (65.50) and the oracle-routed average of individual experts (66.61); same prompts used single-domain average 65.38.
- Continual addition: with experts arriving as instruction→science→code→math, the average rises with every addition (50.41 → 60.99 → 61.67 → 63.67 → 67.83) without retraining any existing module; independent prompt training beats progressive training at every stage (67.83 vs 65.17) and progressive training erodes the earliest capability (IFBench down to 52.71/50.88 mid-sequence).
- Module interactions: removing the math prompt causes the largest average drop (−4.16 points, including −6.54 on GPQA-Diamond); removing the instruction-following prompt costs −12.88 on IFBench; negative cross-domain interactions exist (adding code lowers IFBench 66.71 → 64.58), but the overall average still rises with every addition.
- Out-of-domain generalization: on six held-out benchmarks (XSTest, TruthfulQA, BFCL, TriviaQA, BBEH, FinQA) CSPD achieves the best mean 56.6, leading on four of six (XSTest 88.2, BFCL 55.6, BBEH 25.8, FinQA 72.5) vs MOPD 53.5 and TIES 54.1.
- Serving: input-space adaptation keeps throughput within 1.5% of the unadapted backbone and reaches 2.40× that of rank-4 LoRA adapters.

## Details

CSPD works because composition happens in input space: prompts are trained independently with prefix-robust training so that concatenation order (MCSI vs reverse ISCM) matters little, and the frozen backbone guarantees that capabilities outside the experts' domains are untouched. Ablations in the appendices cover correct-only vs mixed supervision, prompt length sweeps, optimizer and concatenation order, task-arithmetic coefficient sweeps for the TA/TIES baselines, and out-of-domain evaluation settings. Code is released at https://github.com/UCSB-AI/CSPD.
