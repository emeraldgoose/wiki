# SteerDistill: Quantizing LLM Agents via Closed-Loop Teacher Steering

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.13410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13410) · **Published**: 2026-11-10

## Overview

Low-bit quantization cuts the inference cost of LLM agents, but quantized agents fail tasks in a characteristic way. Examining failed rollouts, the authors find that quantized students overlook task requirements or environmental conditions, so their environment transitions drift away from successful full-precision references — a mismatch the paper calls behavioral divergence. Typical failures are mundane but fatal: an agent tries to read a file before entering its directory even though the reading tool only reaches the current directory, or creates the requested files but leaves them empty.

Fixing these divergences is hard with existing recipes. Offline distillation data is collected independently of the student's interactions, so it misses the exact contexts where the student fails, while the student's own rollouts supply almost no correct responses in precisely those contexts. SteerDistill's answer is quantization-aware distillation with a full-precision teacher in the loop: for each failed rollout, the teacher steers the student to revise its decision, observes the result of executing the revision, and refines its guidance until the correction is locally verified.

The method has two halves. In teacher-steered trajectory revision, student and reference trajectories are aligned by environment transitions, the intervention is placed at the first behavioral divergence, and a frozen student retries from the restored pre-error state under a structured steering prompt that names the overlooked condition and a corrective rule; verification compares resulting environment states against the reference, and verified corrections are retained even when the overall task still fails. In internalization, the student is trained without any guidance to match the steered student's predictions at corrected turns (forward KL) and the full-precision teacher's at all other turns (reverse KL), so inference needs no teacher and no steering prompts.

Across five agentic benchmarks and three models, SteerDistill exceeds the strongest quantized baseline by 1.67–2.18 points in average score under W4A16 and stays within one point of BF16. On Qwen3-8B it reaches 53.18 average under W4A16 — 2.18 above QeRL and only 0.36 below the BF16 reference — and on τ²-Bench it beats QeRL's success rate by 1.84 percentage points while using 67% fewer training GPU hours.

## Key points

- Behavioral divergence, not generic capability loss, is the quantized-agent failure mode: environment transitions diverge from successful full-precision references when task requirements or environmental conditions are overlooked.
- Interventions go at the first behavioral divergence, localized by aligning trajectories on environment states; actions producing matching transitions count as equivalent.
- The teacher writes a structured steering prompt explaining the discrepancy, when the guidance applies, and a corrective rule; the frozen student revises from the restored pre-error history and state.
- Every revision is locally verified against the reference's environment states before it is retained, and corrections are kept for distillation even when the task ultimately fails — the ablation shows dropping local verification costs 4.66 points on BFCL V4 (46.84 → 42.18).
- Internalization uses split routing: forward KL at corrected turns against the guided steered student, reverse KL elsewhere against the full-precision teacher, with no prompts at inference.
- W4A16 on Qwen3-8B: 53.18 average, +2.18 over QeRL, 0.36 below BF16; Qwen3.5-4B +1.71 and Gemma 4 12B-it +1.67 over QeRL, all within one point of BF16.
- Gains persist at lower precision: +1.93 over QeRL under W4A8 and +3.39 over On-Policy QAD under W2A16 on Qwen3-8B.
- Training efficiency on τ²-Bench (8×A100 80GB): the 32-GPU-hour checkpoint hits 31.68% Pass@1, already 2.91 points above On-Policy QAD's converged score at about 40 GPU hours.
- Successful trajectories are shorter: 4.0K output tokens per successful task versus 4.1K–5.2K for quantized baselines (BF16: 3.9K), with fewer interaction steps and tool calls.

## Details

- Ablations (BFCL V4, Qwen3-8B W4A16; full method 46.84 vs BF16 46.89 and On-Policy QAD 44.43) isolate each design choice: intervening at a random eligible turn drops to 43.52 and at the first action mismatch to 45.01, confirming that localization belongs at the behavioral divergence rather than the surface action error.
- Steering beats both alternatives: unguided student retry reaches 44.46 and direct teacher takeover 45.06, versus 46.84 for steering the student — the student must produce the correction itself for the behavior to be learnable.
- The steering prompt should come from the teacher in structured form: free-form teacher prompts give 44.78, student-generated structured prompts collapse to 41.74, and GPT-5.6-Sol as an external structured steering source reaches 45.27.
- Retaining locally verified corrections without terminal-success filtering (46.84) edges out keeping only corrections from fully successful trajectories (46.30), because failed-but-corrected trajectories still carry usable supervision.
- Distillation routing matters: reverse KL throughout gives 45.29 and forward KL throughout 44.58, both below the split routing default; the only benchmark-level exception in the paper is Search QA under W2A16, where BitDistiller scores 34.22 versus 34.05.
