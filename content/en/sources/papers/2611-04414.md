# SphereFlow: Missing Modality Imputation via Geometric Transport on Hypersphere

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04414) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Huihan Wang, Juncheng Wang, Yuxiang Feng, Wenlong Hou, Guoqi Yu, Chao Xu, Yang Liu, Baigui Sun, Shujun Wang

## Overview

Missing modality imputation aims to synthesize unobserved modalities from observed ones. Existing methods treat each modality as living on its own latent manifold, forcing the generator to learn a long, unstructured jump between them. SphereFlow projects all modalities into a shared hyperspherical latent space using a frozen self-supervised encoder, turning cross-modal imputation into short-range geometric transport between nearby points on the sphere. Standard flow matching exposes two geometric conflicts on that surface: Gaussian noise is mismatched with the unit-norm manifold, and linear interpolation leaves the sphere into an unsupported region. Geometry-aware modifications replace the noise source with the observed-modality latent and use a time-weighted decoder loss extending to the traversed near-surface region.

## Key points

- All modalities are projected into a shared hyperspherical latent space.
- Cross-modal imputation becomes short-range geometric transport.
- Gaussian noise is replaced by the observed-modality latent.
- The decoder is trained with a time-weighted loss.
- The formulation yields a dimension-independent off-manifold bound.
- SphereFlow reaches state-of-the-art fidelity on MRI and CT-MRI tasks.

## Details

Experiments on BraTS multi-modal MRI imputation and CT-MRI translation show state-of-the-art generation fidelity in all settings. Downstream brain tumor segmentation using imputed data closes roughly eighty percent of the gap to a full-modality oracle. The gains depend on frozen encoder quality, inter-modality similarity, and whether the geometry assumptions hold for new imaging domains. The approach makes imputation practical when one modality is missing but shared representations are meaningful.
