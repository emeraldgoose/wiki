# Write, Evolve, Read: Streaming Cameras into a Persistent World State for 4D Occupancy Forecasting

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.30573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30573) · **Published**: 2026-11-23 (v1)
**Authors**: ZiAn Wang, Tianyi Wang, Zhihao Chen, Manni Cui, Junkai Chen, ZhiJin Chen, Ruiqi Liu, Junfei Wu, Huanyu Zhang, Shu Wu

## Overview

4D occupancy forecasting predicts the 3D semantic occupancy of a driving scene seconds ahead. Over a few seconds the scene itself changes little while the vehicle moves through it, so most of the space ahead has already been seen by the cameras. The authors make this precise by sorting every occupied voxel of the future scene into three kinds of space: unchanged (seen and still the same), changed (seen but different by then), and unseen (never seen). One second ahead, four fifths of the occupied future is unchanged space, and it is exactly there that current camera-based world models fall short: the strongest prior model, SparseWorld-TC, reaches an IoU of only 51.07 on unchanged space, where the scene as already seen is nearly the answer — its own current-frame estimate simply warped to the target time already gets 48.68. On changed and unseen space, where forecasting is actually needed, SparseWorld-TC is far ahead of carrying the seen scene forward (45.35 and 40.92 IoU vs. 26.33 and 0). The shortfall of camera-based world models therefore lies not in forecasting what will change, but in holding on to what they have already seen — something models given ground-truth occupancy receive for free.

PersistWorld answers with a persistent 3D world state anchored in the scene. Camera frames are streamed into this state, each frame written once, instead of the scene being re-derived from the image history for every forecast (as sparse end-to-end models do) or produced by a separate perception network (as two-stage methods do). Because the state stays in place as the vehicle moves, the evidence every frame provides about a place accumulates at one lattice position. To forecast, an evolver predicts the state features at any queried time along the ego trajectory, and a reader decodes the occupancy in the queried ego frame. The state is reset periodically and keeps only the last few seconds of observation. On Occ3D-nuScenes, PersistWorld raises the IoU of the best camera-based world model by 19.13 points (mIoU by 3.32) at 1–3 s, leads by 18.19 to 24.41 IoU at every horizon up to 8 s, and is the fastest measured method at 23.15 FPS.

## Key points

- Diagnostic split: at 1 s ahead, ~80% of the occupied future is unchanged space; SparseWorld-TC gets IoU 51.07 on unchanged space (vs. 48.68 for its own current estimate warped forward and 46.31 for STCOcc's predicted occupancy), but 45.35 on changed and 40.92 on unseen space — the gap is in persistence, not forecasting.
- World state: a lattice of 1.6 m cells anchored in the scene covering the 80 × 80 × 6.4 m queried region (50 × 50 × 4 cells on Occ3D); each cell packs the features of its 2 × 2 × 2 sub-cells of 0.8 m plus the time of its estimate and of its last observation. Unwritten cells take a learned initial feature of their ego-relative position.
- Writer: a DINOv3 ViT-B/16 image encoder (last blocks fine-tuned) provides key/value maps; each capture group is encoded and written once into the state the evolver has already carried to that capture time. Sub-cell centers are sampled bilinearly at their image projections plus a predicted image-plane offset, with a learned camera embedding. Ray-depth sections of 0.8 m let a light head predict which section holds the first occupied surface; the section's geometry embedding and log-probability bias the attention so evidence is weighted by visibility plausibility. A single-query attention over all cameras plus a null token, gated by a prior/candidate gate, updates the sub-cell — cells no camera sees keep the evolver-carried state.
- Evolver: advances the state from its estimate time to any target time under the ego trajectory. Motion tokens from relative SE(3) pose transforms and time steps; each cell attends only to motion after its own estimate, conditioned on estimate/observation age via adaptive layer norm; a once-per-update axial context over all stored cells is shared by every advance; the output is a time-scaled residual (identity at zero elapsed time). One evolver serves all horizons 1–8 s.
- Reader: unpacks sub-cells, applies a depthwise 3 × 3 × 3 residual block plus point-wise mixing, and a linear head predicts class logits for the 2 × 2 × 2 voxels of 0.4 m per sub-cell, resampled into the queried ego frame. The same reader decodes current occupancy right after writing and future occupancy from evolved states.
- Training: a unified focal objective over grid and ray views (focal term f(q) = −(1−q)² log q, class weights from priors, ω = 4 for free voxels); loss = mean grid loss over supervised horizons (1, 2, 3 s plus one horizon drawn uniformly from 4–8 s) + 0.25 × (current-frame grid loss + ray loss). Memory span: the state resets at scene start and every eight capture groups, matching the history length of SparseWorld-TC.
- Results on Occ3D-nuScenes (camera input, future ego poses): PersistWorld averages 28.72 mIoU / 67.82 IoU over 1–3 s at 23.15 FPS, vs. SparseWorld-TC at 3.48 FPS; the lead over SparseWorld-TC grows with horizon, from 18.19 to 20.14 IoU (3.04 to 3.63 mIoU) at 1 s to 3 s, and with BEV-Planner planned poses instead of ground-truth poses the lead widens to 22.63 IoU. Over 1–8 s it leads at every horizon by 18.19–24.41 IoU (21.58 IoU and 4.21 mIoU on average). IoU on unchanged space rises from 51.07 to 70.86 and on unseen space from 40.92 to 62.71.
- Ablations (avg 1–3 s): ego-centric state instead of scene-anchored costs IoU 67.82 → 63.96; freezing the encoder → 65.37; removing the evolver → 62.25 (unseen space collapses 62.71 → 50.49); windowed (non-persistent) state keeps accuracy (67.97) but halves speed to 12.02 FPS.

## Details

The two-stage baselines are limited by their input rather than their forecaster: with ground-truth occupancy GAST leads I2-World by 5.60 mIoU, but fed STCOcc's predicted occupancy the two are within 0.75 mIoU and both fall below SparseWorld-TC. Forecasting from occupancy (appendix B) shows the same forecaster gains from occupancy inputs up to 8 s, and trajectory conditioning (appendix E) shows the same state can produce different futures from different planned trajectories. Implementation: forecasts use at most the last eight capture groups plus ground-truth future ego poses; speed is measured on one A800 at batch size one, from GPU inputs to CPU occupancy outputs for the current frame and 1, 2, 3 s.
