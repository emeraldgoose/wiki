# Sage-Taiyi: A Metacognitive Architecture for Scientific Discovery

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.12762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12762) · **Published**: 2026-11-09

## Overview

Scientific discovery is an iterative loop in which search generates and tests ideas, interpretation of the resulting findings builds understanding of the problem, and that understanding redirects the search. The paper argues that recent AI scientist systems automate the iteration without preserving the understanding: iteration that depends mainly on feedback from the current round lets earlier failures, unresolved conflicts, and shared dependencies slip out of view, so agents repeat mistakes or keep searching unproductive paths, and search can become overly responsive to recent outcomes instead of noticing that the target, representation, or reasoning method should change.

The authors take their architectural cue from metacognition, the monitoring and regulation of one's own cognitive processes, and specifically from the distinction between an object level that performs a task and a meta level that maintains a dynamic model of that cognition. Mapped onto scientific search, the object level constructs and transforms ideas, while the meta level selectively represents the accumulated understanding — which judgments are supported, which conflicts remain open, under which conditions findings hold — and assesses what it implies for the next step: which premises to examine, which representations to revise, which methods to apply, and which branches to stop.

Sage-Taiyi implements this split as an architecture that revises its understanding of evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions through explicit monitoring and control, and then uses those assessments to adapt research targets and to select reusable reasoning methods. The claim is deliberately narrow: the contribution is regulation of search rather than a new generator of hypotheses.

## Key points

- On Taiyi-Bench, an AI co-scientist ideation benchmark of 265 tasks drawn from 50 papers across ten disciplines, Sage-Taiyi ranks first on all eight metrics covering historical rediscovery and open-ended ideation, with an Overall score of 54.0 against 47.9 for the strongest baseline, AI Scientist-v2.
- It also performs well on two external scientific ideation benchmarks, AgentIdeaBench and IG-Arena.
- Five expert-guided collaborations connect the system's proposals to experimental and computational validation in chemistry, materials science, and engineering, so the evaluation is not confined to benchmark scores.
- In chemistry, reaction-condition optimization raises the yield of cobalt-catalyzed carbonylation from trace amounts to 84%, and photocatalytic allylic amination from 13% to 69%.
- The paper positions itself against iterative AI-scientist workflows such as AI Scientist, which links ideation to experiments, analysis, and paper writing, Co-Scientist, which organizes hypothesis generation, critique, and improvement, and Robin, which combines multi-agent reasoning with experimental feedback: iteration alone does not guarantee that findings from different branches are retained with their evidence and applicability conditions.
- The theory used for the design is also the paper's stylistic touchstone, opening from the observation that science is a way of thinking more than a body of knowledge, and drawing on classical problem-solving research about how representations, operators, and heuristics organize search.

## Details

The framing rests on the claim that a scientific agent has to preserve understanding across a search and then use it to regulate what to investigate and how. That is what the meta level is for: rather than letting the current round's feedback dominate, it maintains assessments of evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions and uses them to steer targets and method selection, which the authors present as the mechanism behind the benchmark and collaboration results. The breadth of the author list — spanning chemistry, materials, engineering, and AI research groups — matches the paper's ambition of carrying ideation through to practical validation, and the reported reaction-yield improvements are the concrete evidence offered that metacognitive regulation helps proposals survive contact with experiment.
