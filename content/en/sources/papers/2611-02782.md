# History Forcing: Distilling History-Dependent Continuations for Long-Horizon Video World Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.02782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02782) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Hangyi Kuang, Chengzhi Wang, Yefei He, Weijie Wang, Yuke Zhao, Zhuokun Chen, Zisu Huang, Yifan Yang, Xiaoxuan He, Feng Chen, Bohan Zhuang

## Overview

Few-step autoregressive video diffusion enables efficient generation, but maintaining consistency over long rollouts remains difficult, especially when subjects or scenes reappear after leaving view. Existing distillation methods do not fully exploit richer historical information to constrain the student's current prediction. History Forcing addresses this with an on-policy distillation framework that strengthens historical supervision for few-step autoregressive video generators. It uses residual predictions and divergence terms to distill history-dependent continuations.

## Key points

- Few-step video diffusion is efficient but struggles with long-horizon consistency.
- Reappearance of subjects or scenes is a key challenge.
- Existing distillation underuses temporal history.
- History Forcing strengthens historical supervision.
- Distillation targets history-dependent continuations.
- The framework addresses long rollout stability.

## Details

The paper argues that long-horizon video generation should be trained to respect what has happened earlier, not only the immediate context. By distilling from richer teacher predictions that encode past information, the student learns continuations that remain coherent when content returns. Residual and divergence mechanisms guide how historical constraints are injected into student targets. The result is more stable long-horizon autoregressive video diffusion.
