# StepX-OPD: Rethinking Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation in Diffusion Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04386) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Chenru Wang, Xuanyang Zhang, Yucheng Han, Wenbo Nie, Wei Cheng, Xianfang Zeng, Gang YU, Chi Zhang

## Overview

Deployable text-to-image diffusion models need several capabilities at once, such as instruction following, text rendering, and human preference, and must sample in only a few steps. Existing pipelines usually separate these stages: a full-step model consolidates capabilities through multi-teacher on-policy distillation, then a second stage compresses it into a few-step model.

StepX-OPD merges those stages. One student acquires every capability while learning to sample in a few steps, so frozen specialists supervise it on the states its own rollout visits rather than on a longer trajectory it never runs. It also regresses the student's velocity on the low-noise part of the rollout because a KL decomposition weights velocity mismatch most heavily near the clean end.

The method further reduces prompt-word leakage, where models render prompt words in images even when text is not requested. Evaluation on SD3.5-Medium and FLUX.1-dev shows StepX-OPD achieves the highest normalized average score among four-step methods compared and exceeds a forty-step Flow-OPD model, with lower GPU training cost than a sequential pipeline.

## Key points

- Merges capability acquisition and few-step sampling into one training stage.
- Frozen specialists supervise states visited by the student's own rollout.
- Applies velocity regression on the low-noise part of the rollout.
- Includes a variant that reduces unintended text rendering.
- Evaluates on instruction following, text rendering, image quality, and preference.
- Trains with about 37% fewer GPU-hours than sequential distillation on SD3.5-Medium.

## Details

The paper discusses the two-stage bottleneck and the inability to start step distillation before capability consolidation completes. Results compare SD3.5-Medium and FLUX.1-dev architectures across many capabilities.
