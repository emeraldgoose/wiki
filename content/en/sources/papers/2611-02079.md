# ParetoSaddler: Pareto-Guided Optimization of Agent Harnesses for Quality and Cost

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.02079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02079) · **Published**: 2026-11-01
**Authors**: Xingzhi Qian, Sungho Park, Rongyuan Tan, Jianbo Yu, Jue Zhang, Lorenzo Cavallaro, Menglin Xia, Yanzhi Chen, Xinnuo Xu, Yu Kang, Xinran Zheng, Gagan Somashekar, Xuchao Zhang, Chetan Bansal, Qingwei Lin, Victor Rühle

## Overview

Harness optimization can substantially improve LLM agents without modifying model weights, but existing methods primarily optimize response quality. ParetoSaddler addresses the question of whether released systems attain the full speedup and quality permitted by parallel drafting and richer harness choices. It identifies three challenges: single requests accept few tokens but read every target weight; batching roughly halves speedup over autoregressive decoding; and speculative decoding is deployment-specific. ParetoSaddler provides an autonomous agentic loop that discovers algorithms and systems optimizations per deployment, locating gaps, exploring and integrating directions, checking outputs for reward hacking, and carrying validated mechanisms forward.

## Key points

- Harness improvements can help without changing model weights.
- Parallel drafters extend speedup by drafting whole blocks in one pass.
- Single requests read every target weight while accepting few tokens.
- Batching reduces per-request speedup substantially.
- Optimizations must be deployment, draft, target, and GPU specific.
- Output-token checking detects reward hacking.

## Details

Although the harness-optimization framing is broader, the reported experimental claims focus on speculative decoding speedup. With one request, speedup over autoregressive decoding rises from 4.46x to 10.87x, and with batching from 1.94x to 3.93x, without training or quality loss. Discovered optimizations include a general lossless 12-bit weight format, tree verification, two-level verification, and batching mechanisms. Results are configuration-specific and should be reproduced on the exact target, draft, and hardware stack. The findings motivate deployment-specific optimization loops rather than universal defaults.
