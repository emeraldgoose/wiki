# VoxMel TTS Technical Report

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20254)
**Date**: 2026-11-15

## Overview

VoxMel is a tokenizer-free framework for autoregressive text-to-speech (TTS) that targets continuous mel-spectrograms directly instead of learned acoustic tokens or autoencoder latents. The authors argue that learned compressed representations cap generation fidelity because quality depends on the encoder, while training-free targets like mel-spectrograms avoid that overhead but have been underexplored as autoregressive targets. VoxMel makes stable mel modeling tractable by applying a fixed, exactly invertible two-axis Haar transform to each mel spectrogram.

The transform first decomposes the mel along the frequency axis into local averages and a frequency residual, then splits the averaged component along the time axis into a Coarse-Mel stream and a temporal residual. A language-model backbone with a flow-matching head autoregressively generates the Coarse-Mel stream, while non-autoregressive residual tiers predict the temporal and frequency residuals in coarse-to-fine order. Neither residual is fed back into the autoregressive history, which keeps the autoregressive conditioning compact.

Trained on 100K hours of the Emilia corpus, the supervised 0.6B model reaches 1.51% WER and 0.738 speaker similarity (SIM) on Seed-TTS-eval test-en, and 1.29% CER with 0.755 SIM on test-zh. Applying GRPO improves these to 1.15% WER / 0.753 SIM and 1.17% CER / 0.776 SIM respectively. The guided flow head is also distilled into two- and four-step student models that need only 2 or 4 head evaluations per coarse frame, with the four-step student retaining competitive SIM and nearly matching the supervised model on WER/CER.

## Key points

- Tokenizer-free design: no learned acoustic codec or VAE is required; the acoustic target is a standard mel-spectrogram reconstructed through a fixed, exactly invertible two-axis Haar transform.
- Two-axis decomposition: frequency is split into local averages plus a frequency residual; the averaged component is then split along time into Coarse-Mel plus a temporal residual.
- Factorized generation: an autoregressive LM backbone with a flow-matching head generates Coarse-Mel; non-autoregressive flow-matching tiers then predict the temporal residual (from Coarse-Mel) and the frequency residual (conditioned on the reconstructed Frequency-Averaged Mel).
- Residuals stay out of the autoregressive history; within each residual tier positions are predicted in parallel, but tiers run sequentially.
- Frame-wise Coarse-Mel prediction halves the number of autoregressive steps (T/2 steps of dimension M/2) compared with frame-level mel generation.
- Training data: 100K hours of Emilia; supervised model size 0.6B parameters.
- GRPO post-training improves both intelligibility and speaker similarity over the supervised baseline.
- Distillation yields two- and four-step students for the guided flow head, cutting sampling cost per coarse frame.

## Details

Method: VoxMel factorizes the mel generation problem as p(C) · p(D^(t)|C) · p(D^(f)|A), where C is Coarse-Mel, A is the reconstructed Frequency-Averaged Mel, D^(t) the temporal residual and D^(f) the frequency residual. Because the Haar transform H is fixed and invertible, |det H| is a constant change-of-variables factor and no information is lost. The transcript is tokenized and combined with projected Coarse-Mel history in a decoder-only transformer; each hidden state conditions a flow-matching head (an MLP predicting the velocity field, integrated with an ODE solver) that samples the next Coarse-Mel unit, alongside a stop head for sequence termination. Inference applies separate guidance scales to the text and speaker-prompt conditions. The temporal-residual tier is a non-autoregressive transformer conditioned on Coarse-Mel; its output is combined to reconstruct the Frequency-Averaged Mel, which conditions the frequency-residual tier, predicting each frame independently. Finally, the inverse Haar transform reconstructs the full mel spectrogram, which a vocoder converts to waveform (the vocoder can be upgraded independently without retraining the generator). All flow-matching heads use the standard conditional flow-matching objective with linear noise-to-data interpolation.

Results (zero-shot voice cloning, LibriSpeech test-clean / Seed-TTS-eval test-en/test-zh; WER/CER in percent, lower better; SIM higher better):

- Supervised 0.6B model (test-en): 1.51% WER, 0.738 SIM.
- Supervised 0.6B model (test-zh): 1.29% CER, 0.755 SIM.
- After GRPO (test-en): 1.15% WER, 0.753 SIM.
- After GRPO (test-zh): 1.17% CER, 0.776 SIM.
- Distilled students require 2 or 4 head evaluations per coarse frame; the four-step student keeps competitive SIM and nearly matches the supervised model in WER/CER.
