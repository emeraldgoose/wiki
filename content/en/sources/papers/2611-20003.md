# The novelty exposed: ablation, reproducibility and transparency in 40 technical reports of open-weight LLMs

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20003)
**Published**: 2026-11-15

## Overview

In 2025 alone, 48 new LLM architectures were added to Hugging Face transformers, most arriving with a technical report that presents the model as new. This paper asks what is actually new in those models and whether it is tested, looking at three things: what the reference code shows about how open-weight architectures differ from earlier ones, what 40 randomly sampled technical reports (stratified by year, drawn from the 64 eligible reports that HF transformers documentation links to) claim as new and whether they support it with ablations, and what they disclose for reproducibility and transparency.

The headline counts are uncomfortable. In the library, 39 of the 48 architectures added in 2025 take at least two of their three core blocks (attention, feed-forward, decoder layer) from another model, most often Llama — though part of this reflects the library's shift, from about 2024, to writing new models as edits of older ones (modular files). Across the library, 288 core blocks are taken from other models; Llama lends the most (80 blocks used by 47 architectures), followed by DeepSeek-V3 (13), Qwen3-MoE (12) and Mistral (10). The rise over time (2024: 17/30, 57%; 2025: 39/48, 81%; 2026 to 13 Nov: 38/43, 88%) cannot be divided between code-writing practice and design, and the author does not try.

In the reports, 28 of the 40 make architectural or training novelty claims (73 claims in total); 12 make none. Only 19 of the 73 claims (26%) come with a controlled with/without comparison at matched size, data and tokens; 20 (27%) come with a comparison that changes other things at the same time, and 34 (47%) come with no comparison at all. Counted by report, 11 of the 28 test at least one claim in a controlled way, and 4 test all of them. No report meets more than three of the five measured coding items, and 24 of the 40 meet none.

## Key points

- Code reuse dominates: 81% of 2025 additions reuse ≥2 of 3 core blocks; attribution in reports is usually honest (no hidden renaming found among five candidate cases — Falcon "multigroup"/GQA, Parallel Prefill, Mamba "selection", Mamba-2 analog, Falcon "monolayer"), but per-additive testing is rare.
- Disclosure is thin: only 2 of 40 reports describe the data mixture fully enough to rebuild it (coder agreement kappa 0.30 — the least certain count); 29 of 40 give pre-training token counts; 12 of 40 give compute; 4 of 40 state more than one training run; 8 of 40 rerun comparison models under one setup; only 1 discloses data mixture + tokens + compute together; 22 of 40 copy comparison scores from other reports; 8 of 40 include any third-party evaluation.
- OLMo 2 is cited as the good example for attribution (cites each adopted component and shows its effect in a figure); OLMoE meets Rules 1, 3 and 5 (controlled comparisons for its four claims, all evaluations run in-house with 5 few-shots, mixture/tokens/compute disclosed) but misses Rules 2 and 4; Mistral 7B presents GQA and sliding-window attention as its main novelties.
- Seven reporting rules are proposed (Box 1): (1) controlled with/without comparison per claimed-new part or state none was run; (2) report more than one run or state single-run; (3) rerun comparisons under one setup and mark copied scores; (4) make the model available for outside evaluation; (5) report mixture, token count and compute; (6) separate introduced vs adopted parts with origins and established names; (7) describe the delta from the closest public baseline in code terms. Rules 1–5 are measured by the coding; 6–7 are proposed, not measured.
- Explicitly out of scope: model quality. An untested claim may still be true; the study judges evidence in reports, not the models.

## Details

- Method: 164 dated causal LM architectures in HF transformers (population); attention/feed-forward/decoder-layer block comparison against lender models (Figure 2 compares Qwen3 vs Llama attention — QK-norm as 2 added + 2 changed lines plus an optional sliding-window switch); 40-report random stratified sample of 64 eligible linked reports; two coders; five items scored per report; seven attribution reports read closely (Qwen2, Qwen3, OLMo 2, Falcon, Falcon Mamba, Mamba, Mamba-2).
- Historical framing: real advances (transformer 2017, BERT 2019, GPT-3/scaling laws 2020, compute-optimal training, RLHF 2022, Switch Transformers, test-time compute 2024) all arrived as methods with targeted experiments; release reports bundle many choices, so bundle-level comparisons cannot isolate any single change (Narang et al. 2021 and follow-ups found most proposed Transformer modifications do not improve in shared implementations).
- Citation ≠ contribution evidence: Qwen2/Qwen3 reports cite GQA/QK-Norm origins but give no isolated test of those parts; OLMo 2 does both.
