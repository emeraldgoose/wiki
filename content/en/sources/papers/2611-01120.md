# StreamDuo: A Live Commentator and a Research Agent for Unseen Games

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.01120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01120) · **Published**: 2026-10-31
**Authors**: Zixian Gao, Penghao Luo, Xiangcheng Cao, Lixin Xiu, Ziqi Cai, Zhen Li, Erwin Wu, Kaipeng Zhang

## Overview

Streaming video LLMs can now commentate live gameplay, but they are trained and tested on the same games. The paper studies cross-game streaming commentary, where a model trained on one game commentates games it has never seen, and finds that current commentators transfer their timing but not their knowledge of the game world. It proposes StreamDuo, which pairs a trainable commentator with a frozen research agent that reads the wiki of the game being played. When the commentator encounters something it cannot name, it sends the current frames, and the research agent returns a few wiki entries about two seconds later while the stream continues. Only the commentator is trained, on a single game, in two stages. Information-gain labels teach it to ask only where an answer would help it say what the human caster said, and on-policy self-distillation from a copy that sees the ideal answer teaches it to use noisy answers. Trained on Baldur's Gate 3 and tested on ten unseen games, StreamDuo raises entity recall from 0.082 to 0.116 over Proact-VL trained on the same data, cuts source-game leakage from 9.9% to 1.0%, and achieves the best timing plus the highest judged liveliness and overall quality among streaming models.

## Key points

- Current streaming commentators transfer timing but not world knowledge when moving to unseen games.
- StreamDuo combines a trainable commentator with a frozen research agent that reads the game's wiki.
- The commentator requests wiki help from current frames and receives entries after about two seconds while streaming continues.
- Training uses information-gain labels for when to ask and self-distillation from an ideal-answer teacher to use noisy answers.
- On ten unseen games, entity recall improves from 0.082 to 0.116, source-game leakage drops from 9.9% to 1.0%, and liveliness/overall quality lead streaming models.

## Details

The system is trained only on Baldur's Gate 3 and evaluated on unseen games, providing a strong test of cross-game generalization. Architecture, latency budget, and evaluation rubric are in the linked source.
