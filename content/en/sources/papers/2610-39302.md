# Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.39302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39302) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Randall Balestriero

## Overview

Whether models trained on different modalities converge to the same representation of the world is central to AI. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis conjectures that they do as they scale, but without a proof it offers neither guarantee nor recipe. This paper turns the hypothesis into a theorem. First, it proves a Platonic Representation Theorem: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures trained separately on any number of modalities learn the same representation up to one rotation whenever their positive pairs are two observations of one scene, called good views, and their misalignment is bounded otherwise. Second, next-token prediction is shown to be a JEPA with bad views, namely parts of a single document, which explains why language models align with vision only through scale. Third, the theory is validated from 145k captions to texts of 940M web images, uncovering a scaling law of alignment that predicts unseen runs within seed noise. Good views need 42 times fewer training samples than next-token prediction on the same corpus to reach the same alignment, and a 109M-parameter text JEPA aligned with DINOv2 and CLIP's image encoders better than every language model evaluated up to 72B, and better than CLIP's text encoder. Next-token prediction is therefore a remarkably inefficient route to platonic representations. As a showcase, one rotation turns separately trained encoders into an image captioner (CIDEr .838 on COCO). Even that rotation needs no pair: on the latest pair-free benchmark, the text JEPA beat every zero-shot accuracy reported with language models up to 8B parameters, such as 64% versus at most 56% top-5 on ImageNet-100. Positive views, not scale alone, are thus the lever to build platonic representations.

## Key points

- The paper proves the Platonic Representation Hypothesis as a theorem for JEPAs.
- Good views are two observations of one scene.
- JEPAs trained on good views share one representation up to rotation.
- Next-token prediction is a JEPA with bad views.
- Good views need 42x fewer training samples than next-token prediction for the same alignment.
- A 109M-parameter text JEPA outperforms much larger language models and CLIP's text encoder in alignment.
- The text JEPA achieved 64% top-5 on ImageNet-100 in pair-free evaluation.

## Details

The theory is validated across scales from 145k captions to 940M web images and yields a scaling law of alignment. The showcase demonstrates encoders can be connected by a rotation and even pair-free retrieval can be strong.
