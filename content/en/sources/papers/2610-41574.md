# BYOD: Build Your Own DLM - Efficient Autoregressive-to-Diffusion Language Model Conversion

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.41574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41574) · **Published**: 2026-10-30
**Authors**: Ruurd J. A. Kuiper, Robert A. Bagheri

## Overview

Unlike autoregressive models, diffusion language models permit parallel token updates, flexible generation, and bidirectional attention. Leading systems still rely on costly pretraining or full-model fine-tuning. Build Your Own DLM (BYOD) is presented as a generalizable, LoRA-only recipe for parameter-efficient autoregressive-to-diffusion conversion. Without architecture-specific tuning, BYOD induces diffusion generation in Gemma 2 9B, Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen2.5 7B, and Ministral 8B. The authors report this as the first AR-to-DLM conversion of a Mistral model. The adapters make up 4.1 to 5.8 percent of the final parameters.

## Key points

- Diffusion language models allow parallel updates, flexible generation, and bidirectional attention.
- Existing DLM systems often require expensive pretraining or full-model fine-tuning.
- BYOD converts AR models using only LoRA adapters.
- The recipe works across several open model families without architecture-specific tuning.
- Adapters account for a small fraction of the final model parameters.

## Details

BYOD targets the cost side of diffusion language modeling by avoiding full model changes. It converts existing autoregressive checkpoints into diffusion-capable checkpoints using small adapters. Experiments cover multiple model families and sizes, including the first reported Mistral conversion. The reported mechanism is parameter-efficient, with adapters representing only a modest share of final parameters. The focus is on making DLM capability attainable from existing AR checkpoints rather than training a new diffusion model from scratch.
