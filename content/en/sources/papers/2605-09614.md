# Reflection Anchors for Propagation-Aware Visual Retention in Long-Chain Multimodal Reasoning

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09614) (accepted at NeurIPS 2026)
**Published**: 2026-05-10

## Overview

Long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning has become a cornerstone of large language model progress, and recent work has adapted it to large vision–language models (LVLMs). Unlike text-only LLMs, however, LVLMs struggle to sustain visual information across long reasoning sequences — a failure mode the authors call "visual forgetting." Existing remedies either re-inject visual evidence at inference time or train policies for stronger grounding, but both treat downstream visual dependence as an indirect consequence of context injection or policy bias, rather than specifying an explicit rule for how native decoding should be modified at each step to preserve visual influence on future reasoning.

This paper studies visual retention from an information-theoretic standpoint. The authors derive a lower bound on the downstream visual gain of a one-step intervention, which identifies two governing factors: local branching room (token entropy) and downstream visual propagation potential (the divergence of the suffix distribution from a vision-marginalized reference). Guided by this analysis, they propose RAPO (Reflection-Anchor Policy Optimization), a GRPO-based policy optimization method that selects high-entropy "reflection anchors" and optimizes a chain-masked, finite-window KL surrogate aimed at strengthening downstream visual dependence rather than transient local evidence.

Experiments across reasoning-intensive and general-domain benchmarks show that RAPO improves over strong baselines on multiple LVLM backbones, and mechanism analyses indicate that reflection anchors concentrate at visually sensitive decision points and that RAPO raises contrastive visual-dependence signals along generated trajectories. The work was accepted at NeurIPS 2026 and comes from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Shanghai AI Laboratory.

## Key points

- Visual forgetting in long-chain multimodal CoT is analyzed as a sparse, instance-level policy-reshaping problem: change only a few decoding steps, but make each change propagate downstream.
- A derived lower bound on the optimal downstream visual gain of a one-step intervention factors into local branching room — token entropy, via a (e^{H(m)} − 2)+ term — and downstream visual propagation potential — the KL divergence between the true suffix law and a vision-marginalized masked-reference suffix law.
- RAPO is GRPO-based: it selects the top-20% highest-entropy tokens as reflection anchors, applies a per-trajectory budget, and optimizes a chain-masked finite-window KL surrogate (window w = 3 for Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct, otherwise 1; KL coefficient γ = 0.01).
- On Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct, RAPO-G reaches 43.01 average mean@8 accuracy vs. 40.79 for GRPO (+5.85 over the 37.16 base model); on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct it reaches 55.11 vs. 53.38 for GRPO.
- On Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, RAPO-D averages 41.65 (vs. 38.37 GRPO); on Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, RAPO-D averages 37.09 (vs. 33.50 GRPO).
- Compute-matched comparison: extending GRPO from 100 to 120 steps lifts the Qwen3-VL-2B average only from 40.79 to 41.45, still below RAPO-100 at 43.01 (RAPO-100 takes ~12h31m vs. ~12h43m for GRPO-120).
- Ablations: top-20% entropy anchors (43.01) beat top-10% (41.78), top-50% (41.80), top-100% (34.75), bottom-20% (41.24), random-20% (39.96), and noun-based selection (40.02); the fixed three-token window (43.01) beats entropy-gated (41.32) and inter-anchor (42.04) adaptive windows.
- Chain-mask (structured anchor-chain masking) outperforms image-level masking: replacing it drops the average from 43.01 to 40.43.
- Mechanism analysis (500 ViRL validation instances, Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct): RAPO increases token-level visual-dependency KL divergence later in the CoT where GRPO does not; reflection anchors are enriched for visually sensitive decision points.
- γ = 0.1 collapses training; γ = 0.01 was best among {0, 0.001, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.1}.
- Robustness: RAPO improves both greedy decoding (31.41 vs. GRPO's ~27.87) and temperature-1.0 sampling (43.01), with a smaller greedy–stochastic gap (11.46 vs. 12.92), and holds up on longer context lengths (43.8 vs. 41.4 avg in Table 13).

## Details

**Problem formulation.** The authors define the marginal downstream visual gain Δ_t(π) as the difference between the conditional mutual information I(V; Y_{≥t} | h̃_t) under a one-step-then-rollback intervention and under the native policy — rewarding persistent downstream visual influence, not just transient evidence at the current token. Given a native trajectory, they jointly select a set of anchor positions A (at most ρT of T steps) and a local policy π whose per-step KL to the native decoder is at most ε, maximizing the summed Δ_t. In Appendix B they prove a lower bound on the optimal gain, Δ_t⋆ ≥ [c₁√ε · E[(e^{H(m_{t,V})} − 2)₊ · √KL(p_{t,V} ‖ p_{M,t})] − c₂ε]₊, which motivates the two-factor anchor score: entropy (branching room) times suffix divergence from a vision-marginalized reference (propagation potential).

**Method.** RAPO implements the analysis on top of GRPO. At each decoding step it masks previously selected anchor positions (chain masking) rather than the current token or future suffix, scores candidate steps by entropy, keeps the top-20% as reflection anchors subject to a sparse budget, and optimizes a chain-masked finite-window KL surrogate that pushes the policy's suffix predictions toward stronger image dependence. The vision tower is frozen during RL; reward is binary accuracy. Models are trained on the ViRL dataset for long-CoT multimodal reasoning (100 RL steps, rollout batch 256 for group i; 200 steps, batch 384 for groups ii–iii; learning rate 1e-6). Evaluation uses vLLM on six benchmarks — MathVision, MathVerse, EMMA, LogicVista (reasoning-intensive) and MMMU-Pro, RealWorldQA (general-domain) — with temperature 1.0, max 8192 generation tokens, 8 samples per input, reported as mean@8.

**Main results.** Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct: base 37.16 → GRPO 40.79 → PAPO 40.44 → VPPO 40.90 → RAPO-G 43.01 (+5.85 over base; best on both reasoning and general splits). Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct: base 49.56 → GRPO 53.38 → PAPO 53.50 → VPPO 53.85 → RAPO-G 55.11 (+5.55). Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct: base 31.94 → GRPO 38.37; released reasoning models MM-Eureka 39.99, VL-Rethinker 40.96, R1-ShareVL 40.09; RAPO-G 40.98, RAPO-D 41.65 (+9.71 over base). Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct: base 15.75 → GRPO 33.50 → TVC 29.94 → MINT-CoT 27.32; RAPO-G 35.21, RAPO-D 37.09 (+21.34 over base). A DAPO-based variant (RAPO-D) also wins on Qwen3-VL-8B (57.39, +7.83 over base). RAPO's extra cost is training-only; inference uses standard autoregressive decoding. Limitations (Appendix J): the approach relies on native-entropy anchors and further gains may require broader propagation objectives; see the paper for full limitation and impact statements.
