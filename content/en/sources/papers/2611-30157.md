# UUBench: Understanding the Unspoken toward Full-Duplex Multimodal Interaction

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.30157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30157) · **Published**: 2026-11-23 (v1)
**Authors**: Ye Pan, Chenfei Liao, Bin Ren, Hanqian Li, Haocong He, Jialei Chen, Jinlin He, Kunyu Peng, Mengyu Jin, Shijie Li, Wenqian Cui, Yuanqi Yao, Yuqian Fu, Kailong Ruan, Yuan Gao, Wei Wang, Xuming Hu, Irwin King, Nicu Sebe, Xu Zheng

## Overview

In everyday conversation, users convey unstated communicative needs through intonation, facial expressions, gaze, and gestures, not just literal words. UUBench defines "implicit intent" as a communicative goal not fully stated in verbal content but inferable from verbal cues, observable nonverbal behavior, and context — and asks whether digital-human systems translate that understanding into responses that actually address the need. Prior benchmarks (MIntRec2.0, VAGUE, GesBench, AvaMERG, VidChatBench, LiveTalk) cover intent recognition or generation quality separately, but not whether generated replies fulfill implicit intent evidenced by multimodal cues.

From 28,548 candidate user clips drawn from TalkVerse, TalkCuts, TalkVid, and SpeakerVid-5M, reviewers selected 216 high-quality evaluation clips; annotators labeled each speaker's intent, the auditory and visual primitives, and the words/behaviors supporting their interpretation. Because implicit-intent inference is inherently subjective, a single overall rating can hide which aspects of a need a reply addressed, so UUBench introduces two complementary scoring perspectives grounded by a Fact-Grounded Scoring JEV module: structured, timestamped audiovisual observations (silent frames sampled at 6 FPS) of user and reply are scored one dimension or criterion at a time by jev-1.14.0 on a [-3, +3] scale.

Process-Oriented Scoring rates, for each of 8 applicable primitive dimensions (e.g., facial behavior, gaze, head movements, gestures), how well the reply addresses the need conveyed by that cue, then aggregates with weights derived from each primitive's relevance to the annotated evidence: s_ip = Σ w_ik x_ipk, and the system-level intent-fulfillment score S_p averages s over all N = 216 inputs. Outcome-Oriented Scoring measures the reply itself: Q_ip is the mean of nine modality-criterion pairs — modality (Audio, Visual, Omni) x criterion (Naturalness, Appropriateness, Human-likeness) — and Q_p averages Q over inputs.

## Key points

- Nine systems evaluated on the same 216 inputs with one shared prompt: closed-source Emma, Tavus, Vidu, D-ID, bitHuman; open-source CyberVerse (SoulX-LiveAct), OpenAvatarChat (LiteAvatar), Open-LLM-VTuber (Live2D), EmpaAva (GaussianAvatars + DEEPTalk).
- Headline finding: all nine systems score positive overall audiovisual quality (Q = 1.01–2.03) yet every system scores negative on all four visual primitive dimensions (facial behavior, gaze, head movements, gestures), with means from -0.35 to -0.02 — natural-looking replies can systematically miss visually conveyed needs.
- The two perspectives rank systems differently: Emma has the highest Q (2.03), Tavus the highest evidence-weighted intent fulfillment S (0.47); among open-source systems CyberVerse leads both (Q 1.64, S 0.18).
- Human-likeness splits by modality: Vidu leads audio human-likeness (2.13), Emma leads visual (1.94) and audiovisual (1.76); open-source systems all round to ~1.92 audio human-likeness but -1.24 to -0.30 visual. Across all nine, speech is rated more human-like than visible behavior; all nine are positive on visual naturalness, five negative on visual human-likeness.
- Even the best reply quality coexists with weak cue response: Emma's means for responding to user facial behavior, gaze, head movements, and gestures are -0.08, -0.15, -0.13, -0.15 despite its top visual human-likeness.
- Coverage rules: Q requires all nine valid q scores; s requires a non-empty applicable dimension set with defined weights and valid x scores; system totals require N = 216 valid responses per system, with missing totals reported alongside coverage.

## Details

Timing conventions differ by system type: for closed-source systems, response time runs from the end of user-video playback until the avatar begins speaking (API expenditure also reported); for open-source systems it is reply generation plus avatar rendering/recording. Figure 6 plots Q and S against mean response time with time-score frontiers. Illustrative failure cases: Tavus asks whether "I'm dead" is a joke while missing the speaker's distress about quitting a job; Vidu responds playfully to an account of performing for parents' guests while overlooking the obligation in "I had to do a play." The evaluation pipeline (Fig. 5) iterates clips x systems, obtains full-frame user observations U_i, frozen primitive annotations A_i with source weights w_i, selects the approved face-cropped reply clip, extracts reply observations V_ip and auxiliary measurements M_ip, and scores each modality-criterion pair and each applicable primitive dimension with JevScore.
