# CORE: Improving Compositional Reasoning in MLLM Embedding via Reranker Distillation

Source: Tingyu Song and 7 other authors, arXiv:2609.04083, Sep 3, 2026.

**Lead**: MLLM-based embedding models fail at compositional retrieval — they cannot distinguish scenes with the same concepts but different attribute–object bindings. Yet the *same backbone* resolves these distinctions as a cross-attentive reranker. **CORE** distills the reranker's compositional judgments into the embedding model: it synthesizes candidate lists spanning five compositional matching levels and trains with a **Rank-KL** objective that reproduces the reranker's fine-grained ranking. Under identical data and tuning budgets, listwise Rank-KL and pairwise CoSENT both use multi-level supervision more effectively than contrastive learning, with Rank-KL strongest overall.

## What the results show

| Model | Result |
|---|---|
| CORE-RERANKER-8B | **82.7%** total average across COLA, SUGARCREPE++, NEGBENCH — **+10.7 points over Jina-Reranker** |
| CORE-EMBED-8B | **0.666** total average — best among all evaluated embedding models |
| Transfer | Gains carry to MCMR with no retrieval loss on COCO and Flickr30K |

The graded evaluation protocol (five matching levels instead of binary positives/negatives) is part of the contribution: it makes the supervision signal dense enough for listwise distillation to work.

## Caveats

This summary is based on the arXiv abstract and metadata; full-text details (the five matching levels, Rank-KL formulation, per-benchmark tables) were not re-verified and are omitted rather than reconstructed. Embedding scores (0.666) are benchmark averages, not directly comparable across different evaluation protocols.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- If your retrieval confuses "red cube on blue sphere" with "blue cube on red sphere", the fix may be distillation from your own reranker, not a bigger embedding model.
- Listwise objectives beat contrastive ones when supervision is graded — invest in multi-level labels before scaling negatives.
- Compare training objectives under matched data and tuning budgets, or you are measuring budget, not method.
- Verify no regression on standard retrieval (COCO/Flickr30K here) — compositional gains that cost general retrieval are a trade, not a win.
