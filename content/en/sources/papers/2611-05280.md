# YANAMI: A Large-Scale Multimodal Dataset and Benchmark for Multiturn Stateful Interaction

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05280) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Shengyu Wang, Tengyue Nong, Junming Liu, Haosong Peng, Siyu Li, Ruiqi Lei, Fushuo Huo, Yuhao Pan, Yunfeng Fan, Ding Wang, Nan Cheng, Haozhao Wang, Wenchao Xu

## Overview

Unified multimodal models integrate visual understanding, generation, and editing in one framework, but existing datasets and benchmarks remain dominated by single-turn tasks. Even recent multi-turn datasets often have turns solvable independently without carrying information across turns. YANAMI addresses this gap as the first large-scale multimodal dialogue dataset and benchmark for stateful interaction. It comprises YANAMI-400K with 400K multi-turn multimodal dialogues containing more than 2M images and 2.6M dialogue turns, plus YANAMI-Bench with 400 multi-turn dialogues. The dialogues span multi-image fusion, image editing, world modeling, and visual reasoning, requiring models to track, preserve, and revise evolving dialogue states.

## Key points

- YANAMI provides 400K multi-turn multimodal dialogues.
- More than 2M images and 2.6M dialogue turns are included.
- Four scenarios cover fusion, editing, world modeling, and visual reasoning.
- Existing single-turn benchmarks provide limited stateful supervision.
- YANAMI-Bench has 400 dialogues for systematic evaluation.
- It evaluates both state understanding and visual realization.

## Details

Fine-tuning BAGEL on YANAMI-400K improves image editing, generation, and multimodal stateful interaction by 10.3, 2.0, and 13.23 percent respectively while preserving general visual understanding. YANAMI-Bench characterizes the gap between understanding evolving dialogue states and faithfully realizing them in visual outputs. Evaluations reveal that existing unified multimodal models still struggle to maintain, update, and visually realize evolving multimodal dialogue states. The gap metric helps distinguish state comprehension failures from generation failures.
