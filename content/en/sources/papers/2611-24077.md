# AeroRSI: Recursive Self-Improvement for Aerial Vision-Language Navigation

**Source**: [huggingface](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24077) · **Published**: 2026-11-18
**Authors**: Shiwei Wu, Yuwen Pan, Zhi Li, Yaowen Li, Yan Zhou, Xuemiao Xu, Yu Liu

## Overview

Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to follow natural-language instructions, yet still fail frequently in closed-loop flight. Correcting these failures requires repairs — actions that avoid them at the states the policy itself reaches — which human pilots cannot label at scale and rule-based planners cannot supply reliably. AeroRSI is a recursive self-improvement framework in which an aerial navigation policy iteratively learns from its own failures. The key insight is that a failing policy should judge its own repairs by flying on from them. Judged in this way, its sampled actions and even rule-based proposals that hurt it when imitated directly become reliable supervision. An episode terminates at its first failure, concealing subsequent failures until earlier ones are repaired. AeroRSI therefore renews its supervision each round, anchoring failures of the current policy, verifying repairs by continuation, and distilling them while preserving behavior elsewhere. On TravelUAV, three rounds raise the success rate of a supervised 2B-parameter policy from 46.4% to 67.5% and reduce collisions by half, surpassing the previous state of the art by 10.6 points from the same initialization without extra human labels or inference cost.

## Key points

- VLA models for UAV navigation fail frequently in closed-loop flight despite natural-language instruction following.
- Repairs needed to avoid failures are hard to collect from human pilots and unreliable from rule-based planners.
- AeroRSI improves an aerial policy recursively by learning from its own failures and verifying candidate repairs by flight continuation.
- Verification by continuation turns sampled actions and rule-based proposals into reliable supervision, even when direct imitation hurts performance.
- Episode termination at first failure hides later failures; repairing earlier failures reveals the next ones.
- On TravelUAV, three rounds raise success rate of a supervised 2B-parameter policy from 46.4% to 67.5% and halve collisions.
- The method surpasses previous state of the art by 10.6 points from same initialization without extra human labels or inference cost.

## Details

AeroRSI anchors each round to failures of the current policy, generates candidate repairs, and checks them by continuing flight from the repaired states. Successful continuations provide supervision that is distilled back into the policy while keeping behavior elsewhere unchanged. Because supervision is renewed each round, the framework progressively uncovers and fixes deeper failures hidden behind earlier ones. The approach does not require additional human annotations and adds no inference-time cost. Experiments on TravelUAV show consistent gains across rounds, with collision rate reduced by half and success rate climbing from 46.4% to 67.5% after three iterations.
