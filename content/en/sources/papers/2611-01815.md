# When Does Latent Prediction Error Support Physical Surprise Claims in Video World Models?

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.01815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01815) · **Published**: 2026-11-01
**Authors**: Anton Hristov, Aleksandar Slavov, Vasil Vasilev

## Overview

Prediction errors from video world models are often treated as physical surprise, but they can reflect appearance, static content, motion, or ordinary prediction difficulty. The authors present an evaluation framework that separates what frozen representations contain, how they organize appearance and physical differences, and what changes a model's prediction error. On IntPhys2, a benchmark of physically possible and impossible events, they compare representations from latent prediction, masked pixel reconstruction, and simple visual statistics. Appearance remains easy to decode, while V-JEPA2 signals physical structure more strongly.

## Key points

- Prediction error is not always physical surprise.
- Errors may come from appearance, motion, or prediction difficulty.
- The framework separates representation content and organization.
- It compares multiple visual representation families.
- IntPhys2 contrasts physically possible and impossible events.
- V-JEPA2 captures physical structure more strongly.

## Details

The paper calls for more careful language when interpreting model errors. A large latent error does not necessarily mean the event is physically impossible or surprising; it may indicate the visual features are underrepresented. By evaluating frozen representations, the framework explains whether appearance is decodable and whether physical differences affect structure. The findings caution against conflating visual reconstruction quality with physical reasoning.
