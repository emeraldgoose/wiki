# CUA-Master: Scaling Computer-use Agents through Open-World Recursive Improvements

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03126) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Xinlei Yu, Gen Li, Ji Zhao, Zhangquan Chen, Congcong Wang, Shuai Dong, Zelin Peng, Yuqi Xu, Hongrui Wu, Kaiwen Tuo, Cheng Yang, Kaituo Feng, Yifan Wu, Jiayi Zhang, Dongming Wu, Wenqi Shao, Jiaqi Wang, Xiangyu Yue

## Overview

Despite recent advances in computer-use agents, development remains constrained by limited diversity, controllability, and reliability of interaction data. Conventional optimization pipelines also lack iterative feedback, while recursive improvement often relies on self-generated experiences, causing limited ability expansion and diminishing returns. CUA-Master introduces a nested double-loop recursive improvement framework. At the system level, an outer world-data-model flywheel incorporates open-world corpus and leverages evolving agent capability to expose weak abilities. At the interaction level, an inner data-model feedback loop records, curates, and refines training data.

## Key points

- CUA development is limited by interaction data diversity.
- Conventional pipelines lack iterative feedback.
- Self-generated improvement yields diminishing returns.
- CUA-Master uses a nested double-loop framework.
- The outer flywheel incorporates open-world data.
- The inner loop curates and refines trajectories.

## Details

The two loops separate system scaling from trajectory refinement. The outer world-data-model loop expands training signal by pulling in open-world corpus and focusing on weak capabilities. The inner data-model loop uses feedback to curate data rather than blindly reusing self-generated experience. Together these loops aim to scale computer-use agents through higher-quality interaction data and continuous capability assessment.
