# DRAP: Dynamics-Risk Adaptive Plasticity for Continual Learning in Physical AI

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05029) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Oswaldo Ludwig

## Overview

Continual learning in physical AI differs from conventional continual learning because small per-step policy errors can accumulate over a trajectory. Each deviation may move the agent outside the training distribution, increase subsequent prediction errors, and ultimately cause closed-loop task failure. The paper introduces Dynamics-Risk Adaptive Plasticity (DRAP), a replay-free method that estimates the historical importance of adaptable model tensors from sample-level action gradients weighted by local trajectory-amplification risk. A ridge-regularized local linear model is fitted to expert state-action transitions, and the largest singular value of its state-transition matrix provides a policy-independent, Lyapunov-inspired estimate of one-step perturbation amplification. After each task, risk-weighted squared gradients are accumulated in tensor-level importance memory. During subsequent tasks, DRAP uses normalized cumulative importance to scale each tensor's effective AdamW learning rate, immediately reducing plasticity of components that supported previous behavior at dynamically sensitive trajectory points. DRAP is instantiated on a fixed KappaTune-selected subnetwork of OpenVLA and evaluated with replay-free sequential adaptation across the LIBERO Spatial, Object, and Goal suites. Compared with standard AdamW fine-tuning and EWC, results show substantially improved retention of previously learned behaviors while preserving strong new-suite acquisition.

## Key points

- In physical AI, small policy errors accumulate over trajectories and can cause closed-loop failure.
- DRAP is replay-free and weights gradients by local trajectory-amplification risk.
- A ridge-regularized local linear model estimates one-step perturbation amplification via the largest singular value.
- Tensor-level importance memory scales each tensor's effective AdamW learning rate on later tasks.
- On selected OpenVLA subnetworks across LIBERO suites, retention improves while new-task acquisition stays strong.

## Details

The method runs without replay buffers. It focuses on per-tensor plasticity control and trajectory-aware risk. Architecture choices and suite results are detailed in the linked source.
