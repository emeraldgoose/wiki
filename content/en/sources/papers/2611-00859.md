# Wave Forcing: Towards Speed-of-Light Streaming Video Generation

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.00859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00859) · **Published**: 2026-10-31
**Authors**: Yijie Jin, Yuxuan Liu, Jiajun Xu, Ye Huang, Chuzhi Wang, Jiayang Li, Ruiyang Chen, Chenling Hong, Xu Wang, Zhijie Deng

## Overview

Real-time long-video generation requires continuous output with consistent appearance and natural motion. Chunk-wise autoregressive methods face efficiency and quality challenges: serialized denoising and history-cache refresh require two forward passes even with one-step denoising, while longer rollouts can degrade quality and cause camera drift. Wave Forcing (WF) co-designs generation, training, and execution for continuous video output with multi-step denoising. It uses chunk-wise causal attention to access earlier, cleaner chunks before denoising the current chunk and adds a wave-forced context pipeline for reliable history propagation.

## Key points

- Continuous long-video generation needs stable appearance and motion.
- Serialized denoising adds forward-pass overhead.
- Longer rollouts can degrade quality and cause camera drift.
- WF supports continuous output with multi-step denoising.
- Chunk-wise causal attention accesses cleaner earlier chunks.
- A context pipeline propagates history more reliably.

## Details

The method targets both speed and temporal stability. By combining attention scheduling with a propagation pipeline, Wave Forcing avoids treating chunks as isolated denoising tasks. The goal is to reduce redundant computation while preserving long-form coherence. The approach spans model design and execution planning, emphasizing streaming video generation rather than offline clip synthesis.
