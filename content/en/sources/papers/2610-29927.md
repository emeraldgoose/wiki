# Your Benchmark Is an Empirical Measure Over Difficulty

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.29927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29927) · **Published**: 2026-10-21
**Authors**: Yifan Sun, Naicheng Yu, Jiawen Gong, Jingyan Shen, Huan Zhang

## Overview

A benchmark is not merely a collection of test questions; it naturally induces an empirical measure over difficulty. This paper develops a measure-based view of benchmark evaluation and uses it to identify and formalize two central failure modes. Measure mismatch occurs when the benchmark difficulty distribution differs from the intended difficulty distribution. Coverage gaps occur when uncovered regions along the difficulty axis prevent benchmarks from detecting meaningful localized training progress. The paper proposes two diagnostic metrics for these failure modes with rigorous theoretical guarantees. Empirically, it analyzes 25 popular benchmarks spanning 7 domains using carefully estimated item difficulties. The results show that many benchmarks implicitly overweight certain difficulty regions, so their leaderboard conclusions should be interpreted with caution; others leave substantial portions of the difficulty axis uncovered, limiting their ability to monitor model improvement. Finally, the paper turns diagnosis into action by introducing BenchFill, a simple difficulty-targeted item rewriting algorithm that effectively reduces local coverage gaps.

## Key points

- Benchmarks induce an empirical difficulty measure.
- Measure mismatch means benchmark and intended difficulty distributions differ.
- Coverage gaps hide localized training progress.
- Two diagnostic metrics are proposed with theoretical guarantees.
- 25 benchmarks across 7 domains were analyzed.
- BenchFill rewrites items to reduce local coverage gaps.

## Details

The diagnosis relies on carefully estimated item difficulties. Many benchmarks overweight certain difficulty regions, while others leave large portions of the difficulty axis uncovered. BenchFill is presented as a simple algorithm that converts the diagnosis into action by rewriting items toward undercovered difficulty regions.
