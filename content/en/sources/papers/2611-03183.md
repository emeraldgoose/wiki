# Auto-Research Agent on MSA Organization to Improve Protein-Protein Complex Structure Prediction

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03183) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Zilu Zhang, Jing Bao, Lifeng Qiao, Sheng Xu, Qiantai Feng, Siqi Sun, Yang Yang

## Overview

Protein-complex structure prediction with AlphaFold 3 depends on multiple sequence alignment content, depth, and cross-chain pairing. Current pipelines apply uniform recipes, typically maximizing depth and species-based pairing, yet even whether cross-chain pairing helps AlphaFold 3 remains contested. The paper shows that the value of both depth and pairing is strongly target-dependent, so no single recipe is optimal across interfaces. It introduces an autonomous agent that treats a frozen AlphaFold 3 as a black-box experimental instrument. For each target, the agent formulates biological hypotheses, analyzes and reorganizes the alignment, and iteratively refines sequence selection and pairing using predictor label-free signals alone.

## Key points

- MSA depth and pairing value vary substantially by protein-protein interface.
- No single fixed MSA recipe is optimal across all targets.
- The autonomous agent works without modifying AlphaFold 3 itself.
- Sequence construction and pairing policy both contribute to improvements.
- Deliberate non-pairing can sometimes be the better policy.
- The strongest gains appear for inter-species heteromers with unreliable species-based pairing.

## Details

Across 1,015 protein-protein interfaces from three benchmarks released after AlphaFold 3's training cutoff, the agent improves overall interface prediction against the default pipeline and fixed MSA recipes on every benchmark while using on average 60 percent fewer MSA sequences. Mean quality increases from 0.465 to 0.612 on FoldBench and from 0.482 to 0.562 on HD439 for inter-species heteromers. These results recast MSA design as target-specific and show test-time adaptation via biological reasoning and confidence-guided experimentation. The reported gains depend on the specific predictor, alignment sources, and target classes, so extension to other predicted structures requires its own validation.
