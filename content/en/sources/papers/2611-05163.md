# RoboTryOn: Synthesizing Dexterous Robot Demonstrations from Human Videos via Virtual Try-On

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05163) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Seungyong Lee, Suhyeok Jang, Minyoung Kim, Jinwoo Shin

## Overview

Robot foundation models perform well in control, yet adapting to a new task still relies on teleoperated demonstrations. Egocentric human videos offer a scalable substitute, but naive robot overlays or pose retargeting leaves significant visual or action gaps for high-degree-of-freedom embodiments. RoboTryOn treats human-to-robot translation as virtual try-on: it keeps the demonstrated motion and swaps the human for the target robot given a reference image, while preserving task, objects, and background. It translates selected keyframes autoregressively and guides video synthesis with motion conditioned on the human hand skeleton. An inverse dynamics model incorporating depth features infers action labels from synthesized videos for policy learning.

## Key points

- Human videos are translated into target-robot videos.
- Task, objects, and background are preserved during conversion.
- Selected keyframes are translated autoregressively.
- Motion is conditioned on the human hand skeleton.
- An inverse dynamics model infers action labels using depth features.
- Synthetic-data policies can improve novel-task success with lower collection cost.

## Details

Across multiple humanoid embodiments, policies trained with synthetic data improve average success on novel tasks, including challenging bimanual manipulation, from 2 to 60 percent without real demonstrations of those tasks. Training with synthetic data matches policies trained solely on teleoperated demonstrations at roughly one third of the collection cost. These gains depend on the quality of generated robot appearances, skeleton tracking, inverse dynamics predictions, and transfer to the target embodiment hardware.
