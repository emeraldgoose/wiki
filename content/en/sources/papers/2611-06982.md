# Decode Before You Hear: Speculative Listening for Chunk-Interleaved Full-Duplex Speech Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.06982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06982) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Yutong Bian, Yanglin Zhang, Yuxiang Wang, Luoyuan Zhang, Rongshen He, Zihao Wang, Junwen Qiu, Jiaqi Li, Zhizheng Wu

## Overview

Chunk-interleaved full-duplex speech language models listen and speak in fixed time chunks, and each chunk must be processed before the next arrives. Their workload is asymmetric: listen chunks leave compute idle while waiting for audio, whereas speak and tool-call chunks require serial generation, causing delays before responses and tool calls.

Speculative Listening exploits this idle time. Assuming silence for the next chunk, the model drafts it in advance. Observations show the next audio chunk does not change output in 95.3% of cases, and 88% of changes begin at the first output token. Using silence-based drafts plus a second branch from the runner-up first token, the method keeps hypotheses in a cross-chunk pool and verifies them when audio arrives.

On the TickTock-Bench eight-task benchmark, Speculative Listening speeds speak chunks by 2.6 to 2.8 times and tool-call chunks by up to 23.4 times. It reduces decoding delay before response starts by 73% to 74 to 241 milliseconds, near the 200-millisecond gap between human turns, and reduces maximum real-time lag by 94.4%.

## Key points

- Identifies idle compute during listen chunks in full-duplex systems.
- Uses silence as a predictive assumption for the next chunk.
- Notes 95.3% of next audio chunks do not change output.
- Notes 88% of changes begin at the first output token.
- Proposes training-free, lossless input-time self-speculative decoding.
- Achieves large speedups on speak and tool-call chunks.

## Details

The 13-page paper includes six figures and eight tables. The method is explicitly lossless and requires no model training changes.
