# Beam Model Technical Report

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.26358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26358) · **Published**: 2026-11-19 (v1)
**Authors**: Reflection Team et al.

## Overview

Beam is a sparse Mixture-of-Experts model with 501.3B total and 23.4B active parameters, designed and optimized for coding, reasoning, and agentic workloads. It delivers frontier-level reasoning while requiring 3–4x less inference compute than many models of its class. Pretraining consumed 23.8T diverse, curated, high-quality tokens, and a novel two-stage midtraining phase on verifiable, correctness-anchored data extended the effective context window to 1M tokens without degrading general capabilities.

Post-training centered on a large-scale, fully asynchronous reinforcement learning campaign across 10.5K GB300 GPUs, generating over 100M rollouts over four weeks — one of the largest RL runs conducted by an open lab to date. Capabilities kept improving as RL compute increased, with no sign of plateau; held-out scores during RL rose monotonically (e.g., BrowseComp improved from 50.1 at 27% of training to 57.9 at 83%, despite no search/browsing tasks before that point, showing cross-domain generalization). Expert merging used on-policy full-vocabulary distillation in the asynchronous RL system rather than weight averaging.

Serving efficiency comes from deep hardware–model co-design: FP8/NVFP4 quantization and a 1.93B-parameter speculative-decoding drafter (DFlash 2). On safety, averaged across conversation turns and harm categories, Beam refuses or pivots on 88.9% of harmful requests while still complying with 81.5% of harmless requests — the highest refusal/pivot rate among open-weight models on the Pareto frontier.

## Key points

- Architecture/scale: sparse MoE, 501.3B total / 23.4B active parameters; 23.8T pretraining tokens; 1M-token context via two-stage midtraining on correctness-anchored data.
- Post-training: fully asynchronous RL on 10.5K GB300 GPUs, 100M+ rollouts over 4 weeks; scores kept rising with compute (no plateau); experts merged via on-policy full-vocabulary distillation.
- Agentic/coding benchmarks: SWE-bench Verified 80.6, SWE-bench Multilingual 75.7, SWE-bench Pro v1 62.2, DeepSWE v1.1 43.4, Terminal-Bench v2.1 77.7, SWE Atlas Codebase QnA 29.3→32.5 (teacher vs merged student columns shown in report).
- Reasoning/knowledge: AIME 2026 97.3, GPQA Diamond 90.2, HLE (no tools) 36.1, SciCode 47.2; general capabilities include AA-LCR 66.3→78.7, LongBench v2 66.8, IFBench 77.
- Efficiency: 3–4x less inference compute than peer-class models; FP8/NVFP4 quantization; 1.93B-parameter DFlash 2 speculative drafter.
- Safety: 88.9% refusal/pivot on harmful requests vs 81.5% compliance on harmless ones — best refusal rate among open-weight models on the Pareto frontier.

## Details

Training pipeline: pretraining recipe covers depth-scaled residual connections, web/code/STEM/PDF data curation (including OCR internals with per-accelerator tuning, repetition detection, blank-page filtering, OCR-confidence filtering, HTML-to-Markdown conversion), deduplication and decontamination, and tail-patching with data-limited subsampling (DLS). Midtraining has two stages: stage 1 builds STEM/niche knowledge, competitive programming, software engineering (repository-level understanding, feature implementation), and terminal use; stage 2 extends context. Post-training includes an agentic expert trained with SFT then high-compute asynchronous RL (asynchronous rollouts, sampling/routing/numerical consistency, reward integrity, reasoning-effort and token-efficiency controls), plus task curation over SWE, terminal use, math/science, competitive coding, web search, professional knowledge work, and general tool use. An alignment teacher (SFT + RL) aligns the model to human values, and on-policy distillation merges the agentic and alignment experts. Held-out RL evaluation (pass@1, reasoning effort 512) shows scores rising with training progress across benchmarks, with no plateau.
