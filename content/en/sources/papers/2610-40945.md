# On the Origin and Evolution of Training-Inference Mismatch in LLM Reinforcement Learning

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40945) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Yu Li, Yufa Zhou, Tian Lan

## Overview

On-policy reinforcement learning for LLMs assumes training responses are sampled from the current policy. In practice, an inference engine often samples responses while a separate trainer computes gradients, creating a training-inference mismatch that can destabilize training.

This paper traces the mismatch to its origin in two system layers: infrastructure and algorithm. Infrastructure determines how far the sampler departs from the trainer, while the algorithm corrects mismatches that reach the gradient.

## Key points

- Identifies numerical discrepancy and self-induced policy lag as key sources of mismatch.
- Split sampled-token error into numerical discrepancy and policy lag.
- Corrects drift through centering and importance-style reweighting.
- Finds score centering stable with a quantized sampler but weaker with stale samplers.
- Importance sampling is better suited to staleness.
- PPO can train under staleness but fail under quantization.
- Analyzes validity range of importance weighting under bias and effective sample size.

## Details

The mismatch originates from deploying separate sampling and training systems, a trend pushed by asynchronous RL and efficient attention engines. The paper factorizes importance ratios at sampler weights and decomposes residual bias into prefix, continuation, token, and tail components.

Different corrections work in different regimes. Score centering keeps quantized-sampler training stable, while importance sampling performs better when the sampler is stale. Evaluation spans multiple sampler/trainer pairings, including quantized and FP8 samplers, as well as model size and asynchronous settings.
