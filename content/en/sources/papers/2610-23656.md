# ABF-Bench: A Verifiable Benchmark for Agentic Blockchain Forensics

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.23656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23656) · **Published**: 2026-10-16
**Authors**: Xiaohang Yu, Hejia Geng, William Knottenbelt

## Overview

Blockchain forensics is adversarial and open-ended: money launderers split, swap, and bridge funds, cut their trail at mixers or custodial services, and change tactics between incidents, while investigators must keep adapting. Because public ledgers are immutable, every recorded movement remains independently verifiable. ABF-Bench is an interactive benchmark that makes evidence-backed fund tracing a verifiable agent task, requiring every claimed movement to cite ledger records retrieved by the agent. A case-to-task pipeline builds well-posed tasks across ledgers: conservative rules halt tracing where records leave a continuation unresolved, and each task's reference graph is the answer those rules determine. The tasks are built from real laundering activity and combine multi-hop transfers, DeFi exchanges, cross-chain round trips, custodial and mixer deposits, and address poisoning. The paper also proposes an ABF agent harness built around working memory and evaluates it against general-purpose agents and fixed-order tracers. The harness reaches 75% edge recall versus 47% for Codex with the same model and 10% for Claude Code, which loses recall to overconfidence by citing unretrieved records. Compared with fixed-order tracers, the harness acquires evidence faster early in the budget and recognizes undisclosed custodial services that tracers walk past (89% versus 0%), with 98% fidelity. Agents tend to stop early and lose recall on larger tasks, keeping their final recall below the best tracer. ABF-Bench opens blockchain forensics to LLM agents as a verifiable domain where scoring verdicts may also provide training signals.

## Key points

- Every claimed movement must cite retrieved ledger records.
- The benchmark combines multi-hop transfers, DeFi exchanges, cross-chain round trips, mixers, and address poisoning.
- The ABF harness reached 75% edge recall versus 47% for Codex and 10% for Claude Code.
- The harness recognizes undisclosed custodial services that fixed-order tracers miss (89% versus 0%).
- Agents stop early and lose recall on larger tasks, so final recall trails the best tracer.

## Details

The benchmark is constructed with conservative halting rules that prevent fabricating continuations where records are incomplete. Its reference graph is therefore the answer the rules determine rather than a manually supplied ground truth. The working-memory harness is compared against both general-purpose coding agents and fixed-order tracers, separating evidence acquisition speed, recall, and fidelity.
