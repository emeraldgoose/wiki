# On the Opportunities and Challenges of Prefill-to-Autoregressive Cross-Model Transfer

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.06976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06976) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Zhenyi Shen, Junru Lu, Wei Liu, Jiangnan Ye, Jiazheng Li, Di Yin, Xing Sun, Tameem Adel, Yali Du, Lin Gui, Yulan He

## Overview

Prefill-to-Autoregressive Cross-model Transfer combines a larger model's prompt representations with a smaller model's efficient generation, balancing capability and inference cost through latent communication. PLatour provides an open PACT framework connecting frozen models through lightweight zero-initialized linear bridges trained directly with task rewards and supporting hybrid-attention architectures.

PLatour outperforms open-source baselines on generation tasks and surpasses reported Mostik results on shared mathematics and coding benchmarks using a substantially smaller sender. However, controlled experiments show gains on some tasks can be reproduced by uninformative sender signals, self-communication, or receiver-only training, meaning trainable bridges may adapt the receiver without exploiting useful sender information.

The paper introduces PACT-Check to quantify how much gain genuinely comes from communication. It concludes by discussing both opportunities and challenges of PACT systems.

## Key points

- Combines sender prefill representations with receiver autoregressive generation.
- Provides an open framework with zero-initialized linear bridges.
- Supports direct task-reward training and hybrid attention.
- Outperforms open baselines on generation tasks.
- Shows some gains may come from receiver adaptation rather than communication.
- Introduces PACT-Check to measure genuine communication gain.

## Details

The paper spans 35 pages, includes code, and focuses on a smaller sender achieving strong collaboration results. It emphasizes independent evaluation of large-small collaboration.
