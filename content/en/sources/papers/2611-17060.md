# Ropedia Xperience 2: A Gold Standard for Scaling Physical AI Environments and Behaviors

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.17060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17060) · **Published**: 2026-11-12
**Authors**: Hao Li, Fangzhou Hong, Zhaoxi Chen, Hui En Pang, Kairui Hu, Mingze Sun, Runmao Yao, Xinhao Ji, Xiyuan Zheng, Xuanli Chen, Yalun Dai, Yap Kim Hui, Dingwen Zhang, Yuanyuan Gao, Ziang Cao, Cheng Huang Yean, Jackson Peng, Rui Duan, Yaobo Liu, Zetong Wang, Ziwei Liu

## Overview

Large foundation models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 have recently been shown to control robots directly from visual observations. GPT-6 Astra attains a reported average success rate of 22.48% on the 42-task RoboDojo benchmark, surpassing the 40 public policies in that comparison, and reaches 92% on RxR instruction following and 82% on HM3D object search. Yet these models remain far from practical deployment: an evaluation across eight robot configurations reports no successes in 11 plug-insertion trials across three platforms and 9 successes in 23 navigation-and-search trials for GPT-6 Astra.

The paper argues that enabling reliable long-horizon control requires two complementary resources — demonstrations of human behavior and interactive environments — and poses the physical-AI analog of the scaling-law question: how does capability scale with environments and behaviors? As a data foundation, the Ropedia team introduces Ropedia Xperience 2, comprising 1,589 released metric-scale 3D scans and a collection of 4,230 egocentric recordings of 74 tasks totaling 131 hours, spanning 596 locations (443 of them homes). Capture needs a single operator with a handheld LiDAR scanner and the HOMIE head-mounted recorder — four global-shutter fisheye cameras, inertial measurements, and four-channel audio — with no robots and no motion-capture infrastructure on site.

An automated processing pipeline turns each scan into fisheye images with poses, a sparse COLMAP point model, a triangle mesh, and a 3D Gaussian splatting model in one metric scene frame, and turns each recording into synchronized video, a device trajectory, depth, hand and body motion, and hierarchical activity-language annotations; a registration pipeline places recordings into the scanned geometry. Ropedia Xperience 2 extends Xperience-10M, which provides about 10 million egocentric interaction clips totaling 10,000 hours, with metric scene scans and registration to them.

The reconstructions serve as reference geometry for three benchmarks — depth and geometry estimation, 3D object detection, and spatial reasoning — and each capture is reused along four routes to robot data: real-to-sim conversion, human-robot retargeting, virtual teleoperation, and long-horizon trajectory generation. Capture thus scales with operators rather than robots, and captured data can be expanded into robot data without further on-site collection.

## Key points

- Scale: 1,589 released metric-scale 3D scans; the broader collection inventory spans 596 locations, 74 task definitions, 4,230 recordings, and 131 hours; home interiors account for 1,389 scans (87.4% of the release).
- Quality control is explicit: of 1,610 completed scans with a release grade or delete decision, 1,589 (98.7%) are retained and 21 (1.3%) are deleted — 12 for door motion, 8 for failed reconstructions, 1 for moving people or vehicles; the retained set splits into Perfect (9.3%), Usable (58.8%), and Reuse (31.9%).
- Depth and geometry benchmark: following SpatialBench categories, models are evaluated on Sparse (5 frames), Medium (11–100), and Dense (335–500) inputs with depth AbsRel, pose AUC at 30 degrees, trajectory ATE, Chamfer distance, and surface F-score at 5 cm; pi-cubed achieves the lowest trajectory ATE and the highest surface F-score in all three settings, while DA3-Giant has the highest pose AUC in Medium and Dense.
- 3D object detection on 20 evaluation scenes: Boxer, which lifts open-vocabulary 2D detections to 3D boxes, leads on all three metrics — AP 25, AP 50, and mIoU at 0.25 — while the other methods stay below 7 AP 25, and LLaVA-3D and SceneScript produce no box reaching IoU 0.25.
- Spatial intelligence: 500 question-answer pairs from the 20 evaluation scenes are grouped into translation, rotation, distance, depth, height, and relative-position questions with MMSI-Bench's model roster; Qwen2.5-VL-7B scores 49.40% overall.
- Real-to-sim: a single scan becomes an interactive digital twin that preserves the original layout and appearance, incorporates realistic physical properties, and supports part-level object interaction.
- Human-robot retargeting converts recorded human behaviors, including long-horizon activities, into wheeled-humanoid demonstrations from estimated body and hand motion — with recordings slowed 2 times before retargeting — without on-site robot deployment.
- Virtual teleoperation lets operators collect robot demonstrations inside the converted simulation scenes, with automatic scene resets instead of manual physical resets.
- Long-horizon trajectory generation: one eight-subtask task (fetch a mug, heat it in the microwave, open a bedroom door, place it on the bedside table) runs as a single continuous rollout of 3,869–4,255 control steps at 30 Hz (2.2 minutes on average), taking 10–16 minutes of wall-clock per valid trajectory on one simulation worker, so more trajectories cost only compute.
- Context for the field: on the RoboDojo benchmark GPT-6 Astra's 22.48% average success still falls far short of practical reliability, which the authors use to motivate environment-and-behavior scaling data.

## Details

### A two-stage capture protocol

Each scene is first scanned once with the handheld 3D scanner, whose freely chosen viewpoints observe high, low, and partially occluded regions; the assigned tasks are then recorded with HOMIE, each recording beginning before the task and ending after it. One scan provides the spatial context for multiple recordings. The 4,230 uploaded recordings concentrate in home scenes: placing, taking out, and folding dominate in bedrooms, and wiping in living rooms and bathrooms. Because geometry is measured by LiDAR, the shared scene frame is metric.

### Registration and what it enables

The registration pipeline brings body motion, camera poses, and language into the scene's metric frame, with global initialization followed by localization and fitting, plus independent checks and labels; registered recordings support retargeting and simulation-based reuse. The scan exports (COLMAP sparse model, triangle mesh, 3DGS model) share the scene frame, which is what allows the same capture to feed benchmarks, simulators, and demonstration pipelines at once.

### Four routes from one capture

The paper demonstrates real-to-sim conversion scaling environments and the other three routes scaling behaviors. In the trajectory-generation demonstration, each of the eight subtasks is a policy program acting through a whole-body controller that observes objects only through perception, not simulator state, and the eight run in one continuous rollout without resets; success is checked from the simulator's object states and task rules such as nothing tipped or dropped and appliances left as required. The authors position these routes as lower-cost complements to real-robot teleoperation, at different levels of fidelity, as the substrate for scaling laws of physical AI over environments and behaviors.
