# Probing Spatial State Representations in Visual Foundation Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40636) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Baiqiao Yin, Zhongang Cai, Ruisi Wang, Qiwei Liang, Lei Yang, Ziwei Liu

## Overview

Visual foundation models excel at recognizing individual images, but spatial perception requires a persistent state that tracks objects, locations, and geometry across views. This paper probes whether such models internally encode spatial state representations rather than only current-frame appearance.

It defines a spatial state as the configuration of the environment relative to the observer, including unseen objects and updated self-location. The study evaluates four levels of capability: spatial structure, object persistence, predictive spatial update, and actionability, ordered by the amount of information they require beyond the current observation.

## Key points

- Introduces a controlled probing framework for spatial state in visual foundation models.
- Tests spatial structure, object persistence, predictive update, and actionability.
- Finds that capabilities differ across representations and layers.
- Shows that spatial readout depends on layer choice and streaming prefix.
- Studies how prediction changes the spatial readout interface.
- Connects represented state to actionability and goal-directed progress.

## Details

The paper argues that an observer sees only part of the world and must still track hidden objects and account for self-motion. Classical object-file theory, spatial updating, and path integration motivate the four probed levels.

Rather than assuming an explicit 3D map, the work probes whether available representations support spatial inference. It examines temporal prefixes, interventions, controls, and predictive objectives to determine what spatial information is accessible and how robust the readout is.
