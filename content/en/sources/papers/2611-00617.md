# Words Caught in the Heart: Simulating Person-Specific Expression for Self-Opaque Clients in Psychotherapy

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.00617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00617) · **Published**: 2026-10-31
**Authors**: Mengxi Xiao, Jingjing Chen, Ziyan Kuang, Jiarong Song, Wenhao Zhao, Zuchen Han, Yihuai Xu, Xiyang Huang, Jie Gong, Yuechen Jiang, Xueqing Peng, Yankai Chen, Xiaoyan Qu, Min Peng, Qianqian Xie

## Overview

Psychotherapy clients may experience distress before they can coherently describe it, and their expression changes with emotion, perceived safety, and trust. Large language model-based client simulators often rely on clinically organized case descriptions, which can encourage responses that explain a client's difficulties more clearly than their current understanding warrants. Pandora introduces an explicit model of the process by which psychological experience becomes situated, individual expression. It tracks psychological and relational states across therapist interventions, constructs subjective appraisals and attributions, and plans the intended response content and strategy. A separate expression planner then combines those decisions with language patterns distilled from the client's own utterances to guide linguistic realization, aiming at expression that reflects individual experience rather than generic clinical narration.

## Key points

- Pandora separates the planning of therapeutic content and stance from the linguistic realization of that content.
- Psychological and relational states are updated in response to therapist interventions, supporting moment-to-moment client simulation.
- The expression planner distills language patterns from the client's utterances instead of relying solely on case descriptions.
- Pandora reaches the highest expression similarity and disclosure-alignment score among five baselines on 115 cases from four counseling and motivational interviewing datasets.
- Removing or skipping expression planning increases AI-like judgments in the evaluated human study.
- The ablation results support explicit expression planning for better alignment and less perceived AI-like expression.

## Details

The evaluation covers 115 cases across four counseling and motivational interviewing datasets. In a four-evaluator study of 45 matched disclosure items, Pandora receives AI-like judgments in 7.2% of ratings, compared with 43.3-67.2% for baselines and 35.6% without expression planning. These figures are specific to the compared settings and should be read alongside the datasets and evaluator protocol. The source paper also includes ablations, case retrieval, evaluation metric definitions, and qualitative case studies that situate the quantitative results.
