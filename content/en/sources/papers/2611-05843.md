# Dual Flow: Joint Image-Measurement Interpolation for Inverse Problems

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05843) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Sevket Ege Yesilyurt, Figen S. Oktem

## Overview

Generative model-based approaches to linear inverse problems — deblurring, super-resolution, compressed sensing and their relatives — almost always treat the acquired noisy measurement as a conditioning signal for sampling in the image domain: a diffusion or flow prior over images is steered by data consistency terms, guidance, or posterior sampling schemes. Dual Flow departs from this pattern by refusing to keep the measurement on the sidelines. It formulates the task as a joint estimation problem: estimate the clean image and the corresponding noise-free measurement together from the single noisy observation, using a coupled flow model that runs in the image and measurement domains at the same time.

The mechanics are asymmetric in a useful way. The acquired noisy measurement is used only to initialize the measurement state; from then on, the image and measurement states are jointly updated based on their current values, with no further direct dependence on the raw observation. Because both states evolve under the coupled flow, the final image and measurement estimates are guaranteed by construction to satisfy the underlying forward model — the pair is interpolated to a point where image and measurement agree with the physics that connects them.

The paper derives the sampling updates for this joint flow, which involve the conditional mean of the clean image given the joint state, and proves a closed-form expression for a sufficient statistic of the joint state. That reduction matters practically: the required conditional mean can be obtained by solving an image-domain Gaussian denoising problem — one whose noise is operator-dependent and correlated — so a single velocity-prediction network trained under this formulation estimates the conditional mean from the derived sufficient statistic. Experiments across various linear inverse problems and image datasets report state-of-the-art performance in both distortion metrics and perceptual quality, with no hyperparameter tuning beyond the number of inference steps and shorter reconstruction times than competing methods.

## Key points

- Joint formulation: image reconstruction and measurement denoising are cast as one coupled estimation problem, solved by coupled flows in the image and measurement domains rather than by conditioning an image-only sampler on the measurement.
- One-time use of the observation: the noisy measurement only initializes the measurement state; afterwards the joint updates depend solely on the current image and measurement states.
- Forward-model consistency by construction: the final image and measurement estimates satisfy the underlying forward model, so the reconstruction and its denoised measurement are mutually consistent outputs of the same trajectory.
- Theoretical reduction: the joint-flow sampling update requires the conditional mean of the clean image given the joint state, and this conditional mean is computable from a closed-form sufficient statistic of the joint state.
- Practical training target: the conditional mean reduces to an image-domain Gaussian denoising problem with operator-dependent, correlated noise; a velocity-prediction network is trained to estimate it from the sufficient statistic.
- Empirical results: state-of-the-art performance across various linear inverse problems and image datasets in both distortion metrics (e.g., error-based measures) and perceptual quality.
- Usability: no hyperparameter tuning is needed beyond selecting the number of inference steps, and reconstruction times are shorter than those of competing methods.
- Beyond the point estimate: additional analyses cover robustness to model mismatch, generation of diverse plausible reconstructions, and characterization of reconstruction uncertainty.

## Details

### How the joint flow is set up

The model maintains two states — an image state and a measurement state — governed by coupled flows. Initialization is the only place the raw data enters: the noisy measurement seeds the measurement state, while the image state starts from its prior. From that point the sampler alternates joint updates in which each state's velocity depends on the joint state of the system. The estimation of the clean image is thereby intertwined with the denoising of the measurement: as the measurement state is purified, it constrains the image state through the forward operator, and as the image sharpens it feeds back into what a plausible noise-free measurement looks like.

### Why the sufficient statistic matters

Sampling updates for the joint flow would naively require expectations over the joint posterior. The paper's closed-form sufficient statistic collapses what the sampler needs to know about the joint state into a quantity from which the conditional mean of the clean image can be computed. Because that conditional mean can be cast as a Gaussian denoising problem — with a noise structure that depends on the forward operator and is correlated across pixels — the whole sampler reduces to repeated evaluation of a trained denoising-type network. This is what makes the method concrete: a single velocity-prediction network, trained under the derived formulation, serves every update.
