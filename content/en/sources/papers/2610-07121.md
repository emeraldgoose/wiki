# SoloQ: Calibration-Free Quantization for Diffusion Language Models

**Source**: Donghyun Lee et al., arXiv, 2026-10-05

**Lead**: Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) face steep deployment costs from repeated full-sequence denoising and KV-cache memory pressure. Existing post-training quantization methods require calibration data, but activation distributions shift across masking states and denoising steps. SoloQ introduces a calibration-free framework using a structured K-RPBH rotation with lightweight rescaling, yielding predictable marginal distributions that enable data-independent 4-bit quantization.

## Background

Autoregressive LLMs have dominated language modeling, but diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) such as LLaDA, Dream, Fast-dLLM v2, and Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion offer a bidirectional alternative. dLLMs generate tokens through iterative denoising, which requires many forward passes. Full-sequence dLLMs repeat compute-intensive passes over the entire sequence; block-diffusion variants add a persistent KV-cache. Both properties make low-bit quantization highly desirable.

Prior quantization approaches for dLLMs rely on calibration data to determine quantization parameters. However, activation distributions in dLLMs shift significantly across denoising steps and masking configurations, rendering static calibration ineffective. Calibration also adds deployment friction and data-dependency.

## SoloQ Method

SoloQ maps weights and activations into a normalized rotated basis using a structured Kronecker-product Random Permuted Block Hadamard (K-RPBH) rotation. This rotation yields predictable marginal distributions—near-Gaussian and near-uniform—enabling distribution-matched codebooks without any calibration data.

Key components:
- **Structured K-RPBH rotation**: Efficiently computable, hardware-friendly, and preserves expressivity.
- **Scale-only bias correction**: A lightweight rescaling step applied at commit time for weights and at runtime for activations, correcting residual distribution mismatch.
- **KV-cache quantization**: For block-diffusion models, SoloQ quantizes the persistent KV-cache at commit time without perturbing the actively denoised block.

The predictable post-rotation distribution also enables direct mapping to hardware-native NVFP4 (4-bit floating point) formats.

## Results

Across LLaDA, Dream, Fast-dLLM v2, and Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion at multiple scales:
- SoloQ retains accuracy under 4-bit weight–activation quantization, outperforming calibration-based baselines on knowledge- and reasoning-intensive benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, BBH, ARC).
- With NVFP4, peak memory is reduced by up to **2.61×** and end-to-end inference latency by up to **2.24×**.
- The method adds negligible overhead: rotation and rescaling are folded into the model or executed as a single kernel.

## Relevance to SW Engineers

- **Deployment**: SoloQ removes the calibration-data requirement, simplifying the quantization pipeline for dLLMs in production.
- **Hardware efficiency**: NVFP4 support aligns with Blackwell GPUs and future accelerator roadmaps.
- **Memory-constrained environments**: 2.6× memory reduction enables larger dLLMs on fixed GPU memory budgets.
- **Open research**: The structured rotation approach generalizes beyond dLLMs to other architectures with shifting activation distributions.
