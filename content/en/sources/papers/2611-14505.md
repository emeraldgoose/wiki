# Continual-ARC: Measuring Skill Acquisition over a Working Lifetime

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.14505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505) · **Published**: 2026-11-11
**Authors**: Daniel Lenton, Yusha Arif, Haris Mahmood

## Overview

Continual-learning benchmarks for LLM systems tend to have three blind spots: they stay inside one applied domain, they serve each task instance only once, and they quietly assume a single family of learner. As a result, memory-based systems (agent harnesses that persist notes, files, and skills) and weight-based systems (fine-tuning and distillation) are almost never scored on the same scale, even though both claim to turn experience into skill. The authors — an Unify team — build a benchmark that removes all three blind spots.

Continual-ARC is a lifelong task stream assembled from the three ARC-AGI editions, whose puzzles require no domain knowledge. Tasks do not disappear after one exposure: they return after dormant gaps with freshly generated instances while new tasks keep arriving. On each instance the learner may pay from a shared feedback budget — buying demonstrations, submitting answers (incorrect submissions are paid for, with retries permitted), or acting inside its harness. An instance is scored by the share of that budget it consumes, and because a learner may keep anything between instances, a harness with persistent files and a weight update are judged by exactly the same yardstick.

After the stream ends, the learner is frozen and faces two tests: fresh instances of the tasks it already met, which measures memorization (S_seen), and unseen tasks, which measures generalization (S_held). The paper compares agent harnesses with frozen weights — prime-agent, hermes-agent, and openclaw, as shipped and with persistence on — against two prompting floors, a program-induction reference, and Qwen3.5-9B weights trained with DAgger to imitate GLM-5.3 Flash. A second, collective regime lets one system learn from several users at once.

## Key points

- Demo-cache floors (stateless except for caching each task's paid demonstrations) saved S_seen = 0.37 for Qwen3.5-9B and 0.44 for GLM-5.3 Flash — already a bar most harnesses failed to beat, while saving nothing on unseen tasks (S_held −0.01/−0.02).
- Program-induction libraries beat the caches on memorization (S_seen 0.65 and 0.81) but saved almost nothing on held-out tasks (0.03/0.05), since a program verified on one task's demonstrations does not solve another.
- Harnesses as shipped: across three harnesses, four models, and three effort levels, only GLM-5.3 Flash saved measurably (S_seen 0.25); Qwen's persistence saved −0.01, and neither expert guidance, nor asked-to-store prompts, nor a teacher's 106 notes and 104 files moved Qwen beyond S_seen 0.01–0.06.
- Explicitly asking harnesses to "save very liberally" after every verdict (H-A) helped only prime-agent (S_seen 0.42 on GPT-6 Luna, 0.26 on GLM) — hermes-agent (0.18) and openclaw (0.07) stayed within noise.
- Expert guidance that told harnesses to write, verify, and reuse Python programs unlocked real memorization: GE saved S_seen 0.74, GPT-6-Luna-driven harnesses 0.62–0.72, and Qwen reached 0.34 only when the teacher wrote its notes under the same guidance (QETE).
- On generalization no harness method saved anything measurable — despite Re-ARC deriving all 400 ARC-AGI-1 training rules from just 160 shared primitive functions. In contrast, DAgger-trained Qwen weights saved S_held = 0.29 (about half of its S_seen 0.57) and solved 47–52 of 100 held-out tasks versus 18 for untrained Qwen in prime-agent.
- In the collective regime, DAgger with two users (50 tasks) reached S_held 0.40 and with four users 0.39 — diminishing returns beyond two users.

## Details

Scoring is budget-based rather than accuracy-based: U(ℐ), the budget consumed over a stream of instances, is compared against a stateless reset run U₀ and a perfect-memory bound U*, giving a "share learned" L = (U₀ − U)/(U₀ − U*). Streams are deterministic, so every system meets the same instances in the same order, and 95% intervals come from task resamples. Reported results are on ARC-AGI-1, with early ARC-AGI-2 and ARC-AGI-3 tracks in the appendices; a private held-out set is used to check that test tasks were genuinely unseen.

The harness findings are traced to specific design steps in an appendix: prime-agent's Python skills did not even load in the authors' runs, hermes-agent's stored advice changed from instance to instance, and openclaw's consolidation could not promote its stores into behavior. Untrained Qwen3.5-9B could barely drive prime-agent at all (18 of 100 held-out solves, versus 31 for a stateless loop), so part of what DAgger learned was simply how to operate the harness; providing the untrained model expert guidance lifted held-out solves to 22–33, still below DAgger's 47–52, which indicates DAgger also learned something about ARC itself. Appendix results cover interruption handling, what each harness persists, per-user scaling, and recorded experiment spending. The paper is v3 (15 November 2026) of the HF-listed submission; the benchmark code, adapters, and run records are released, while the private set's rules are withheld by design.
