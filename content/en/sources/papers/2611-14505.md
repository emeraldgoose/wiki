# Continual-ARC: Measuring Skill Acquisition over a Working Lifetime

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.14505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505) · **Published**: 2026-11-11
**Authors**: Daniel Lenton, Yusha Arif, Haris Mahmood

## Overview

Agent harnesses have become a common way to study continual learning together with test-time parametric learning, yet existing benchmarks are limited. Most focus on a single domain, present each task once, and assume one family of learner, so memory-based and weight-based systems are rarely compared fairly.

Continual-ARC is proposed as a lifelong evaluation stream built from the three ARC-AGI editions. Tasks require no domain knowledge and reappear after dormant gaps with fresh instances while new tasks keep arriving. On each instance the learner can pay for demonstrations, submit answers, or act inside the harness, with retries permitted at a cost. Scoring is based on the share of the feedback budget consumed.

Because the learner may retain anything between instances, memory-based and weight-based approaches are evaluated on the same scale. After the stream the learner is frozen and tested on fresh instances of seen tasks to measure memorization, and on unseen tasks to measure generalization.

## Key points

- Continual-ARC provides a lifelong ARC-AGI stream with repeated tasks, fresh instances, and a unified feedback budget.
- The benchmark enables direct comparison of memory-based and weight-based continual learners.
- Experiments with frozen-weight harness agents on Qwen3.5-9B and GLM-5.3 Flash show persistence alone rarely improves memorization without explicit expert guidance.
- Harness-based methods still underperform a simple demonstration-caching floor across models.
- Harness-based methods gain little on unseen tasks, whereas weight-based learning shows substantial gains, suggesting future work on weight-based continual learning to drive harnesses.

## Details

The harness framework allows paying for demonstrations, submitting answers, and in-harness action with paid retries. Each instance score reflects feedback budget usage. The evaluation separates memorization on revisited tasks from generalization on novel tasks.

The authors test frozen-weight agents with and without persistence, compare prompting floors, a program-induction method, and a weight-imitation setup where Qwen3.5-9B is trained to imitate GLM-5.3 Flash. Results indicate that persistence in a harness does not reliably improve memorization and is often outperformed by caching demonstrations. Weight-based adaptation shows clearer generalization improvements.
