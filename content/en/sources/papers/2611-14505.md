# Continual-ARC: Measuring Skill Acquisition over a Working Lifetime

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505)
**Published**: 2026-11-15 (v3)

## Overview

Continual-ARC is a lifelong benchmark built from the three ARC-AGI editions, designed to measure how agent harnesses acquire skills over a working lifetime rather than on a single-pass task. It addresses three limitations of current continual-learning benchmarks: they generally focus on one applied domain, serve each instance only once, and assume a single learner family, so memory-based and weight-based systems are rarely compared on the same scale.

The benchmark presents tasks requiring no domain knowledge. Known tasks return after dormant gaps with fresh instances, while new tasks keep arriving. On each instance, the learner may pay for demonstrations, submit answers, or act inside the harness; retries on incorrect submissions are paid for. Each instance is scored on the share of its feedback budget consumed, which places memory-based and weight-based learners on the same scale. After the stream, the learner is frozen and tested on fresh instances of tasks it met (memorization) and on unseen tasks (generalization).

The authors run a frozen-weight agent harness with and without persistence on Qwen3.5-9B and GLM-5.3 Flash, compared against two prompting floors, a program-induction method, and Qwen3.5-9B trained to imitate GLM-5.3 Flash.

## Key points

- Persistence in the harness generally does **not** improve memorization unless provided with explicit expert guidance (persisting rules, digests, and advice does not help on its own).
- Most harness-based methods still learn **less** than a floor that simply caches each task's demonstrations, across all tested models.
- Harness-based methods gain little or nothing on unseen tasks, whereas weight-based learning shows substantial gains.
- The benchmark scores memory and weight learners on the same feedback-budget scale, enabling a like-for-like comparison that earlier single-domain, single-family benchmarks could not provide.

## Details

The paper identifies two learning regimes, the individual and the collective, and describes harness implementation details in Appendix D, including interruptions, the server's window and give-ups. Appendix E examines what harnesses persist and how faithfully, covering prime-agent on GPT-6 Luna (nothing crossed a session), prime-agent on GLM-5.3 Flash (rules the digest seldom shows), hermes-agent (advice that changes every instance), and openclaw (a consolidation that cannot promote). Appendices F and H cover harnesses asked to store with expert guidance and the methods applied to open models.

The frozen-learner protocol — test on fresh instances of met tasks versus unseen tasks — separates memorization from generalization. Weight-based learning showed particular strength on unseen tasks, opening the door to weight-based continual learning driving harness behavior in new domains.
