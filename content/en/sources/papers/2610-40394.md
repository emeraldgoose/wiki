# Scaling Mathematical Proof Supervision from Structured Textbooks

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40394) · **Published**: 2026-10-29

## Overview

Mathematical supervision for language models is usually flattened into isolated problem–solution pairs, whether the source is competition archives, web exercises, or synthetic augmentation. A textbook, by contrast, is a structured body of knowledge: definitions introduce concepts, theorems develop them, proofs establish them, and exercises reinforce them — all arranged in a chapter hierarchy that encodes prerequisites and pedagogical progression. TOME-Math (Textbook-Oriented Mathematical Evidence) is a large-scale corpus that preserves that structure instead of discarding it: 380,292 proof-oriented QA records built from 2,118 mathematics textbooks spanning 29 subjects, with chapter order, proposition–proof links, dependency references, and source provenance retained.

Structure is kept at three levels. Hierarchical structure retains the native organization of chapters, propositions, and their ordering. Dependency structure connects theorem-level proof questions with the intermediate proof obligations derived from gaps in their proofs — Gap QA records make a locally omitted proof obligation explicit. Curriculum structure organizes supervision by textbook order and proof granularity, so training can proceed over related examples rather than independent pairs. A separate inventory holds the textbooks' own exercises, which also serve as held-out evaluation material.

The effectiveness test is supervised fine-tuning of Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base on the 364,631-record training split (64 evaluation books and their 15,661 records are excluded for book-level separation). Across six evaluations — TOME-LongForm (300 proof-heavy exercises from held-out books, scored by a reference-guided step verifier with eight independent reviews per solution), TOME-Objective, MiniF2F, ProofNet, MATH-500, and AIME 2026 — the unweighted mean rises from 29.17% to 45.83%, with the best score on five of six benchmarks and second on AIME 2026.

The comparison is not a story about corpus size. OpenMath1 contains 1.82M examples — 4.8 times TOME-Math — yet scores lower on all six benchmarks (29.13 vs 45.83 average), and similarly sized MetaMath (395K) reaches only 34.64. Controlled studies then isolate what the structure buys: structure-guided retrieval lifts proof-generation pass@1 from 13.25% to 17.38% at a matched context budget, forward textbook curricula beat shuffled and reversed orderings in all 18 subject–scale cells, and spreading a fixed token budget across twice as many books adds 3.3 points on average.

## Key points

- Corpus: 380,292 Concept and Gap QA records from 2,118 textbooks across 29 subjects, plus a separate inventory of textbook-authored exercises; chapter progression, proposition–proof links, dependency references, and source provenance are all retained.
- Full-corpus SFT on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base: 29.17% to 45.83% unweighted six-benchmark mean (+16.7), best on five of six evaluations (TOME-LongForm 43.86, TOME-Objective 43.96, MiniF2F 57.43, ProofNet 42.98, MATH-500 76.55), second on AIME 2026.
- Size is not the explanation: OpenMath1 (1.82M examples, 4.8 times larger) averages 29.13 and loses on all six benchmarks; MetaMath (395K) averages 34.64; MathInstruct (262K) averages 41.10.
- The largest margins over the strongest public baseline are on the proof-oriented evaluations: TOME-LongForm 43.86 vs 35.87 (MathInstruct) and ProofNet 42.98 vs 32.93.
- Evaluation is book-held-out: 64 books reserved for testing, all 15,661 of their QA records excluded from training; TOME-LongForm problems are scored with 16 samples per problem, eight gpt-5.6-luna reviews each, and acceptance only when no review finds a substantive error.
- Structure-guided retrieval (training-free) improves proof-generation pass@1 from 13.25% to 17.38% under a matched context budget, showing the dependency structure helps at inference time, not just training time.
- Curriculum: across six subjects and three budgets (0.75M/1.8M/2.4M tokens), Forward–Gap wins all 18 subject–scale cells; its macro-average pass@16 rises 45.38% to 50.93% with scale and its margin over Shuffle widens from 4.34 to 7.72 points.
- Chapter direction dominates the curriculum effect: forward vs reverse ordering is worth 5.65/7.41/8.50 points at the three scales, while Gap-first ordering adds a smaller but consistent 2.37/1.91/1.53 over Forward–Mix.
- Source breadth is a distinct scaling axis: at the same 0.75M-token budget, spreading supervision over 16 books instead of 8 improves every subject by 1.6–4.8 points (3.3 average), with topology (+4.8) and differential geometry (+4.4) gaining most.
- Cross-subject transfer is strong: the macro-average target-normalized coefficient is 0.92, eight of 30 ordered pairs exceed 1.0, and both topology and differential geometry checkpoints reach 1.29 on probability targets — subject-specific supervision develops reusable proof patterns.

## Details

- Record types: Concept QA and Gap QA, where Gap QA derives from proof gaps — a theorem-level question is linked to the intermediate proof obligations its published proof leaves implicit — and exercises are kept in a separate inventory rather than merged into the QA stream.
- Six core evaluation subjects (largest usable textbook collections): abstract algebra, differential geometry, discrete mathematics, mathematical analysis, probability, and topology; TOME-LongForm balances 300 problems across these subjects and across the earlier and later halves of each book.
- Verifier design: each of the 16 sampled solutions is reviewed eight times by gpt-5.6-luna against a reference-guided rubric and accepted only if no review identifies a substantive error; the pessimistic verifier was human-calibrated (Appendix D).
- Curriculum protocol: within each subject–scale cell, Shuffle, Forward–Gap, Reverse–Gap, and Forward–Mix reorder the same frozen QA set with identical examples, token budgets, initialization, and optimization settings, measured by pass@16 on held-out-book exercises.
- The breadth study holds the Forward–Gap 0.75M-token budget fixed and varies only the number of source books (8 vs 16); per-subject narrow–dense scores are algebra 46.8, analysis 65.6, probability 42.0, differential geometry 32.0, discrete mathematics 41.6, and topology 44.4.
- The authors position the controlled retrieval, curriculum, breadth, and transfer studies as the bridge to the full corpus: scaling adds source coverage and cross-domain supervision without changing example counts alone. The corpus will be released; project page is tome-math.github.io.
