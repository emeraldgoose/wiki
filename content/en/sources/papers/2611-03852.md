# Why Pay for Long Reasoning When Rewrite Do Trick? Causal Effects of Reasoning History Compaction on Future Generation and Agent Cost

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03852) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Anton Hristov

## Overview

Reasoning substantially improves language models' ability to solve difficult problems, but long reasoning traces make these gains expensive. Efficient reasoning should express useful analysis in fewer tokens by reducing repetition and low-value text. Progress in proprietary models motivates this goal, yet their hidden scratchpads limit direct study of how it is achieved. The paper investigates whether open-weight models can generate less verbose reasoning by changing the form of their own earlier thoughts, without retraining. Its intervention rewrites prior reasoning into compact state records before pending tool results are revealed, then compares continuations from original and compact histories. Across tested models, accepted compact histories usually shorten future reasoning, and a coding case study shows less repeated verification after a shared candidate solution. In software-agent deployments, compaction can reduce total spending, although task quality and rewrite-inclusive time depend on the target and compressor. These findings identify reasoning history as one way to reduce future reasoning-token use and motivate model-specific compaction policies evaluated for both efficiency and retained capability.

## Key points

- Long reasoning improves problem solving but makes gains expensive.
- The study rewrites prior reasoning into compact state records without retraining the model.
- Accepted compact histories usually shorten future reasoning.
- A coding case study shows less repeated verification after a shared candidate solution.
- In agent deployments, compaction can reduce spending, but quality and latency depend on model and compressor.

## Details

The intervention manipulates the form of past reasoning rather than adding retrieval or tools. Tradeoffs between efficiency, fidelity, and rewrite cost are discussed in the linked source.
