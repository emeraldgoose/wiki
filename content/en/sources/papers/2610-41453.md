# Evidence-Guided Recovery for AI Agents with Ambiguous Tool Outcomes and Partial Execution

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.41453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41453) · **Published**: 2026-10-30
**Authors**: Genliang Zhu, Xiaobo Chen, Ziyuan Wang, Chu Wang

## Overview

When a tool call times out, an AI agent is left in a genuinely ambiguous state: the action may have failed, may have committed on the provider side, or may still be in flight, while other steps of the workflow have already completed. Repeating the entire workflow can duplicate irreversible effects such as double-charged fees or repeated notifications; stopping can leave legitimate obligations unfulfilled. The paper frames this as evidence-guided task recovery: the agent reasons over approved obligations, declared provider capabilities, and the states that remain compatible with public evidence, instead of treating an HTTP error as a certificate that nothing happened.

Two mechanisms carry the design. A shared admissibility check screens every proposed action for key expiry, authorization timing, dependencies, and residual effects before execution. A terminal certificate then separates actual private goal satisfaction from publicly supported safe completion, keeping unknown and handoff outcomes explicit; guarantees are tied to the declared provider capabilities rather than to assumed third-party exactly-once execution. The known-model implementation pairs standard observation-contingent planning with a separately implemented effect-history adjudicator.

The empirical work has two parts. A finite study compares five policies on 37 assignments. A preregistered, assignment-held-out comparison then asks whether model-facing planning advice helps two local models recover, with every one of the 116 recorded trajectories independently replayed.

## Key points
- Across 37 assignments, planning and three active controls (verify–wait rules, capability rules, and greedy selection) each certify 28 safe completions; immediate stop certifies only 2, corresponding to goals already satisfiable at the start.
- No policy arm produced a recovery-introduced invariant violation or a false completion claim; three assignments per arm carried an absolute historical violation from the outset.
- The completion tie hides a resource trade-off: joint planning uses 188 synthetic fee units and 103 logical actions versus 192 and 105 for capability rules, saving four fee units and two actions at the cost of 9.585 additional selection seconds.
- In the preregistered assignment-held-out study, 58 paired model–assignment units across 19 task families ran with two local models, openai/gpt-oss-20b and zai-org/glm-4.7-flash, under one common shield.
- Planning advice certified 30 of 58 completions versus 9 under evidence prompting — a 36.2-percentage-point difference (exact family sign-flip p = 0.0078); 21 pairs were completed only with advice, 9 by both, and 28 by neither.
- Advice also used fewer resources: 272 proposals versus 399 and 202,508 versus 282,293 observed prompt-plus-completion tokens, with rejected unsafe proposals falling from 317 to 123 and false completion claims from 218 to 76.
- All 116 trajectories pass independent Python replay, and both models show a positive completion difference for advice.
- The frozen configuration used temperature 0, a 2,048-token input cap, a 96-token output cap, a reasoning budget of 128, and at most eight model proposals per episode.
- Advice executes 101 admissible actions versus 34 under evidence prompting; four episodes in each condition are already certifiable without any model call.

## Details

### The recovery formulation
Each task is specified as approved obligations, the provider capabilities declared by a contract, and the public history an agent may observe. A common action check handles key expiry, authorization timing, dependencies, and residual effects identically for every condition, so the zero-new-violation result is attributed to the shared execution contract rather than to any planner. Certification enumerates every future effect prefix, including equal-time serializations: a compensation followed by a restoring write is rejected, a finite same-valued replacement remains certifiable, and an unknown-delay sentinel stays unresolved.

### Study one: five policies on 37 assignments
The deterministic frame uses 20 synthetic templates and independent effect-history adjudication. Joint planning incurs 188 fee units and 103 logical actions, capability rules 192 and 105, verify–wait rules 191 and 106, and greedy selection 195 fee units with only 94 actions — no arm dominates all resource measures. Each active arm leaves six handoffs and three other unresolved assignments. A retained earlier collection, in which a sequential depth-first planner produced 27 completions instead of 28, shows why completed search layers must be compared under one objective: that planner repeatedly waited on the missed retirement assignment and never activated the replacement by the deadline.

### Study two: preregistered model comparison
The held-out axis is the latent assignment: after an eight-assignment pilot, all 29 remaining assignments join two models and two strategies, giving 116 episodes and 58 matched pairs. The evidence-prompt condition exposes public recovery evidence and the action schema; the advice condition additionally supplies the bounded planner's recommended next action, while the model still proposes and the common shield still decides admissibility. Advice certified 30 of 58 units (51.7%) versus 9 (15.5%), executed 101 admissible actions versus 34, and reduced proposals from 399 to 272. Both LM Studio-based bundles completed their entire frozen frames without interface errors or output truncation.

### Scope and limitations
The comparison measures the evaluated advice interface under a common shield; search-based versus rule-derived advice was not isolated as a causal mechanism, and rule-derived advice and a legal-action-list interface were not model conditions. Synthetic fee units, logical actions, and durations are reported separately from monetary prices and wall latency, and selection wall time is only a compute proxy. Native persistence was qualified through eight executor cases, with an Atomix demo and an UndoBench smoke run kept as pipeline qualifications rather than model baselines.
