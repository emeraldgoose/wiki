# Think Less, Locally: Local Preference Learning for Reasoning Compression

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04814) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Muyao Yuan, Liyan Xu, Weizhan Zhang, Muyan Jiao, Jiangnan Li, Jinchao Zhang, Jie Zhou

## Overview

Large reasoning models achieve strong performance, but their long chain-of-thought often incurs substantial inference costs by continuing after sufficient information has been obtained. Existing compression methods mainly operate on trajectories and pay less attention to the local decision of whether further reasoning is necessary. LoCo detects candidate overthinking states from model traces, freezes corresponding reasoning prefixes, regenerates shorter verified continuations from the same states, and trains on resulting state-conditioned preference pairs with DPO. This teaches the model when to continue and when to conclude, rather than simply encouraging shorter trajectories overall.

## Key points

- The method focuses on state-level continuation decisions rather than global trajectory shortening.
- Candidate overthinking states are detected from the model's own traces.
- Reasoning prefixes are frozen during preference generation.
- Shorter verified continuations are generated from the same states.
- Training uses DPO on state-conditioned preference pairs.
- LoCo works on math-only training data but transfers to code.

## Details

LoCo constructs training data from self-generated mathematical traces and evaluates on five math and three code-generation benchmarks. It reduces completion length by 41.7 to 45.4 percent in mathematics and 23.4 to 36.3 percent in code, while maintaining or improving accuracy and achieving the best trade-off on code benchmarks. The results suggest significant inference savings, though compression thresholds depend on verification and model behavior on out-of-domain tasks.
