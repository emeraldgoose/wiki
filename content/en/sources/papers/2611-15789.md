# VibePrevisBench: Evaluating Agentic Coding for 4D Scene Generation in Blender

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.15789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15789) · **Published**: 2026-11-11
**Authors**: Yuzhe Chen, Muxi Diao, Lele Yang, Wenbin Xu, Xuyang Liu, Qile Su, Changyi Liu, Jiankang Chen, Kaiyu Jiang, Haoyu Zhang, Hongyi Fu, Jianxiong Wang, Tianke Zhang, Haonan Fan, Yingxin Li, Bingyi Li, Xu Wang, Xuanyu Zheng, Bin Wen, Tingting Gao, Wentao Zhang, Jiale Cao

## Overview

Controllable real-world video generation models can be steered by a rendered graybox "previs" proxy video that pins down layout, motion, and camera while a video model paints the appearance on top. Coding agents can produce such proxies by reconstructing a 4D scene in Blender from a brief — but no existing benchmark checks both the rendered video and the long, multi-shot scenes behind it. A team from Tianjin University, Kling AI, BUPT, Zhongguancun Academy, and PolyU fills that gap with VibePrevisBench: an agent turns a brief into an executable 4D scene, renders its proxy video, and the delivery is scored on two complementary dimensions.

Rule-based Scene Check re-runs the agent's code and compares the exported 4D scene against hidden ground-truth facts across nine dimensions; VLM-based Screen Check then asks a VLM judge whether the facts that passed can actually be recognized on screen in the proxy video. The two are complementary by design — in one example a weak agent's car passes the in-frame and turning checks yet is too distant to recognize, and the real-world videos generated by Seedance 2.5 inherit exactly such proxy errors.

The benchmark's 242 tasks span four input kinds — 94 scripts, 86 reference videos, 39 photos, and 23 continuations — over seven scene domains and 33 places, with briefs of 153–1,200 words (median 368) and target outputs of 6–60 s (median 40 s).

## Key points

- Demands are heavy by construction: 88% of tasks have two or more shots, 80% a moving camera, 79% two or more people, 69% hand–object interaction, 61% two or more places, and 71% combine several shots, a moving camera, and two or more people.
- Scoring is fine-grained: 20–2,175 checks per task (median 408; 99.0k in total), exercising a median of eight of the nine dimensions; events hold 57% of checks but the scene score averages dimensions so event checks do not dominate.
- GPT-6 Astra leads 11 frontier coding agents with an overall 77.7, ahead of GPT-6.1 Sol (76.1), Gemini 4 Argon (75.0), and Opus 5.5 (74.1); the top four lead the rest by at least 3.9 points, and the ranking holds across 18 scoring variants (Kendall τ ≥ 0.85).
- Nobody solves the benchmark: on only 35 of 242 tasks does any agent reach a Scene Check score of 90, and an oracle taking the best of all 11 agents per task reaches just 80.6 — the top four alone already reach 80.5, so the gap is shared, not divided.
- Cost and score come apart: per-task cost varies 45-fold ($0.73 for GLM 5.3 Flash to $32.59 for GPT-6 Astra); GPT-6.1 Sol reaches 98% of the leader's score at 21% of the cost, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 90% at 6%, while Kimi K3 is second-cheapest but only seventh in score.
- Three failure findings: multimodal understanding separates agents (especially spatial layout and camera motion); all agents already master basic camera work and staging but fail on challenging requests; and agents inspect their own renders yet fail to correct the errors they see.

## Details

Evaluation details are engineered for trustworthiness: gates and replay protect the rule-based check, chance rates and physical validity are calibrated per dimension, and the VLM judge runs a two-turn blind-recap-then-verdict protocol; ranking robustness is confirmed by paired bootstrap over tasks (five of ten adjacent pairs are statistically inseparable) and human checks. Capability profiles show generational gains concentrating on visual input, picture-structure scores splitting agents into two tiers, and reference-video reading limiting video tasks. The authors' implications are concrete: agents need better visual understanding of space and camera, explicit checks for challenging requests, and item-by-item verification of renders against the brief. Appendix material covers task construction fairness, per-dimension results by scene domain and screen dimension, event legibility analysis, case galleries, and agent cost statistics.
