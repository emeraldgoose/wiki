# Duo: A Reciprocal World Model for Full-Duplex Interaction

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.15131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15131) · **Published**: 2026-11-11
**Authors**: Hengyuan Cao, Biao Gong, Guoshan Lu, Qiyuan Zhang, Lin Liu, Yiding Tian, Yilei Jiang, Yuxin Ma, Chengzhang Wu, Kun Chen, Yiqian Xie, Ting Liu, Xin Zheng, Xijun Gu, Tianren Ma, Jiancheng Pan, Chi Zhang, Xuzhe Zheng, Siyi Li, Zhenzhong Lan

## Overview

Existing world models simulate the physical side of a world — given past observations and control signals, they predict how scenes evolve — but intelligence stays outside: nothing inside the generated world perceives the user in real time or decides how to respond. The Reciprocal World Model (RWM) proposed by this twenty-author report treats intelligence and physical reality as equally fundamental. A world should be inhabited by living presences that continuously perceive the user's audio and video and can respond, interrupt, or remain silent while the other side is still acting — full-duplex interaction beyond keyboard, mouse, or text prompts.

The RWM identifies two equally fundamental capabilities: Intent, the cognition underlying interaction, and Realization, which generates coherent audiovisual behavior. Duo v1 is the first implementation, built as a modular system combining a multimodal language model with a diffusion model. Interaction proceeds in chunks of 17 video frames — roughly 0.71 seconds at 24 fps. At every chunk boundary the Intent module reasons autoregressively over the ongoing conversation and makes participation decisions, emitting a speak action followed by text or a wait action, conditioned on user input so far and its own earlier language. Crucially, its hidden states — not just decoded text — carry each decision to the Realization module.

Three technical designs make this work: autoregressive intent rather than using the language model as a mere instruction encoder; chunk-specific conditions and synchronize tokens across both modules on a unified timeline with module-specific attention masks enforcing causality; and dual rollout distillation to close the gap between training on recorded histories and inference on self-generated ones. Users can share real objects, reshape virtual scenes, guide assistants, or play audiovisual games conversationally. The model is released as Duo v1, with homepage, GitHub, and Hugging Face links, and an updated version is promised.

## Key points

- Each streaming chunk spans 17 video frames at 24 fps — 17/24, approximately 0.708 seconds of captured audio and video — with text, audio, and video outputs appended to histories for the next chunk.
- Audio is compressed to 40 latent vectors per second per stereo channel with 32 dimensions each, so each audio latent corresponds to 25 milliseconds; video groups of 17 frames in a 1-4-4-4-4 arrangement are encoded into 5 latent frames of 24 dimensions with spatial resolution reduced 16×.
- Action-aware rebalancing prevents the Intent module from collapsing into perpetual waiting, since most chunks contain no new assistant speech; training under imperfect assistant history plus history perturbations teaches recovery from earlier mistakes.
- In the Realization module, noisy media tokens of a chunk can attend to the reference, intent features, and clean history — but not to their own clean targets or any later chunk.
- Training runs in three stages: supervised fine-tuning of the Intent module, causal joint audiovisual training of the Realization module on recorded histories and frozen-intent features, then distribution matching distillation with dual rollout into a four-step chunk-causal generator.
- The streaming runtime runs on a single server with eight GPUs, using unified KV management (sliding window for intent, periodic resets for realization), chunk-level overlap, and deadline scheduling; VAE decoding of one chunk overlaps Intent inference for the next.
- Human evaluation on 30 real-time duplex cases judged six binary criteria: of 180 checks (30 cases × 6), 168 passed — 93.3%.
- All 90 checks on language adherence, speech intelligibility, and visual identity stability passed; vocal emotion matched facial expression and body language in 96.7% of cases and lip movement stayed aligned with speech in 90.0%.
- Content responsiveness was the weakest criterion at 73.3% (22 of 30 cases), with misses attributed to conversational long-tail remarks and to intent that only becomes clear when speech and video are read together.
- Synthetic data pipelines generate two-person split-screen video calls from control cards, LLM-written screenplays, and a text-to-video model block by block on a unified timeline, preserving dialogue timing, reactions, overlaps, and interruptions.

## Details

### Causal chunk conditions on a unified timeline

User speech, user video, intent tokens, and assistant media are synchronized on one timeline anchored at the beginning of the user's input audio, and each chunk receives exactly the conditions available at its decision boundary. In the Intent module, temporal patches are completed inside each chunk so no future frame leaks into a decision through the visual frontend. Temporal coordinates for audio and video are aligned with actual playback times, with video positions accounting for the nonuniform mapping from 17 source frames to five latent frames. Generated text and audiovisual latents are added back to the corresponding histories, allowing the system to continue from its own outputs.

### Dual rollout distillation

Before distillation, the Realization module is trained on intent features of recorded assistant text and recorded media history, whereas in a live exchange both are produced by the system itself. Dual rollout brings inference-time conditioning into the training window: the frozen Intent module autoregressively generates the final assistant turn, and the Realization module conditions on the resulting intent features and its own generated audiovisual history. Distribution matching distillation on these rollouts — preferred over consistency distillation for matching the distribution of its own samples — plus an auxiliary camera-rotation-aware weighting yields the four-step streaming generator trained under conditions that match live interaction.

### Streaming pipeline and acceleration

Sustained playback requires producing output chunks at least as fast as they are consumed while keeping memory bounded over long interactions. The orchestrated pipeline coordinates intent inference, joint audiovisual latent generation by the DiT, and VAE decoding across GPUs. A unified KV manager uses a sliding window for the Intent module and periodic resets for the Realization module, discarding historical media KV at reset boundaries while retaining reference and shared conditioning, then reconstructing the active cache by replaying completed latents in order. Chunk-level overlap and deadline scheduling reduce the interval between successive outputs.

### Evaluation protocol and open headroom

Each of the 30 cases pairs the user's audio-visual input with the model's generated reply, replayed side by side at normal playback speed with sound. The six binary criteria each have written failure conditions and no partial credit. Beyond the headline pass rate, the authors are explicit that content responsiveness — substantively addressing what the user just said and shown while speech and video are still streaming — is the hardest open problem, the central focus of the next iteration.
