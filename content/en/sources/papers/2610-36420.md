# SO-101 Bench: Accessible Spatial Manipulation

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.36420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36420) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Truman Hickok

## Overview

Low-cost arms such as the SO-101 are the most common entry point to robot learning, yet no released policy performs spatially constrained, language-conditioned tasks on them out of the box, and existing benchmarks run only on physical hardware with simple tasks and few held-out objects. SO-101 Bench is a simulated benchmark for language-conditioned spatial manipulation built on an Isaac Lab digital twin of a real SO-101 setup. It has four tasks scored as five families, 138 objects across seen, unseen-instance, and unseen-class splits, 916 evaluation episodes with subsets for held-out appearances, rotate-to-fit planning, and occlusion memory, plus 3,629 simulated demonstrations on which every learned policy is fine-tuned under an equal compute budget. The twin ranks policies as the real robot does: five policies trained without real data, four VLA models, and an LLM agent kept their exact ranking when the same weights ran on the physical arm in an unseen environment with unseen objects (Spearman rho = 1.00, MMRV = 0.000), although simulation overestimates success. Across nine VLA and world-action models, the best learned policies score near 61%, with spatial placement, unseen object classes, and rotate-to-fit planning remaining open challenges. An LLM agent that writes code and delegates grasping to a learned policy scores 83.2%, and an ablation shows this lead comes from the choice of harness and language model.

## Key points

- SO-101 Bench is a simulated benchmark on an Isaac Lab digital twin of a real low-cost arm setup.
- It includes 4 tasks, 5 scoring families, 138 objects, and 916 evaluation episodes.
- Every learned policy is fine-tuned on 3,629 simulated demonstrations under equal compute.
- The simulator reproduces the real robot's policy ranking exactly (Spearman rho = 1.00).
- Best learned policies score near 61%, while an LLM agent with learned grasping reaches 83.2%.
- Spatial placement, unseen classes, and rotate-to-fit planning remain open.

## Details

The benchmark is designed for accessible hardware rather than specialized lab robots. Its simulator ranks policies identically to the real arm, but it also overestimates absolute success, so ranking claims are stronger than absolute performance claims. The LLM-agent result isolates the contribution of harness and model choice through ablation.
