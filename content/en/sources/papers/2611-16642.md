# Dataset Distillation via Drifting

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16642)
**Date**: 2026-11-19

## Overview

Dataset distillation is the task of learning a small set of synthetic samples that can act as an effective stand-in for a large real dataset on downstream tasks. Most methods proposed since the problem was introduced optimize the distilled data primarily with respect to a classifier, whether through gradient matching, trajectory matching, feature matching, or confidence maximization. The authors argue that data distilled against a decision boundary inherits the classifier's biases, which can limit both transfer to other tasks and generalization to other models.

This paper proposes a different objective: learn synthetic samples that directly approximate the per-class data distribution. Inspired by recent work in generative modeling, the method "drifts" distilled samples in the latent space of a pre-trained feature extractor so that each sample is attracted toward real data while being repelled from the other synthetic samples, all without using a classification objective. The competing forces encourage the distilled set to spread across the real distribution, promoting both coverage and diversity.

Because a few images alone cannot adequately approximate a distribution, the method drifts many augmentations of each synthetic image at a time. The authors find that the resulting drifted data cover the real distribution significantly better than existing methods while remaining powerful training samples for classification. They also demonstrate the method as a visualization tool for interpreting models and show that it extends beyond images to other modalities. The work argues for distribution-based metrics as a complementary evaluation for dataset distillation.

## Key points

- Replaces classifier-centric distillation objectives with a distribution-matching objective over per-class real data.
- Uses a drifting field with attractive (toward real samples) and repulsive (away from other synthetic samples) forces, weighted by a softmax over distances.
- Operates in the latent space of a pre-trained feature extractor rather than in pixel space; the choice of feature extractor defines the space in which the distribution is matched.
- Drifts many augmentations (random resized crops, horizontal flips, Gaussian noise) of each synthetic image simultaneously, so images plus augmentations together cover the target distribution.
- Reports better coverage of the real distribution than prior methods, along with strong performance as classification training data.
- Showcases the method as a visualization tool for interpreting models and extends it beyond images to other modalities.
- Proposes distribution-based metrics as a complement to classification-accuracy evaluation.
- Accepted at NeurIPS 2026.

## Details

**Method.** The approach builds on the drifting framework from generative modeling, reinterpreted as a "force" field acting on each synthetic sample. For a synthetic sample x, the drifting field V(x) decomposes into a positive (attractive) component, an expectation over real samples weighted by a kernel κ(x, y) = exp(-||x - y|| / τ), and a negative (repulsive) component, a kernel-weighted sum over the other synthetic samples. The synthetic samples are optimized with a stop-gradient loss that pulls each sample toward x + V(x), with the drifting loss back-propagated directly to the synthetic data rather than to a generator's weights. The field is computed in the latent space of a pre-trained feature extractor φ applied to augmented views of each image, combined with normalization terms adopted from the drifting prior work. The method was demonstrated with DINOv3 features, producing, for example, 10 images per class for selected ImageNet-1k classes initialized from random noise, with attractive and repulsive forces yielding high quality and diversity.

**Results and findings.** The authors report that drifted synthetic datasets cover the real distribution significantly better than existing distillation methods, while still serving as strong training samples for classification. Additional analyses show that drifting distillation generalizes to other modalities, that distilled data can elucidate model biases, and that distilled samples can be used as a visualization tool for better interpreting models (for example, "an image is worth a thousand paintings"). The paper also discusses implicit regularization via Laplacian pyramids and includes limitations, compute, broader impacts, and license notes in the appendix. The paper is accepted at NeurIPS 2026.
