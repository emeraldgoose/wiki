# A Cross-Scenario Survey and Analysis of Self-Evolving Multimodal Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.14256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14256) · **Published**: 2026-11-10
**Authors**: Jingjing Jiang

## Overview

Multimodal agents increasingly improve themselves without recurring human annotation, but every self-improvement mechanism was designed and evaluated inside a single scenario. This survey, authored by Jingjing Jiang, asks why those mechanisms do not transfer equally well in both directions of a scenario pair. Majority voting can harvest label-free rewards for multimodal reasoning from consistency across sampled answers, yet it has no counterpart in embodied manipulation, where action trajectories admit no equivalence check and physical rollouts carry steep execution and reset costs. Conversely, world models that replace expensive environmental interaction with imagined rollouts raise a factor that is not scarce at all in reasoning, where sampling directly from the policy is cheap.

To make the argument precise, the survey formalizes its object with a genus of four criteria — autonomy, freshness, durability, and recurrence — plus a differentia of self-reference: the agent must realize at least one component of its own loop, whether the goal sampler, the feedback function, or the evolution operator. Learning loops that satisfy the genus but lack self-reference are called fixed loops and serve as controls, letting the author separate limits imposed by a scenario from limits intrinsic to self-evolving design.

The analysis then rests on two pillars. The first is a three-stage self-evolving loop — experience generation, feedback acquisition, update consolidation — whose stage bottlenecks are governed by rollout count and information gain, feedback fidelity and credit localization, and retention and generalization. The second is a positioning matrix built from two ceilings each scenario imposes: the rollouts affordable per cycle and the feedback fidelity attainable by admissible evaluators. On top of the matrix, every mechanism is recast as a trade-off that raises a scarce factor while presupposing a task-structural resource such as output equivalence or checkable outcomes, yielding the survey's central vocabulary of portable, conditional, blocked, and redundant transfer verdicts.

## Key points

- The surveyed object is defined by four criteria (autonomy, freshness, durability, recurrence) and the differentia of self-reference, requiring the agent to realize the goal sampler, the feedback function, or the evolution operator of its own loop.
- The shared analytical framework is a three-stage cycle: experience generation, feedback acquisition, and update consolidation, formalized through six governing factors.
- Each scenario sits in a two-dimensional matrix of ceilings: multimodal reasoning where both rollout-count and feedback-fidelity ceilings are high, multimodal generation where the fidelity ceiling is low, GUI and Web navigation where the rollout ceiling is low, and embodied manipulation where both are low, with simulation raising both.
- Transfer verdicts follow fixed rules applied in order: observability first (unobservable outcomes are blocked), then scarcity (decides redundancy), then resources (decides portable versus conditional).
- Majority voting finds no output equivalence in manipulation, whereas world models raise a factor that is not scarce in reasoning — the two directions of a scenario pair meet structurally different obstacles.
- Self-judging navigation loops such as Agent S and Mobile-Agent-E run on goals whose screen-visible outcomes no check inspects, which leaves feedback fidelity scarce and self-judgment portable there.
- The verdicts are inferred from single-scenario evidence and remain untested: they would fall if an unadapted mechanism lacking its resource, raising a non-scarce factor, or judging an unobservable outcome beat an equal-budget control.
- The evaluation chapter catalogs task-specific benchmarks, including MathVista with 6,141 math questions, MathVerse with 2,612 problems in 6 versions, MMMU with 11.5K college questions across 30 subjects, and ChartQA with 32.7K chart questions.
- A project homepage accompanies the paper at jingjing12110.github.io/SE-MMAgents.

## Details

### A genus of four criteria, a differentia of self-reference

The genus comprises autonomous learning loops satisfying four conditions: autonomy, meaning the agent operates without human intervention across goals, actions, feedback, and updates after initialization; freshness, meaning experience is generated under the current policy; durability, meaning updates persist beyond the episode boundary; and recurrence, meaning the loop iterates across cycles. The differentia, self-reference, requires the agent to realize at least one component of its own loop. Loops in the genus lacking self-reference are termed fixed loops, and they serve as controls in every scenario so that scenario-imposed constraints can be disentangled from the intrinsic effects of self-evolution — for example, replacing answer keys with sampled consensus in reasoning.

### Two ceilings and the positioning matrix

Each stage's bottleneck is captured by two factors: rollout count with information gain, feedback fidelity with credit localization, and retention with generalization. Every scenario imposes a ceiling on the rollouts affordable per cycle and a ceiling on the feedback fidelity attainable by admissible evaluators. These two numbers place each scenario in a two-dimensional positioning matrix. The matrix identifies which ceiling binds where, but the author is explicit that it cannot by itself determine whether a given mechanism is portable.

### Four portability verdicts and their decision rules

In a target scenario a mechanism is portable if the target holds the task-structural resource it presupposes; conditional if the resource is held only for a sub-family of tasks or after an adaptation, such as converting outputs into answers or adding an external judge; blocked if the outcome it judges or must check is unobservable; and redundant if the factor it raises is not scarce. The rules apply in a fixed order — observability first, scarcity second, resources last — and a portable verdict certifies only that a precondition holds, not that the transfer actually improves the target loop. The asymmetry itself is attributed to divergent combinations of scarce factors and resources met by the two opposite transfer directions.

### Benchmarks and the measurement agenda

The evaluation section distinguishes benchmarks that supply a method's goals, host their own goals, or drive its updates, and classifies them by reset needs and scorer type, noting that discrete-answer benchmarks admit voting without an answer key. The author then frames a falsifiable measurement agenda: three claims — that a gain stems from self-evolution, that a scenario occupies its assigned matrix position, and that the portability verdicts hold — are untestable without matched-budget measurements, and the verdicts would collapse against an equal-budget control under the conditions described above.
