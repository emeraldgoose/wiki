# EgoX: Egocentric Cross-Embodiment Manipulation with Embodiment Dreaming

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.16728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16728) · **Published**: 2026-11-12
**Authors**: Yaru Niu, Binghong Chen, Changwei Yao, Shangtao Li, Zhenlong Fang, Revanth Krishna Senthilkumaran, Shuai Zhou, Yuemin Mao, Kalie Ching, Po-Ju Chao, Yudong Luo, Peiqi Yu, Heng Zhang, Hao Zhang, Bingqing Chen, Chen Qiu, H. Eric Tseng, Ye Yuan, Umar Iqbal, Changliu Liu, Jonathan Francis, Ding Zhao

## Overview

A generalist manipulation policy would like to draw on three complementary sources of experience: egocentric human data that captures diverse physical interactions beyond existing robot deployments, demonstrations from real robots with different bodies and end-effectors, and simulation that scales data collection. The obstacle is that these demonstrations encode interactions through distinct morphologies, viewpoints, proprioception, action semantics, and motion dynamics, so naively pooling them produces little transfer. EgoX is built on a single methodological premise: transfer should occur through semantically corresponding modalities, while sensing and control interfaces stay embodiment-specific.

The framework has three components. First, heterogeneous demonstrations are organized in a shared observation-action representation: each embodiment is decomposed into anatomically or functionally corresponding modalities (egocentric image, torso pose, left and right wrist poses, end-effector keypoint positions, embodiment-specific proprioception), with poses expressed in a unified frame system — a ground-projected root frame, an egocentric main-camera frame, and a wrist frame per hand — so that corresponding parts across bodies carry comparable signals. End-effector keypoints describe geometry such as fingertips, gripper contacts, and tool contact points rather than actuator joint coordinates, which is what lets five-finger hands, two-finger grippers, and a fixed scoop share a vocabulary. Second, MXT+ (Modularized Cross-Embodiment Transformer with Embodiment Dreaming) gives each observation modality its own tokenizer and each action modality its own expert within each embodiment, all connected through a shared Transformer trunk. Third, embodiment dreaming is a training-only auxiliary objective: a dreaming expert predicts compact latent representations of future visual and proprioceptive states, with JEPA-style EMA teachers and stop-gradients, so the shared trunk learns how each embodiment's state evolves without pixel reconstruction or deployment-time computation.

Training is two-stage: pretrain MXT+ on the mixed human, physical-robot, and simulated data, then fine-tune on the target embodiment's demonstrations for the target task, excluding that task-embodiment pair from pretraining. Evaluation spans 13 task-embodiment pairs across five manipulation tasks — Kitchen-Bread (sauce spreading), Kitchen-Cleanup (tool organization), Sort (block sorting), Insert (T-shaped block insertion), and Scoop (cat litter scooping) — and six robot embodiments (five physical robots plus one simulated humanoid), with egocentric human demonstrations as an additional pretraining source.

## Key points

- Pretraining lifts average normalized task progress from 0.63 to 0.80 across the 13 task-embodiment pairs; improvements appear on 11 of 13 pairs under in-distribution evaluation and all 13 under out-of-distribution evaluation.
- The out-of-distribution effect is the headline: mean task progress rises from 0.53 to 0.77, with the largest improvements on G1-Dex5 Insert and Z1-Tool Scoop. Pretrained MXT+ ranks first on 12 of the 13 pairs among all evaluated methods.
- Baselines: ACT trained on target-embodiment demonstrations, and pi-0.5 fine-tuned from its pretrained checkpoint on the same data; MXT+ without pretraining is trained from scratch on the same target data, with the same architecture and dreaming objective.
- Pretraining composition ablation: human-only pretraining already improves OOD performance on five of six tested pairs (most strongly G1-Dex5 Insert), adding other robots' demonstrations raises ID performance on five of six, and adding the target robot's demonstrations on other tasks improves OOD on every pair. The full mixture beats no pretraining by 0.12 ID and 0.23 OOD.
- Embodiment dreaming ablation: MXT+ versus MXT (dreaming components removed) improves average task progress from 0.63 to 0.78 across seven settings spanning five embodiments, improving in all seven, with the largest gain on G1-Dex5 Scoop.
- Modular versus coarse interfaces: after pretraining, the modular design matches or exceeds a coarse variant (one tokenizer for all cameras, one for concatenated pose and proprioception, one action expert) in every tested ID and OOD setting. Pretraining actually degrades the coarse variant on xArm7-Dex4 Scoop and both simulated kitchen tasks; on Kitchen-Cleanup the pretrained coarse variant scores zero task progress while modular MXT+ retains substantial performance.
- Why modularity matters: Kitchen-Cleanup starts with locomotion, so the 3D velocity command is critical despite being a small share of the 51-dimensional action space, and dedicated action experts preserve that modality; separate camera tokenizers also help when wrist views appear only during fine-tuning.
- Latent geometry: Gromov-Wasserstein distances between embodiment-specific trunk latents from Scoop demonstrations fall for 9 of 10 embodiment pairs with pretraining (mean minus 21.30 percent) and for all 10 pairs with embodiment dreaming (mean minus 38.52 percent), showing the trunk converges on more similar latent geometry for the same task across bodies.

## Details

### The embodiments and trial protocol

The six robot embodiments span very different bodies: G1-Dex5 is a bimanual Unitree G1 humanoid with five-finger Inspire RH56F1 dexterous hands; xArm7-Dex4 is a tabletop xArm7 with a four-finger LEAP Hand; G1-Sim-Dex3 is a simulated Unitree G1 with three-finger Dex3-1 hands; LocoMan-Dex2 is a quadruped with two-finger grippers mounted on its front legs, evaluated unimanual; R1Lite-Gripper is a Galaxea R1 Lite wheeled bimanual mobile manipulator with parallel-jaw grippers; Z1-Tool is a tabletop Unitree Z1 arm with a fixed, non-actuated scoop. Human-Dex5 egocentric demonstrations join them for pretraining only. Each real-world pair runs 20 trials (13 ID, 7 OOD) and each simulated pair 50 trials (32 ID, 18 OOD), with identical trial configurations across methods. The normalized task-progress score credits intermediate stages and adds a one-point completion bonus, normalized by the task maximum including the bonus.

### How embodiment dreaming works

Dreaming sources are the egocentric image, robot joint states, and end-effector joint states, whichever the embodiment provides; robot joint states sit outside the action space but carry body-specific dynamics. An embodiment tokenizer aggregates the selected features by cross-attention from 8 learnable queries into 8 latent tokens, and EMA copies of the student encoders summarize future observations into targets at each predicted offset. The dreaming expert reads the same trunk output as the action experts and is supervised with a cosine alignment loss plus an optional smooth-L1 magnitude term on token norms, averaged over future offsets and token indices. Because the targets carry stop-gradients and evolve only through EMA updates, no pixel reconstruction is needed and the branch disappears at deployment.

### What the composition and geometry results imply

The mixture ablation says the three data sources contribute at different margins: human data supplies transferable manipulation priors despite the embodiment gap, other robots' experience mainly helps in-distribution competence, and the target robot's own demonstrations on other tasks sharpen OOD robustness on every tested pair. The latent-geometry analysis connects the behavioral gains to representation learning — both pretraining and dreaming make the trunk's internal geometry for the same task more similar across bodies, with dreaming the larger effect — which explains why a trunk shared across embodiments can serve a new body after only target-embodiment fine-tuning.
