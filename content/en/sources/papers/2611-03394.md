# TaoMate-HD: Progressive-Resolution Autoregressive Audio-Video Generation

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03394) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Qijun Gan, Chenwei Zhang, Xinyang Li, Meiguang Jin, Junfeng Ma, Shen Qiu

## Overview

Streaming digital humans must produce detailed video while continuing speech as new text arrives. High-resolution denoising is costly, and text updates can cause repeated or interrupted speech. The paper presents TaoMate-HD, an autoregressive model for high-resolution audio-video streaming. For each short segment or chunk, two denoising steps establish coarse video structure at low resolution, and a final step refines the video to high resolution while audio is generated throughout the same trajectory. Each stage passes its audiovisual predictions to the same stage of the next chunk as history. Across text updates, the model retains this history to infer what has already been spoken. Continuation training uses asymmetric text conditioning with separate text windows for generation and distillation. Experiments show a 1.6x speedup over direct high-resolution generation on the same model and hardware, and optimized stage-parallel deployment provides a further 1.9x overall speedup over serial progressive generation. Continuation training reduces character error rate by 11.18 percentage points relative to the baseline, and the model supports hour-long audiovisual generation.

## Key points

- Streaming digital humans need detailed video and continuous speech as text updates arrive.
- High-resolution denoising is expensive and naive text updates can interrupt or repeat speech.
- TaoMate-HD uses two low-resolution denoising steps plus one high-resolution refinement step per chunk, with audio generated through the trajectory.
- Cross-chunk history sharing lets the model infer what has already been spoken after updates.
- Achieves 1.6x speedup vs direct high-resolution generation, 1.9x with stage-parallel deployment, 11.18-point CER reduction, and hour-long generation.

Continuation training uses asymmetric text conditioning with different windows for generation and distillation. Latency, quality, and error-rate details are in the linked source.
