# ProVe: Proposer-Verifier Decoding

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.27815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27815) · **Published**: 2026-10-20
**Authors**: Ziqi Jin, Weiwen Xu, Jiaxi Li, Yujie Zhong, Qi She, Aixin Sun

## Overview

Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive language models by letting a small model draft tokens serially and a large model verify them in parallel. ProVe reverses these roles. Starting from a standard autoregressive checkpoint, the large model is fine-tuned into a proposer that predicts a block of future tokens in a single forward pass. One additional forward pass then verifies the proposed block. The verifier can be a smaller autoregressive model or the proposer itself, yielding ProVe-lossless, which reproduces the proposer's greedy autoregressive decoding.

Block proposals already provide substantial acceleration room. With a block size of 16, a proposed block has an average autoregressive-matching prefix of 6.6 tokens on GSM8K. Across six benchmarks with a 32B proposer, ProVe-lossless reaches 2.5x average autoregressive throughput without training a separate draft head. With a small 3B verifier, ProVe reaches 2.7x average throughput while scoring 2.6 accuracy points below autoregressive decoding on the six-task average, giving a controllable speed-accuracy tradeoff.

## Key points

- ProVe reverses the usual speculative decoding roles.
- The large model becomes a block proposer in one forward pass.
- Verification happens in one additional forward pass.
- A proposer-as-verifier mode gives lossless greedy decoding.
- Block size 16 produces an average 6.6-token matching prefix on GSM8K.
- Results show strong throughput gains with and without a small verifier.

## Details

The method avoids training a separate draft model by reusing and fine-tuning the base checkpoint. Because the proposer can also verify, throughput can improve while preserving greedy outputs. Using a smaller verifier increases speed further at a measurable accuracy cost, making the tradeoff explicit. This is useful when serving large language models has strict latency or throughput constraints.
