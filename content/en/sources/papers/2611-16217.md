# WorldDraft: If Code Builds the World, Can Video Models Just Render It?

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.16217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16217) · **Published**: 12 November 2026
**Authors**: Zhiyu Hou, Wen-Jie Shu, Harold Haodong Chen, Weilin Ruan, Chenfei Liao, Liuzhou Zhang, Disen Lan, Xiaogang Xu, Qi Chen, Ser-Nam Lim

## Overview

Multimodal coding agents are changing how dynamic video is produced: instead of asking an end-to-end model to invent both the world and its pixels, they write executable MuJoCo or Blender scripts that prescribe geometry, kinematics, and camera paths, leaving the video model to turn the low-fidelity simulation into a photorealistic clip. This division of labor repurposes video models from world generators into generative renderers and raises the question in the paper's title: if code builds the world, can video models just render it?

A reliable generative renderer must satisfy two criteria at once. World preservation requires the output to retain the prescribed 4D kinematics, entity identities, event timelines, and camera motion without prompt-countermanded structural hallucinations. Visual realization requires it to instantiate text-specified target attributes, such as color, material, and lighting, and keep them bound to the right entities across motion, occlusion, and viewpoint changes. To measure both, the authors introduce WorldDraft, a benchmark of 432 strictly paired test cases across 48 executable 4D world families spanning six dynamic challenge categories, evaluated under a 3-by-3 factorial design that orthogonally crosses draft granularity, from abstract proxy to previs, with appearance alignment, from neutral to conflicting, while holding dynamics identical.

The evaluation across 12 representative models delivers three sobering takeaways: world preservation and visual realization remain in stark tension, richer draft scaffolding anchors structure but paradoxically impairs appearance binding, and conflicting source cues trigger severe source leakage. As a first remedy, the authors propose WorldDraft-Primer, a plug-and-play visual prompting strategy that cues the first frame and consistently improves both objectives across frozen video renderers.

## Key points

- The benchmark contains 432 strictly paired test cases over 48 executable 4D world families in six categories: multi-entity orchestration, multi-stage dynamics, occlusion and reappearance, camera motion, non-rigid deformation, and hybrid interactions.
- Worlds are built by multimodal coding agents (GPT-6-Astra) translating real videos into MuJoCo or Blender scripts, producing deterministic 4D rollouts with ground-truth state tuples of geometry, dense trajectories, event timestamps, and camera poses; each family passes a three-tiered admission audit.
- The 3-by-3 grid crosses three draft granularity levels (abstract proxy, simulation, previs) with three appearance alignments (neutral, aligned, conflicting), each family prompt specifying 2 to 4 verifiable attributes.
- Twelve models were benchmarked across three paradigms and ten open-weight architectures, plus the commercial Wan 3.0 and Seedance 2.5.
- The tension is stark: dedicated renderers lead preservation (SolarRenderer-33B at 63.28 WP) but lag realization (69.97 RF), while Wan 3.0 maximizes realization (80.40 RF) at the cost of the worst preservation (44.37 WP).
- Commercial leaders do not top the benchmark: Seedance 2.5 scores 64.73 Overall, edged out by LynnReal-Omni-32B at 64.89, with Seedance's 58.66 WP revealing sub-optimal physical preservation.
- Advancing draft granularity reliably improves structure, but appearance and binding gains are negative for 9 of 10 models (OmniWeaving drops by 14.47 and 15.95); Seedance 2.5 is the sole exception at 2.51 and 3.21.
- Conflicting cues cause widespread leakage: source leakage reaches 64.17% for Wan2.1-VACE and 52.24% for UniVideo, and even Wan 3.0 and MiniMax-H3 leak around 28% to 30%; conflict penalties climb to 10.63 for OmniWeaving.
- Failure analysis shows near-zero spatial preservation on an occlusion case (consistently at or below 18.8, dipping to 6.2), and LynnReal-Omni's realization collapses from 73.1 to 46.2 under aligned and to 17.3 under conflicting cues when drafts advance from simulation to previs.
- WorldDraft-Primer, which intervenes only on the initial frame, gives OmniWeaving 2.99 WP and 4.56 RF gains while cutting leakage by 3.42%, and lifts MiniMax-H3's realization to 80.21 and Overall to 65.56, surpassing both LynnReal-Omni and Seedance 2.5; text prompt engineering manages only 0.90 and 1.26.

## Details

### Benchmark design

The six challenge categories act as primary stressors rather than disjoint splits, each family classified by its dominant challenge while incorporating secondary dynamics. The footage used to design a world serves as a dynamic blueprint and world reference rather than a pixel-level reconstruction target, and prompts enforce observable verification and alignment-invariant references that index entities spatially or semantically rather than by source appearance. Evaluation metrics cover world preservation, rendering fidelity, appearance persistence, and visual quality, combined into per-case and overall scores; appearance probes agree with human judgments at 94.2% concordance for rendering fidelity and 91.5% for appearance persistence.

### Main results and failure modes

The composite results expose fragile cross-modal disentanglement. Dense previs drafts introduce synthetic shading biases that actively override text-conditioned cross-attention, so models either latch onto previs textures or hallucinate unprescribed objects, such as LynnReal-Omni synthesizing an extra cluster of green sailboats to compensate for prompt colors, directly violating draft topology. Under neutral scaffolds, models scramble target colors in an ordered 3-by-4 sailboat layout because they cannot resolve relative spatial hierarchies.

### WorldDraft-Primer

The method exploits the fact that video diffusion models propagate initial appearances through spatiotemporal attention, editing only the first frame with object emphasis, geometric corner brackets that anchor entity coordinates, and attribute cueing that binds target colors and materials. An ablation on MiniMax-H3 shows both parts matter: removing the brackets collapses counting metrics (global count falling from 30.02 to 28.70), while removing attribute cueing drops rendering fidelity from 80.21 to 76.61 and raises source leakage to 26.75%. The authors position the method as a lightweight proof of concept whose efficacy remains tethered to the base model, with fully overcoming dense-prior interference left to future work.
