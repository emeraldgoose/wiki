# Flexible Multi-Stack Super-Resolution of Clinical Brain MRI Using Hetero-Orientation Variational Encoding: Expert Reader Evaluation in a Nigerian Cohort

## Overview

High-resolution isotropic brain MRI is important for neuroradiological assessment, yet routine clinical protocols often rely on fast, anisotropic 2D acquisitions, particularly in resource-constrained settings. These acquisitions can differ in orientation, alignment, and anatomical coverage, producing partially overlapping image stacks. Existing learning-based super-resolution methods typically assume a single, under-constrained input or a fixed multi-stack configuration with idealised acquisition geometry, which limits their usefulness on real clinical scans.

This paper introduces MS-HVED, a sequence-agnostic framework for flexible multi-stack brain MRI super-resolution. Available axial, coronal, and sagittal stacks are independently encoded into multi-scale Gaussian posteriors and fused with a field-of-view-aware Product-of-Gaussians (PoG) formulation that limits contributions from unsupported regions. Physics-informed domain randomisation simulates heterogeneous clinical acquisition conditions, including obliqueness, partial coverage, and imaging artefacts.

Beyond synthetic benchmarks, the authors performed external clinical validation on 30 routine brain MRI examinations from a Nigerian cohort — an under-represented population in medical imaging research. These examinations were the largest component of a blinded reader study involving 44 real cases and seven expert neuroradiologists, providing evidence on real clinical image quality rather than full-reference metrics alone.

## Key points

- MS-HVED reconstructs from any non-empty subset of {axial, coronal, sagittal} anisotropic stacks at inference time — no retraining or architecture change is needed when one or more orientations are missing, and per-orientation FOV masks modulate each stack's precision contribution voxel-wise.
- Physics-informed domain randomisation models acquisition obliqueness, partial field-of-view, and artefacts, making fusion robust to real clinical scan geometry rather than idealised grids.
- On synthetic benchmarks, MS-HVED achieved competitive or superior reconstruction fidelity and improved downstream tissue segmentation relative to low-resolution inputs.
- In the Nigerian external validation cohort, MS-HVED was preferred over the supervised baseline for overall image quality in 187 of 210 paired assessments.
- Across all clinical cohorts, MS-HVED achieved higher overall-quality scores with all available inputs (5.93 vs. 4.49, p<.001), and the preference persisted when axial or coronal inputs were omitted (p<.001).

## Details

**Method.** Each available orientation is processed by an independent variational encoder that predicts latent multi-scale Gaussian posterior parameters. The orientation-specific posteriors are fused voxel-wise with a Multi-Scale Product-of-Gaussians, where per-orientation FOV masks weight each stack's precision by its spatial extent; fusion is defined over the set of available orientations, so the same network handles any non-empty subset O ⊆ {Ax, Cor, Sag}. Samples from the fused latent multi-scale posterior pass through a shared super-resolution decoder to reconstruct the isotropic HR volume (optional orientation-specific decoders reconstruct the input stacks). Spectrally normalised variational heads and physics-informed domain randomisation are used to stabilise training and simulate heterogeneous clinical acquisitions. A caveat noted by the authors: full-reference fidelity metrics do not capture all reconstruction artefacts, motivating the expert reader study.

**Results.** On synthetic benchmarks with known references, MS-HVED was competitive with or superior to baselines in reconstruction fidelity and improved downstream tissue segmentation over low-resolution inputs. On clinical data, external validation used 30 routine examinations from a Nigerian cohort within a blinded reader study of 44 real cases and seven expert neuroradiologists. MS-HVED was preferred for overall image quality in 187 of 210 paired assessments in the Nigerian cohort, and across all clinical cohorts it scored higher on overall quality with all inputs (5.93 vs. 4.49, p<.001), with the advantage persisting under missing axial or coronal inputs (p<.001).
