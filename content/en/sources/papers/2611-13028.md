# GTAGym: Real AAA Game Tasks Environment for Evaluation and Reinforcement Learning

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.13028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13028) · **Published**: 2026-11-09
**Authors**: Jiajun Chen, Shicheng Fang, Jiahao Lu, Yuxin Wang, Xipeng Qiu

## Overview

Evaluating foundation-model agents in real games usually forces a trade-off: sandbox platforms like MineDojo offer programmatic outcome verification but not commercial-game complexity, while work in commercial 3D worlds (Cradle, SIMA, Lumine, OctoGTA) gets realism but relies on OCR or human judgment for scoring. GTAGym, from Fudan University and the Shanghai Innovation Institute, closes this gap for GTA V: the authors manually decompose 75 main-story missions into subtasks, each specified by its goal, interaction order, completion constraints, and dynamic entity behaviors, and reconstruct them in a Babylon.js 3D environment that checks outcomes directly from engine states. The result ships with a shared screenshot-and-action web server for parallel evaluation and a template-based generator that samples task parameters in user-defined ranges to produce batches of RL environments with automatic reward and termination signals.

The evaluation suite, GTAVBench, contains 103 online 3D tasks across nine categories (road, on-foot, aquatic, vertical, aerial navigation; indoor; following, interception, evasion) plus 47 image-based shooting and sniping tasks built from source gameplay imagery — 150 tasks in total, grouped into navigation, interaction, and gun use. A second branch, GTAVBench-AGI, presents the task instruction only inside the first screenshot, as in real gameplay, and runs the agent under the Codex harness with automatic context compression, against a conventional protocol where a textual instruction plus a capped screenshot history feed a JSON action loop.

Results expose a sharp capability hierarchy. Six models (GPT-6-Astra, GPT-6.1-Sol, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, GLM-5.3-Flash, Kimi-K3, Qwen3.8-27B) were evaluated under both protocols; the best configuration (GPT-6-Astra with Codex) reaches 70.9% overall accuracy, still 18.9 percentage points below the 89.8% mean human accuracy. Aerial navigation is 0.0% for every model (humans: 45.8%), interception and evasion stay low for all but GPT models, and the AGI-style protocol raises accuracy for all six models by 0.9-10.0 points, most consistently in navigation.

Beyond evaluation, the paper demonstrates the RL loop end to end: LoRA fine-tuning (rank 32) with GRPO inside the Codex harness on 50 generated on-foot navigation instances lifts Qwen3.8-27B from 54.2% to 72.9% on held-out on-foot tasks, transfers to unseen categories (vertical 46.7%→73.3%, aquatic 57.6%→69.7%) and even to 100 native GTA V missions, where recorded success rises from 15% to 23%.

## Key points

- Task suite: 150 tasks = 103 online 3D (nine categories: road 20, on-foot 16, aquatic 11, vertical 5, aerial 8, indoor 8, following 6, interception 13, evasion 16) + 47 image-based (shooting 24, sniping 23). Tasks derive from 75 GTA V main-story missions decomposed from walkthrough videos.
- Task construction: manual extraction and specification of goals, interaction order, completion constraints, and entity behaviors; online scenarios rebuilt in Babylon.js with traced minimap routes matched to comparable turn counts in the environment geometry; shooting tasks annotate hit regions on gameplay frames, sniping uses distinct unscoped/scoped views with magnification-dependent rendering.
- Every subtask instantiates a template with initial state, instruction, budget, and executable success/failure predicates over movement mode, height/depth, target distance, and interaction events; pursuer and evader controllers implement following, interception, and evasion dynamics.
- Interaction: closed-loop screenshot-action protocol with keyboard-mouse control; each key carries a hold duration d in (0, 1 s] by default, keys execute concurrently, simulation pauses during inference by default; online history capped at ten screenshot-reply pairs (oldest five dropped) in the JSON protocol.
- GTAVBench-AGI: instruction only in the first screenshot (read the goal from the game view), Codex harness compresses context automatically as history grows.
- Main results (task accuracy): GPT-6-Astra 66.4% / 70.9% (GTAVBench / AGI), GPT-6.1-Sol 66.0% / 66.9%, GLM-5.3-Flash 38.9% / 41.8%, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 33.8% / 38.2%, Qwen3.8-27B 24.2% / 34.2%, Kimi-K3 28.4% / 33.1%; human mean 89.8%.
- Dynamic control is the bottleneck: aerial navigation 0.0% for all models both protocols; interception 0-41.0% (only GPT-Astra above 16%); evasion 8.3-25.0%; while on-foot, indoor, and vertical navigation are near-saturated by GPT models (up to 100%).
- Efficiency: GPT models keep the best accuracy-token trade-off; DeepSeek spends roughly 26x Astra's output tokens under the JSON protocol without matching its success.
- RL: GRPO + LoRA (rank 32, alpha 64, lr 1e-6) on Qwen3.8-27B, 8 rollouts x 4 tasks per update; reward = success + 0.3 x normalized traversable-distance progress on failure, minus 0.1 for exceeding 100 rounds; 50 generated on-foot instances spatially separated from benchmark routes.
- RL outcomes: on-foot 54.2%→72.9% (ID), overall 34.2%→38.9%, OOD categories 31.8%→34.8% (vertical 46.7%→73.3%, aquatic 57.6%→69.7%); road stays 3.3%, aerial and interception stay 0%, shooting regresses 73.6%→68.1%; native GTA V transfer 15%→23% over 100 missions.
- Failure modes: mistimed steering (late/overheld corrections on roads), late descent correction in aerial landing, failure to sustain blocking after collision in interception, and wall entrapment in evasion — difficulty anticipating motion and switching control strategies.
- Error recovery: across 900 trajectories (565 with a categorized first error among Perception/Planning/Calibration/Regulation), Calibration and Regulation account for 63.2% of first errors; GPT models recover within ten decisions in 65.7% (Astra) and 49.3% (Sol) of cases versus 21.6-25.6% for the others.
- Evaluation settings matter: shrinking the action-duration cap to 0.5 s raises accuracy from 62.1% to 66.3% (more frequent visual feedback, but mean rounds rise 62.6→121.2 and input tokens 4.32M→11.20M); 2 s cap lowers it to 60.5% with 66.6% of actions exceeding 1 s. Removing inference-time pausing drops accuracy 22.3 points to 39.8%, zeroing following, interception, and evasion while on-foot/indoor stay at 100% — inference latency is the bottleneck for dynamic interaction.
- Artifacts: code at github.com/ljbro/GTAGym, dataset at huggingface.co/datasets/OpenMOSS-Team/GTAVBench; the reconstructed 3D environment builds on the open GTA_SZ project.

## Details

### Task construction pipeline

Mission decomposition starts from collected walkthrough videos of 75 main-story missions; each subtask corresponds to a distinct mission stage and is annotated with goal, required interaction order, completion constraints, and entity behaviors. The 3D environment (Babylon.js) exposes task-specific state controllers; navigation paths are hand-traced on the source minimap and matched to routes with comparable turn counts in the environment's city geometry, with climbing surfaces and landing areas added where tasks require them. Image-based tasks follow a parallel pipeline: gameplay frames with annotated target hit regions for shooting, and directly captured unscoped/scoped views for sniping. Template instantiation attaches executable success and failure predicates checked against engine states, so outcome verification needs no human or VLM judgment.

### Evaluation protocols and models

Both branches share task instances, action interface, and scoring, differing in instruction presentation and context management. GTAVBench follows the screenshot-based computer-use recipe: fixed system prompt, textual instruction, screenshot-reply history (capped at ten pairs), JSON actions validated by an adapter. GTAVBench-AGI embeds the instruction in the first screenshot and hands history management to the Codex harness, which compresses context as it grows. Episodes end on success, failure, or budget exhaustion (300 rounds for navigation/interaction, 20 for gun use); scores are category-level and micro-averaged over 150 tasks. The evaluated models are GPT-6-Astra, GPT-6.1-Sol, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, GLM-5.3-Flash, Kimi-K3, and Qwen3.8-27B; a human baseline (mean 89.8%) covers all eleven categories.

### Analysis beyond the main table

Trajectory inspection identifies four recurring failure mechanisms — mistimed steering, late descent correction, failure to sustain blocking, and wall entrapment — all pointing at anticipation of motion and switching of control strategies. First-error annotation across 900 trajectories shows Calibration and Regulation dominate (63.2% of 565 categorized first errors), a closed-loop-control bottleneck rather than a perception or planning one, and GPT models recover from such errors within ten decisions far more often (65.7%/49.3%) than the other models (21.6-25.6%). Harness comparison shows longer compressed histories help agents remember attempted strategies and explored areas, but can also preserve mistaken interpretations and cause goal drift after compression (Kimi-K3 substituting a driving objective for a rooftop one; GLM-5.3-Flash carrying reversed minimap markers). Timing ablations on GPT-6.1-Sol with Codex show shorter action caps improve accuracy at the cost of more rounds and tokens, and that removing inference-time pausing is devastating for tasks with moving targets, framing real-time agent performance as a function of the inference-and-action cycle fitting inside the task's response window.
