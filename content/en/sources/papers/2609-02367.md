# The Missing Temporal Link: Temporal Context Routing for Script-Driven Audio-Video Generation

Source: Yichen Liu and 10 other authors, arXiv:2609.02367, Sep 2, 2026.

**Lead**: Joint audio-video generators have grown strong on visual quality and audio-visual sync, but they offer little control over *when* shot transitions land and dialogue is spoken — fatal for script-driven creation, where timing errors wreck narrative coherence. The diagnosis: current models align video and audio on a shared temporal axis, yet shot/dialogue timing from the structured prompt lives only in the text representation, unaligned with either modality's temporal coordinates. So video and audio can stay synced with each other while both miss the script. The fix is **Temporal Context Routing (TCR)**, which maps script timing onto the shared temporal axis and routes each prompt's guidance to the matching positions in both modalities. On 200 test scripts, TCR cuts Shot Boundary MAE by **96% (1.11s → 0.042s)** and raises Dialogue Acc@0.5s from **28.3% to 84.1%**, with visual quality and AV-sync on par with baselines — and users prefer it on all five evaluated dimensions.

## The misalignment nobody was measuring

The paper's conceptual contribution is naming the third axis. Prior work treated temporal alignment as a two-body problem (video ↔ audio); TCR frames it as three-body (script ↔ video ↔ audio). Each prompt segment's guidance gets routed to corresponding temporal positions in both generation streams rather than broadcast as a global text condition. That turns "follow the script" from a prompt-engineering hope into an architectural guarantee.

## What the numbers say

| Metric (200 test scripts) | Baseline | TCR |
|---|---|---|
| Shot Boundary MAE | 1.11s | **0.042s** (−96%) |
| Dialogue Acc@0.5s | 28.3% | **84.1%** |
| Visual quality / AV-sync | — | comparable |
| User study (5 dimensions) | — | preferred on all 5 |

The two headline metrics move together — shot cuts and dialogue land on time simultaneously — which is exactly what the shared-axis routing predicts. Quality parity matters: timing control costs nothing in perceptual quality.

## Caveats

This summary is abstract-based: architecture details of the routing mechanism, the five user-study dimensions, and generalization beyond the 200-script test set need the full text. Results are reported against the paper's own baseline; how TCR composes with other controllable-generation methods is unstated. Absolute dialogue accuracy (84.1%) still leaves one-in-six lines mistimed — good enough for drafts, not for final cut without review.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- Building script/storyboard-driven media tools? Global text conditioning cannot enforce timing — you need prompt guidance indexed by temporal position, which is TCR's core pattern.
- When two outputs look mutually consistent but both wrong, suspect a missing third alignment axis rather than tuning the pairwise loss.
- The 96% error reduction with quality parity is the deployment-relevant shape: timing control as a routing change, not a quality tax.
- For evaluation, pair machine metrics (MAE, Acc@window) with preference studies — timing errors are perceptual, and users are the ground truth.
