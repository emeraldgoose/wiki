# HiFOR: Hierarchical Foresight with Action-Guided World Grounding for Vision-Language-Action Model

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40373) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Rongxing Ding, Hongyu Qu, Kehong Wu, Xudong Wang, Fan Zhang, Xinguang Xiang, Xiangbo Shu

## Overview

Vision-Language-Action models integrate perception, language understanding, and action generation for robotic manipulation, and recent work predicts future states to improve environmental understanding. However, many methods model either short-term dynamics or long-term objectives in isolation, and directly injecting predicted future information into action policies ignores how much of it is actually relevant to current actions.

HiFOR proposes hierarchical foresight with action-guided world grounding. It jointly models short-term state evolution, key semantic milestones, and final goal states while using selective retrieval to bring in relevant future information according to evolving action requirements.

## Key points

- Addresses VLA foresight across multiple temporal horizons.
- Models short-term dynamics, intermediate milestones, and final goals through hierarchical dependency modeling.
- Uses action-guided factorized world retrieval instead of directly conditioning on all predicted futures.
- Improves relevance and reduces redundant or noisy foresight signals.
- Targets complex robotic manipulation tasks with action chunks and multi-view observations.
- Reports hierarchical foresight planning and structured world modeling components.

## Details

HiFOR is framed as language-conditioned sequential decision-making: at each timestep the policy receives multi-view observations, a language instruction, and robot state, then predicts a future action chunk. End-effector translation, rotation, and gripper control are included.

The key idea is that future information should be selected and grounded by actions rather than blindly concatenated. Hierarchical foresight connects local dynamics to long-term objectives, while action-guided retrieval filters foresight by the current action need. This aims for more coherent task progression and better use of predictions.
