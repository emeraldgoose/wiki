# AskStream: When and What to Ask Proactively under Uncertainty in Streaming Video Understanding

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.38678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38678) · **Published**: 2026-10-28
**Authors**: Lu Wang, Zhuoran Jin, Longxiang Wang, Junhong Chen, Yupu Hao, Yao Xu, Daojian Zeng, Yubo Chen, Kang Liu, Guang Liu, Jun Zhao

## Overview

Recent multimodal large model advances have increased interest in interaction paradigms for video understanding, and the evolution from offline to streaming and proactive interaction has moved models from passive responders toward proactive agents that can decide when to answer. Existing proactive paradigms typically assume the information required to answer a query is complete, leaving models to only wait or answer. In the real world, information is often incomplete because of uncertainty from either the video or the user, and under proactive paradigms such uncertainty can cause a model to wait forever or generate highly hallucinatory responses. This paper argues that proactive interaction should be redefined with a third action, ask, allowing models to actively request missing information. To study when and what models should ask before answering, AskStream is introduced as the first multi-turn benchmark spanning video and user uncertainty for streaming video understanding. It has four task categories and eight subtasks and contains 438 videos with 2,714 manually annotated frame-level time windows across three types: wait, ask, and answer. Across multiple-choice and open-ended settings, even the strongest model attained only 52.18% on the proposed Ask-F1 metric, highlighting substantial limitations in proactive information seeking under uncertainty.

## Key points

- Existing proactive video models mainly choose between waiting and answering.
- AskStream adds an ask action for requesting missing information.
- The benchmark spans video uncertainty and user uncertainty.
- It has 4 task categories and 8 subtasks.
- It contains 438 videos and 2,714 manually annotated frame-level time windows.
- Time window types are wait, ask, and answer.
- The strongest model reached only 52.18% Ask-F1.

## Details

The benchmark is multi-turn and covers both multiple-choice and open-ended settings. By annotating when to wait, ask, or answer, it measures proactive information seeking rather than answer accuracy alone.
