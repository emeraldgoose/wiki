# GameWM: Toward Next-Generation Video Games with Interaction-Centric World Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.19247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19247) · **Published**: 2026-11-14

## Overview

For more than half a century, video games have pursued better graphics, more natural interaction, and more engaging gameplay. Generative video models now bear on all three. Neural networks already draw most of the pixels in shipped games, and recent world models let a lightweight game engine keep world state while a video model turns the engine’s coarse proxy video into final frames. These systems, however, treat the video model as a renderer that only restyles motion the engine has already authored, leaving most of its generative capacity unused and limiting diversity.

GameWM presents a design for next-generation games built on a clear division of labor. The GameWM-engine keeps what must be exact: rules, state, camera, and world layout, sparing the video model from having to remember it. The GameWM-VM generates what is expensive to author by hand: the look of the world and detail of player interactions. One game mechanic can then be realized in many visually and physically distinct ways while its outcome stays exactly as the engine computed it.

## Key points

- Division of labor separates exact engine state from generative video rendering.
- Engine preserves rules, state, camera and layout; VM generates visuals and interaction detail.
- Data engine centered on interactions rather than exploration.
- Interface accepts authored character sheets, scene art, and skill descriptions.
- Model family 5B to 33B parameters with real-time causal variant for play.
- Each player receives a fresh version of the same game with personal character, world and skills while events keep engine timing and outcomes.

## Details

GameWM is built as a complete system with an interaction-centered data engine, an authoring interface, and a model family. The design enables per-mechanic personalization without sacrificing determinism of game logic.
