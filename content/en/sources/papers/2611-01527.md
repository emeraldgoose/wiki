# From Tool State to Scientific Action: Auditing Decision Provenance in Biomedical Research Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.01527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01527) · **Published**: 2026-11-01

## Overview

Biomedical research agents retrieve literature, inspect molecular and cellular features, and choose what to examine next, but the final verdict captures only part of that process: two executions can agree on a claim while selecting different documents or citing different sentences. The paper asks whether silently corrupted tool values change those evidence choices, and how much of a tool's output must be restored to undo the change.

BioAgent-DF frames this as an audit of decision provenance. Evidence-path fidelity means agreement with a specified reference execution in recorded retrieval, read, and citation endpoints, while scientific decision fidelity (the DF in BioAgent-DF) is the broader audit covering actions, item selection, and verdicts as well. The setting is silent data corruption (SDC) on faulty GPUs, which come in two classes — Case 1 (size-dependent) GPUs fail more often on tested large-matrix paths than on short probes, and Case 2 (expanded-trigger) GPUs fail even on those short probes — and whose finite, erroneous outputs can pass ordinary NaN/Inf monitoring and reach a tool result.

The method is a paired audit: BioAgent-DF saves reference and candidate values at one scientific computation site, restores or transplants them at their original coordinates, and executes the remaining tool and agent steps on healthy hardware. Across eight SciFact batches, Qwen3.5-4B shows repeatable citation changes in three capture–claim combinations and Gemma 3 4B IT in four; these seven combinations cover two claims. Full restoration recovers the reference evidence sets while verdicts remain unchanged, and the verdict changes observed under the baseline prompt did not recur under an explicit-example prompt. Complementary tool and optimizer experiments separate recovery of numerical state, recovery of ranked lists, and restoration of the agent's visible history.

The stakes are highest where agents direct real experiments. In hERG cardiac-safety assay allocation — a three-member Chemprop D-MPNN ensemble scoring 2,009 held-out compounds, with a GPT-6.1 Sol agent in Biomni A1's native workflow selecting the Top-402 for measurement and assigning patch-clamp triage — silent numerical faults changed testing decisions while the fixed-budget shortlist kept the same number of compounds labeled as blockers. After the agent had used faulty values, restoring its earlier execution state and supplying certified scores recovered all 39 changed decisions, whereas replacing the prediction file recovered none; no incorrect action was committed. A streaming implementation of the arithmetic checks cut total forward-execution and verification time by 42.4% relative to the original FP64 implementation, with identical flags and recovery outputs.

## Key points

- Stable verdicts can conceal changed evidence paths: audits should record what an agent reads and cites alongside its final answer.
- The repeatable evidence-path changes are real but concentrated — seven capture–claim combinations over two claims and two 4B models, in Case 2 captures selected for upstream retrieval changes; without that selection, candidate values cause no more discordance than a healthy repeat.
- Original-coordinate interventions localize responsibility: full restoration returned the reference reads and citations in every confirmed case, while no tested partial-row mask both restored the reference path and reproduced the candidate path.
- When the agent model itself ran on faulty hardware, all 220 Case 1 and 240 Case 2 candidate requests triggered vLLM's request-level NaN diagnostic and no tool calls completed — so the replay experiments isolate corrupted tool values, not a broken agent.
- In hERG allocation, aggregate metrics hid the failure: the Top-402 still held 351 labeled blockers while its membership and triage decisions changed — K5612 (CHEMBL3804918, published hERG IC50 434 nM) left the list in four sources, and labeled blockers K12252 and K1461 were assigned advancement without testing.
- Detection needs the right checks: integrity and probability-range checks flagged none of the 16 faulty sources, while row/column checksums with a rounding bound flagged all 16 with no false alarm across eight healthy verifier runs.
- Recovery obeys a cost hierarchy (decision recovery costs no more than full-state recovery): restoring 11–254 coordinates recovered changed decisions while hundreds of cohort predictions still differed, and the R1+M rule (margin 0.009354, recomputing the 68 compounds nearest decision boundaries) matched the healthy allocation.

## Details

- SciFact protocol: an agent executing search, read, and finish returns SUPPORT, REFUTE, or NOT_ENOUGH_INFO with sentence citations, under one search, up to six document reads, 16 calls per episode, and 384 output tokens, at temperature zero. The mechanism study uses 27 Case 2 capture–claim combinations over 11 claims, three repeats each across seven conditions (567 attempts per model), and 1,386 attempts per prompt with content-masking and unselected controls; baseline Qwen completed all 1,386 attempts while baseline Gemma completed 800, with 559 budget exhaustions and 27 invalid-JSON outputs.
- What the audit separates: numerical diagnostics distinguish arithmetic anomalies supported by an applicable bound from ordinary precision effects, which also change membership between two healthy references (disabling intermediate BF16 reduction in PBMC removed 174 Top-10 disagreements).
- Decision-commitment view: when a research agent directs an automated laboratory, a changed decision means a changed experiment, and aggregate measures need not reveal it. Verification therefore belongs where a decision is committed — flagged and unresolved scores are recomputed on trusted hardware, an agent that used superseded values resumes from the execution state saved before it read them, and only certified decisions are committed.
- Future work: a recovery agent that chooses which calculations to verify or repeat, which dependent execution state to restore, and which measurements to acquire under a shared computation budget, plus a benchmark that hides evaluation references from the agent and scores fault detection, trusted-evidence acquisition, decision recovery, and correct commitment separately.
- Tooling: Aritheia, a public tool for detecting numerical anomalies on NVIDIA GPUs and collecting diagnostic records, is available at https://github.com/INTOSYN/Aritheia_GPU_Test.
