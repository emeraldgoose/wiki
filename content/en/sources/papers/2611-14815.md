# BirdDreams: Joint Perspective and BEV Video Generation for a Plausible, Reactive Driving World Model

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.14815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14815) · **Published**: 2026-11-10
**Authors**: Junyoung Seo, Jaewoo Seo, Seungryong Kim, Laura Leal-Taixé, Xiuming Zhang, Seung Wook Kim

## Overview

A driving world model has to do two things at once: generate plausible motion for surrounding vehicles and follow precise ego-trajectory commands. Learning vehicle motion from perspective video alone is hard because motion is entangled with appearance and vehicles get occluded, so prior systems like OmniDreams condition generation on external layouts that specify vehicle motion — which means those vehicles simply replay logged trajectories and cannot react when the ego does something different. BirdDreams, built by Junyoung Seo, Jaewoo Seo, Seungryong Kim, Laura Leal-Taixé, Xiuming Zhang, and Seung Wook Kim at NVIDIA and KAIST, takes scene structure out of the input and makes it a prediction target.

The model jointly generates two synchronized videos from a single camera image: photorealistic perspective video and a schematic bird's-eye-view video rendered during training from scene annotations. The BEV draws roads as polylines and vehicles as oriented cuboids at a fixed metric scale, dropping texture and perspective foreshortening so that relative motion and lane relationships are exposed directly. Joint attention between two video diffusion transformers — one per stream — lets each branch inform the other: the BEV contributes explicit scene dynamics while the perspective stream contributes details the BEV omits, such as traffic signals and object appearance.

The predicted BEV also becomes the control surface for the ego vehicle. At inference the latest BEV frame is translated and rotated along an input ego trajectory, and the reprojected frames serve as a soft condition showing how the scene would appear to move if only the ego vehicle moved. No HD map or vehicle-state inputs are required, and when no trajectory is given, ego motion is predicted as part of generation itself.

## Key points

- BirdDreams jointly generates perspective and schematic BEV video from one initial camera image; the BEV is rendered from the map and 3D object annotations shipped with the driving datasets.
- Each BEV frame covers a square of approximately 69.3 m per side on both datasets, so the same pixel displacement represents the same physical distance across scenes.
- The architecture couples two causal video diffusion transformers with joint attention at every transformer block, while keeping separate attention projections, feed-forward layers, and output heads per stream; the BEV branch is initialized with a copy of the pretrained OmniDreams backbone.
- A frozen pretrained video VAE from Cosmos-Predict2.5 encodes each stream into latents partitioned into chunks of three latent frames, with bidirectional attention within a chunk and causal attention across chunks.
- Ego control works by translating and rotating the latest predicted BEV frame along the input trajectory; sampled noise levels are independent across the two streams, which improves FVD and FTD over sharing one noise level.
- Without ego-action conditioning, BirdDreams reduces minADE relative to OmniDreams (Vanilla) by 21% on PhysicalAI-AV and 37% on nuScenes, and achieves the lowest minADE, minDTW, FTD, FVD, and FID among evaluated methods on both benchmarks.
- With ego-action conditioning it reduces ADE by 35% on PhysicalAI-AV and 36% on nuScenes relative to OmniDreams (Plücker), with lower FVD and FID alongside.
- In counterfactual rollouts with changed ego maneuvers, surrounding vehicles react — a crossing vehicle stops as the ego proceeds, a following vehicle slows after an ego cut-in — and extreme maneuvers can end in a collision, which the authors consider a plausible outcome.
- A pairwise human evaluation showed each of 25 participants 15 pairs drawn from a pool of 119, yielding N = 375 judgments: BirdDreams (HDMap) was preferred in 57% of comparisons versus 24% for OmniDreams (HDMap), with 19% ties; the baseline was flagged for impossible motion in 27% of comparisons.
- Users can also edit an initial BEV frame in a top-down editor to add vehicles or move them, and BirdDreams generates the full modified scenario including the first perspective frame.

## Details

### Causal Mixture-of-Transformers with per-stream noise

The perspective branch inherits the pretrained OmniDreams backbone and the BEV branch starts from a copy of its weights. At every block, joint attention lets each branch's queries attend to keys and values from both streams, so scene dynamics learned in the BEV's metric space can guide perspective generation while the BEV branch reads back visual details. During training, noise levels are sampled independently for the two streams inside the diffusion-forcing scheme, deliberately exposing each branch to cases where its companion stream is cleaner or noisier; an ablation on RDS-HQ-1M validation clips shows this per-stream sampling yields lower FVD and FTD than a variant sharing one noise level per chunk.

### BEV-aware action conditioning and causal rollout

Because the BEV is metric, action conditioning is geometric: the latest predicted frame is reprojected along the ego trajectory, producing soft condition frames that tell the BEV branch how ego-only motion would look, and joint attention conveys that information to the perspective branch. Cached history supports generation of successive chunks, and both streams keep a consistent world state across the rollout. The conditioning is optional — with an empty condition the model predicts ego motion as part of the video, which is how the unconditioned trajectory metrics were obtained.

### Data construction and evaluation protocol

BEV training videos are rendered from map and 3D object annotations, with road boundaries magenta, crosswalks purple, pedestrians yellow, and vehicle cuboids shaded red at the front and blue at the rear to make headings visible. Evaluation follows the DrivingGen protocol largely: FVD, FID, and reference-free CLIP-IQA+ image quality for video, plus Fréchet Trajectory Distance and ego-trajectory agreement measured by reconstructing trajectories from generated videos with Depth Anything 3 and comparing ADE and DTW against logged motion. Under counterfactual ego actions, BirdDreams (HDMap) was additionally compared against an OmniDreams (HDMap) variant given the same static road geometry, producing the human-preference result above.
