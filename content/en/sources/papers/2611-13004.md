# G-STAR: Group-Scaled Token Capacity for Deeply Compressed Autoregressive Generation

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.13004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13004) · **Published**: 2026-11-08
**Authors**: Xu Huang, Ye Huang, Xiaotong Li, Zhengrong Yue, Jialv Zou, Minhao Jing, Haodong Li, Wenhao Yan, Yaxin Luo, Duomin Wang, Daquan Zhou

## Overview

Discrete autoregressive image generation faces a stubborn trade-off: long token sequences preserve representation quality but slow decoding, while aggressive spatial compression strips away capacity. G-STAR, a collaboration led from Peking University with Meituan Longcat and several other institutions, analyzes the nominal bit budget of product-quantized tokenizers and shows that, at a fixed budget, the group size should scale with the square of the per-axis spatial compression factor — so capacity survives even when the spatial grid collapses.

The framework trains a 32x tokenizer whose encoder is initialized from Qwen-ViT and aligned to its features with a Gram loss on second-order correlations. Lossless 2x2 packing then converts the native 32x grid into an effective 64x interface, dividing global autoregressive steps by four while raising codes per step from G to 4G. Because larger groups introduce intra-group dependencies, a semi-autoregressive decoder computes features for all group slots in parallel and then applies a lightweight single-layer causal Transformer over the codes.

Two models instantiate the idea: GSTAR-Pro-8B (32x tokenizer, G=16) and GSTAR-Flash-1.7B (effective 64x, G=32), evaluated on five text-to-image benchmarks plus instruction-guided editing on GEdit-Bench.

## Key points

- Scaling rule: scaling the per-axis compression by a factor while scaling group size by its square preserves the nominal bit budget and cuts global steps by the square of that factor.
- GSTAR-Pro-8B (initialized from Qwen3-8B): GenEval 0.84, DPG 83.38, PRISM 6.98, LongText 0.935, Qwen-Image 50.07.
- GSTAR-Flash-1.7B (initialized from Qwen3-1.7B): GenEval 0.76, DPG 80.18, PRISM 6.81, LongText 0.853, Qwen-Image 46.88.
- Latency: 2.5 seconds per 1024x1024 image on one NVIDIA H200 — a 20x speedup over prior discrete autoregressive models.
- Efficiency breakdown at 1024x1024: causal AR baseline 22.23 seconds; Semi-AR head 14.93 (1.49x); CUDA graph capture 5.85 (2.55x); fused Triton kernels 3.37 (1.74x); SGLang serving 2.50 (1.35x), compounding to 8.9x end-to-end.
- Head comparison under matched training at 512x512: Causal AR reaches GenEval 0.8386 at 6.44 seconds, Parallel 0.7800 at 1.77, and the Semi-AR head 0.8253 with the best PRISM 5.46 at 3.41.
- Group scaling on ImageNet with codebook size 16,384: raising G from 1 to 32 improves rFID from 2.0624 to 0.4325 and linear probe from 81.45% to 84.55%, though gFID degrades from 2.1057 to 17.05.
- Late-blooming capacity: G=16 converges more slowly than G=8 but overtakes it from 25k steps, and in text-to-image training G=16 reaches GenEval 0.8386 versus 0.8060 for G=8.

## Details

### Spatial-group co-scaling

Group-wise quantization assigns each spatial position G codes drawn from G channel groups, so per-step capacity grows linearly in G while the number of global steps shrinks quadratically in the compression factor. The paper instantiates the principle by packing an f32g8 tokenizer into an effective f64g32 interface and verifies in the appendix that this lossless packing retains strong text-to-image quality. The tokenizer encoder starts from Qwen-ViT and adds semantic-alignment and Gram losses to the usual reconstruction and vector-quantization objectives.

### Semi-autoregressive decoding

Larger groups force the decoder to model dependencies among the G codes of one position. The Semi-AR head predicts all slots in parallel through the backbone feature and connects them with a single-layer causal Transformer, keeping PRISM at 5.46 — equal to causal decoding — at roughly half the latency (3.41 versus 6.44 seconds), while pure parallel decoding is fastest at 1.77 seconds but loses clearly more quality.

### Efficiency pipeline and ablations

After the Semi-AR head, four system-level optimizations compound multiplicatively to 8.9x end-to-end, and the resulting 2.5-second generation at 1024x1024 is what delivers the 20x advantage over prior discrete autoregressive models. Group-scaling ablations show reconstruction and semantic understanding improve steadily with G — the linear probe reaches 84.55% at G=32 — but generative modeling gets harder before late-stage training recovers the advantage, and the authors note larger models may benefit even more from the extra capacity.
