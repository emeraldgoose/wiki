# How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Max Neuwinger, Hanna Yukhymenko, Imanol Schlag

## Overview

Safety alignment for large language models is heavily focused on English with limited multilingual representation, particularly damaging to low-resource language performance. Related open training datasets are often limited in size or do not follow region-localized harmful topics, restricting the scope of alignment beyond cultures. The paper proposes a scalable template-based augmentation pipeline to produce massive multilingual refusal and benign corpora. It extracts safe templates from harmful prompts, translates them, and then fills the slots with culturally authentic entities, both harmful and benign. The result is MSafeTemp, a validated corpus spanning 60 languages for multilingual safety alignment and evaluation. Using the dataset, the authors show that English-only evaluation misjudges multilingual safety: attacks that fail in English can succeed once translated, while culturally localized variants expose further failures that translation alone misses. They also confirm that augmenting safety training data through templates allows higher diversity and quality of alignment, resulting in improved safety performance beyond English. MSafeTemp and the pipeline are aimed at facilitating research and development of safer models for less-represented cultures.

## Key points

- English-centric safety alignment underrepresents multilingual and low-resource language performance.
- Template extraction, translation, and slot filling with culturally authentic entities scale refusal/benign corpora across 60 languages.
- MSafeTemp is a validated 60-language corpus for alignment and evaluation.
- English-only evaluation hides failures that appear when harmful prompts are translated or culturally localized.
- Template augmentation increases diversity and quality of alignment, improving safety beyond English.

## Details

The study evaluates multilingual safety across several models and compares English results with translated and culturally localized attacks. The validation pipeline and corpus construction process are described in the linked paper.
