# How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Max Neuwinger, Hanna Yukhymenko, Imanol Schlag

## Overview

Safety alignment for large language models is heavily focused on English, which leaves low-resource languages underrepresented and weakly protected. Existing open training datasets are also small or lack region-localized harmful topics that would extend alignment beyond a single cultural framing. This paper proposes a scalable template-based augmentation pipeline that extracts safe templates from harmful prompts, translates those templates, and fills their slots with culturally authentic harmful and benign entities. The result is MSafeTemp, a validated corpus spanning 60 languages for multilingual safety alignment and evaluation. Using the dataset, the authors show that English-only evaluation misjudges multilingual safety: attacks that fail in English often succeed once translated, and culturally localized variants expose failures that translation alone misses. They also show that template-based augmentation yields higher diversity and quality in alignment data, improving safety performance beyond English.

## Key points

- English-only safety evaluation underestimates failures in other languages, including cases revealed by translated and culturally localized prompts.
- The pipeline extracts safe templates from harmful prompts, translates the templates, and fills them with culturally authentic entities.
- MSafeTemp spans 60 languages and supports multilingual safety alignment and evaluation.
- Template augmentation increases both the diversity and the quality of safety training data.
- The work targets safer and more responsible models for underrepresented cultures.

## Details

The method addresses three limitations at once: the English-centric focus of current safety alignment, the narrow cultural scope of many multilingual datasets, and the small size of available open data. By separating prompt structure from localized content, templates can be translated without preserving language-specific wording, while slot filling keeps examples grounded in local contexts. The paper evaluates refusal behavior and harmful-content generation across models and languages, finding that translate-and-test protocols miss failures found through culturally adapted prompts. Training with the augmented corpus strengthens refusal and safety performance across languages, suggesting that scalable template-based data is a practical route to broader safety coverage.
