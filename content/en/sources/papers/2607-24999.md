# CogBattery: Human-Referenced Evaluation of Cognitive Task Behavior and Structure in Language Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2607.24999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24999) · **Published**: 2026-07-27

## Overview

Human cognitive tasks compare conditions that differ in a controlled way, and published human data record how people respond in each — a direct model-versus-human comparison that aggregate accuracy cannot give. The catch is that a model can reproduce the direction of a human effect while making different errors. CogBattery evaluates 54 open-weight models from 23 families on text adaptations of 13 cognitive paradigms, comparing each paradigm with human data from its own source study — mostly participant-level records — by condition and response type, spanning groups such as working memory, interference control, memory, mindreading, and metacognition.

The design answers questions earlier evaluations leave open. Closest prior work covers 1 to 16 tasks and 4 to 40 models (CogBench 7/40, CogEval 1/8, CogProbe 16/4) and uses human data only as norms or scaling constants, if at all. CogBattery instead asks how widely specific error differences and confidence-report differences recur across families, whether the gaps survive minimal-performance gates, and whether task-score structure carries cognitive meaning. Structure is compared only in four human cohorts that tested the same people on several tasks, because robust condition effects do not guarantee reliable individual differences.

The behavioral findings are stark. Shared directions hide different errors: in 2-back, both populations respond MATCH more often to lures than to controls, but models also accept ordinary non-targets at a rate .257 above humans (39 of 54 models), and Stroop interference is far larger (error increase with conflict 0.371 versus 0.051, with word answers on .376 of incongruent trials versus .042). Verbal confidence separates correct from incorrect answers worse than humans' does (Type-2 AUROC .573 versus .714, lower in 47 of 54 models), while token probabilities do about as well as human reports — and 37 models always choose the same wager, including accurate classifiers. Gaps persist among gate passers and in models of 27B or more.

The structural findings temper cognitive interpretations. Model scores share two dimensions, and the first is closely tied to scale, yet larger models are not consistently closer to humans; the predefined five-group cognitive taxonomy predicts held-out families' scores no better than random partitions of the same size. The paper's conclusion is methodological: matching human effect directions does not establish human-like behavior, so comparisons need errors and confidence reports, not only directions — and CogBattery releases the same text materials, condition-level human references, and scorers for testing new models.

## Key points

- Scope: 54 open-weight models, 23 families, 13 paradigms, each compared with condition-level human data from its source study — versus prior evaluations covering 1–16 tasks and 4–40 models with norms-only human references.
- n-back: the shared lure effect sits on an excess of false alarms to ordinary non-targets (+.257 [.159, .341], 39/54 models, 19/23 family means), and models barely separate targets from lures (hit minus lure rate .048 versus human .367); the excess persists in 27B+ models (.351, 7 of 9).
- Stroop: model accuracy falls .926 to .555 from congruent to incongruent trials (humans .976 to .925), and models answer with the word itself .376 of the time versus human .042; the gap shrinks but remains in 27B+ models (.089, 4 of 9).
- Digit span runs in the same direction at different sizes: models beat humans on forward length 9 (.750 versus .327) but lose on backward length 3 (.611 versus .961), a forward−backward gap of 0.419 versus the human 0.047.
- CVLT recall slope is inverted: humans gain 1.549 words per learning trial, models lose 0.287 — a −1.836 difference, the largest in the battery.
- Verbal confidence is weaker than human metacognition (Type-2 AUROC .573 versus .714) while models are simultaneously more confident (.859 versus .793) and less accurate (.659 versus .722) on calibration items; token probabilities reach .763 in the 41 well-measured models, .049 above humans.
- Wagering: 37 of 54 models always choose the same wager (29 low, eight high) across 19 of 23 families — including Gemma3 27B at 86.7% accuracy — and constant wagers mostly track response labels rather than confidence.
- Minimal-performance gates do not erase the gaps: among models passing each task's gate, DS differs by 0.378 (53 passers), NB by 0.266 (32), ST by 0.348 (51), and FL by 0.319 (18).
- Structure: PCA with permutation parallel analysis and MINRES factor analysis over the 13 task scores finds two shared dimensions, the first tied to scale; the predefined grouping's family-held-out predictive gain is δ* = −.011 [−.078, .075] after reliability correction — no reliable advantage over random partitions, though a positive control detects human-sized grouping in 97 of 100 simulations.

## Details

- Version note: the paper was renamed from CogArena; v1 appeared 2026-07-27 and v2 (2026-10-30) is the current revision — this summary follows v2.
- Key endpoints (model minus human, Table 4): DS forward−backward +0.372; NB non-target false alarms +0.257; OS letter-position accuracy −0.182; ST conflict error increase +0.320; FL +0.065 and GN +0.040 (both intervals include zero; none of the nine 27B+ models makes a no-go false alarm versus human .214); CVLT slope −1.836; DRM new−lure false alarms +0.293; SM source accuracy +0.052; FB false-belief accuracy −0.210 (human .983); EP mental-state slope +0.013; CAL Type-2 AUROC −0.141.
- Token-probability rerun: the 51 direct-answer models were rerun with the same Ollama build and weights (over 95% step agreement), reading the two option probabilities at the first answer position renormalized over the top 20 tokens; 41 models qualify with both options in the top 20 on at least 40 of 80 items, and within models the two confidence measures correlate only weakly (median rho = .16).
- Family-held-out prediction: leave one family out, fit standardization, the first principal component of the other 12 tasks, and the residualized same-group mean on training families, with the ridge penalty fixed at 1e-06; gain is assessed over 20,000 paired family-error-block draws against a reference of all 600,600 partitions into group sizes 3/3/3/2/2.
- Human cohort comparisons: in SRO the human grouping contrast is δH = .158 versus model δM = −.022, a difference of .180 [−.102, .371] that spans zero; the weak model working-memory correlation (.076 versus human .317) is not a reliability artifact (model reliabilities at least .972); in Ball the comparison depends on DRM scoring (native false-alarm scoring .249 [.064, .402]; pooled balanced accuracy .493 [.195, .704]); in two further cohorts model correlations exceed human ones in five of six task pairs, reliably only for DS–ST (.44 versus .06).
- Constant-wager caveat: high-minus-low accuracy is undefined — not zero — for constant wagerers, and the formal run's 1-or-2-sweets labels were not counterbalanced; under both A/B label mappings in the rerun, 30 of 51 models pick the same letter on at least 90% of wagers.
- Code, task materials, and scorers: github.com/dengzhe-hou/CogBattery.
