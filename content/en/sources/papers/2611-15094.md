# A frontier machine intelligence for human neuroimaging

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.15094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15094) · **Published**: 2026-11-11
**Authors**: Chenhui Zhao, Akshay Rao, Akhil Kondepudi, Yiwei Lyu, Xinhai Hou, Rikhil Seshadri, Cheng Jiang, Soumyanil Banerjee, Rushikesh Joshi, Samir Harake, Ashok Srinivasan, Shruti Mishra, Francisco Rivas-Rodriguez, Seunghong Rhee, Nathaniel Roddenberry, Neeraj Chaudhary, Zach Wilseck, Paul Park, Honglak Lee, Vikas Gulani, Aditya Pandey, Todd Hollon

## Overview

General-purpose frontier models lack access to private clinical imaging, and most medical AI systems handle narrow tasks. The University of Michigan Machine Learning in Neurosurgery Lab and collaborators take the opposite route: build a specialized model on the full clinical imaging stream of a health system. Neuro-VL is a vision-language model trained on 911,506 neuroimaging studies — 392,236 MRI and 519,270 CT — collected from routine care, covering the full spectrum of neurological pathology from neoplastic and vascular to infectious, degenerative, and traumatic conditions. The authors describe it as trained on nearly one million studies from 450,000 patients comprising 10 million volumetric scans, the largest neuroimaging dataset to date.

The model interprets complete imaging examinations and holds natural-language conversations about the findings, generating auditable reasoning traces. Its visual encoder uses a 24-layer ViT-Large backbone that divides each scan into non-overlapping 6×16×16 volumes projected into 1024-dimensional tokens, extended with a hierarchical attention mechanism operating at three levels — slice, scan, and study — and pretrained on radiology reports with a novel dual contrastive objective that pairs sentences (focal) and full summaries (holistic) with image content. The final model was trained on 970,000 visual instruction examples totaling 3.6 billion tokens, and a longitudinal sibling model adds training on paired prior-current studies.

Evaluation is deliberately broad: the new NeuroBench benchmark combines internal held-out studies with 24 international public datasets spanning 21 countries, and the model faces frontier baselines (GPT-5.4, Gemma 4) on closed-ended questions, report generation judged by an expert-validated LLM judge, longitudinal change assessment, and adversarial red-teaming. Code, 8B model weights, and the benchmark are publicly released.

## Key points

- Training partition: 911,506 studies (392,236 MRI, 519,270 CT) acquired before 1 July 2025; 50,177 held-out studies, of which 1,552 went to validation and 48,625 (28,474 MRI, 20,151 CT) to testing, with no patient overlap.
- NeuroBench comprises 31,975 closed-ended questions and 3,933 report-generation tasks across 10,481 unique studies and 46,826 volumetric scan inputs, organized into 7 evaluation groups including internal MRI and CT, MR-RATE, CTRG, a 20-dataset Open-Brain-20 collection, and CQ500 and RSNA head CT.
- Averaged equally across 17 task-group combinations, Neuro-VL outperformed GPT-5.4, Gemma 4, and NeuroVFM by 15.9, 16.9, and 23.0 percentage points respectively (P under 0.001 for all three).
- NeuroJudge report preferences across the 3,933 studies: wins outnumbered losses 2.4:1 against GPT-5.4 and 5.0:1 against Gemma 4 (P under 0.001).
- The visual encoder's hierarchical attention runs at slice, scan, and study levels, and the dual contrastive objective weights a focal sentence-level CLIP loss and a holistic full-summary loss equally.
- Neuro-VL-Longitudinal was instruction-tuned on 230,000 longitudinal examples — about 1.3 billion tokens — from paired prior and current studies, and evaluated on a benchmark of 2,294 questions spanning 734 study pairs and 10,527 scans.
- Longitudinal accuracy: 87.5% change characterization and 96.9% disease trajectory assessment versus GPT-5.4's 81.6% and 86.5%; 80.3% intervention characterization versus Gemma 4's 68.9%; report wins 1.4:1 and 7.8:1.
- Pediatric subgroup: Neuro-VL 89.2% accuracy (CI 86.8-91.7%) versus 61.8% for GPT-5.4 and 65.1% for Gemma 4.
- Red-teaming with misleading clinical context: fabrication resistance stayed at 96.1%, 95.6%, and 97.3% for Neuro-VL under neutral, pathology-assumed, and pathology-plus-location prompts, while GPT-5.4 fell from 87.7% to 64.1% and 51.4%.
- On 246 studies with expert-annotated glioma or infarct, replacing the correct diagnosis in the prompt with an incorrect one dropped success from 95.1% to 90.2% for Neuro-VL but from 99.6% to 44.5% for GPT-5.4; mislocalization resistance fell from 100% to 91.5% versus 57.7%.

## Details

### Data scale and visual encoder design

The train/held-out split used a 1 July 2025 acquisition cutoff, assigning all studies of patients with studies in both periods to the held-out pool to prevent leakage. Diagnostic labels were derived from radiology reports with an LLM pipeline. The encoder follows a two-stage FLIP-style pretraining with 50% patch dropout in the first stage, and outperformed HLIP and BiomedCLIP on zero-shot classification across MR-RATE, RSNA, and CQ500. Neuro-VL then combines this encoder with an LLM through a vision-language connector that projects visual features into the language model's embedding space.

### Longitudinal disease monitoring

Serial comparison is central to radiology, so the team built Neuro-VL-Longitudinal by further instruction-tuning on pairs of prior and current studies. The evaluation covers three clinically relevant scenarios — change characterization (n = 718), disease trajectory assessment (n = 710), and intervention characterization (n = 132) — plus longitudinal report generation on 734 pairs judged by NeuroJudge, where wins outnumbered losses 1.4:1 against GPT-5.4 and 7.8:1 against Gemma 4. The model also estimates lesion size on BraTS glioma and meningioma studies with mean absolute errors of 9.1 mm (anteroposterior), 7.2 mm (transverse), and 8.2 mm (craniocaudal).

### Safety red-teaming

The red-teaming suite asks whether an interactive system overweights user-supplied assumptions over objective imaging findings. On 320 normal studies, Neuro-VL avoided asserting significant pathology across all three context types with success rates of 94.4-99.7%, compared with 73.4-89.1% for GPT-5.4. The sharpest contrast appears under discordant context: GPT-5.4, which nearly matched Neuro-VL when the supplied diagnosis was correct (99.6% versus 95.1%), collapsed to 44.5% when it was wrong, while Neuro-VL lost only 4.9 points.
