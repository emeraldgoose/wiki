# Reviso: Not All Visual Evidence Deserves Equal Attention in Video Captioning

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03602) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Tengfei Liu, Yang Shi, Yuran Wang, Yuqi Tang, Yizhuo Jia, Zhuoran Zhang, Qixun Wang, Shujie Li, Xuanyu Zhu, Xinyu Liu, Minghui Guo, Shixuan Liu, Haotian Wang, Yuanxing Zhang, Pengfei Wan

## Overview

Multimodal large language models can generate detailed video captions, yet they may overemphasize minor details while overlooking essential context and key events. The authors argue that not all visual evidence deserves equal attention, because importance depends on video content. Reviso is an agentic video captioning framework that learns what to observe and how to gather relevant visual evidence. From a low-frame-rate global preview, it builds a hierarchical Context--Focus--Points observation plan that identifies key evidence sources.

## Key points

- Video captions can overemphasize minor details.
- Essential context can be overlooked.
- Evidence importance depends on video content.
- Reviso learns selective observation strategies.
- It starts from a low-frame-rate global preview.
- It builds a hierarchical observation plan.

## Details

The framework treats captioning as an evidence acquisition process rather than a single-pass generation. By planning observation at multiple levels, it can focus on key sources while avoiding noise that distracts from central events. The hierarchical plan connects global context to local details, aiming to produce captions that preserve what matters. The result is more balanced video captioning from multimodal models.
