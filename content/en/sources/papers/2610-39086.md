# HALO: Human-Grounded GrAnular EvaLuation of PrOactive Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.39086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39086) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: HALO Authors

## Overview

Proactive agents infer user needs from observed activity and initiate assistance without an explicit request. Existing evaluations often rely on automated scores or binary verdicts, giving limited insight into whether evidence supported the timing, scope, and content of an intervention. HALO introduces Human-Grounded GrAnular EvaLuation of proactive agents, combining executable task environments with fine-grained human annotation.

HALO-Arena spans 4 proactive need types (Dependency, Blockage, Risk, and Opportunity) across 10 everyday domains and 50 personal environments. Agents must retrieve evidence through structured database querying and unstructured file retrieval before deciding whether and how to act. On this benchmark, HALO-8K contains 8,000 responses from 10 models across 400 scenarios, annotated by 20 experts for action level, acceptability verdicts, and rejection diagnoses.

## Key points

- Evaluates whether, when, and how far proactive agents should assist.
- Combines evidence retrieval with human-grounded, fine-grained annotation.
- HALO-Arena covers 4 need types, 10 domains, and 50 personal environments.
- HALO-8K includes 8,000 responses from 10 models across 400 scenarios, annotated by 20 experts.
- Over-proactivity is the dominant failure mode: premature timing or excessive intensity accounts for 61.20% of rejected responses.
- Best proprietary and open-source models reach 72.88% and 56.63% acceptability.
- Restraint and retrieval effort are important factors in acceptability, not just intervention success.

## Details

The benchmark models cases where retrieved evidence may justify a reminder but not a financial action, illustrating how observation, evidence, and permission must be separated. It evaluates interventions using human diagnoses covering timing, intensity, unsupported evidence, irrelevance, and factual contradiction. No-action responses are also distinguished as accepted reserve versus unacceptable missed opportunity.

The reported analysis highlights tradeoffs among intervention, restraint, timing, intensity, and retrieval effort. Rather than a single binary score, HALO provides a granular picture of where proactive agents succeed or overstep.
