# Measuring and Teaching Language Model Agents to Act on Delayed Consequences

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.28158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28158) · **Published**: 2026-11-19 (v1)
**Authors**: Shardul Bansal, Seth Schilbe, Jarrod Barnes

## Overview

Language model agents are increasingly asked to run ongoing multi-day operations rather than isolated single-turn tasks, where today's choices reshape later observations and cash constraints — empty shelves hide lost walk-in demand, short-shipped customers quietly shrink future orders weeks later, and emergency buying drains working capital. The paper introduces SMB Bench, a calibrated 90-day wholesale simulator with 18 causally paired tasks (matched Day-0 normal vs consequence conditions, non-LLM reference policies, deterministic replay from any morning), plus an 859-run frontier trace dataset across 16 models that connects controlled cover trajectories, written-forecast audits, and restock replays.

The headline finding is that diagnosing a delayed effect, acting on it, and improving the business are separate achievements. Across all 16 frontier models and 859 scored runs, only three finish ahead of a Fixed Buying Rule (+$6,412/quarter on average) — GPT-6 Astra (+$7,749), Gemini 4 Carbon (+$7,703), Claude Fable 5.2 (+$7,275) — while the worst, GPT-6 Luna, lands at −$8,667; no model beats the fixed rule reliably in every run, and all trail a Scripted Expert Buyer (+$10,795) by over $3,000 per quarter. Behavioral audits show models often record a relevant diagnosis without changing later purchases: stockouts ran below 60% of the agent's own written forecasts in 78.7% (107/136) of audited cases, only 21.4% of reactive-account quarters contained a written link between falling orders and prior shortfalls, and just 3.7% both recorded that link and extended protection to all accounts within three mornings.

To study learning, the authors train an open model (Qwen3.8-27B) not on copied transcripts but on continuations from archived frontier-quarter starting states, with operational rules extracted from training-business records and prompt/optimizer choices refined on a validation business. On a sealed unseen test business under identical evaluation instructions, the trained policy followed customer-protection commitments more consistently — cutting protection reversals from 5.8 to 1.0 per quarter and reducing severe-loss (>$10k) quarters — finishing ahead of 8 of the 16 leaderboard models, though the added-profit benefit remained uncertain because early-quarter stocking habits left the buyer reliant on emergency supply and a deteriorating supplier.

## Key points

- SMB Bench: calibrated 90-day wholesale simulator, 18 causally paired tasks (Day-0 normal vs consequence conditions), non-LLM reference policies, deterministic replay from any morning; couples daily purchasing, inventory, supplier lead times, and cash across weeks.
- 859-run frontier evaluation over 16 models: only 3 beat the Fixed Buying Rule (+$6,412/quarter) on average — GPT-6 Astra +$7,749, Gemini 4 Carbon +$7,703, Claude Fable 5.2 +$7,275; range down to GPT-6 Luna −$8,667; all models trail the Scripted Expert Buyer (+$10,795) by >$3,000/quarter.
- Diagnosis-to-action gap: 78.7% of audited stockout cases ran below 60% of the agent's own written forecast; written diagnosis→action link appeared in 21.4% of reactive quarters, and full timely action (link + protection extended to all accounts within 3 mornings) in only 3.7%.
- Bought-vs-profit correlates with the fixed rule across the 9-model behavioral cohort (485 runs, overall Spearman ρ = +0.84), separating recorded diagnosis from consistent operational action.
- Training method for Qwen3.8-27B: extract operational rules from training-business records, refine prompts/optimizer on a validation business, and train on continuations from archived frontier mornings (starting states) rather than copying transcripts.
- Result on a sealed test business: protection reversals fell 5.8 → 1.0 per quarter and severe-loss (>$10k) quarters dropped, finishing ahead of 8/16 leaderboard models (−$4,495 vs +$1,969 for the top model), but profit gains stayed uncertain — fulfilling commitments exposed emergency-supply costs and supplier deterioration.

## Details

The benchmark separates three nested achievements: noticing/attributing a delayed consequence, changing behavior in time, and coordinating surrounding decisions so the response actually improves profit. The 18 tasks are causally paired so consequence conditions differ from matched Day-0 normals only through the agent's own earlier choices; non-LLM reference policies (Fixed Buying Rule, Scripted Expert Buyer at 64% of the Solver's +$16,923) bound what is achievable without an LLM. The trace dataset links controlled cover trajectories, written-forecast audits, and restock replays to measure whether a recorded diagnosis turned into a purchase change. The training pipeline deliberately uses archived decisions and their consequences as reusable experience: operational rules from training-business records, validation-business tuning of prompt and optimizer, and continuation training from archived frontier-quarter mornings, then evaluation on a sealed business with fixed instructions to avoid eval leakage.
