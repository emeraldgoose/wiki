# ResWAM: Learning What Changes, Not What Stays, for World-Action Models

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19559)
**Published**: 2026-11-14

## Overview

World-action models (WAMs) add future-frame prediction to vision-language-action (VLA) policies so the policy can anticipate how its actions affect the physical world. Existing WAMs supervise complete future observations, but observations change little over an action chunk, so the future loss is dominated by copying static background rather than learning task-relevant robot and object motion. They also rely on large video generation models or separate world models, incurring heavy training and inference compute that hinders closed-loop deployment.

ResWAM instead applies visual residual learning: supervising only the feature change from the current frame to the future frame in a frozen DINOv3 space. The residual target yields higher feature rank with richer, more diverse representations of scene change, and spatially concentrates on the robot arm and manipulated objects rather than the background. ResWAM is a 2.3B-parameter model with a lightweight 0.5B dual-branch DiT that jointly trains an action branch and a residual branch via conditional flow matching — without any robot pretraining on external trajectories.

## Key points

- Top average success rates without robot pretraining: LIBERO-Plus 81.1%, RoboTwin 2.0 93.8%, RoboDojo 9.40% success / 13.41 score.
- Lowest compute among compared methods: training 2.7–4.0 TFLOPs, inference 1.0–1.4 TFLOPs.
- Real-robot manipulation (Open, Put, Stack): 80.0% average success, +10.0% over π₀.₅, at 92.7 ms latency.
- Residuals have higher effective rank than complete future features and concentrate on the robot arm and manipulated objects rather than static background.
- Ablations: residual branch quality predicts policy performance; residual prediction quality and rank of predicted features improve across training steps; a dual-branch DiT with conditional flow matching trains action and residual branches jointly.

## Details

- Architecture: VLM backbone plus a 0.5B dual-branch DiT (action branch + residual branch); 2.3B parameters total; no video-generation model and no separate world model required.
- Training: conditional flow matching on the residual branch; residual computed in frozen DINOv3 feature space between current and future frames; no external robot pretraining beyond benchmark demonstrations.
- Inference: lightweight closed-loop deployment profile; latency 92.7 ms on the real-robot setup; FLOPs measured under a fixed protocol (appendix D).
- Benchmarks: LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin 2.0, RoboDojo (simulation) and real-world Open/Put/Stack manipulation; baselines include WAMs built on large video generation models and separate-world-model designs.
- Caveats: results are reported without robot pretraining; RoboDojo absolute success remains low (9.40%); residual quality depends on the frozen DINOv3 space.
