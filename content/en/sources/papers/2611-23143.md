# TCP4D: A Unified Tool-Center-Point Tracking and Generation Model for Robot Manipulation

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23143)
**Published**: 2026-11-17

## Overview

Robot manipulation videos provide rich visual experience, but recovering robot actions and gripper-state annotations from RGB observations remains challenging. Using the embodiment-agnostic tool-center-point (TCP) pose as the action space, TCP4D is a unified model in which a shared 4D scene representation both tracks observed TCP motion and generates future TCP trajectories. Given a monocular video and initial 2D TCP queries, the tracker directly recovers metric TCP poses in SE(3) and gripper states alongside scene geometry and camera parameters. The generator samples local features from the same 4D representation along the recovered TCP tracks, then combines them with robot observations and language instructions to predict future TCP states.

A cross-embodiment augmentation pipeline converts existing RGB videos into demonstrations for alternative embodiments by retargeting the recovered motion and synthesizing corresponding observations, without recorded source-robot states. Experiments show the tracker achieves absolute trajectory errors of 7.60 and 16.07 mm on DROID and RoboLab and adapts to a new embodiment with only ten demonstrations. The generator reaches 69.19% mean success on RoboTwin and beats established policy models on two real-world tasks, while joint training on source and augmented demonstrations improves target-robot success by 41.5% over augmented-only training. ## Key points

- Tracking (DROID / RoboLab): APD 74.78% / 43.71% — more than four times the strongest baselines; ATE reduced to 7.60 / 16.07 mm; 100% success rate on both datasets (best baselines 89.64% / 96.33%). Outperforms VGGT-Omega, DA3, MapAnything, 4RC, VDPM, Any4D, Track4World, CoTracker3, MoSE3.
- Real world (CoBoT Magic, two arms, 60 demos per task, 30 trials): TCP4D succeeds 83.33% (placing pens) / 73.33% (stacking bowls), averaging 78.33% vs DP3 63.33% and ACT 31.67% (+15.0 / +46.7 pp); mean progress 3.50/4; advantage over DP3 comes from directly inferring geometry + TCP motion from RGB without depth at inference.
- Cross-embodiment: the augmentation pipeline retargets recovered motion to alternative embodiments and synthesizes observations; joint source+augmented training improves target-robot success by 41.5% over augmented-only training.
- Generator on RoboTwin: 69.19% mean success; few-shot adaptation to a new embodiment with ten demonstrations.
- Method: shared 4D scene representation; tracker emits metric SE(3) TCP poses, gripper states, geometry and camera parameters from one monocular video; generator conditions on features sampled along recovered TCP tracks plus language instructions via conditional flow matching.

## Details

- Architecture: TCP4D Tracker (metric TCP recovery + geometry/camera from RGB) and TCP4D Generator (future TCP state prediction with conditional flow matching); cross-embodiment augmentation pipeline (retargeting + observation synthesis from RGB alone, no recorded source-robot states).
- Evaluation: RoboTwin simulation; DROID and RoboLab tracking benchmarks; real-world CoBoT Magic tasks (placing pens, stacking bowls); baselines DP3, ACT, plus nine 4D/point-tracking methods; metrics ATE/RRE/RTE, APD/MPE, AbsRel/MAE/δ₁, gripper-state accuracy, SR.
