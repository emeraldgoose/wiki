# Factored One-sided Adam-Moment for Practical and Scalable SOAP

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.16905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16905) · **Published**: 2026-11-12
**Authors**: Jaemyung Yu, Byeongho Heo, Sangdoo Yun, Dongyoon Han

## Overview

Second-order optimizers such as Shampoo and SOAP have emerged as compelling alternatives to AdamW for transformer pre-training. SOAP, the Adam-in-eigenbasis version of Shampoo, reports 35–40% improvements in wall-clock time and step count over AdamW at large batch sizes, building on Distributed Shampoo baselines that won the external-tuning track of the first AlgoPerf competition. The catch is cost: per weight matrix, SOAP maintains two dense Kronecker factor matrices plus a dense rotated second moment — about 4.5 times the optimizer state of AdamW — and spends substantial extra time on their periodic eigendecompositions.

FOAM (Factored One-sided Adam-Moment), from the NAVER AI Lab, keeps SOAP's optimization quality while approaching AdamW-level cost through two structural simplifications. First, input-only preconditioning drops the output-side Kronecker factor and keeps only the input-side factor, removing that factor's memory and one of the two eigendecompositions. Second, it replaces the dense rotated second moment with a factored Adam-moment estimator based on row-by-column statistics — two bias-corrected running means of the squared rotated gradient with a trace-matching scalar — shrinking the buffer from m-by-n to m-plus-n.

The contribution is less the individual tricks — SOAP's own paper had already ablated one-sided and factored variants — than the explanation of which side to keep, when factorization is justified, and why the combination works. The Kronecker structure of the Hessian implies that all output rows of an affine layer share the same input-side outer product, while transformer Hessians are empirically near-block-diagonal along output neurons or heads, making the input side the natural one to keep. Under the K-FAC Fisher assumption with a diagonal output curvature, the factored estimator exactly recovers SOAP's dense second moment, and the measured K-FAC violation is only 15–20%.

Empirically, across GPT-2-scale LLM pre-training from 124M to 760M parameters — with supporting evidence at 1.56B and on ViT — FOAM matches strong SOAP and Muon baselines in validation loss, uses half of SOAP's optimizer state, and runs at Muon-class wall-clock time: SOAP's gains need not require SOAP's cost.

## Key points

- SOAP needs roughly 4.5 times the optimizer state of AdamW; FOAM uses half of SOAP's matrix-parameter state at every size, and measured peak memory at GPT-2 small is +7.4% over AdamW versus +12.9% for SOAP.
- Input-only preconditioning removes one of SOAP's two eigendecompositions. Gauss-Newton alignment analysis shows removing the output-side rotation costs 0.160 of alignment on average against 0.375 for removing the input side, and the output factor's eigenvalue ratio is 5.0 against 67.9 on the input side, so output axes already capture most of the structure.
- The factored second moment stores two vectors instead of the dense m-by-n rotated buffer. Under K-FAC with a diagonal output curvature it exactly recovers the dense moment; measured violation stays in a 15–20% band with negligible effect on the update.
- On GPT-2 large (760M parameters, 29.55K steps), SOAP ends at validation loss 2.791 versus FOAM's 2.796, within about one seed standard deviation; on wall-clock, normalized so AdamW finishes at 1.0 times, Muon, FOAM, and SOAP finish at 1.15, 1.17, and 2.31 times, and FOAM reaches SOAP's quality band at 51% of SOAP's wall-clock time.
- Time to AdamW's final loss (2.847): FOAM arrives as fast as AdamW itself (10.53 vs 10.55 hours) and slightly ahead of Muon, while bilateral SOAP needs 2.15 times as long.
- The pre-training recipe is modern: GPT-2 small/medium/large on FineWeb at a 1-times Chinchilla budget (about 20 tokens per parameter), i.e., 2.49B, 7.10B, and 15.49B tokens over 4,750, 13,550, and 29,550 steps at 0.5M-token batches, in bfloat16 on 8 H100 GPUs.
- Across scales FOAM trails SOAP by only 0.004, 0.008, and 0.005 nats at 124M, 350M, and 760M — each within about one seed standard deviation — while staying ahead of Muon, AdaMuon, and AdamW at every scale.
- Hyperparameter robustness: FOAM's loss bowl tracks AdamW and SOAP within 0.05 nats across learning-rate, weight-decay, and beta sweeps over a three-fold learning-rate range; AdamW collapses to 3.86 at beta-1 of 0.99, where SOAP and FOAM degrade by only 0.02 nats.
- Batch-size scaling: raising the batch from 0.5M to 2M tokens lifts AdamW's loss from 3.370 to 3.956 but FOAM's only from 3.309 to 3.364; at 2M tokens per step FOAM cuts the time to AdamW's 0.5M-batch quality from 24.9 to 8.2 minutes, about a 3-times reduction.
- At GPT-2 XL (1.56B, 31B tokens) FOAM and SOAP tie within 0.001 nats; on ViT-Tiny on Tiny-ImageNet the matrix-preconditioned optimizers beat AdamW by 6.5 to 8.5 top-1 accuracy points, with SOAP first and FOAM second; on Qwen3-1.7B post-training, FOAM is best in SFT and tied with AdamW in on-policy distillation.

## Details

### Why the input side is the side to keep

Under the K-FAC factorization, an affine layer's gradient covariance is a Kronecker product of an input-side and an output-side factor, and every output row shares the same input-side outer product; the transformer Hessian is empirically near-block-diagonal along output neurons or heads, so off-diagonal curvature lives on the input side. Per-module Gauss-Newton measurements at step 2,000 quantify the asymmetry: output-only preconditioning scores 0.132 alignment, barely above diagonal Adam's 0.090, and input-only exceeds output-only in every module type at both scales, by 1.5 to 10.9 times at 124M and 1.9 to 11.5 times at 350M.

### A factored second moment that loses nothing

Rotating by the input eigenbasis turns the covariance into a Kronecker product whose diagonal, reshaped to m-by-n, is a rank-one matrix — the output diagonal times the rotated input eigenvalues. Storing row and column EMAs of the squared rotated gradient with a trace-matching scalar therefore keeps every coordinate's own variance and loses nothing relative to the dense buffer under the K-FAC assumption. The eigenbasis is refreshed only every pf steps, guarded by the Purifying Shampoo skip rule, and the violation remains around 15–20% throughout training with update costs of about +0.003 to +0.004, so no stage of training makes the factored update meaningfully worse.

### Robustness, batches, and post-training

The GPT-2 small ablation shows the construction pays: full FOAM reaches 3.293 validation loss at 2.59 GiB of state versus SOAP's 3.295 at 2.92 GiB, while the input-only factored variant lands at 3.302 with 1.78 GiB and the fastest wall-clock, 0.44 hours. Beyond the main setting, FOAM holds its quality as batch size grows where AdamW degrades sharply, and in post-training on Qwen3-1.7B — SFT on 100K NuminaMath-CoT examples and on-policy distillation — FOAM is best in SFT within 0.002 NLL and tied with AdamW in OPD. The authors note limitations: experiments stop at 1.56B parameters, the input factor and its eigenbasis are not yet sharded across devices, and the factored update is least accurate at the key projection. Code is available at github.com/naver-ai/foam.
