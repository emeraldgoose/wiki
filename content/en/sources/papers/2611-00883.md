# Random Is Almost Enough? Stable and Low-Cost Benchmark Compression via BestRand

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.00883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00883) · **Published**: 2026-10-31
**Authors**: George Ling, Shanshan Zhong, Lijin Yang, Hao Yang, Nanxiang Li, Zhongzhan Huang

## Overview

Benchmark compression aims to reduce the cost of model evaluation by scoring models on a small subset that closely matches full-benchmark results. This paper begins with a quantitative analysis of estimation error, showing that most random subsets yield small errors while a few yield much larger ones, producing a long-tailed error distribution.

That distribution means random sampling can work well with high probability, but it remains unstable because of rare large errors. The instability is characterized as inherent to modern foundation models and persists as more models are evaluated. The paper then introduces BestRand to retain random sampling's efficiency while reducing the high-error tail.

BestRand is validated across more than 70 text, multimodal, and agent benchmarks, plus VLA evaluation. It reduces high-error risk and estimation error while using substantially less construction cost, and it reduces out-of-distribution estimation error across score-range, model-family, and temporal shifts.

## Key points

- Most random benchmark subsets yield small estimation errors, but rare large errors create instability.
- Error instability persists as more foundation models are evaluated.
- BestRand preserves random sampling efficiency while reducing high-error cases.
- The method is validated on more than 70 benchmarks plus VLA evaluation.
- BestRand lowers high-error risk and average estimation error.
- It also improves out-of-distribution estimation across several shift types.

## Details

The paper provides an analytical approximation to the error distribution and practical guidance for predicting benchmark compression accuracy and reliability. The released code and data support further research. The work is a preprint.
