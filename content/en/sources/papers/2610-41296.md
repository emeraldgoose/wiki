# Self-Improvement with Auxiliary Rewards: Learning from Verified Traces and Evaluating Transfer

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.41296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41296) · **Published**: 2026-10-30
**Authors**: Minju Gwak, Alan Ritter

## Overview

Answer-level rewards cannot distinguish reasoning traces with equally correct outcomes. This paper studies whether auxiliary trajectory preferences improve small-language-model self-improvement and transfer under limited supervision. It compares confidence-based Self-Consistency (SC) with an entropy-based metric called UID-Conj., which combines global entropy variation and local smoothness. The two signals are used through online reward augmentation and offline training on correct responses. Online experiments adapt Qwen3-1.7B on 30 AIME problems without reference answers, while offline experiments use historical AIME problems.

## Key points

- Answer rewards alone do not differentiate reasoning traces that reach the same correct answer.
- The study compares confidence-based SC with entropy-based UID-Conj.
- UID-Conj. combines global entropy variation and local smoothness.
- Online reward augmentation is evaluated on Qwen3-1.7B using 30 AIME problems without reference answers.
- Offline training uses historical AIME problems.
- The setting explicitly targets self-improvement and transfer with limited supervision.

## Details

The central motivation is that equally correct final answers can hide different reasoning quality, so outcome rewards provide an incomplete learning signal. Auxiliary preferences over trajectories aim to fill that gap. The paper contrasts a confidence-based signal with an entropy-based one, then applies each in two regimes: online augmentation on current problems and offline training on historical problems. The reported comparison focuses on how these auxiliary rewards affect small model self-improvement and transfer, not on changing the base optimizer itself.
