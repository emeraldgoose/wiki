# QFabric: Guarded Cross-Domain Task Recovery and Its Limits on a Linux-RTOS Board

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.01598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01598) · **Published**: 2026-11-01
**Authors**: Ayushman Bhattacharya, Anwesha Chakraborty

## Overview

Heterogeneous embedded boards commonly pair a Linux application processor with a real-time microcontroller (MCU), and that pairing invites an obvious tactic: move a task from Linux to the MCU when its timing degrades. QFabric reframes that idea not as free migration but as guarded recovery from a violated empirical soft real-time contract. A bounded task is compiled for both domains, monitored in windows with hysteresis, and switched only after semantic and capacity admission at a quiescent epoch boundary; after the switch it is committed or rolled back following probation.

The prototype is evaluated on an Arduino UNO Q with a Cortex-A53 Linux side and a Cortex-M33 running Zephyr. The authors characterize the stock Bridge RPC interconnect and measure five workloads in both domains under idle conditions, CPU load, and higher-priority SCHED_FIFO contention, with ordinary and real-time callers.

## Key points

- Every MCU invocation costs about 19 ms of dispatch regardless of compute, and the MCU runs the heaviest kernel roughly 34x slower than the Linux side.
- Because every call traverses Linux userspace, Linux contention inflates the MCU path far more than the Linux path: p99 latency reaches up to 400 ms versus 17.0 ms.
- Across 30 cells of a four-node chain, Linux meets every contract within 0.8% misses, and QFabric correctly never migrates.
- A controller without hysteresis migrates in 7 cells, raising p95 latency by up to 177x, and probation rolls back 5 of those moves.
- The paper supersedes an earlier artifact release whose reported improvement came from identified measurement defects.

## Details

The central lesson is negative but precise: on Linux-dispatched heterogeneous boards, migration cannot isolate a task from Linux interference, because the MCU path still depends on Linux userspace. Cross-domain placement therefore fails as a latency-isolation mechanism in this setup. The recovery guards still matter, but chiefly by preventing harmful moves rather than by completing useful ones.

QFabric's contribution is consequently the engineering of denial: hysteresis keeps transient contract violations from triggering switches, admission checks keep infeasible moves from starting, and probation makes an attempted migration reversible. The comparison between the guarded controller and the unguarded controller quantifies how much damage hysteresis avoids, not merely how smooth the guarded path feels.

The paper also publishes source code, raw traces, and generators, making the characterization reproducible.
