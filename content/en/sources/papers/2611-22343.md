# PURE: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Multi-Granularity Multimodal Identity Matching

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.22343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22343) · **Published**: 2026-11-17 (v1)
**Authors**: Hengyu You, Xintong Wang, Jin Cao, Liang Ding

## Overview

Identity matching asks whether two records denote the same identity, but the answer depends on the target granularity: two records may match at the base level (same underlying product design) while differing at the variant level (attributes such as color or size). Aggregate matching accuracy does not separately characterize the capabilities involved, so a model can look strong on average while failing precisely where distinctions matter. To study this, the authors use e-commerce as a challenging real-world domain and introduce PURE, a diagnostic benchmark for multi-granularity multimodal identity matching.

PURE covers four capability dimensions — Perception, Understanding, Reasoning, and Evidence-grounded Judgment — through 1,457 multiple-choice questions across 14 tasks derived from real-world matching pairs and verified by two domain experts. Evaluating ten recent multimodal large language models, overall scores range from 47.1% to 86.0%. Understanding receives the highest mean score and Judgment the lowest, with weaknesses concentrated in visual difference localization and variant-level rule judgment.

## Key points

- **Granularity-aware diagnosis**: base-level vs. variant-level judgments are scored separately, revealing what aggregate accuracy hides — not only whether a model judges a pair correctly, but where it struggles to interpret evidence and apply matching criteria.
- **Score profile across 10 MLLMs**: overall 47.1%–86.0%; Understanding highest on average, Judgment lowest; weak spots are visual difference localization and variant-level rule judgment.
- **Modality ablations**: models benefit from multimodal evidence but can incorrectly identify conflicts between matching records.
- **Asymmetry by granularity**: in direct matching, most models recognize matching pairs more reliably than non-matching pairs at the base level, whereas the reverse holds at the variant level.

## Details

The benchmark's four dimensions decompose the matching decision: Perception covers whether the model can see the relevant visual differences at all; Understanding covers grasping what those differences mean for identity; Reasoning covers applying matching rules across granularities; and Evidence-grounded Judgment covers basing the final call on the actual evidence rather than on surface similarity. Because questions are multiple choice and expert-verified, failures can be attributed to a capability dimension rather than to label noise.

The variant-level reversal is the diagnostic core: at the base level models are comparatively good at confirming that two records match and at rejecting obvious non-matches asymmetrically; at the variant level the pattern inverts, so models that reject non-matching variants too eagerly (or accept matching ones too loosely) dominate the error profile. Modality ablations further show that adding images helps on average but that models can treat conflicting multimodal evidence as confirming rather than contradicting a match.
