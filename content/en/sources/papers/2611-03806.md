# BEND 2.0: Benchmarking the Capabilities Needed to Design DNA

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03806) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Andreas Bjerregaard, Rachael DeVries, Dionysia Danai Brilli, Panagiotis Antoniadis, Yan Li, Anders Krogh, Wouter Boomsma, Ole Winther, Frederikke Isa Marin

## Overview

Genomic foundation models are increasingly used to propose DNA sequences and edits, but successful design requires capabilities that current benchmarks do not measure. BEND 2.0 is a benchmark for DNA language models that tests two capabilities: predicting the effects of DNA changes and whether such predictions can guide sequence design. Across eight tasks built on five experimental datasets, it studies model choice, representation access, transfer beyond individual predictors, and the gap between benchmark accuracy and design utility.

## Key points

- The benchmark has eight tasks across five experimental datasets.
- It measures both effect prediction and design guidance.
- Linear readouts on frozen representations can match or exceed native model outputs.
- Functional post-training substantially improves capabilities.
- Linear readouts transfer across assays and genes.
- Strong average predictions can hide weaker context-specific predictions.

## Details

Results show that model choice and how representations are accessed both affect performance. Linear readouts transfer well across assays and genes, suggesting capture of functional patterns beyond individual predictors. However, accurate prediction of average effects does not imply accurate prediction of context-specific effects, and accurate prediction of combined mutation effects does not imply accurate prediction of their interactions. In design tasks, model predictions can reduce candidate requirements but still leave substantial room for better choices, especially for context-specific and multi-cell objectives. The benchmark indicates genomic models show general utility signs but have not saturated even simple tasks.
