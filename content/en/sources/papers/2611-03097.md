# DR Pixel: Page-Native Deep Research with Vision-Language Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.03097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03097) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Tiansheng Hu, Yichuan Wang, James V. Talwar, Nicholas Tomlin, Chen Zhao, Sewon Min

## Overview

Complex documents such as scientific papers contain rich information in tables, figures, and equations, yet deep research agents often reduce them to OCR-extracted text. DR Pixel asks whether vision-language models can enable page-native deep research by retrieving and reasoning directly over rendered document page images. It is introduced as the first open-source end-to-end multimodal deep research agent that uses rendered page images as its sole retrieval and reasoning unit, operating over a corpus of 1.06 million pages. The associated DRPixelBench evaluates this setting.

## Key points

- Document tables, figures, and equations carry important information.
- OCR text reduction loses much document structure.
- Page-native deep research reasons over rendered page images.
- DR Pixel is an open-source multimodal deep research agent.
- Page images serve as the retrieval and reasoning unit.
- The corpus contains 1.06 million pages.

## Details

The core idea is to preserve visual document structure instead of flattening it. Rendered pages keep layout, figures, and equations in their original context, allowing a VLM to reason holistically over the page. DR Pixel builds an end-to-end system around this unit, including retrieval across a large page corpus. The benchmark supports evaluation of answer quality and grounding in visual documents rather than text extraction alone.
