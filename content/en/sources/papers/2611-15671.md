# The Noise Floor of a Quantization Comparison: Engine, restart, batch and kernel drift, measured against 8-bit and 4-bit effects in two decision models

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15671)
**Published**: 2026-11-11

## Overview

A quantized model is usually judged against its BF16 original, but that comparison changes more than the weights: it often changes the serving engine, always runs on a different server instance, may change request batching, and on some stacks the library silently selects a different kernel depending only on which packages are imported. This paper measures each of those sources on the same 2,400 public decisions for two of Cloudflare's decision models — Clef-flash (9B) and Clef (27B) — on an NVIDIA GB10, and states every quantization effect as the excess over them.

Non-quantization sources move between 0 and 0.92% of decisions. llama.cpp restarts are bit-identical on every record for both models. Inside one engine, quantization moves far more: 0.88%/1.04% at Q8_0, 2.12%/1.62% at FP8, 5.79%/3.50% at Q4_K_M, and 5.79%/5.67% at NVFP4 (Clef-flash/Clef). On both models, every 4-bit interval excludes every non-quantization source. Two of five pre-registered predictions failed — FP8 is not inside restart noise, and an engine change moves fewer decisions than 8-bit quantization. Accuracy hides most of this: over 31,275 public questions, Clef's Q4_K_M build shifts accuracy by a net six questions (-0.02 points) while 436 of its decisions get worse.

## Key points

- Non-quantization drift, same 2,400 decisions: llama.cpp BF16 restart 0 flips (both models); vLLM BF16 restart 0/4; vLLM FP8 restart 5/0; vLLM NVFP4 restart 20/22; engine change at BF16 (transformers→llama.cpp) 6/16 and (transformers→vLLM) 8/13; batch size 1→8 8/15; Gated DeltaNet kernel fallback→fla 10/14.
- Quantization within one engine (vs BF16): Q8_0 0.88%/1.04%, FP8 2.12%/1.62%, Q4_K_M 5.79%/3.50%, NVFP4 5.79%/5.67% (Clef-flash/Clef) — 4-bit exceeds all non-quantization sources on both models, with intervals excluding zero.
- At 8-bit the ordering mostly holds: of 32 differences between an 8-bit build and a non-quantization source across both models, 29 exclude zero; the three exceptions pair Q8_0 against NVFP4 restarts (both models) and, for Clef, against the llama.cpp engine change.
- Pre-registered predictions (written 2026-10-17): two of five failed, identically on the 27B rerun — FP8 is not inside restart noise, and changing engine moves fewer decisions than 8-bit quantization.
- Accuracy hides decision churn: over 31,275 public questions, Clef Q4_K_M nets six questions (-0.02 pts [-0.20, +0.16]) while 436 decisions worsen (exploratory); Clef-flash Q4_K_M nets 114 questions while 527 worsen. NVFP4 costs Clef-flash 0.95 points and Clef 0.65.
- The 27B's non-quantization sources (other than restarts) are about twice Clef-flash's (engine/batch/kernel: 13-16 flips vs 6-10), yet its 4-bit GGUF build moves less on this unit (84 flips vs 139).

## Details

- Setup: same 2,400 public decisions per model; Clef-flash column holds the pre-registered measurement, Clef reruns it (exploratory). 95% Clopper-Pearson intervals; bit-identical record shares reported per pair.
- Metrics: decision flips (top-option changes), not just accuracy — the deployed behavior of a decision model is its top option, so flips are the operator-relevant quantity.
- Batch-size row is a rerun plus a batch change at once; on Clef-flash the drift is attributed to batching (fallback path reproduces bit-for-bit at batch 1) — an inference, not a measurement, and not repeated on the 27B.
- A third vLLM instance (exploratory) leaves Clef FP8 at 0 but puts NVFP4 at 4-22 flips depending on which two instances are compared — instance identity alone can move 4-bit builds.
- Code, quantization recipes, per-record outputs, and the pre-registration are released.
