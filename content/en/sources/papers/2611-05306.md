# You don't need long context

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05306) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Peter Belcak, Saurav Muralidharan, Jindong Jiang, Yonggan Fu, Wonmin Byeon, Hanrong Ye, Ryan Angilly, Ali Taghibakhshi, Shih-Yang Liu, Siru Ouyang, Greg Heinrich, Pavlo Molchanov

## Overview

Later tokens are costlier to generate than earlier tokens; on frontier open-weight models the disparity may be as large as seven times. The paper shows that, through frequent compaction, frontier language models sacrifice little to no task accuracy while maintaining short contexts across a broad range of long-horizon and long-input tasks. It further demonstrates that LLM performance does not collapse on long-horizon software engineering tasks even when the model takes at most one action per context. Based on this, the authors outline semantic state space models, questioning the need for long-context language models in some agentic workflows.

## Key points

- Generating later tokens can cost much more than earlier tokens.
- The reported cost disparity can reach 7x on frontier open-weight models.
- Frequent compaction preserves short contexts with minimal accuracy loss.
- The claim is tested across diverse long-horizon and long-input tasks.
- Performance remains viable with at most one action per context on software tasks.
- The paper questions universal demand for very long context windows.

## Details

The report has 31 pages and 8 figures and is a technical benchmark style study rather than a deployment guideline. Its findings suggest that context maintenance through compaction can be an alternative to long windows, but accuracy preservation depends on state extraction quality, task type, and evaluation setup. Long-context models still matter for workflows requiring exact preservation of large source material.
