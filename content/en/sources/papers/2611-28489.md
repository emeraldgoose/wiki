# Open Vision Banana: An Open Framework for Unified Visual Perception through Generation in RGB Space

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.28489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28489) · **Published**: 2026-11-21 (v1)
**Authors**: Xu Lin, Ruilin Yao, Haoran Xu, Zixiang Wang, Ming Ma, Yu Zhao, Pu Miao, Chonghan Liu

## Overview

Vision Banana showed that a closed image generator, instruction-tuned to paint perception outputs as RGB images, becomes a generalist vision model. Open Vision Banana asks whether this carries over to open generators, and if not, what fails. OVB adapts the 7B diffusion generator of Qwen-Image-2.1 into one instruction-driven model covering metric depth, semantic, instance, and referring segmentation, using a single shared low-rank adapter and public training data. Each task is a "task contract": its RGB encoder, instruction, and decoder share one specification and are checked for compatibility before training, which keeps the tasks composable in one generator.

The study externally evaluates 42 checkpoints from two 100k-update runs, six exact weight-space merges, and eight controlled shorter runs. The gap to the closed system turns out to be specific and measurable rather than uniform: relative geometry is learned early, but absolute scale is not committed by any single sample; depth and 2D segmentation compete for the shared adapter; and weight merging moves along that trade-off without escaping it. Finally, lower validation flow loss comes with lower Cityscapes accuracy, so the training loss cannot be used to select checkpoints.

## Key points

- Metric depth: median-aligned NYUv2 delta-1 stays at 0.89-0.93 throughout training, while unaligned delta-1 wanders between 0.11 and 0.50 — the model learns relative scene structure early but not absolute scale. A ten-seed ensemble lifts unaligned delta-1 from 0.348 to 0.516 without retraining, showing scale is not committed by a single sample.
- Per-image median (scale) alignment reveals how much of the depth error is scale: unaligned delta-1 0.497 becomes 0.926 on NYUv2, 0.225 becomes 0.974 on ETH3D, 0.353 becomes 0.926 on DIODE-Indoor.
- Task competition: continued adaptation improves unaligned NYUv2 delta-1 from 0.278 (init) to 0.407 (17.5k updates) and 0.497 (100k), while Cityscapes mIoU declines from 0.386 to 0.355 and 0.298 over the same runs — depth and 2D segmentation pull the shared adapter in opposite directions. Base checkpoint numbers: NYUv2 0.278 (aligned 0.873), ETH3D 0.292 (0.924), DIODE-Indoor 0.300 (0.844), Cityscapes mIoU 0.386, RefCOCOg cIoU 0.466, ReasonSeg gIoU 0.417.
- Checkpoint selection: Spearman rho about -0.9 between validation flow-loss reduction and Cityscapes mIoU — lower loss means lower segmentation accuracy, so the loss cannot pick checkpoints. Six exact weight-space merges move along the depth-vs-segmentation trade-off without escaping it, and none of the three compared models dominates all benchmarks.
- Evaluation hygiene: NYUv2, ETH3D, and ReasonSeg contribute no training data at all; DIODE and RefCOCOg contribute training splits and are reported as same-source held-out; all scores come from one shared evaluation protocol with disjoint train/eval image identifiers.

## Details

OVB releases code, task contracts, training data, and all evaluation outputs, plus a Hugging Face model zoo. The task-contract mechanism (shared RGB encoding specification, instruction format, decoder, with pre-training checks) is what allows four heterogeneous perception tasks to live in one frozen-base generator with a single LoRA, and the checkpoint study is a practical guide for open-generator perception training: trust per-benchmark external evaluation, not the training loss.
