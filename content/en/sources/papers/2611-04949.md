# HERIT: Enabling Global Access to Hanja-to-English Historical Translation by Mitigating Temporal Bias

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04949) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Dayeon Jeong, Kyeongpil Kang

## Overview

Historical archives are invaluable for multidisciplinary research but remain globally inaccessible due to severe language barriers. Manual translation is prohibitively expensive and slow, and simply applying existing machine translation models is inadequate because of unique linguistic nuances and temporal bias inherent in scarce human-labeled corpora. The paper proposes HERIT, a systematic domain-adaptation pipeline that leverages retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to generate high-quality pseudo-labeled data from abundant Hanja-Korean corpora. This substantially expands the training dataset, mitigating both data scarcity and temporal overfitting across historical periods. Extensive automated and human evaluations show HERIT consistently outperforms baselines. The authors then translate previously untranslated portions of the archives with their model, enabling global access to these resources for researchers.

## Key points

- Historical archives are valuable but globally inaccessible because of language barriers.
- Existing MT models struggle with Hanja's linguistic nuances and temporal bias from scarce labels.
- HERIT uses RAG over abundant Hanja-Korean corpora to generate pseudo-labeled data.
- Expanded training mitigates scarcity and temporal overfitting across periods.
- Automated and human evaluations show consistent gains over baselines, with new translations released.

## Details

The study includes automated and human evaluation. It uses Hanja-Korean corpora to expand training data and focuses on historical archive translation. Full experimental setup and evaluation protocols are in the linked source.
