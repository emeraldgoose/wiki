# Agent-R^2: Scaling Agents for Realistic Productivity Work via Real-Source Data Synthesis

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.08219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08219) · **Published**: 2026-11-05
**Authors**: Teng Pan, Zixuan Wang, Yuchen Yan, Qifei Wu, Yao Chen, Yingqi Qu, Ruiqing Zhang, Yongliang Shen

## Overview

Language-model agents are increasingly expected to carry out realistic productivity work: following long-horizon professional workflows, reading heterogeneous files, and producing usable deliverables. The paper locates the bottleneck in post-training data — manually authored workflows do not scale, and LLM-generated files lack the layouts, gaps, and cross-file references of real documents. Agent-R^2 answers with a synthesis pipeline grounded in authentic material at every stage: occupational activity seeds mine file-oriented workflow blueprints from web corpora, a search-and-verification loop retrieves public files supporting each workflow, and instructions plus fine-grained rubrics are synthesized from the verified workflow-file pairs.

From 10k web-corpus candidates the framework distills 4k task instances, each pairing real multi-format attachments with deliverable-level rubrics and averaging 2.51 attachments; files over 50 pages or 2 MB are discarded. Claude-Opus-4.5 serves as the expert policy that solves each task in a sandbox, producing reference files and trajectories used for rubric verification before a task is retained.

The authors then train Agent-R^2-4B on Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 in two stages — SFT on 3k instances, then rubric-based RL with GRPO on 1k tasks using Qwen3.6-Plus as an MLLM-as-a-judge. Despite only 4k training instances, the model surpasses much larger baselines on GDPval and OfficeQA, and the analysis shows it mitigates shortcut behavior: producing plausible-looking deliverables before sufficient grounding, construction, or verification.

## Key points

- Agent-R^2-4B reaches 51.0% on full GDPval versus 48.0% for Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507, and 14.8% versus 13.8% on out-of-domain OfficeQA, improving its Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 base by 18.6 points on GDPval Pass@1 (32.4 to 51.0).
- On office tasks it comes within 4.7 points of Gemini-2.5-Pro on Pass@3; OfficeQA Pass@3 is 26.8 versus 25.6 for the 235B model.
- The pipeline keeps files authentic: public web files are searched and verified per workflow, anything over 50 pages or 2 MB is discarded, and PDFs, spreadsheets, and presentations retain their original layouts and cross-file dependencies.
- SFT transforms exploration: average tool-call steps rise from 6.7 in the base model to 39.0, errors rise from 2.88 to 16.3, and completion rate dips from 86.8% to 78.2%.
- RL converts exploration into reliable completion: completion rate climbs to 90.9%, tool-call steps settle at 33.1, and errors fall from 16.3 to 12.9.
- Shortcut metrics improve at every stage on the 220 GDPval tasks: deliverable re-checking goes from 0.5% to 95.8% after RL, writing despite an unfixed error falls from 55.0% to 11.0%, placeholder text drops to 5.7% — matching expert-written GDPval deliverables — and traceable numbers rise from 31.2% to 38.8%.
- Ablations confirm both grounding choices: SFT on real files scores 43.3 GDPval Pass@1 versus 41.4 with synthetic files, with expert trajectories taking 2.54× more tool calls (20.4 vs. 8.0), and removing rubric rewards lowers RL Pass@1 from 51.0 to 49.5.

## Details

### Synthesis pipeline

Starting from occupational seeds, the pipeline extracts workflow blueprints from web corpora, then runs a multi-turn search-and-verification loop that checks titles and snippets, downloads and inspects candidates, and retains only files that jointly satisfy the workflow's information and operation needs. Task synthesis instantiates each workflow-file pair in a sandbox, renders paginated files to images and spreadsheets to tables, and jointly designs instruction and rubric; the expert policy (Claude-Opus-4.5) solves the task, and a verifier cross-checks reference deliverables against the rubric before retention.

### Training and evaluation

SFT teaches trajectory-following, file inspection, and deliverable construction; rubric-based RL optimizes outcome quality with GRPO in the persistent sandbox. Deliverables are converted to model-consumable form — PDF, PPTX, and DOCX rendered to images, XLSX keeping recalculated values, formulas, and rendered pages. The reward is the judge's normalized rubric score with no penalty for intermediate tool-call errors. GDPval uses an agent-as-a-judge protocol with Gemini-3.1-Pro as judge, OfficeQA combines rule-based matching with model assessment, and both cap episodes at 100 steps.

### Behavior analysis

Effective steps — tool-call steps not ending in errors — correlate with GDPval Pass@1 across model scales, and the paper frames shallow interaction followed by premature output as shortcut behavior. In a materials-test report case study, the base model writes a report from dummy spreadsheet evidence and Gemini-2.5-Pro silently converts key measurements to NaN, while Agent-R^2-4B recovers from parsing, computation, and plotting errors and verifies the final plots. Results are shown with a 4B backbone; the rubric-verification loop depends on a strong expert policy, and the non-office GDPval subset (audio and video deliverables) remains harder, with RL contributing the larger gains there.
