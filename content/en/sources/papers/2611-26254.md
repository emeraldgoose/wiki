# Prism: Hierarchical Text Detection with Minimalist Design

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.26254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26254) · **Published**: 2026-11-19 (v1)
**Authors**: Yuchen Su, Xuesheng Wang, Zhilong Ji, Jinfeng Bai, Shijian Lu, Zhineng Chen

## Overview

Hierarchical text detection localizes words, text lines, and paragraphs and parses their structural relationships, giving richer layout cues for text-rich scenes and documents. Existing methods chase accuracy with "architectural stacking": complex multi-scale necks (FPN/PANet/BiFPN, deformable attention), heavy high-resolution heads, and explicit relation-reasoning modules such as LayoutFormer's word-to-line and line-to-paragraph modeling. Prism departs from this paradigm and asks whether a sufficiently pre-trained backbone can replace all of those external modules.

Prism performs multi-granularity detection by attaching parallel lightweight heads to a single-scale backbone feature map — covering words, lines, and paragraphs — and reconstructs the structural hierarchy with a simple geometric inclusion algorithm instead of learned relation modules. The authors also systematically compare four lightweight head archetypes and find that pixel-level regression gives the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off in this setting.

On HierText, Prism's minimalist design outperforms the complex-architecture SOTA OmniParser V2 on every level of the test set: Word PQ 75.8 vs 61.6 (+14.2), Line PQ 71.1 vs 64.5 (+6.6), Paragraph PQ 66.8 vs 55.2 (+11.6). On CTW1500 it runs 1.7× faster than LRANet while achieving higher accuracy, and it is positioned as a plug-and-play detection front-end for emerging vision-language models.

## Key points

- Prism's core claim: a strongly pre-trained backbone on a single-scale feature map can replace multi-scale fusion necks, heavy heads, and explicit hierarchy-reasoning modules.
- Architecture: parallel lightweight heads attached to one single-scale backbone feature map jointly detect words, text lines, and paragraphs; the word→line→paragraph hierarchy is recovered by geometric inclusion of the predicted regions, not by learned relation modules.
- Head-archetype study: among four representative lightweight heads, pixel-level regression offers the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off for this single-scale setting.
- HierText test set vs OmniParser V2 (previous SOTA): Word PQ 75.8 vs 61.6, Line PQ 71.1 vs 64.5, Paragraph PQ 66.8 vs 55.2; validation set shows the same ordering (Word PQ 75.3 vs 60.0).
- Efficiency: 1.7× faster than LRANet on CTW1500 at higher accuracy (all FPS measured uniformly on a single RTX 3090); at a uniform 1024×1024 input on Total-Text, Prism's inference time is dominated by the backbone with almost no neck/head overhead.
- The authors argue the field should shift focus from intricate architectural engineering toward stronger backbones and pre-training, and position Prism as a plug-and-play detection extension for large VLMs.

## Details

The gap over OmniParser V2 is attributed mainly to three factors (ablation in Sec. 4.4): the hierarchical relationships parseable by geometric inclusion, the single-scale feature map sufficing for multi-granularity detection, and the pre-trained backbone providing features that make heavy heads unnecessary. Compared methods on HierText include UniDec, LayoutFormer, Hi-SAM-B/L, and OmniParser V2; scene-text benchmarks in Table 1 cover MSRA-TD500, ICDAR19 ArT, Total-Text, and CTW1500 with recall/precision/F-measure and uniform FPS. Code is to be released.
