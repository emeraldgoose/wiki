# TinyAstral: Transferring Self-Evolving Harnesses to Tiny Generalist Manipulation Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.41216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41216) · **Published**: 2026-10-30
**Authors**: Kehong Gong, Xiaoxu Zheng, Guanli Hou, Seunghun Lee, Mengqi Guo, Runjia Tan, Zhengyu Wen, Qi Wang, Xiaoyu He, Mingyuan Zhang, Michael Bi Mi

## Overview

Robot agents built on vision-language models depend on both the planner and its harness: the tools, feedback, memory, and recovery logic around it. Two lines of work attack this from different sides. Harness evolution improves the system around a frozen planner, while agent distillation trains compact models from a stronger agent's trajectories. TinyAstral connects them by asking whether a large teacher's harness can be improved first and the resulting decision-making capability then transferred into a small model, so the improved system runs with substantially less inference compute.

The method has two stages. First, the weights of an open-weight teacher, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, are frozen and its harness is improved through a master-guided recursive self-improvement loop. Humans specify the rules and budget in advance; frontier LLM agents inspect the teacher's failures, propose harness edits, and the system accepts or rejects each edit using pre-registered paired statistical tests. Each round classifies failures into ten classes, proposes general edits such as new primitives, more honest tool returns, perception fixes, or failure handling, and rejects anything that lacks evidence of improvement or causes regressions. Second, the final harness is frozen and the teacher collects trajectories containing reasoning, tool calls, and recovery from injected failures. Together with auxiliary offline real-robot supervision, these trajectories train a compact 0.8B to 4B student to interpret feedback, choose actions, recover from failure, and decide when to finish. At deployment the student replaces the teacher in the same frozen harness.

Harness evolution raises the frozen teacher's RoboTwin success from 33.4% to 63.6%, and distillation produces a 4B student at 62.8%. Harness gains also transfer to other frozen planners without retraining, including DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and the untrained 4B initialization, and the same evolved harness improves them. Distilled students at four harness versions score 36.2%, 49.8%, 60.0%, and 62.8%, each close to its corresponding teacher, showing that system-level improvements carry into compact models.

The final 4B student outperforms every open competitor the authors ran on identical episodes, including their own teacher model placed inside three competitors' harnesses (93.6% versus 78.9% on Guava; 78.3% versus 57.3% and 53.0% on LIBERO-PRO), stays close to its teacher on the three training benchmarks, and scores close to a 552B planner on three benchmarks never used for improvement or training. On the measured deployment episodes it uses about one fifth of its teacher's accelerator time.

## Key points

- Two-stage approach: improve a frozen teacher's harness through statistically gated recursive self-improvement, then distill its reasoning and recovery behavior into a compact student deployed in the same harness.
- Harness evolution gains transfer: the evolved harness improves other frozen planners, and students distilled at four harness versions track their corresponding teachers.
- Compact, lower-compute agents: a single 4B model covers six benchmarks, beats open competitors, stays close to its teacher on training benchmarks and to a 552B planner on unseen ones, and uses about one fifth of the teacher's accelerator time per episode.
- Distillation ingredients matter: keeping the teacher's reasoning, adding recovery branches, strict-success filtering, and offline real-robot supervision.
- Recursive self-improvement accepts an edit only with paired statistical evidence of improvement; the final integration phase accepted three of seven candidates, and edits must be general rather than branching on task names.

## Details

A planner model controls the robot through a harness: at each turn the harness gives an observation and the planner returns a reasoning trace followed by exactly one tool call, which the harness executes and returns a structured result. The harness determines how an action is carried out: perception via SAM 3 segmentation lifted to depth point clouds, grasp and placement geometry, collision-free motion planning, and closed-loop corrections. The planner decides what to do: which object, which part, which reference and spatial relation, which arm, in what order, and when the task is finished. The model never outputs joint angles unless a tool requests them. An episode ends when the agent calls done with predicates that the harness checks against cameras and sensors, or gives up, or exhausts its budget of 30 tool calls on RoboTwin. Strict success counts an episode only if the agent ended it with done and the benchmark's success predicate holds in the final state.

Teacher data comes from training seeds of all 50 RoboTwin tasks, 7 seen Guava tasks, and LIBERO-90, yielding 83,753 decision samples of which roughly half are simulated. Training fine-tunes all language-model parameters of Qwen3.5-4B for two epochs. Three training-family benchmarks are reported: RoboTwin 2.0 with 500 episodes, Guava with 450 episodes (7 seen and 8 unseen tasks), and LIBERO-PRO with 300 episodes. Three zero-shot benchmarks are SimplerEnv, VLABench, and EB-Manipulation. At deployment the student is served in bf16 on one accelerator.

Distillation keeps the teacher's reasoning text in each training target, because emptying it lowers success by 19.3 points on LIBERO-PRO, 11.2 points on SimplerEnv Google VM, and 10.0 points on VLABench. Recovery branches replay a successful teacher episode and inject a perturbation at a branch point, keeping the branch only if the perturbation took effect, the replay reproduced every recorded result code, and the teacher still reached strict success; on RoboTwin these are subsampled to 20% of samples. Only strict successes are kept. Offline real-robot supervision comes from AgiBot World, Fractal, BridgeData V2, DROID, and Berkeley UR5 from Open X-Embodiment, cast as state and failure judgment, sub-goal planning, and next tool call tasks in the harness format.
