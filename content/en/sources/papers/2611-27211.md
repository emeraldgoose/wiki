# Explicit Style Extraction for Personalized Long-Form Text Generation

**Source**: huggingface
**Published**: 2026-11-20
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27211

## Overview

Personalized long-form generation asks a language model to write new text in the way a specific user would. The standard recipe, retrieval-augmented generation, places a few of the user's past texts in the prompt but leaves the model to decide what to take from them. This implicit approach makes it hard to inspect or control which stylistic habits are being transferred.

The paper introduces StylePrism, a taxonomy of 28 stylistic aspects organized into seven categories covering lexical choice, syntactic patterns, pragmatic and tone, structural organization, discourse markers, and cross-task fingerprints. StylePrism provides an explicit schema for describing writing style in terms that are concrete and checkable, from word frequency and hedging behavior to rhetorical role distribution and closing moves.

To use the taxonomy at inference time, the authors propose SP-Refine. Given retrieved user samples, SP-Refine first fills StylePrism with observed patterns, quoted evidence, and habits to avoid. It then drafts from the samples and revises the draft twice against this profile, scoring its own revision on style criteria and fixing the weakest parts. An even cheaper variant, SP-Infer, performs a single implicit-inference call with the profile.

## Key points

- SP-Refine outperforms LongLaMP's retrieval-augmented baseline in 25 of 27 ROUGE and METEOR comparisons across three long-form tasks and three LLM families, with an average improvement of 10.2% and a peak of 35.8%.
- Annotating users' own texts with extracted profiles shows StylePrism captures concrete, checkable habits, from the order of rhetorical moves to a reviewer's sign-off.
- Ablations show the profile helps as revision guidance rather than as a replacement for samples: writing from the profile alone is worse in all 27 comparisons.
- Style fidelity gains are largest with DeepSeek and smaller with GPT; format improves by almost one point on LLM-judged style criteria.
- Oracle variants that allow the reviser to see the reference show remaining gains require information unavailable at inference time.

## Details

Evaluation uses the three long-form LongLaMP tasks with three LLM generators and both reference-based metrics and a rubric-based LLM judge that scores style fidelity and task quality separately. Style fidelity is assessed on lexical choice, syntax, tone, discourse, and format, while task quality covers coverage, factuality, fluency, and length.

The authors also analyze where users depart from their own extracted profile and present per-criterion LLM-judge scores in Appendix F. The paper notes that a single implicit-inference call comes close to SP-Refine at lower cost, providing a practical trade-off.
