# WorldFlow: Joint Video and 3D Motion Prediction via Large-Scale Pretraining

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.39278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39278) · **Published**: 2026-10-29
**Authors**: Yuhao Lu, Jiongxu Chen, Hexiang Wei, Jiajin Wu, Juelin Li, Mingjian Liang, Fan Yang, Zhuowei Liang, Zihao Liu, Zhennan Wang, Sheng Wang, Lin Shao

## Overview

Predicting future physical states is a central challenge in embodied intelligence. Existing approaches leave important gaps: video world models often keep 3D dynamics implicit, action-based formulations remain tied to specific robot configurations, and 3D motion prediction models rely on flow-only supervision, which gives a limited learning signal for physical dynamics. WorldFlow addresses these limitations through a generative world model that jointly predicts future video and query-based 3D trajectories.

By predicting both visual futures and structured motion queries, WorldFlow aims to make physical dynamics explicit and reusable across configurations. The joint formulation turns 3D motion into a prediction target alongside video, providing supervision beyond optical flow and supporting large-scale pretraining for embodied prediction.

## Key points

- Future physical-state prediction remains difficult for embodied intelligence systems.
- Video-only world models leave 3D dynamics implicit.
- Action-based interfaces are constrained to particular robot setups.
- Flow-only 3D motion supervision provides a weaker signal for physical dynamics.
- WorldFlow jointly predicts future video and query-based 3D trajectories.
- The joint task exposes structured 3D motion as a direct prediction target.

## Details

The paper frames physical prediction as a cross-modal problem. Video supplies rich visual expectations, while 3D trajectories supply geometric and kinematic structure. Training both targets together may make the model less dependent on robot-specific action spaces and less reliant on flow-only motion cues. The resulting world model is intended to support embodied tasks that require both what will be seen and how objects or agents will move.
