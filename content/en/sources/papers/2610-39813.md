# EvoTeam: Multi-Agent Collaboration via Evolving Teams

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.39813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39813) · **Published**: 2026-10-30
**Authors**: Qinuo Cao, Xin He, Dianxuan Fu, Lizhi Zhang, Zhaoqing Xing, Jiapu Wang, Xin Wang, Na Zou, Yi Chang, Qinggang Zhang

## Overview

LLM-based multi-agent systems organize specialized agents into collaborative teams so that their complementary capabilities can be combined to solve complex tasks. Existing methods usually improve these systems by refining the prompts, skills, and execution strategies of individual agents from task feedback. However, improving individual agents does not necessarily lead to better team collaboration, so recent work has extended evolution to the collaboration level, using collective execution experience to adjust team organization and inter-agent interactions.

Evolving collaboration across heterogeneous tasks remains difficult. Existing approaches often constrain team reorganization within a shared structure and reuse interaction rules across incompatible team contexts, which can hurt adaptation. EvoTeam addresses this with a collaboration-centric evolution framework that jointly evolves team organization, interaction rules, and individual agent skills through execution experience.

The framework maintains a library of reusable teams. It selects or creates an appropriate agent team for a task and guides collaboration through team-specific interaction rules. Collaboration experience accumulates within compatible team contexts, while individual skills remain reusable across teams. Experiments across four benchmarks are reported to demonstrate effectiveness, and the source code is released.

## Key points

- Improving individual agents alone is insufficient for better team collaboration in LLM multi-agent systems.
- Collaboration-level evolution is needed, but prior approaches are limited by constrained team reorganization and inappropriate reuse of interaction rules across heterogeneous tasks.
- EvoTeam jointly evolves team organization, interaction rules, and individual agent skills using execution experience.
- A library of reusable teams supports selecting or creating teams and applying team-specific interaction rules.
- Collaboration experience accumulates in compatible team contexts, while agent skills stay reusable across teams.
- The approach is evaluated across four benchmarks and its code is made available.

## Details

The paper's preliminary analysis highlights two potential failure modes when experience is transferred across heterogeneous tasks: (1) constrained team reorganization, where a source team template does not support the division of labor required by the target task; and (2) inappropriate reuse of interaction rules, where inherited handoffs or stopping conditions are unsuitable for the new task. It concludes that distinct collaboration structures and scoped rule sharing are needed for effective accumulation of experience.

EvoTeam's framework comprises three parts: adaptive agent team composition (selecting or creating a team and recruiting agents from its candidate roster), team-scoped collaboration (agents execute assigned subtasks using accumulated skills and applicable team-specific rules), and multi-level experience evolution (reflectors refine teams, team-specific rules, and agent skills using execution traces). Results on the reported benchmarks support the claim that flexible team formation and selective rule reuse help experience transfer.
