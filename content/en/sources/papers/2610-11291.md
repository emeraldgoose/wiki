# When Do We Need On-Policy Distillation? Distilling on Offline Student Rollouts Is Often Better

**Source**: Zhao et al., arXiv, 2026-10-08

**Lead**: On-policy distillation (OPD) has emerged as a dominant paradigm for transferring teacher model capabilities to student models, but this work demonstrates that a simpler alternative—Semi-OPD, which distills from offline rollouts generated by the initial student—frequently outperforms OPD in both accuracy and training efficiency across diverse model scales.

## Background

Knowledge distillation typically trains a student model to mimic a stronger teacher. On-policy distillation (OPD) extends this by having the student generate its own training data during distillation, theoretically providing better coverage of the student's error regions. OPD has been adopted in recent work including On-Policy Distillation of Language Models (Agarwal et al., 2024), MiniLLM (Gu et al., 2024), and various follow-ups from Thinking Machines Lab and DeepSeek. The prevailing assumption has been that on-policy sampling is essential for effective distillation because it adapts the training distribution to the student's current weaknesses.

## The Semi-OPD Alternative

The authors propose **Semi-On-Policy Distillation (Semi-OPD)**: instead of generating fresh rollouts at each distillation step, they generate a fixed set of offline rollouts from the *initial* (untrained) student once, then use these static rollouts for all subsequent distillation steps. This eliminates the computational overhead of repeated on-policy generation while preserving the student-induced distribution shift that makes OPD effective.

Key design choices:
- Rollouts are generated once from the randomly initialized student
- The same offline dataset is reused across all distillation epochs
- No teacher queries are needed during distillation (unlike OPD which queries the teacher on student-generated prompts)

## Empirical Results

Across **17 teacher–student pairs** spanning 1.5B to 235B parameters (including LLaMA, Qwen, and DeepSeek model families), Semi-OPD outperforms OPD in **14 of 17 cases**:

| Metric | Semi-OPD Advantage |
|--------|-------------------|
| Accuracy | Up to +13.6% over OPD |
| Training efficiency | Up to 11.4× faster (no repeated rollout generation) |
| Win rate | 82% (14/17 pairs) |

The three cases where OPD wins involve very large teachers (235B) distilling into very small students (1.5B), where the student's initial policy is too weak to produce useful rollouts.

## Why OPD Often Underperforms

The authors identify two failure modes of standard OPD:
1. **Length inflation**: On-policy rollouts progressively grow longer during training, wasting compute on verbose generations that don't improve quality
2. **Distribution collapse**: The student's policy drifts toward degenerate outputs (repetition, shortcuts) that the teacher then reinforces

Semi-OPD avoids both by fixing the rollout distribution at initialization.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

For teams building model distillation pipelines, Semi-OPD offers a practical simplification: **generate rollouts once, distill many times**. This reduces infrastructure complexity (no need for on-policy generation loops), cuts compute costs by an order of magnitude, and often improves final model quality. The method is particularly attractive for:
- Resource-constrained distillation (single GPU / limited API quota)
- Large-scale distillation across many student checkpoints
- Scenarios where teacher access is expensive or rate-limited

The code and rollouts are released at https://github.com/NVlabs/Semi-OPD.
