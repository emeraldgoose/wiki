# Fast-JEPA: Advancing the Success-Latency Frontier for Latent World Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.25927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25927) · **Published**: 2026-11-19 (v1; v2 2026-11-22)
**Authors**: Yao Zhang, Bin Yu, Shijie Lian, Haishan Liu, Changti Wu, Yu Wu, Kai Chen

## Overview

Latent world models predict the outcomes of action sequences, but comparing candidate plans through recursive rollout stays expensive at test time, forcing a trade-off between planning quality and latency. Fast-JEPA reorganizes predictive computation so that action-sequence search stays effective at low planning cost: during action construction, outcomes predicted from the observed anchor under the selected actions inform the next action choices, and once a candidate sequence is complete, its directly predicted terminal outcome is enough to compare candidates under the visual-goal objective — avoiding recursive state rollout during refinement. The planning objective is to pick a plan A = (a_0,…,a_{H−1}) of H chunks × B primitive actions whose predicted terminal latent representation z is close to the encoded goal z_g; the controller executes L primitive actions before re-observing.

Across PushT, Reacher, Cube, and TwoRoom, Fast-JEPA reaches 96.50% average success (±0.66) with planning 2.41× faster than INTACT (10.88 ms vs 26.2 ms-scale planning in the reported table), and search-budget sweeps show the success-latency advantage holds across candidate counts and refinement depths. To test practicality, the authors introduce DynaGoal, a benchmark over the same four environments with three task families — Shift (changing targets/geometry: drifting-reversing PushT target, switching Reacher/Cube targets, moving TwoRoom doorway), Deadline (time-constrained completion), and Delay (delayed planning responses) — reusing models trained on the original environments with no extra fine-tuning. Fast-JEPA scores 73.83% on DynaGoal-Shift and 87.67% on DynaGoal-Deadline, showing low-latency predictive planning pays off under changing control conditions and execution-time constraints.

## Key points

- Fast-JEPA keeps sequential dependence in action construction but replaces recursive state rollout in candidate evaluation with direct prediction of each complete candidate's terminal outcome in the latent space shared by observation and goal encoders.
- 96.50% average success (±0.66) across PushT/Reacher/Cube/TwoRoom (per-task: 90.00, 96.33, 100.00, 99.67) with 2.41× faster planning than INTACT; the Pareto gain extends across candidate counts and refinement depths in budget sweeps.
- New DynaGoal benchmark: Shift / Deadline / Delay task families on PushT, Reacher, Cube, TwoRoom, reusing base-trained models without fine-tuning; measured planning delay directly links planning cost to control performance.
- DynaGoal results: 73.83% average on DynaGoal-Shift (dynamic OOD targets and geometry) and 87.67% on DynaGoal-Deadline (execution deadlines).
- The framework is a planning front-end over JEPA-style latent world models, comparing predicted terminal latent states to the goal latent state rather than rolling out every candidate step by step.

## Details

Problem setup: at decision time t, an image encoder maps observation o_t and goal image o_g into a common latent space (z_t = E_θ(o_t), z_g = E_θ(o_g)); the plan has H chunks of B primitive actions each, and the controller executes a feedback interval of L actions before the next observation. Related predictive-search methods differ in how they guide and score candidates (differentiable rollouts, retrieval initialization with hybrid scoring in IMWM, neighborhood-graph goal distances in NGC); Fast-JEPA instead matches world-model queries to the information actually needed at each search stage. Experiments measure success and planning latency (ms) per environment, with budget sweeps over candidate count and refinement depth; DynaGoal variants inject drifting targets, target switches, a moving doorway, deadlines, and controlled response delays without retraining.
