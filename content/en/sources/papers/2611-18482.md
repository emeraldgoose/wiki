# MMFlash: Faster Speculative Decoding for Vision-Language Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.18482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18482) · **Published**: 2026-11-13
**Authors**: Sicheng Feng, Yichen Zhou, Yifan Liu, Xiangyu Peng, Zhanghao Wu, Kun Li, Minhao Chen

## Overview

Speculative decoding accelerates LLM generation by having a lightweight drafter propose tokens that a larger target model verifies in parallel. The speed gain depends on the acceptance rate — how often the target model agrees with the drafter's proposals. Most prior work on speculative decoding focuses on text-only tasks, leaving multimodal (vision-language) drafting underexplored.

MMFlash addresses this gap in two steps. First, the authors introduce a token-level visual dependence metric based on the KL divergence between the target model's next-token distribution with and without visual input. They find that acceptance rates drop sharply: from 46% in the lowest visual-dependence quartile to 22% in the highest, and this gap persists even after multimodal training. This means that in visually heavy contexts, the drafter's proposals are much less likely to be accepted.

Motivated by this, MMFlash adaptively upweights visually dependent tokens that the drafter predicts poorly, and for long video inputs further applies sparse attention to suppress redundant computation. The result is a simple framework that, across a range of multimodal benchmarks, achieves lossless decoding speedups of up to 3.58× — outperforming EAGLE-3 by 195.4% and DFlash by 17.1%.

## Key points

- Visual dependence metric: KL divergence between next-token distributions with and without visual input predicts speculative decoding acceptance rate; rates fall from 46% (lowest quartile) to 22% (highest).
- Disparity persists after multimodal training: the visual-dependence effect is robust, not something training can erase.
- Adaptive upweighting: MMFlash boosts the drafter's influence on visually dependent tokens it predicts poorly, counteracting the natural acceptance drop.
- Sparse attention for long video: reduces redundant attention computation when the input is a full video sequence, improving both drafting quality and efficiency.
- Speedup results: up to 3.58× lossless decoding speedup on multimodal benchmarks, beating EAGLE-3 by 195.4% and DFlash by 17.1%.
- Speculative decoding is not just for text: MMFlash shows the framework generalizes to vision-language models when the visual-dependence-aware modifications are applied.

## Details

The paper's experimental setup spans several open-source VLMs (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, etc.) and multimodal benchmarks (MMBench, MVBench, etc.). Each benchmark measures token-level acceptance and end-to-end generation latency.

The visual-dependence metric works as follows: for each generated token position, the authors compute the target model's next-token distribution in two conditions — with the current visual context visible, and with it masked out. The KL divergence between these two distributions is the visual dependence score. Positions with high KL (high visual dependence) are exactly those where the drafter's proposals are least likely to be accepted by the target model.

The key innovation of MMFlash is the adaptive upweighting: rather than treating all drafter proposals equally (or ignoring visual dependence entirely), MMFlash re-scales the drafter's probability so that tokens the drafter is uncertain about get more influence, while well-predicted tokens get less. This partially compensates for the natural acceptance drop at high visual dependence.

For long video inputs, the attention mechanism becomes the bottleneck. MMFlash introduces sparse attention patterns (sliding-window plus task-relevant token selection) so the model only attends to visually informative frames and tokens, cutting the redundant computation that would otherwise dominate a full-video forward pass.

The speedup numbers are striking. On the suite of multimodal benchmarks, MMFlash consistently outperforms prior speculative decoding systems (EAGLE-3 and DFlash) by wide margins. The 3.58× figure is the harmonic mean across benchmarks; per-benchmark gains range from roughly 2× to 5× depending on the model and data length. Critically, all speedups are "lossless": generated token distributions are statistically indistinguishable from standard (non-speculative) generation, so the acceleration does not trade correctness for speed.

The authors open-source both the MMFlash code and the visual-dependence metric computation scripts, so the community can apply these techniques to new VLMs and data types.
