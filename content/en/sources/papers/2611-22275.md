# Heave-Ho: Decentralized Long-Horizon Multi-Humanoid Collaboration

**Source**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22275)
**Published**: 2026-11-18

## Overview

Collaboration promises to extend humanoid capabilities beyond what individual robots can achieve, but it requires coordinated whole-body interactions, coherent long-horizon planning, and precise synchronization across robots. Heave-Ho is a decentralized framework for long-horizon collaborative multi-humanoid loco-manipulation. At the low level, a multi-agent post-training framework adapts a humanoid foundation tracking model to physically coupled interactions between humanoids, enabling coordinated decentralized control from local observations alone. At the high level, a vision-language planner and a speech-based leader–follower coordination mechanism synchronize decentralized execution without network communication.

Trained with two identical humanoids, the system scales to teams of up to ten robots and generalizes to heterogeneous teams without retraining. The authors also introduce HH-Bench, comprising 12 tasks involving teams of 2–10 humanoids, including heterogeneous teams. Systematic evaluations show Heave-Ho consistently outperforms baselines, and real-world experiments demonstrate robust decentralized collaboration on challenging tasks. Project page is linked from the paper.

## Key points

- Real world: two-robot teams pass a narrow door, follow an L-shaped corridor, hand a box down from a truck, and place a box in a target region; a person can substitute for one robot (carrying a box through the door or up stairs with a G1). Across all tasks, teams succeed in 92 of 100 trials; stair carry is hardest (7/10).
- Scaling without retraining: the two-G1 policy deploys to four G1s (two pairs), three G1s + a person, a G1 + person + PM01, and a PM01 + person, succeeding in 37 of 40 trials; in simulation it drives teams of 4, 8 and 10 G1s. Parameter sharing is essential — independent actors would need a new actor per added robot.
- High-level planning: fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-2B planner matches a manual operator on all three simulated tasks and approaches it on the two real-world tasks; one decision takes 52.5 ms on a single RTX 4090 (5.3 GiB) vs 78 ms for CHORUS and 6.46 s for GPT-6 Astra (~120× slower). Astra zero-shot fails far more often on every task (42% avg); CHORUS fails every trial except the handover (6/10, where coupling is brief).
- HH-Bench: 12 tasks spanning constrained transport, team scaling/heterogeneity, diverse interaction modes and composed long-horizon tasks, for teams of 2–10 humanoids.
- Low level: multi-agent post-training turns a frozen humanoid foundation tracking model into a shared controller for physically coupled whole-body interaction; composable team skills, reference motions, residual post-training and multi-agent RL; trains for composition and generalization; teams beyond training supported via speech-based leader–follower sync requiring no network.

## Details

- Method: low-level multi-agent post-training of a humanoid foundation tracking model (local observations only, decentralized); high-level VLM planner (Qwen3-VL-2B fine-tuned) selecting team skills; speech-based leader–follower synchronization; everyone must start skills together — a late lift tilts the object, a lone step pulls a partner off balance.
- Limitations: synchronization relies on followers hearing the leader (needs a reasonably quiet environment and reliable sound pickup); planner training for a new task requires manually collected teleoperation demonstrations, which is time-consuming.
