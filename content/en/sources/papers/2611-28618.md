# Distilling Visual Agents from Limited Interaction Experience

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.28618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28618) · **Published**: 2026-11-21 (v1)
**Authors**: Siyu Zhou, Jing Jiang, Chengqi Zhang, Guodong Long

## Overview

Visual agents interpret images and feedback to complete multi-step tasks, and distillation normally transfers a strong teacher's behavior to a smaller agent — but a changed action alters subsequent observations, so it is unclear whether distillation must span many different tasks or whether a few recorded histories also support transfer. The paper studies few-task online training alongside fixed-history replay. Both generate fresh student responses, but online distillation (OPD) additionally interacts with the environment on selected tasks, while replay reuses recorded trajectories.

Trajectory selection matters: a shared skill-context inventory labels coverage opportunities and ordered visual-decision descriptions capture representativeness, and the combined "coverage-first representative selection" picks the trajectories to learn from. Across Navigation, Sokoban, and spatial mapping, even one online training task supports transfer to other tasks.

## Key points

- Four fixed trajectories (K4) raise a 4B student's success from 49.17% to 82.08% on standard Navigation and from 60.16% to 85.55% on Sokoban, recovering over 90% of the measured gap to a 27B teacher on these panels. On an independent Navigation panel, replay improves success from 45.83% to 76.67%, retaining 91.36% of the same-task online gain with no new interaction during optimization.
- K4 offline OPD (82.08 / 22.08 long-horizon / 85.55) beats matched K4 offline SFT (74.17 / 20.42 / 76.17) on the same four trajectories, and full-task online OPD reaches 86.25 / 20.00 / 87.50. A 9B student starts at 67.08 on Navigation and 64.06 on Sokoban and reaches 86.25 / 87.50 with full-task online OPD.
- Selector ablations: the complete selector (82.08 Nav / 22.08 long / 85.55 Sokoban) beats random subsets (74.17-77.08), coverage-only (78.75), representativeness-only (79.17), and unordered matching (81.25); across four additional training runs the complete selector averages 81.98%/23.75% vs 80.73%/23.54% for the next best.
- Format compliance is not task performance: on Navigation, K4 OPD/SFT format correctness is 97.99%/98.00% while success is 82.08%/74.17%; on Sokoban 93.05%/93.44% format vs 85.55%/76.17% success — similar format rates accompany very different task performance.
- Spatial transfer: on held-out single-room SpatialGym, four selected trajectories recover 77.4% of the initial-student-to-teacher gap, and transfer spans multiple behaviors beyond format compliance.
- Cost and provenance: results are reported with training repetitions, an independent evaluation panel, and data/cost accounting; code is at https://github.com/elated-sawyer/visual-agent-distillation.

## Details

The method has three stages: build a shared skill-context inventory over tasks, label each opportunity for coverage, describe ordered visual decisions for representativeness, then run coverage-first representative selection. Distillation then runs either as fixed-history OPD (fresh student responses at fixed histories), matched teacher-response SFT, or few-task online OPD (fresh interaction on selected tasks), with optional experience refresh. Appendices cover the SpatialGym splits, protocol/information boundaries (what learner and teacher see), selection mathematics, selector diagnostics, and a conditional-interpretation discussion — the authors explicitly caution that the result is not a success guarantee.
