# CaPtain: A Pipeline for Post-Training Code-as-Policy Robot Agents

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.40799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40799) · **Published**: 2026-10-27
**Authors**: Yijia Fan, Xilin He, Yilei Jiang, Dongming Wu, Jiaxu Wang, Xiangyu Yue

## Overview

Code-as-policy puts robot manipulation in the same form as language-model agent work: a policy executes inside a fixed harness. While coding, web, and tool-use agents are commonly trained with SFT followed by multi-turn RL, code-as-policy agents have often skipped training and relied on prompted frontier models or semantic skills.

CaPtain is an open post-training pipeline built on the observation that applying the recipe at the low level is harder because the policy must make precise grasp and approach decisions without semantic skills. It trains and deploys the policy inside CaPtain-Harness, exposing 22 low-level perception, geometric measurement, and guarded-motion primitives.

## Key points

- Brings the SFT-then-RL post-training recipe to code-as-policy robot agents.
- Provides CaPtain-Harness with 22 low-level primitives and no grasp/place skills.
- Applies the pipeline to Qwen3.5-4B to produce CaPtain-4B.
- Improves held-out LIBERO success from 0% to 55.4% after SFT, then 73.8% after RL.
- Reaches 78.5% on LIBERO-PRO, above several frontier-model agents.
- RL benefits are greater when pre-trained agents are stronger and rewards are sparse.

## Details

By training inside a low-level harness, the policy learns manipulation decisions directly rather than delegating them to components the agent cannot improve. This addresses a gap that does not arise for language agents because low-level control requires precise geometry and timing.

The paper reports held-out transfer and comparative results on LIBERO benchmarks, providing an open pipeline for low-level code-as-policy agents.
