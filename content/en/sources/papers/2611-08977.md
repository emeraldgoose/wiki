# Energy-Based Reasoners: Your Recurrent Reasoner Should Be an Energy-Based Model

**Source**: [huggingface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08977)
**Published**: 2026-11-19

## Overview

Looped (recurrent) architectures scale the effective depth of a model by repeatedly applying the same block to a latent state, and they have recently shown strong results on hard algorithmic reasoning tasks. Their weakness is that the iterated updates carry no explicit measure of progress: nothing tells the model how good the current latent state is, when to stop iterating, or how to improve it. Chain-of-thought solves the analogous problem for token-based reasoning by exposing intermediate steps that can be scored; looped models in latent space lack that handle.

Energy-Based Reasoners (EBRs) address this by learning a scalar energy function over latent reasoning states and defining the looped update as gradient descent on that energy. The energy value scores each candidate state, its gradient drives refinement of the state, and the magnitude of that gradient provides a principled stopping signal for the recurrence. Because the state is refined by descent rather than by an unconstrained recurrent update, stationary points of the dynamics correspond to vanishing gradients, which makes convergence-based halting well defined.

Training is completed with an exploration-based scheme the authors call Explorative Modeling (SXM): model-generated trajectories are steered and matched to suitable targets so the energy landscape is shaped over a broader set of states, including additional modes corresponding to distinct valid solutions. Inference adds light RMS normalization and noise to the iterates and stops early when the projected gradient norm falls below a threshold.

## Key points

- Looped reasoning is recast as energy minimization: updates are gradient descent steps on a learned energy E(z; x) over latent state z given problem x.
- The same energy serves three roles — scoring latent states, guiding their refinement via gradients, and signaling when to stop iterating.
- Exploration-based training (SXM) broadens the energy landscape and helps the model discover multiple solution modes, not just a single attractor.
- Across four algorithmic reasoning benchmarks (Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Hard, N-Queens, Graph Coloring), EBRs match or beat prior recurrent reasoning models.
- On Sudoku-Extreme, EBRs reach 99.9% accuracy while using 345x less inference compute than the next-best model, at comparable training compute and without an external verifier.
- On all multi-solution benchmarks, EBRs achieve state-of-the-art coverage of distinct valid solutions while keeping near-100% single-sample accuracy.

## Details

**Method.** Given an instance x, EBR samples an initial latent state z ~ N(0, I) and runs S segments of iterative refinement. Each step normalizes z (RMS), optionally injects noise of scale sigma, takes a step of size alpha along the negative energy gradient, and projects back onto the state manifold M. A convergence loss encourages correct predictions to approach stationary points of the energy (vanishing projected gradients). Training unrolls n gradient steps per segment with randomly sampled n and alpha, backpropagates through the descent, and adds the SXM exploration objective so that states visited along model-generated trajectories receive supervision matched to appropriate targets; training breaks early when the residual r_n falls below a threshold delta. Inference follows the same descent but halts a segment when the projected gradient norm drops below tau, then decodes the answer with a prediction head on the final state. Unlike HRM/TRM-style hierarchical recurrence, EBR uses a single latent state without hierarchy; unlike IREM/IRED/Energy-Based Transformers, the energy scores latent reasoning states rather than candidate predictions directly; and unlike task-constrained energy approaches such as ERM, the energy is learned end-to-end with no hard-coded task features.

**Results.** The paper reports four challenging algorithmic reasoning benchmarks: Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Hard, N-Queens, and Graph Coloring. EBRs match or outperform prior looped reasoning models on these benchmarks. Headline numbers: 99.9% accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme with 345x less inference compute than the next-best model and comparable training compute, and state-of-the-art coverage of distinct valid solutions on every multi-solution benchmark while maintaining near-100% single-sample accuracy. The appendix additionally compares training compute against GRAM, probes Maze-Hard limitations and shortest-path accuracy, and analyzes Reverse SXM with partial solution sets.
