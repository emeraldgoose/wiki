# Principia: Relational Physics Tests for Video Models

Source: Varun Varma Thozhiyoor and 3 other authors, arXiv:2609.04200, Sep 3, 2026. Project page at principiabench.github.io.

**Lead**: Judging physical realism in generated video is hard because absolute motion measurements depend on frame rate, object scale, and camera calibration — all ambiguous or missing in generated clips. **Principia** sidesteps calibration entirely: when two objects in one scene obey the same law, their motions must satisfy **predictable relationships that hold regardless of calibration**. The benchmark tests Newtonian physics through this relational consistency across **8 phenomena** (gravity, restitution, friction, rotational inertia, projectile motion, momentum, pendulum, mass-spring oscillation) in real-world scenes shot under controlled protocols. Result: across thousands of generations from **6 state-of-the-art video generators, no model exceeds 0.42** despite all scoring ~0.8 on VBench — and the best vision-language model detects violations at only **67% accuracy**, most near chance.

## The relational trick

The core instrument is a **calibration-independent consistency score** computed directly in image space: instead of asking "does this ball fall at 9.8 m/s²" (unanswerable without scale and frame rate), it asks "do these two falling objects satisfy the relation gravity demands." Paired objects sharing a scene factor out the unknowns, turning an ill-posed absolute measurement into a well-posed relative one spanning translational, rotational, collisional, and oscillatory dynamics.

## What the gap means

| Signal | Score |
|---|---|
| Best video generator on Principia | ≤ 0.42 |
| Same models on VBench | ~0.8 |
| Best VLM at detecting violations | 67% |

Visual quality (VBench) and physical correctness are nearly orthogonal — current generators render beautifully wrong physics, and current VLMs mostly cannot tell. For anyone selecting a video model for robotics, simulation, or embodied training, VBench-style scores are insufficient.

## Caveats

This summary is abstract-based (full-text HTML not verified here): the consistency-score formula, per-phenomenon breakdowns, and the six models' identities need the PDF. Eight controlled phenomena cannot cover full real-world physics, and relational tests pass models that are jointly wrong in correlated ways.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- If generated video feeds a downstream system (sim-to-real, synthetic training data), add a physics-consistency gate — perceptual quality metrics will not catch violations.
- The relational-testing pattern generalizes: when absolute ground truth is unavailable, test **relations between co-observed entities** that must hold under any calibration.
- A 0.42-vs-0.8 gap is a selection-criteria warning: match your benchmark to the property you actually need (physics, not prettiness).
- 67%-at-best VLM detection means automated violation filtering is not yet reliable — keep human or rule-based checks in the loop.
