# FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.06910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910) · **Published**: 2026-11-04
**Authors**: Bingran You et al.

## Overview

AI agents are playing an increasingly important role in physics research. However, existing physics benchmarks only evaluate fixed questions, isolated subtasks, or paper reproduction with given solutions, and rarely check research quality alongside correctness. The paper introduces FrontierPhysics, an end-to-end benchmark with 56 tasks made by 42 physicists based on their own research, covering nine domains across theoretical, computational, and experimental physics. Each task requires agents to review the literature, develop a research plan, conduct research and experiments, and produce structured outputs and scientific manuscripts. Combining deterministic verifiers with expert-authored rubrics, FrontierPhysics evaluates whether agents' research is correct and publishable. Across 22 models in 26 configurations, no configuration passes more than 46 of its 168 rollouts. Manual review of agent trajectories shows that frontier agents' main weaknesses are insufficiently rigorous reasoning and limited expert intuition, rather than gaps in physics knowledge or execution errors.

## Key points

- Prior physics benchmarks evaluate fixed questions or reproduction with given solutions, not end-to-end research.
- FrontierPhysics has 56 tasks authored by 42 physicists across nine theoretical, computational, and experimental domains.
- Tasks require literature review, planning, research/experiments, structured outputs, and manuscripts.
- Verification combines deterministic checks with expert rubrics assessing correctness and publishability.
- Even top configurations fail to pass more than 46 of 168 rollouts; weaknesses are reasoning rigor and expert intuition.

## Details

The benchmark covers nine physics domains and manuscript-level deliverables. Correctness and publishability are evaluated separately. Participant list and domain breakdown are in the linked source.
