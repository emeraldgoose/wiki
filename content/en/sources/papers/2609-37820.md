# Information Games: Strategic Crowding and Firm Repositioning in Language-Model Space

**Source**: [arXiv:2609.37820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37820) · **Published**: 2026-11-05
**Authors**: Marcus Gawronsky, Chun-Sung Huang

## Overview

When firms respond to new market opportunities, their capacity choices change the future discounted free cash flows available to competitors and investors. This paper studies that strategic reallocation by building a model of costly competitive adjustment in which firms shift capacity in response to free cash flow shocks.

The approach builds on Xu et al. (2022) and represents news and analyst reports as dense language-model embeddings. The information geometry of these embeddings reflects how firms’ capital structure, product mix, leadership, risk exposure, and operating procedures change over time, letting the model connect qualitative public signals to quantitative financial responses.

The paper introduces a sandwich estimator to analyze the sign and magnitude of the predicted adjustment. It tests the model with multi-agent simulation and validates it on news and analyst reports from Nasdaq-listed companies between 2018 and 2022.

## Key points

- Firms’ costly capacity adjustments alter competitors’ profitability and the free cash flows investors anticipate.
- Language-model embeddings of public news and analyst reports make firm repositioning measurable over time.
- A sandwich estimator is used to assess the predicted adjustment.
- Multi-agent simulation and a 2018-2022 Nasdaq sample support the empirical analysis.

## Details

The model gives equilibrium properties and comparative statics that link gradual operating adjustment to financial responses when new economic opportunities arrive. In a restricted two-opportunity economy, firms with greater initial exposure experience larger return responses to positive exogenous free cash flow shocks, and news distributions of expanding firms converge toward those of incumbent competitors.

The framework also addresses reporting effects, measurement uncertainty, and rival feedback. Supporting appendices give the two-opportunity mechanism, static response and exposure support, and estimator recovery results.
