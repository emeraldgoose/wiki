# CoSTA: Compressed Speech Token Adaptation for Efficient Spoken Dialogue Models

**Source**: [arXiv:2610.21143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21143) · **Published**: 2026-10-15
**Authors**: Yingzhuo Deng, Chong Li, Bingxin Liu, Yi Shu, Tianyu Peng, Can Xie, Siyao Song, Shuo Ren, Jiajun Zhang

## Overview

Spoken dialogue models usually share a speech-token space for generation and synthesis, leading to long autoregressive sequences and costly inference. Lower-rate speech tokenizers shorten sequences but introduce compression-quality trade-offs and costly replacement without breaking this coupling. CoSTA addresses this limitation as a compatibility-preserving post-hoc framework that decouples generation and synthesis token spaces while retaining the original speech tokenizer and decoder. It extends the generation token space with compressed representations via mean-initialized vocabulary extension, pronunciation adaptation, and dialogue adaptation, then deterministically expands them back to the original speech-token space before synthesis. On bilingual speech-dialogue benchmarks, CoSTA achieves approximately 1.7x expanded speech-token generation speed, lower full-response latency, and higher throughput while preserving response quality. Analyses show more sustained interaction under limited budgets and a practical trade-off among compression, decoding efficiency, and quality; further experiments show cross-architecture transfer and complementarity to low-rate tokenization.

## Key points

- SDMs usually share one speech-token space, making sequences long and inference costly.
- CoSTA keeps the original tokenizer and decoder while decoupling generation from synthesis.
- Compressed representations are added by vocabulary extension, pronunciation adaptation, and dialogue adaptation.
- It reports roughly 1.7x expanded speech-token generation speed on bilingual benchmarks.
- Later experiments show cross-architecture transfer and complementarity with low-rate tokenization.

## Details

The framework is post-hoc: instead of replacing the speech tokenizer, it extends the generation vocabulary with compressed tokens and deterministically expands them before synthesis. This avoids costly replacements while shortening generated sequences. The paper also analyzes the trade-off between compression, decoding efficiency, and quality, and demonstrates more sustained interaction under limited token budgets.
