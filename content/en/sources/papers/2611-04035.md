# Recursa: Toward Recursively Self-Improving Foundation Model Systems

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.04035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04035) · **Published**: 2026-11-02
**Authors**: Erkang Zhu, Yin Lin, Xiaorong Shi, Xuanrui Lin, Jingren Zhou

## Overview

Foundation models in changing environments can encounter unfamiliar domain knowledge that their original training did not cover. New domains rarely provide large expert-created training data or reliable supervisory signals. Recursa adapts to new domains by learning from its own experience without human experts or a stronger teacher model. It uses a knowledge repository storing learned domain-specific skills, instructions on when to apply them, memories of past task experiences, and reflections on what to improve. Recursa bootstraps the repository from domain materials and then refines it through recursive self-improvement. Parallel learners execute tasks and synthesize local improvements, which are then validated and merged into the repository.

## Key points

- The system adapts without human experts or a stronger teacher.
- A knowledge repository stores skills, application rules, and memories.
- Bootstrapping starts from domain materials.
- Recursive self-improvement refines the repository over time.
- Parallel learners synthesize local improvements.
- Validation and merge reduce unchecked repository drift.

## Details

On Harvey LAB's Firm Knowledge benchmark, Recursa raises all-pass rate on held-out test tasks from 30.0 to 55.0 percent with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and from 35.0 to 46.0 percent with Qwen3.8-Flash. These gains are task-benchmark specific; autonomous self-improvement can introduce errors or overfitting if validation is weak. The results nevertheless show that learning from one's own experience can close domain gaps in low-data settings, provided merged changes remain auditable and task-relevant.
