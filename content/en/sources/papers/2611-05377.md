# DreamX: Video Diffusion Model is a Universal Latent Action Learner

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.05377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05377) · **Published**: 2026-11-03
**Authors**: Jisoo Kim, Chaehyun Kim, Junsu Kim, Jiho Park, Seongchan Kim, Eunbeen Hong, Soowon Son, Fu-En Yang, Seungryong Kim

## Overview

Learning a universal action representation from video requires modeling dynamics that are consistent across executions of the same action yet invariant to embodiment and appearance changes. Existing latent action models infer actions from visual transitions, but their representations often entangle the underlying action with irrelevant visual variations, limiting transfer across embodiments. DreamX repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model as a universal latent action learner. It extracts compact latent actions from the video model's rich spatiotemporal features, compressing each latent video frame by 300 times from 1,200 to 4 tokens. Using the video model's generative prior, these latent actions capture action-relevant dynamics disentangled from visual appearance. The resulting latent space serves as a shared action interface across human and robot embodiments, environments, and action parameterizations.

## Key points

- Universal actions should be consistent and invariant to appearance.
- Existing transition-based latent actions often entangle visual variations.
- DreamX uses a pretrained video diffusion model as action learner.
- Latent frames are compressed from 1,200 to 4 tokens.
- The learned space supports any-to-any video transfer and zero-shot action transfer.
- Shared latent actions reduce computational cost of video-diffusion policies.

## Details

On LARY-Bench, DreamX achieves state-of-the-art performance among latent action models. Encoded actions remain effective in another setting, enabling any-to-any video transfer and zero-shot action transfer across diverse embodiments and environments. The resulting interface enables policy learning in a compact latent action space. Results depend on video quality, diffusion prior, and execution coverage; diffusion-based priors may encode biases from training videos.
