# Innata-0: A Native Embodied World System with Model-Agent Co-Design

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.30254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30254) · **Published**: 2026-11-23 (v1)
**Authors**: Innata Team et al. (39 authors, led by Yu Bai, Ziyi Bai, Yuxuan Cai; full list in the arXiv abs page)

## Overview

To support reliable, adaptive behavior in the physical world, the authors introduce Innata-0, a native embodied world system built around model-agent co-design: the world model and the agent are trained and aligned together rather than bolted on. On the model side, the World Action Model combines two 8B towers (each 36 layers) for world understanding and video generation with a separately parameterized ~1B action-and-tactile generation tower (hidden width 1280, feed-forward 3840). On the agent side, Innata-0-Agent acts as a system coordinator supporting long-horizon planning, failure recovery, and continual evolution, using unified annotation records that capture shared context, interval-level descriptions, semantic subtasks, execution outcomes, and evidence — one record reused across preparation branches.

Embodied data is constructed through a unified information extraction and annotation pipeline: a single structured pass with Qwen3.8-Flash-Next produces semantic, relational, temporal, and perceptual-quality fields, with quality filtering by DOVER, VTSS, LAION aesthetics, and PaddleOCR. Training data spans vision-language understanding, video generation, gripper and dexterous manipulation (normalized gripper opening, a fixed 128-channel joint layout, Hand32 functional angles, Ego128 egocentric representation), and tactile sensing. Hand reconstruction uses a model trained on 13 datasets with joint and MANO supervision, built on Wan2.2-Fun-5B-Control features, to map egocentric video to hand skeletons and functional angles aligned to the canonical wrist.

Evaluation shows state-of-the-art open-source embodied performance: EWM 67.63 on WorldArena 2.0 Track 1 (highest among open-source models) and 60.2% overall success on RoboCasa365, ranking first on Atomic-Seen tasks at 88.4%. The infrastructure section reports the training recipe at scale: with two gradient-accumulation steps, BF16, full recomputation, and static compilation, the text-to-video recipe supports 3,072 U / 98,304 V tokens per microbatch; on 128 GPUs the online T2V audit reaches 31.04% MFU (25.28% for the transformer alone), packing raises DROID-action throughput from 9.64 to 22.39 samples/s, and fixed shapes plus compilation reach 36.76 samples/s (39.09 with more workers), with host memory cut from 695 to 335 GiB at similar throughput.

## Key points

- WorldArena 2.0 Track 1: EWM 67.63, highest among open-source models; RoboCasa365: 60.2% overall success, 88.4% on Atomic-Seen (rank 1).
- World Action Model = two 36-layer 8B towers (understanding + video generation) + ~1B action/tactile tower at reduced width (1280 hidden / 3840 FFN).
- Unified annotation records (shared context, interval descriptions, subtasks, outcomes, evidence) are reused across data-preparation branches and feed the agent's memory/verification/recovery loop.
- Action representations: fixed 128-channel joint layout, gripper opening normalized to [0,1], Hand32 functional angles, Ego128 egocentric wrist+hands+camera bundle; hand reconstruction from 13 datasets with MANO supervision on Wan2.2-Fun-5B-Control features.
- Infra: 128-GPU T2V at 31.04% MFU; compiled static buffers reach 36.76–39.09 samples/s (vs 9.64 packing-only on DROID Action); 8.9% higher throughput in the 64K→96K token transition; host memory 695→335 GiB; attention (FlagGems) got a 1.39–1.41× kernel speedup and was 34% of inference kernel time.

## Details

Sections 2–3 detail data construction (2.1–2.6: overview, unified extraction/annotation, VLU data, video-gen data, action data incl. gripper 2.5.1 and dexterous 2.5.2, tactile data) and the World Action Model (3.1 architecture, 3.2 infrastructure, 3.3 training). Section 4 covers the agent (task structure/execution, verification and adaptive recovery, experience-driven evolution, infrastructure). Section 5 evaluates understanding, video generation, policy, and policy with tactile feedback. Appendices 8–13 give unified annotation records, understanding training, dexterous hands (egocentric hand reconstruction, Hand32/Ego128), tactile modeling, infrastructure details (experimental conditions, execution tradeoffs, FLOP accounting, correctness/recovery with 11,136 logical states retained across 8-GPU continuation), and agent implementation/tool set.
