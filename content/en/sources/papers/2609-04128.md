# Environment Evolution for Terminal Agents

Source: Zhiyuan Fan and 11 other authors, arXiv:2609.04128, Sep 3, 2026. Hunyuan Team, Tencent.

**Lead**: RL environments are becoming the scalable lever for training terminal agents, but environments synthesized from scratch are now too easy for frontier models — solved across repeated rollouts, they get discarded and provide no learning signal. Recent co-evolution methods synthesize new environments from on-policy rollouts, but the results stay tied to the rollout model and go stale as training saturates. This paper proposes **environment evolution**: incrementally harden existing environments **off-policy**, generation by generation, and schedule the lineage during training. With simple 200-step long-horizon RL, it improves **Qwen3.6-27B by 14.4 and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B by 18.0 percentage points on Terminal-Bench 2.1**, beating co-evolution and ensemble baselines.

## Difficulty, derived rather than measured

Instead of estimating difficulty with a model's pass rate, the paper derives an off-policy difficulty measure from the multi-turn learning objective. A trajectory decomposes into interleaved **scenarios** (situations reached) and **skills** (actions applied), giving three policy-independent difficulty factors: **execution length** (number of solver turns), **scenario novelty**, and **skill rarity** under a reference distribution grounded in broad world knowledge.

This framing clarifies what on-policy co-evolution misses: it mostly targets skill-selection errors under seed scenarios the current model already reaches, without controlling trajectory length or systematically increasing scenario novelty. Environment evolution operates on the full difficulty space instead.

## How evolution works

A loop-engineered multi-agent harness evolves each environment along one of three directions — **length** (insert scenario–skill pairs), **scenario** (replace a scenario, keep the skill), or **skill** (replace a skill) — through two gated loops: rubric-reviewed plan refinement, then environment refinement with oracle, invalid-test, and quality verifiers. An **evolution effort** parameter (low/high/max) controls edit scope; high is the default.

Measured with Claude Opus 5, single-step effects under high effort are:

| Direction | Pass-rate change | Avg-turns change |
|---|---|---|
| Length | −7.1pp | +9.4 |
| Scenario | −4.7pp | +13.5 |
| Skill | −4.0pp | +12.5 |

Length cuts pass rate most with the smallest total mutation; 15-step lineages confirm the direction profiles persist. Rollouts with Hy4 preview, Claude Opus 5, and GPT-5.6 Sol show evolved environments get consistently harder across models despite off-policy synthesis.

## Training results

Seeds are filtered ruthlessly: 47,678 candidate environments down to a balanced pool of 500. An **Evolution-Lineage (EL) scheduler** admits generation g+1 only after generation g passes a threshold (B=8, τ=6/8), keeping rollout groups partially solved so GRPO retains non-zero advantage. Peak Terminal-Bench 2.1 accuracy over 200 RL steps:

| Method | Qwen3.6-27B | Qwen3.6-35B-A3B |
|---|---|---|
| **Environment evolution** | **71.5%** | **64.9%** |
| Co-evolution | 62.9% | 55.1% |
| Ensemble | 60.0% | 52.8% |

Evolved environments keep providing signal even after their seeds were already used for SFT.

## Caveats

Difficulty estimates come from three specific rollout models, and the 15-generation cap exists because pass rate loses resolution in the zero-pass regime. Results are on Terminal-Bench 2.1 with the Claude Code harness and GRPO; transfer to SWE or computer-use agents is stated future work. Human-in-the-loop review during development means the quality bar partly depends on rubrics distilled from human judgment.

## Relevance to SW engineers

- If your agent RL loop discards "too easy" environments, you are paying synthesis cost for zero gradient — evolving survivors beats synthesizing from scratch.
- Separate *environment difficulty* from *model weakness*: difficulty relative to one model does not transfer, difficulty relative to the environment family does.
- Curriculum scheduling matters as much as data: admitting unsolvable tasks too early yields all-failure groups with zero advantage.
- The length/scenario/skill decomposition is a reusable rubric for hardening any task family, including coding benchmarks.
