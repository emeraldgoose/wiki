# Cross-Prompt Reuse of Entity-Replacement Responses

**Source**: [arXiv:2611.00352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00352) · **Published**: 2026-10-30
**Authors**: Jinkun Chen, Xuemin Yu, Sijia Han, Vlado Keselj

## Overview

This study asks how much of an entity-replacement response transfers between different English factual questions when model weights stay fixed. Rather than focusing only on whether answers change, the authors compare the predicted change in relative log probability between two candidate answers with the measured change under defined alignment conditions. The work separates hidden-state reuse, where an aligned change is injected at a specific depth, from direct reuse, where source output score changes are applied more directly. The result is not a single transfer success story: small plain-text checkpoints can remove substantial no-change error under some conditions, but larger checkpoints, chat formatting, and entity changes bring format- or cohort-dependent failures.

The central methodological contribution is the explicit comparison between predicted and measured log-probability shifts after known answer prefixes. For a target question about the same entity, the authors derive the expected shift from two source questions and then test whether injecting those aligned changes reproduces the expected effect. Hidden reuse is applied before the target's remaining layers, while direct reuse imports source output-level changes. This distinction makes it possible to analyze whether the useful information is better carried by intermediate representations or by already-computed answer scores.

The findings suggest three conclusions. First, other-relation hidden reuse reduces no-change error on small 0.5-1B Qwen and Llama checkpoints by roughly forty-five to forty-nine percent. Second, Gemma-2-2B is more format-sensitive, with plain-text behavior improving dramatically when its default beginning-of-sequence token handling is restored. Third, even successful transfer does not imply replacement-specificity: with target-relation sources, hidden reuse shows only a small post hoc numerical advantage outside chat format, and with some source pairings the change mainly reproduces a shift shared by both relations rather than relation-selective information.

## Key points

- The measured quantity is the transfer of entity-replacement responses between English factual questions, not merely answer flipping.
- Hidden reuse injects an aligned hidden-state change at 50% depth before the target's later layers.
- Direct reuse adds source output-score changes rather than injecting intermediate-state changes.
- On 0.5-1B Qwen and Llama, other-relation hidden reuse removes 45% and 49% of no-change error.
- Gemma-2-2B removes about 22% in one plain-text comparison without its default BOS handling.
- Restoring the BOS-related protocol gives 58% to 68% and passes all comparisons across three cohorts.
- Larger checkpoints, chat format, and new entities show replication but also format- or cohort-dependent failures.
- On small plain format checkpoints, other-relation hidden reuse mainly reproduces the shift common to both relations.
- Target-relation sources give only a 0.2 to 0.9 nat post hoc advantage for hidden reuse outside chat format.
- A relation-neutral change applied at both questions' entity tokens lowers relation-selective error more than random or other-replacement changes, indicating dependence on the receiving question.

## Details

- **Known-answer alignment.** Comparison is based on published answers after known prefixes, allowing predicted and measured log-probability shifts to be contrasted cleanly.
- **BOS sensitivity is large.** The same model moves from partial to nearly complete comparison passing when sequence-start handling is restored, which means interface details should not be treated as incidental.
- **Replacement specificity is unconfirmed.** The extra advantage of hidden reuse under target-relation sources is small, not yet replacement-specific, and unconfirmed under many conditions.
- **Implication.** Intermediate-state reuse can be useful, but its behavior is parameter-count, format, and cohort dependent; claims about entity replacement need matched controls.
