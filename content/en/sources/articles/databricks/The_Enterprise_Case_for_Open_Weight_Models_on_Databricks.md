# The Enterprise Case for Open-Weight Models on Databricks

**Source**: [Databricks Blog](https://www.databricks.com/blog/enterprise-case-open-weight-models-databricks)

## Overview

Field engineers at Databricks observe enterprises reevaluating single-provider frontier-standardization strategies across seven pressures: spend not generating sufficient returns, rapidly changing model rankings, frontier guardrails blocking legitimate work, evolving data-retention policies, demand for model governance visibility, harness effects on measured performance, and open-weight models catching up. Databricks recommends an architecture in which models and harnesses can change without weakening governance. Open-weight models improve cost efficiency, control, and flexibility; Unity Gateway serves both open-weight and frontier models through a unified API with centralized governance. Shared data references illustrate growing open-weight adoption, smart routing, fallback chains, budgets, audited tools, and cross-provider governance.

## Key points

- AI budgets have grown faster than measured returns for some enterprises.
- Model rankings change frequently and should remain swappable.
- Frontier guardrails can block legitimate defensive analysis.
- Data retention policy changes can create compliance risk.
- Open-weight models now benchmark comparably to frontier models at lower cost.
- Unity Gateway provides unified governance across model classes.

## Details

Databricks reports that average unique models per customer rose from 4.2 to 7.1, with roughly 40 percent of used models open-weight. Its routing and budget features shift routine tasks to cheaper models, while fallback chains handle rate limits or outages. GLM 5.2 reportedly became roughly 40 percent of open-weight tokens within about one month of availability. The claims depend on internal data and a specific customer period through July 2026, and costs are broader than single numerical figures. Enterprises should evaluate model-plus-harness performance and governance controls before migration rather than assuming open-weight parity automatically.
