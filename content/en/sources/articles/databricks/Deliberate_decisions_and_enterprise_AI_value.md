# Deliberate decisions and enterprise AI value

**Source**: [Databricks Blog](https://www.databricks.com/blog/deliberate-decisions-and-enterprise-ai-value) · **Published**: 2026-10-30

## Overview
The DataIQ report "Deliberate Decisions: How Grounded Leadership Is Advancing the Path to Enterprise AI Value", produced with Databricks, argues that enterprise AI pressure is shifting from whether AI is possible to where organizations should commit, prioritize and proceed while evidence is still incomplete. Based on two days of peer discussion among senior data and AI leaders at DataIQ Guild 2026, the report describes five recurring pressures — rising expectations outpacing organizational absorption, proliferating pilots before teams can govern and support them, and fragmented ownership as AI crosses functions — and responds with five leadership disciplines. It identifies a four-stage adoption journey (concerned, curious, comfortable, competent) and frames Databricks' four foundations — Context, Control, Cost and Choice — as ways to balance commitment with flexibility as conditions evolve. The aim is not to predict winning technologies, but to provide practical judgment frameworks and shared language for making trade-offs visible to executive sponsors.

## Key points
- Early AI success can raise expectations faster than an organization can absorb, so pilots and proofs of concept must be assessed against governance, support and cost before scaling.
- Five leadership disciplines emerged: confidence grounded in reality; enterprise choices rather than demand-led reactions; leadership through influence rather than control; human judgment retained as a strategic capability; and stability without rigidity.
- Databricks' four foundations for navigating tension are Context (trusted governed data), Control (responsible and secure innovation), Cost (sustainable adoption against ambition) and Choice (ability to evolve technologies and priorities).
- Distinguish progress from readiness: a successful pilot is not a production-capable AI capability; teams must validate operational support, governance, skills, monitoring and the total cost model.
- AI demand should be treated as a portfolio: make a range of initial bets, increase investment as evidence strengthens and stop or redirect work that no longer justifies commitment.
- Ownership must include business accountability for outcomes and their consequences, not just decision rights across functions.
- Human authority must be explicit: people need the authority, context and time to challenge AI outputs, and organizations should avoid automating away the capability to develop expertise.

## Details
The report uses DataIQ Guild 2026 peer discussion and frames four foundations reflected in Databricks capabilities such as Lakebase, as a governed transactional foundation for AI applications and agents, and Genie, which helps people and agents access organizational context. It decomposes the readiness gap into operational support, governance, skills, monitoring and cost model, quotes practitioners on how invisible support costs make business cases incomplete, and proposes validating whether an initiative can remain useful, reliable and affordable beyond a bounded experiment. On portfolio management, leaders are encouraged to connect use cases to business outcomes and evidence for next investment rather than presenting isolated technical projects, with specific quotes from CDOs on differentiated value generation and business accountability. Ownership discussions stress distributing business outcomes, workflow change, risk, adoption and ongoing costs across the organization, with business owners owning impact on the long-range plan. The closing message is that progress does not require waiting for certainty, but making informed commitments while preserving choice, especially through reversible technology decisions, anchored standards, and human authority maintained as automation increases. This restores executive conversation quality without prescribing which model will win.
