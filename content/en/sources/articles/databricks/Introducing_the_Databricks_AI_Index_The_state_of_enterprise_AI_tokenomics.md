# Introducing the Databricks AI Index: The state of enterprise AI tokenomics

**Source**: [databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/introducing-databricks-ai-index-state-enterprise-ai-tokenomics) · **Published**: 2026-11-16

## Overview

Developers have more AI models and coding agents to choose from than ever: since May 2026, a new flagship proprietary or open-weight model ships roughly every week — every 3.7 days when regional labs are included. Knowing which models perform best, what they cost, and when to switch gets harder by the month. Databricks sees this landscape from a rare vantage point: thousands of enterprises reach and govern AI models through Unity Gateway (real adoption and spend data), Databricks uses AI internally across 12,000+ employees, and the company has invested heavily in evaluating frontier models on enterprise tasks. The Databricks AI Index is the public resource that brings those insights together.

## Key points

- **What launched**: the Databricks AI Index — a public, continuously updated resource customers asked for, comparing models and showing what Databricks sees across enterprise AI adoption.
- **Enterprise AI usage and spending**: aggregated, anonymized data from 10,000+ companies using Unity Gateway — digital natives, Fortune 500, and Global 2000 enterprises — including traffic to external providers.
- **Continuously updated coding-agent evaluations**: building on the coding-agent benchmark published in July, the latest evaluations draw on real coding-agent workflows from Databricks engineers, captured through Unity Gateway.
- **Themes the Index covers** (from the source outline): model preferences changing faster than ever; enterprises embracing multi-AI and open-weight models; no single model or harness dominating the cost-quality frontier; token prices not telling the full cost story; the highest-performing model rarely being the best value; AI spend still highly concentrated; and applying these insights with Unity Gateway.

## Details

The Index answers practical enterprise questions: how do you compare AI models for coding tasks, why can cheaper models cost more to use, and are open-weight models competitive with proprietary ones? Its evidence base is unusual — production gateway traffic (usage + spend) rather than synthetic benchmarks alone, layered with coding-agent evaluations derived from real internal engineering workflows. The article positions Unity Gateway as the enforcement point where these insights become actionable (routing, governance, and spend control across providers).

Databricks emphasizes that headline token prices are a poor guide to end-to-end coding cost: agentic workloads are dominated by harness behavior, task success rates, and rework, which is why the evaluations use real workflow traces rather than single-shot benchmarks.
