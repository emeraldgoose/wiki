# Brickster Voices: Meet Clara Kurian

**Source**: Databricks Blog, 2026-10-12

**Lead**: Brickster Voices spotlights the people behind Databricks' work. This installment interviews Clara Kurian, Senior Recruiter for University Recruiting in APJ and the company's first university recruiter for the region. She discusses building campus hiring from the ground up in India, running the first structured 51-intern Bengaluru program, and the fundamentals-plus-mindset bar for early-career data and AI talent.

## Background

Brickster Voices is a Databricks series covering career journeys, behind-the-scenes project work, and how teams collaborate. The interview centers on India, where Databricks has invested $250M+ and plans ~50% workforce expansion — making university recruiting a strategic function rather than a back-office one.

## Databricks' growth in India

Kurian describes the investment as tangible in Bengaluru rather than a headline: India is treated as a strategic hub, not a satellite office. Figures cited since 2022:

| Area | Growth |
|------|--------|
| India overall | +270% |
| Leadership presence | +350% across business units |
| Engineering | 14x |
| Field Engineering & Professional Services | +260% |

Community investment accompanies hiring: engineering networking events with senior tech leaders, Bharat Bricks Hacks hackathons across IITs and IISc, and Bengaluru Tech Week sponsorship — positioned as embedding in India's tech ecosystem, not just filling roles.

## The first structured India intern program

Kurian's vision for the inaugural 51-intern Bengaluru program was that interns experience Databricks itself, not observe from outside: real work from day one, plus deliberately designed moments such as speed mentoring with engineering leadership and social events (sneaker art, pickleball). The design goal was a genuine taste of pace, people, and culture — what a career there could look like.

## What makes early-career candidates stand out

Her advice to students emphasizes strong fundamentals over chasing a specific team, since curiosity and continuous learning transfer across deep-systems and applied-AI work. On mindset: curiosity first ("I don't know that yet, but I'll figure it out"), plus a builder mindset and critical thinking. What separates memorable candidates is how they show up — genuine curiosity, comfort with unseen problems, authenticity and self-awareness over rehearsed answers. On values, she points to hiring as a true multi-team partnership reflecting "Customer Obsessed": stakeholders invest beyond their share while trusting recruiting's expertise.

## Relevance to Software Engineers

- **Career signal**: for students targeting data/AI roles, fundamentals plus demonstrable curiosity outweigh team-specific preparation; saying "here's how I'd figure it out" beats a rehearsed answer.
- **Evaluating employers**: community investment (hackathons, campus events, tech-week presence) indicates whether a regional office is a strategic hub with real scope or a satellite.
- **Internship design**: if you mentor interns, the article's checklist — real work day one, direct access to senior builders, cultural immersion — is a compact template.
