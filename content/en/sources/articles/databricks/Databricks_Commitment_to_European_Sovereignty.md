# Databricks' Commitment to European Sovereignty

**Source**: [Databricks' Commitment to European Sovereignty](https://www.databricks.com/blog/databricks-commitment-european-sovereignty) · **Published**: 2026-11-09

## Overview

European organizations want to build AI and analytics on their most sensitive data under European control, a shared ambition reflected in frameworks such as DORA, NIS2, the EU AI Act and the EU Data Act, along with emerging proposals like the European Cloud and AI Development Act. Databricks frames its response as an expansion of sovereignty commitments rather than a new direction: it will launch on the AWS European Sovereign Cloud (AWS ESC), and it is committed to making Azure Databricks meet EU sovereignty requirements and customer expectations.

The company's stated philosophy is that customers decide where their data is stored and who holds the keys that protect it — Databricks positions itself as the engine that processes data, not a vault that holds it. Combined with open formats and a multi-cloud architecture, the argument is that sovereignty does not have to come at the cost of lock-in. The concrete commitments are three new European regions: Azure Italy, Azure Spain and AWS ESC.

## Key points

- Databricks has been building in Europe since its first regional launch in the Netherlands in 2017 and now serves thousands of European enterprises across 17 European cloud regions, with more than 2,000 European employees in 15 offices including several engineering hubs.
- European customers can now contract directly with a Databricks European Union entity, simplifying procurement for those that require a European counterparty.
- On Microsoft Sovereign Public Cloud, Databricks and Microsoft are working to extend sovereignty controls to Azure Databricks, including operational oversight, personnel controls and transparency measures aimed at European regulators and customers; Databricks is classified as a regional service on Azure.
- AWS ESC is described as a physically and logically independent cloud operated within the EU that reached general availability in early 2026, designed for the highest levels of operational autonomy, resilience and data protection — the argument being that customers will not have to choose between sovereignty and a modern data and AI platform.
- Compliance is presented as a track record rather than a promise: Databricks was an early leader in GDPR readiness, among the first to build right-to-be-forgotten deletion management, and has been Azure EU Data Boundary compliant since the EUDB launched.
- The post describes digital sovereignty as a journey rather than a switch, naming future work such as support for any open-standard-compatible storage so customers can keep data on premises or in European clouds, and it carries a forward-looking statement that plans and timelines may change.

## Details

The sovereignty argument rests on how the platform is architected. Because customers choose where data is stored and hold the encryption keys, and because the platform uses open formats in a multi-cloud deployment, moving or extending workloads does not require adopting a proprietary store. Databricks notes that meeting strict compliance and residency requirements is not new territory for it, having evolved the platform alongside regulations and customer expectations — including support for the EU Data Boundary, so customer data is stored and processed where customers choose — and that it already operates under some of the world's most rigorous frameworks for highly regulated workloads.

Immediate focus is on bringing Databricks to the Microsoft Sovereign Public Cloud regions and to AWS ESC, with additional controls added over time, and the company says it will keep evaluating further sovereign options based on customer and partner needs. The commitment it restates is unchanged: customers should control where their data lives, how it is protected, and who can access it.
