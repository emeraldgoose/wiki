# From Weeks to a Day: How We Made LLM Evaluation Fast Enough to Iterate On

**Source**: Airbnb Engineering (Medium) · **Published**: 2026-07-14

## Thesis

Training an LLM is the easy part; trustworthy, fast iteration is the hard part. Friction comes from infrastructure, not model quality, and the fixes are classical software engineering. The stack has four layers — three engineering enhancements plus the integration layer (easiest to overlook: every component looks fine in isolation; **the seams are where things break**, so you must exercise the full path).

## Layer 1: Name the Noise (Dual Indeterminacy)

Classical metrics (F1, BLEU) are deterministic; LLM-in-the-loop eval is not: judges rescore identical inputs differently, LLM-generated references regenerate as different strings (~3/4 of references differ across runs; same judge drifts ~1% — against a 1–3% real signal, most of what you see is noise more samples won't fix).

Frame: **dual indeterminacy** — epistemic uncertainty (model/judge limits, actionable) vs aleatoric uncertainty (task ambiguity, no judge improvement fixes it; cf. Abbasi Yadkori et al.; Ling et al. 2024 on ICL). A 2% move can be model improvement, judge drift, reference shift, or a mix. Ship criterion: differences must be **meaningful, stable, product-relevant** — survive judge rotation, metric versioning, sample re-stratification (a t-test pass that flips under judge swap isn't shippable). Distinguishing the two requires run-stable judge outputs — Layer 2's job.

## Layer 2: Deterministic Eval Foundation (Per-Sample Cache)

Rejected: majority-vote sampling (converges to judge's central tendency, not accuracy) and Bayesian noise modeling (needs centralized priors/posteriors ≈ same infra as a cache, minus free reproducibility). Key instrumentation insight: **>50% of model outputs across candidates were identical strings** — most changes affect only a subset of inputs, and most reference noise was self-generated by regenerating references. So stabilize judge *inputs*: cache on both axes — **references** keyed by (sample id, generation config), **judge scores** keyed by (sample, output, judge config, metric). Identical inputs return cached results: deterministic, cheaper, cross-run comparable — and failed jobs resume (a job dying at example 8,000 restarts from cache; new candidates/metrics run against cached outputs free).

## Layer 3: Bounded Model Mutation (Micro-Adapters)

Full retraining is neither quick nor safe (high-rank LoRA on an 8B base = days; every weight change risks regressions). Research bounds: factual behavior is partially localizable (Meng et al., NeurIPS 2022 — targeted MLP edits work) but edits ripple (Cohen et al., TACL 2024); LoRA absorbs targeted corrections up to a few hundred examples before reasoning degrades (Pletenev et al. 2025).

**Micro-adapter**: rank <50 LoRA patch layered atop a frozen shared adapter, learning one bug's minimal correction — trains in <1 GPU-hour, ships like a hotfix: scoped to one issue, two gates (no regression on expert-reviewed domains; high-uncertainty outputs to human review), canary + auto-rollback, **same-day turnaround**. Stacked patches interact (CACE — changing anything changes everything; Sculley et al. 2015), so three lifecycle rules: **fuse co-triggering patches** (learnable fusion over shared relational neighborhoods; Gao et al. 2024), plus consolidation/retirement rules keeping the stack from drifting.

## Layer 4: End-to-End Integration

Bounded mutation needs trustworthy measurement (Layers 1–2); end-to-end validation needs the eval foundation fast enough to run the *combined* path, not per-component checks.

## Takeaways for SW Engineers

- **Cache judge inputs, don't model judge noise** — most eval nondeterminism is self-inflicted regeneration.
- Separate epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty before "fixing" eval disagreements.
- Ship model corrections as **scoped, gated, reversible patches** with lifecycle rules, not retrains.
- Validate seams end-to-end; component-green ≠ system-correct.
