# Environment Evolution for Terminal Agents

: RL environments are becoming the scalable lever for training terminal agents, but environments synthesized from scratch are now too easy for frontier models — solved across repeated rollouts, they get discarded and provide no learning signal. Recent co-evolution methods synthesize new environments from on-policy rollouts, but the results stay tied to the rollout model and go stale as training saturates. This paper proposes **environment evolution**: incrementally harden existing environments **off-policy**, generation by generation, and schedule the lineage during training. With simple 200-step long-horizon RL, it improves **Qwen3.6-27B by 14.4 and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B by 18.0 percentage points on Terminal-Bench 2.1**, beating co-evolution and ensemble baselines.

Difficulty, derived rather than measured: Instead of estimating difficulty with a model's pass rate, the paper derives an off-policy difficulty measure from the multi-turn learning objective. A trajectory decomposes into interleaved scenarios (situations reached) and skills (actions applied), giving three policy-independent difficulty factors: execution length (number of solver turns), scenario novelty, and skill rarity under a reference distribution grounded in broad world knowledge.

How evolution works: A loop-engineered multi-agent harness evolves each environment along one of three directions — length (insert scenario–skill pairs), scenario (replace a scenario, keep the skill), or skill (replace a skill) — through two gated loops: rubric-reviewed plan refinement, then environment refinement with oracle, invalid-test, and quality verifiers. An evolution effort parameter (low/high/max) controls edit scope; high is the default.

Measured with Claude Opus 5, single-step effects under high effort 

Training results: Seeds are filtered ruthlessly: 47,678 candidate environments down to a balanced pool of 500. An Evolution-Lineage (EL) scheduler admits generation g+1 only after generation g passes a threshold (B=8, τ=6/8), keeping rollout groups partially solved so GRPO retains non-zero advantage. Peak Terminal-Bench 2.1 accuracy over 200 RL steps:

 Method  Qwen3.6-27B  Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 
---------
 Environment evolution  71.5%  64.9% 
 Co-evolution  62.9%  55.1% 
 Ensemble  60.0%  52.8% 

Evolved environments 

Caveats: Difficulty estimates come from three specific rollout models, and the 15-generation cap exists because pass rate loses resolution in the zero-pass regime. Results are on Terminal-Bench 2.1 with the Claude Code harness and GRPO; transfer to SWE or computer-use agents is stated future work. Human-in-the-loop review during development means the quality bar partly depends on rubrics distilled from human judgment.
