# An Empirical Study on Zero-Data Bootstrapping for Conversational Recommender Systems

: Conversational Recommender Systems normally require domain-specific dialogue data — which is costly, scarce, and often simply unavailable in a new domain. This paper runs a systematic empirical study of **zero-data CRS bootstrapping**: generating synthetic conversational supervision from *non-conversational* signals — item reviews, metadata, and user-item interactions — with no in-domain dialogue corpus at all. Across two information-theoretic selection strategies (**Jensen-Shannon diversity** and **Fisher information**), multiple domain signals, architectures, datasets, and fine-tuning paradigms, domain-grounded synthetic data consistently beat zero-shot prompting and naive synthetic baselines; active selection beats random sampling on data efficiency; and in low-resource settings synthetic data can outright **outperform scarce real dialogues** while still complementing them.

The constraint that makes this interesting: A conversational recommender has to be trained on dialogue: user utterances, system responses, the recommendation that followed. That data is domain-specific by construction — a recommender for outdoor gear has never seen a conversation about camping stoves. Getting it means either building it, buying it, or hoping users generate it, and in a genuinely new domain none of these are available.

What "zero-data" means here: Not "no data" — no dialogue data. The available signals are:

- Item reviews — free text that mentions what the product does, who it's for, what it's compared against
- Item metadata — category, attributes, price band
- User-item interactions — implicit feedback: what users viewed, clicked, bought

From these, the study synthesises conversational supervision. The review text in particular is a rich source: a review is, informally, a conversation about a product that already happened.

The two selection strategies: The study compares two information-theoretic criteria for which synthetic examples to keep:

1. Jensen-Shannon divergence — select for examples that are maximally distinct from what you already have (maximise coverage/diversity)
2. Fisher information — select for examples that most reduce uncertainty about the model (maximise information gain)
