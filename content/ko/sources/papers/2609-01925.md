# CRISP: Cliff-awaRe Input-adaptive Sparse Prefilling with Structural-Mass-Motivated Routing

: The prefilling phase of long-context LLM inference scales quadratically, and fixed-pattern or offline-profiled sparse attention cannot adapt to input-dependent attention structure. Dynamic routers like FlexPrefill route heads in real time but rely on an indirect Jensen-Shannon Divergence proxy (with its own matmul + KL overhead) and cumulative coverage thresholds that ignore the post-softmax mass hierarchy. CRISP fixes both: a structural proxy **C_struct** that reads routing directly off mass at sink/recency positions, reproducing JSD decisions on 94.0% of Llama heads at zero extra cost, and a **sink-aware threshold** grounded in the noise floor. Across InfiniteBench, RULER, and LongBench on two model families, CRISP is the strongest sparse method overall — matching or beating dense attention on retrieval-heavy benchmarks with up to **+28.0pp recovery** and **5.30x attention speedup at 512k tokens**.

JSD routing is indirect and expensive: FlexPrefill sends each head down a Vertical-Slash path (concentrated heads) or Pooled-Estimation path (diffuse heads), deciding via JSD between a pooled attention estimate and the per-query mean. That pooled estimate exists only for the comparison — it costs an extra matmul, a second softmax, and two log/reduction passes per head. The mass cliff defeats cumulative thresholds: Post-softmax mass forms a rigid hierarchy: architectural sinks (sometimes >90% of mass), task-relevant signal blocks, and near-zero background noise thinning as O(1/n). A cumulative coverage threshold gamma cannot navigate the cliff between signal and noise — it either terminates inside the sink (sink-only collapse, selecting zero semantic tokens) or crosses into noise and collects O(n) background tokens. Caveats: Evidence spans two 7–8B model families; Cstruct exploits attention sinks, so it breaks on sink-suppressing architectures (e.g. gated attention) and the paper is explicit about this.
