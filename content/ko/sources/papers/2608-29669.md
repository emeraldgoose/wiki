# Wasserstein-Barycentric Interaction Fields for Spatial Factor Models: Evidence from Language-Model Representations

: Spatial return models take the interaction matrix as given and leave the feedback channel uninterpreted. This paper constructs a **bandwidth-free field** from firms' language-model article embedding distributions using **target-anchored Wasserstein barycentric reconstruction**. A quadratic exposure-adjustment problem then maps that feedback into a **peer-misalignment penalty ratio**. For 52 firms, with the field **frozen from 2018–2022 news** and evaluated on 2023–2026, the method yields a penalty ratio of **3.46** (95% interval [2.89, 4.17]) and higher conditional quasi-likelihood than equal-weighted peer support or RBF weighting of the same distances.

The gap: interactions given, not explained: Spatial factor models assume assets influence each other. The interaction matrix — which asset pushes which — is normally taken as given. That leaves the feedback channel uninterpreted: the model knows that stocks are connected but not what kind of similarity the connection encodes, and certainly not whether it reflects anything economic rather than statistical artefact.

This paper builds the field from text instead. The input is each firm's language-model embedding distribution over news artic

Method: Target-anchored Wasserstein barycentric reconstruction. The key construction: for a target firm, reconstruct its embedding distribution as a barycentric combination of other firms' distributions, where the weights solve an optimal transport problem. The Wasserstein geometry gives a distribution-level distance — how far apart two distributions of embeddings are — rather than comparing single vectors.

Results: For 52 firms, with the field constructed from 2018–2022 news and frozen — then evaluated on 2023–2026 — the paper reports:

- A penalty ratio of 3.46, 95% interval 2.89, 4.17
- Higher conditional quasi-likelihood than both comparison baselines:
  - equal-weighted peer support — the naive no-field baseline
  - RBF weighting of the same distances — same information, conventional kernel

The second baseline is the meaningful one: it holds the distances fixed and changes only the weighting scheme, s

The out-of-sample design: The freeze matters more than usual here. The field is built from 2018–2022 news and never updated, then scored on 2023–2026. That means the reported performance is not a fit statistic from the same period that generated the inputs — it is a genuine forecast-horizon evaluation.

This is also the honest limit on interpreting the numbers: 52 firms is a small panel, and the confidence intervals are correspondingly wide. The construction is the contribution; the point estimate is a demonstration.
