# Select, Compress, Reinvest: A Controlled Study of Visual-Token Allocation in Long-Video MLLMs

: Long-video models cannot look at every frame — an hour sampled at 1fps is 3,600 images against a small fixed token slice — yet frame selection is usually treated as a preprocessing detail. Published selectors are incomparable because they simultaneously change the scorer, prompt boundary, resolution, and answering model. This paper holds everything fixed and varies one decision at a time (selection, spatial compression, reinvestment) across six training-free selection rules, three long-video benchmarks, and two answering models. Headline: **selection is the largest single lever**, compression is nearly free, and compression only pays off once its savings are reinvested in more frames.

What the controlled harness finds: Decision  Result 
------
 Selection  8 query-selected frames beat 16 uniform frames by 6.9 points on LongVideoBench's hour-long bin 
 Off-the-shelf optimizer  Orthogonal Matching Pursuit — a decades-old sparse-approximation algorithm, unmodified — matches or lands within a point of every purpose-built selector on all three benchmarks 
 Compression  Halving each frame's spatial budget at fixed timestamps costs at most 0.44 points 
 Reinvestment  Spending freed tokens on 2x as many compressed fram

Caveats: All numbers are from the arXiv abstract; full-text tables were not re-verified, so per-benchmark and per-model breakdowns are omitted. Findings are for training-free selection rules — learned selectors are out of scope. The reinvestment arithmetic (2x frames at ≤ original cost) holds under this harness's token accounting.

Relevance to SW engineers: - Frame selection is not a preprocessing detail — it is the largest accuracy lever in long-video QA, bigger than resolution.
- Before building a bespoke selector, try classical sparse approximation (OMP): decades-old, dependency-free, within a point of purpose-built methods.
- Compression without reinvestment is pointless savings; budget freed tokens explicitly toward more coverage.
- Cross-paper benchmark comparisons can swing ~4 points on harness alone — re-run baselines in your own harness be
