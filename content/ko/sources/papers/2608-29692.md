# Portfolio Risk Bounds without Cross-Asset Return Covariances: Distributional Fields from Language-Model Representations

: Portfolio risk assessment ordinarily relies on **cross-asset return covariances** — which are hard to estimate in short, high-dimensional panels, where the sample matrix is nearly singular. This paper shows that **firm-level distribution-valued characteristics can instead provide one-sided certificates of portfolio risk**. Under two maintained hypotheses — links from characteristics to systematic exposures, and from exposures to returns — **multi-firm Wasserstein-2 dispersion** yields a sharp upper bound on systematic portfolio variance. In a 52-firm 2018–2022 panel, an allocation built from **Qwen3-Embedding-8B** news representations sits between the **0.69th and 1.33rd in-sample variance percentiles**, against **21.1st–28.6th** for equal risk weighting.

The estimation problem: Portfolio variance is wᵀΣw. That formulation needs Σ — the full covariance matrix across assets. In a short, high-dimensional panel the number of assets exceeds the available time samples, so the sample covariance is close to singular and its estimate is unreliable. Everyone hits this; most respond with shrinkage or factor models, both of which impose structure on Σ.

Under those, multi-firm Wasserstein-2 dispersion — the spread of the firms' embedding distributions under optimal transport — yields a sharp upper bound on systematic portfolio variance, and correspondingly on standardised returns.
