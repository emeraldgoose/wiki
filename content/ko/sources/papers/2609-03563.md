# FlashRender: Few-Step Generative Rendering via Camera-Controlled Video MeanFlow

: Generative rendering — retaking a source video along a target camera trajectory — currently needs many sampling steps. The paper diagnoses **sampling-step-dependent camera control** as a manifestation of discretization error in multi-step models: control that works at many steps breaks at few steps. **FlashRender** resolves the inconsistency first (lowering denoising-trajectory curvature to make distillation easier), then distills. The result matches multi-step baselines in video quality and geometric consistency at **25x lower sampling cost**, with superior camera controllability even on out-of-distribution trajectories.

Three complementary pieces: 1. RETA (Representation Transformation and Alignment) — aligns hidden source-video representations with target-video features from a frozen visual-geometry model, encoding the geometric transformation directly in the source-video stream so camera control is consistent across sampling steps.
2. MeanFlow fine-tuning — trains on the lower-curvature denoising trajectory induced by RETA, addressing discretization error more effectively than distilling a high-curvature path.
3. On-policy flow-map dist

Relevance to SW engineers: - Straighten the trajectory before distilling it: fixing step-dependent conditioning inconsistency first makes few-step distillation dramatically easier.
- Distill in the deployment regime — on-policy correction under the actual few-step sampler beats imitating teacher trajectories.
- Frozen geometry models can supply control signals without retraining the whole stack; alignment in representation space beats prompt-level conditioning.
