# ZipTok3D: High-Fidelity 3D Tokenization with Compact Token Prefixes

: 3D generation pipelines first tokenize object geometry into latent sequences, and sequence length directly sets downstream generative-modeling cost. Existing 3D tokenizers either anchor tokens to spatial regions or compress geometry into a fixed set of global tokens — and both degrade sharply when squeezed to just a few tokens. ZipTok3D instead organizes geometry into **progressively informative global-token prefixes** and unfolds them through **iterative decoding**. During training, nested dropout randomly truncates the latent sequence so every retained prefix must reconstruct the whole object, concentrating essential geometry in the leading tokens; a parameter-shared Transformer block then refines the spatial representation over repeated passes. With the same token width, one ZipTok3D token matches the 32-token COD-VAE baseline on ShapeNet and four tokens match it on TRELLIS — **32x and 8x shorter sequences** at comparable quality.

Why few-token 3D reconstruction is hard: With a moderate token budget, a global tokenizer can spread complementary geometric detail across many latent vectors. Shrink the budget to one or two tokens and each token must summarize a far larger share of the object, forcing global structure and fine detail to compete for tiny latent capacity. Fixed-budget tokenizers (VecSet, COD-VAE) resolve that competition only implicitly through the final reconstruction loss, with no explicit decision about what to preserve first. Nested prefixes plus shared refinement: Training samples a retained length from exponentially spaced budgets {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128} out of a 128-token, 512-wide latent sequence, masks the suffix from selection and refinement, and requires the prefix alone to reconstruct the full shape. Because every shorter prefix is contained in every longer one, leading tokens are forced to carry object-wide geometry while later tokens add residual detail. Caveats: All operating points per dataset come from a single checkpoint and one training seed, so run-to-run variance is unreported; the bootstrap CIs cover evaluation-object variation only. The IoU edge over COD-VAE-32 on TRELLIS (+0.06) has a CI crossing zero — the CD/F1 wins are the statistically firm part. Efficiency numbers are single-GPU (H20) protocol-specific. The paper evaluates reconstruction and class-conditioned generation, not text-to-3D prompting.
