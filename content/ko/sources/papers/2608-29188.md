# Locked at the Entrance, Open Inside: Where RLVR Narrows the Solution Space

: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) substantially improves single-sample accuracy, but it **contracts the policy's solution space**, diminishing the returns of test-time scaling. This paper localises *where* in a reasoning trajectory the breadth is lost: is the policy failing to *access* a valid solution family, or failing to *execute* it once started? Using the Countdown task — whose solution space can be exhaustively enumerated into entrance families defined by first operand and operator — across PPO on Qwen2.5-3B and GRPO on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, **solution coverage falls by up to 67%**. The contraction is heavily concentrated at the entrance: per-token likelihood shifts are **11x–16x larger before the first arithmetic operation** than during downstream reasoning. Supplying only an unselected entrance prefix restores low-access-family completion rates by **over an order of magnitude (0.018 → 0.212 under PPO)** — alternative solutions remain executable, they are simply never initiated. Guided by this localisation, surface prompting fails but entrance-targeted intervention succeeds: **late-layer parameter interpolation with early checkpoints raises coverage 37% at no loss in pass@1**.

The problem: pass@1 up, breadth down: RLVR's headline benefit is well established — verifiable rewards raise pass@1 substantially. The cost is less discussed. Training on a scalar reward narrows the policy's solution space, which in turn diminishes what test-time scaling can buy you. If the model only considers one route to an answer, sampling more doesn't help: you are resampling from a smaller set.

This matters because two techniques that both depend on breadth — test-time scaling and parallel sampling — become less effective pre

Access versus execution: The paper's contribution is to ask a sharper question than "did coverage drop?" It asks where inside the trajectory the loss occurs:

- Access failure — a valid solution family exists in the model's repertoire, but the policy never reaches it
- Execution failure — the policy enters the family but fails to carry it through

These have opposite implications. Execution failure is a capability gap that better training might close. Access failure is a policy problem — the capability is there and simp

The setup: making coverage measurable: To answer this, the paper uses Countdown, whose solution space can be exhaustively enumerated into discrete entrance families defined by the first operand and the operator. That is what makes "coverage" a countable quantity rather than a judgment call: you can ask which fraction of the reachable solution families the policy ever initiates, and check whether it completes them.

This is a much stronger measurement than aggregate accuracy, because accuracy is consistent with many distinct policies 

The finding: the loss is at the door: The decisive measurement is where in the trajectory the likelihood shifts occur. Per-token likelihood shifts are 11x–16x larger prior to the first arithmetic operation than during downstream reasoning.

So the contraction is not a gradual narrowing across the whole chain of thought. It is concentrated almost entirely at the opening move — choosing which family to enter at all. Once inside a family, the reasoning proceeds much as before.
