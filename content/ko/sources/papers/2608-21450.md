# Beyond Visual Similarity: Entity-Aligned Retrieval for Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering

: Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering retrieves external information to answer questions about long-tail entities, and existing pipelines overwhelmingly use **CLIP-style dual encoders that prioritise surface visual similarity over entity-level semantic alignment**. That fails in both directions: semantically identical concepts with large visual variation (different photos of the same landmark), and distinct entities that look similar (different bird species). KBMR is the first **MLLM-based embedding retriever** tailored for KB-VQA, mapping images into a semantic space that preserves concept identity, and pairing it with an MLLM-based **semantic discriminator** that emits continuous entity-consistency weights — enabling effective hard-negative sampling and soft supervision beyond rigid binary labels. Result: up to **14.7% better retrieval Recall@1** and **9.4% higher end-to-end VQA accuracy** over CLIP baselines.

Why visual similarity is the wrong objective: KB-VQA is the setting where a question references an entity the model doesn't know, so it must retrieve the right knowledge. The retrieval step decides whether the pipeline works at all.

CLIP-style dual encoders were trained for image–text alignment at web scale. That objective rewards visual resemblance. But KB-VQA needs entity resemblance. The gap shows up in two failure modes, and they pull in opposite directions:

- Same concept, different appearance — a specific landmark photographed at di

Approach 1 — MLLM as the embedder: KBMR replaces the dual encoder with an MLLM-based embedder, leveraging the model's autoregressive capabilities to map images into a semantic space that better preserves concept identity. The reasoning is straightforward: a model that can reason about what an image depicts produces embeddings that track what the image is, not what it looks like.

The trade-off implied by the paper is the usual one — generative semantic quality against the retrieval efficiency of a dual encoder.

Approach 2 — soft supervision instead of binary labels: The second contribution addresses a data problem: at Wikipedia scale, the retrieval supervision signal is noisy.

The fix is an MLLM-based semantic discriminator that produces continuous entity-consistency weights rather than a binary "this is relevant / not relevant" label. These weights feed a continuous semantic distillation objective, which enables:
