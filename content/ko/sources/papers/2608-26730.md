# Knowing When Not to Reuse: Conditional Experience Transfer in Autonomous LLM Post-Training

: Autonomous post-training systems propose updates, train candidates, and use evaluation feedback to pick the next proposal. As evidence accumulates, a central question emerges: **after subsequent training has changed the parent model, which past update evidence is still actionable?** An update's effect depends on its parent, its data, and its training stage — so treating past success as *context-free permission* wastes compute, and promoting a bad child can degrade everything after it. The paper formalises this as **conditional experience transfer** and introduces **Boundary-Calibrated Intervention Transfer (BCIT)**, which authorizes reuse *before* weight-changing training. On a 4B model across finance reasoning, text-to-SQL, and function calling, BCIT authorizes **fewer harmful updates and reaches higher equal-budget final-model quality** than the alternatives — the argument being that experience *authorization* is its own problem, distinct from experience generation.

The failure mode being named: The standard autonomous post-training loop looks like this: propose an update → train a candidate → evaluate → use the result to choose the next proposal. Each step is automated. What is not handled is the accumulation of evidence and its decay.

Consider: an update U produced a +6pp gain when applied to parent P0. Three post-training rounds later the model is P3. Does U still help? The paper's answer is that you cannot assume so, because:

- The effect depended on the parent (P0's failure mode 

BCIT: authorize before training: The design decision is the ordering. BCIT runs the authorization check before the expensive part:

1. Bind the effect to its source context — record which parent, which data, which stage the observed effect came from
2. Check applicability conditions — is the current state close enough to the source context for the evidence to transfer?
3. Veto candidates with named hard conflicts — explicit, nameable reasons to refuse
4. Obtain current-state evidence via a bounded training trial when the cheap 

Evaluation: Testbed: a 4B model adapted across three distinct domains — finance reasoning, text-to-SQL, and function calling.

The measurement that matters: candidate updates exhibit heterogeneous target and retention effects across the evaluated contexts. The same candidate helps one domain and hurts another. Under matched candidates, evidence, and compute, BCIT:

- authorizes fewer harmful updates, and
- attains higher equal-budget final-model quality

The conceptual contribution: The paper's broader claim: experience authorization deserves to be treated as a distinct problem in autonomous post-training, not as a detail of experience generation. Systems that conflate the two will accumulate a memory of what worked and apply it indiscriminately, which is exactly how a self-improving loop starts to degrade its own foundation.
